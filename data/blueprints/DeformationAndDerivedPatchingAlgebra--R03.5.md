# Patching modules

Part R03.5 of **Commutative algebra for deformation theory and patching**. This document is the mathematical plan; the accompanying suggested file is a non-exhaustive prototype of its proposed interfaces. The packet is a completed target-level planning pass and the stage is **planned**, with three explicit supplier gaps. All implementation statuses are unchecked.

Finite modules at Taylor–Wiles levels usually come without maps between different arithmetic levels. Patching first passes to finite quotients, keeps the actions and comparisons as actual data, and selects compatible representatives. A bounded presentation then produces a finite module over a power-series ring. The difficult conclusions depend on the presentation shape, the dimension of the action ring, and the precise component hypotheses.

This part retains the existing targets `R03.5/balanced-modules-and-module-patching` and `R03.5/kisin-patching-criterion`. It extends the accepted P7 precursor without editing it. The current detail policy is target-level, despite the older issue template’s finer-grained wording. Small cancellation, matrix and cutoff calculations stay in proof sketches.

## Scope and conventions

For the arithmetic theorems, O is the ring of integers of a finite extension of ℚ_p, π is a uniformizer, k is its finite residue field, and E is its fraction field. CNL_O(k) means complete Noetherian local O-algebras with a fixed residue label k and continuous local coefficient maps. The finite-data definitions also make sense for a more general complete coefficient ring; their algebraic prototypes expose the actual rings, module structures and maps.

Write S=O[[z₁,…,z_q]], with maximal ideal (π,z₁,…,z_q), and a=(z₁,…,z_q) for its augmentation ideal. In particular augmentation does **not** kill π. The action ring A and the original ring R are distinct. A map S→A must be constructed with the right action; it is a choice and is not canonical. Every assertion of a finite module refers to finite generation, while “finite ring” in the compactness step refers to finite cardinality.

A presentation has d generators and r relations. Neither existence of such a presentation nor balance implies that the first relation map is injective. The square case r=d has a codimension-one conclusion only after the support-dimension inequality. The free case r=0 gives a different numerical argument. Zero modules are allowed in the construction and in the balanced theorem, but all nonzero depth and positive-rank arguments state their nonzero hypothesis.

In the Kisin construction, m_D^[r] denotes the ideal generated by individual rth powers of elements of the maximal ideal. This is different from the ordinary ideal power m_D^r. For example, in characteristic p the former is zero in k[u,v]/(u^p,v^p), while the latter contains the nonzero monomial u^(p−1)v. The printed bound is r_m=s·m·p^m·(h+j), with multiplication by h+j outside the exponent. The zero-variable boundary is proved directly.

## Construction and targets

The dependency order is: balance and cofinal quotients; finite decorated data; compatible extraction; the presented patched module; continuous actions and augmentation specialization; free/balanced numerical results; simultaneous ring-module patching and the rational criterion. ### Balanced modules

`R03.5/balanced-module` — definition.

For a Noetherian local commutative ring S with residue field k and a finite S-module M, put β₀=dim_k(M/m_S M), β₁=dim_k Tor₁^S(M,k), and defect=β₀−β₁ in ℤ. M is balanced iff defect≥0. Equivalently, it admits an exact presentation S^d → S^d → M → 0 for some d; for balanced M one may take d=β₀. The suggested native predicate uses existence of a square presentation, and its agreement with this source definition is a separate comparison target.

Hypotheses: S Noetherian local; M finite; Tor is endowed with its genuine k-module structure.

Proof or construction:

1. Choose a minimal finite-free resolution; reduction modulo m_S has zero differentials, giving β_i equal to the ith free rank.
1. If β₁≤β₀, pad the relation map by zero columns to make it square. Conversely any square presentation bounds the minimal relation count by its generator count after minimalizing.

Source: [Frank Calegari and David Geraghty](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~fcale/papers/CG.pdf), §2.1, Definitions 2.1–2.2 and following paragraph, PDF p. 11. Defines the defect and balance through the first two Betti numbers and deduces a square presentation.

Uses: CG Proposition 2.3 condition (5c), PDF p. 12: Bounds the relation rank by the fixed generator rank; gives square presentations that survive reduction. This layer, balanced numerical theorem: Turns finite-level balance into a one-step patched resolution without assuming finite-level projective dimension.

### Balanced modules and square presentations

`R03.5/balanced-square-presentation` — comparison.

Under the preceding local Noetherian finite hypotheses, balance, β₁≤β₀, and existence of a square presentation are equivalent. There exists a square presentation with d=β₀ precisely when M is balanced. Square presentations may be nonminimal; they do not imply projective dimension ≤1 until injectivity of the first map is separately proved.

Hypotheses: M may be zero; minimal resolutions need not terminate.

Proof or construction:

1. Use the first two ranks of a minimal resolution.
1. Cancel unit entries in any finite presentation over the local ring, leaving β₀ generators and at most β₀ relations.
1. Preserve the selected basis of the generator free module when padding the minimal relation map.

Source: [Frank Calegari and David Geraghty](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~fcale/papers/CG.pdf), §2.1, paragraph after Definition 2.2, PDF p. 11. Square presentations are the usable consequence of balance; this target also records the converse via minimalization.

Acceptance: S=k[ε]/(ε²), M=k is balanced although its minimal resolution is infinite.

### Taylor–Wiles group rings as series quotients

`R03.5/cyclic-group-ring-quotients` — comparison.

Let O be the integers of a finite extension of ℚ_p, π a uniformizer, q≥0 and N≥1. Set S=O[[z₁,…,z_q]], a=(z₁,…,z_q), a_N=((1+z_i)^(p^N)−1)_i. There is a continuous O-algebra equivalence S/a_N ≃ O[(ℤ/p^Nℤ)^q] sending 1+z_i to the ith standard group element. It identifies a with the group augmentation ideal, and the finite-level transition maps with the quotient maps for a_{N+1}⊂a_N. Consequently S is the completed group algebra in this abelian finite-rank case; no general noncommutative Iwasawa algebra is introduced.

Hypotheses: O complete DVR with finite residue field of characteristic p; coefficient maps local; q=0 allowed.

Proof or construction:

1. The finite group ring is a finite free complete O-module; g_i−1 is topologically nilpotent since its reduction is nilpotent. Use R03.1 local series evaluation.
1. The relation quotient has finite special fibre, because f_i mod π=z_i^(p^N); R03.4 gives O-module finiteness. Its finite-module completeness makes the image of the polynomial group algebra closed and dense, hence surjective.
1. Construct the reverse map from the group generators 1+z_i, which are units of order dividing p^N; check both composites on O and z_i using continuous evaluation uniqueness.

Source: [Frank Calegari and David Geraghty](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~fcale/papers/CG.pdf), §2.2, Proposition 2.3 and proof, PDF pp. 11–14. Uses the finite group rings and their compatible power-series presentation, with augmentation explicitly identified.

Acceptance: At q=0 both rings are O and a=0. Do not identify the finite group ring with a finite ring before also reducing π.

### Finite cofinal patching cutoffs

`R03.5/cofinal-artinian-cutoffs` — theorem.

In the group-ring setting, b_N=(π^N,a_N) are descending open ideals cofinal with the maximal-ideal powers of S and with intersection zero. If H≠0 is a finite R-module finite over O, R is CNL_O(k), and J=Ann_R H, then d_N=(π^N,J^N) are descending open cofinal ideals of R with π^N R⊂d_N⊂π^N R+J. Thus R/d_N and S/b_N are finite, and d_N acts on H through π^N. These cutoffs are one permissible choice, not part of the invariant definition of patching.

Hypotheses: H≠0, so J⊂m_R; the zero-H case is treated separately in the balanced theorem.

Proof or construction:

1. R/J embeds in End_O H and is finite O by the existing faithful-module finiteness criterion; hence radical(π,J)=m_R.
1. Noetherianity implies that d_N contains a maximal-ideal power, while d_N⊂m_R^N.
1. The binomial coefficients in f_{i,N} tend to zero in every fixed low degree, so b_N eventually lies inside any fixed maximal-ideal power; π and the variable powers make each b_N open.
1. Use the pinned finite quotient criterion and completeness from R03.1.

Source: [Frank Calegari and David Geraghty](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~fcale/papers/CG.pdf), §2.2, Proposition 2.3 and proof, PDF pp. 11–14. Constructs the open ideals d_N and b_N used for finite decorated data.

Acceptance: The maps R/d_N→End(H/π^N H) need not be injective. Cofinality, rather than intersection zero alone for an arbitrary filtration, is part of the interface.

### Finite decorated patching data

`R03.5/finite-patch-datum` — definition.

Fix finite O-algebras T=S/I and D=R/J, integers d,r≥0, an augmentation ideal a⊂S, a finite T-module target H₀ killed by a, and a CNL_O(k) action ring A. A datum consists of a based T-linear map P:T^r→T^d, X=coker P (or a specified equivalence with that native quotient), an O-algebra action ρ:A→End_T(X), a continuous local surjection φ:A→D, a surjection q:X→H₀ with ker q=aX, and equality q(ρ(a₀)x)=φ(a₀)·q(x) for every a₀∈A. The D-action on H₀ is fixed and compatible with its T-action. Equivalence preserves the actual action, φ, q and the chosen presentation maps; a module isomorphism alone is insufficient. The presentation is part of the decoration; arbitrary changes of bases are recorded explicitly when used.

Hypotheses: All rings and modules carry the stated O-actions; T and D have finite cardinality. The augmentation ideal action means its image in T; all actions commute.

Proof or construction:

1. Use native finite free coordinate modules, linear-map range quotient and endomorphism ring.
1. Express the augmentation comparison as the exact quotient map q, avoiding a new completed tensor carrier.
1. Define equivalence by a T-linear equivalence intertwining both ring actions, the fixed generator projection and q; relation matrices in fixed bases reduce literally.

Source: [Frank Calegari and David Geraghty](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~fcale/papers/CG.pdf), §2.2, Proposition 2.3 and proof, PDF pp. 11–14. Four-tuples retain φ, X, augmentation comparison and based presentation; this formulation allows rectangular presentations too.

Source: [Richard Taylor](https://www.numdam.org/article/PMIHES_2008__108__183_0.pdf), §4, proof of Theorem 4.1, printed pp. 218–221, PDF pp. 36–39. All finite comparison data, including comparisons modulo λ among finitely many systems, must survive extraction.

Uses: CG Proposition 2.3 proof, PDF pp. 12–13: Finiteness and extraction require the presentation and ring action, not just a finite number of module types. Taylor proof of Theorem 4.1, pp. 219–220: Preserves action-ring maps, specializations and a finite list of mod-λ comparison maps at once.

### Finiteness of decorated patching data

`R03.5/finite-decorated-data` — theorem.

At fixed finite quotients T,D,H₀ and fixed d,r, the set of equivalence classes of finite patch data is finite. In particular there are finitely many possible continuous A-actions, not just presentation matrices. For X generated by d elements, |X|≤|T|^d=K. The commutative image E of A in End_T(X) is a finite local quotient of A with |E|≤K^K (treat X=0 separately); the descending maximal-ideal powers of E stabilize only at zero by Nakayama, giving m_A^(K^K)X=0. Therefore every action factors through a fixed finite quotient A/m_A^(K^K). Add finitely many fixed finite comparison objects without losing finiteness.

Hypotheses: A Noetherian local with finite residue field; fixed generator bound d; quotient T finite. A action is O-linear and commutes with T; no faithfulness of X as A-module is assumed.

Proof or construction:

1. There are finitely many matrices T^r→T^d; choose the canonical cokernel for each.
1. Bound the size of End_T X by all set maps X→X. The image of the commutative local ring A is local and finite, so its maximal ideal is nilpotent with a uniform cardinality bound.
1. Use the pinned finite quotient criterion for A/m_A^e, and count homomorphisms between finite carriers, maps to H₀ and comparison maps.
1. φ factors through a fixed finite quotient as well; take the maximum cutoff required by action and target D.

Source: [Frank Calegari and David Geraghty](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~fcale/papers/CG.pdf), §2.2, Proposition 2.3 and proof, PDF pp. 11–14. The source appeals to topological finite generation; the target expands the bounded-action justification needed for the finiteness assertion.

Source: [Richard Taylor](https://www.numdam.org/article/PMIHES_2008__108__183_0.pdf), §4, proof of Theorem 4.1, printed pp. 218–221, PDF pp. 36–39. The finite cardinality bound is independent of the original large arithmetic level.

Acceptance: A generator bound for X alone is not used as a substitute for an A-action cutoff.

### Compatible presentation towers

`R03.5/compatible-patch-tower` — definition.

For descending cofinal open ideals I_n⊂S and J_n⊂R, a compatible patch tower is a family of finite patch data over S/I_n and R/J_n with specified isomorphisms from reduction of level n+1 to level n. These intertwine presentations in fixed bases, both actions, φ_n and q_n. Their iterated composites define the native ℕᵒᵖ-indexed module functor; identity and composition are genuine equations. For an extracted tower, retain separately the two subsequence indices with their strict growth and original-level≥cutoff-level conditions. A tower is extra selected data; the input modules H_N are not assumed to have transition maps.

Hypotheses: Fixed presentation sizes d,r; each finite-level augmentation target is H/L_n H with compatible L_n. Cofinal ideals and all reduction isomorphisms are specified, not inferred from isomorphism classes.

Proof or construction:

1. Use the native category-theoretic inverse-system functor and actual reduction isomorphisms.
1. Compose the adjacent maps to supply all arrows; quotient coherence follows from their chosen definition.
1. Keep the subsequence witnesses separate from the abstract tower, so no false canonical choice is exposed.

Source: [Frank Calegari and David Geraghty](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~fcale/papers/CG.pdf), §2.2, Proposition 2.3 and proof, PDF pp. 11–14. The selected pairs (M_i,N_i) and fixed reduction isomorphisms provide the actual transition maps.

Source: [Mark Kisin](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v170-n3-p03-p.pdf), §3.3, Proposition 3.3.1 and proof, printed pp. 1157–1159, PDF pp. 75–77. The pair (D_m,L_m) is reduced with the ring and module together.

Uses: CG Proposition 2.3 proof, PDF p. 13: Makes the inverse limit and the limit presentation well defined after diagonal extraction. This layer, action and specialization constructions: Provides the equations required to lift maps, rather than merely coherent isomorphism classes.

### Simultaneous finite-data extraction

`R03.5/patching-extraction` — theorem.

Suppose for each m≥n≥1 one has a finite patch datum D_{m,n}, reduction to all smaller cutoffs is defined coherently, and the presentation sizes and finite decorations are fixed. Then there exist strictly increasing sequences m_i,n_i with m_i≥n_i, both unbounded, and chosen isomorphisms red_{n_i}(D_{m_{i+1},n_{i+1}})≃D_{m_i,n_i} preserving all decorations. Thus the chosen data form a compatible patch tower. No compatibility between the original arithmetic levels m is an input.

Hypotheses: Finiteness of equivalence classes at each cutoff, proved including the action cutoff. Arbitrarily large original levels supply a datum at every fixed cutoff.

Proof or construction:

1. At cutoff 1 select a class occurring for infinitely many original levels; repeat inside that infinite set at successive cutoffs.
1. Keep finite sets of concrete representatives and finite choices of isomorphisms at each finite prefix.
1. Use the pinned finite inverse-system section theorem on the nonempty finite extendible-prefix system; then choose original levels recursively from the retained infinite sets, larger than both their cutoff and the previous original level.
1. Recover explicit reduction maps and retain simultaneous comparisons.

Source: [Frank Calegari and David Geraghty](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~fcale/papers/CG.pdf), §2.2, Proposition 2.3 and proof, PDF pp. 11–14. Extracts a cofinal sequence of pairs and compatible isomorphisms.

Source: [Richard Taylor](https://www.numdam.org/article/PMIHES_2008__108__183_0.pdf), §4, proof of Theorem 4.1, printed pp. 218–221, PDF pp. 36–39. Performs the selection simultaneously for the finite list of character choices and comparisons.

Acceptance: A tower of bare isomorphism classes without chosen action-preserving maps does not meet the conclusion.

### The patched module from a limit presentation

`R03.5/patched-module` — construction.

For a compatible tower of based presentations (S/I_n)^r → (S/I_n)^d → X_n → 0 over a complete Noetherian local ring S, lift every matrix coefficient along the cofinal quotient system to obtain P_∞:S^r→S^d. Define PatchedModule=(S^d)/range(P_∞) using the native module quotient. Equip it with its finite S-module structure and maximal-ideal topology. Its canonical reductions and comparison with the native inverse limit are established in the next target, so this is a single carrier rather than a second definition of inverse limit.

Hypotheses: S complete Noetherian local with finite residue field; I_n descending open cofinal. Fixed finite presentation sizes and fixed bases; genuine matrix compatibility.

Proof or construction:

1. Use completeness to lift each of the finitely many compatible entries; compare cofinal cutoffs with native ideal powers.
1. Use the coordinate linear map and its native range quotient.
1. Finite generation descends from the generator free module.

Source: [Frank Calegari and David Geraghty](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~fcale/papers/CG.pdf), §2.2, Proposition 2.3 and proof, PDF pp. 11–14. Defines the limit module and obtains a finite presentation; this native quotient form is compared with that limit.

Source: [Mark Kisin](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v170-n3-p03-p.pdf), §3.3, Proposition 3.3.1 and proof, printed pp. 1157–1159, PDF pp. 75–77. For free finite-level presentations the patched module remains free of the fixed rank.

Uses: CG Proposition 2.3 proof, PDF pp. 13–14: Patches square presentations, then tests injectivity and applies the imported Auslander–Buchsbaum formula. Taylor Theorem 4.1 proof, p. 221: The zero-relation case returns a finite free S-module with its actual action.

### Presentation and inverse-limit comparison

`R03.5/patched-presentation-completion` — comparison.

For the preceding tower, the canonical map PatchedModule→lim X_n is an equivalence of S-modules and of their adic topological modules; PatchedModule/I_n PatchedModule≃X_n. Hypotheses: The fixed bases make the compatible matrix P_∞ literal. Finite S-modules are complete for the maximal-ideal topology because S is complete Noetherian.

Proof or construction:

1. Right exactness of quotient/base change gives each reduction equivalence from the finite presentation.
1. Thus M is compact and complete. Use the cofinal comparison to identify the canonical module with the limit of its reductions.
1. Alternatively lift the finite preimage fibres of P_∞ by the pinned finite-system section theorem. For completion exactness use the short exact sequence ker(S^d→M)→S^d→M, whose kernel is finite by Noetherianity.
1. Do not apply completion exactness to a presentation matrix assumed injective.

Acceptance: The identity and uniformizer matrices give distinct cokernels; all quotient maps are generator-compatible.

### Continuous action on the patched module

`R03.5/patched-ring-action` — construction.

### A compatible local power-series action

`R03.5/power-series-action-and-finiteness` — theorem.

Let S=O[[z₁,…,z_q]] and let the finite-level scalar image of S on X_n lie in the image of A. For the patched finite S-module M, compactness of A and closedness of the fibres imply image(S→End_O M)⊂image(A→End_O M). Thus M is finite over A. If the finite actions have fixed residue and are local on A/Ann_A M, there exists a continuous local O-algebra map iota:S→A with the same action on M. It is chosen by lifting the images of the finitely many variables to m_A and using R03.1 local series evaluation; the lift is generally not unique. If each finite augmentation action lies in the image of ker φ_n, then iota(a)⊂ker φ_∞+Ann_A M.

Hypotheses: A compact CNL_O(k) ring; M finite S, nonzero for the local image-ring argument. Augmentation containment is a separate hypothesis; it does not follow merely from scalar-image containment.

Proof or construction:

1. For each fixed s, compatible nonempty closed sets {a∈A : a and s have the same action on the first n reductions} give one common lift by compactness.
1. Any S-generating family then generates over A by image containment.
1. The quotient A/Ann M is complete local; the images of z_i lie in its maximal ideal. Lift them to m_A, evaluate power series, and compare actions on the separated limit.
1. To lift the augmentation kernel containment use the compact closed relation {(a,b): action(a)=action(z), φ_n(b)=0} at all cutoffs; quotient by Ann M gives the stated containment.

Source: [Frank Calegari and David Geraghty](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~fcale/papers/CG.pdf), §2.2, Proposition 2.3 and proof, PDF pp. 11–14. The scalar image is closed and the formal power-series variables can be lifted; finiteness and the augmentation ideal containment are separate outcomes.

### Numerical depth bounds for patched modules

`R03.5/patching-depth-numerical` — theorem.

Let S=O[[z₁,…,z_q]], of dimension q+1, and A a Noetherian local ring acting compatibly on a nonzero M finite over both rings through a local O-algebra map iota:S→A. Then depth_S M=depth_A M and dim_S M=dim_A M, where dimensions are support dimensions. (i) If M is S-free, depth_A M=q+1, so dim A≥q+1; if dim A≤q+1, equality holds and M is maximal Cohen–Macaulay over A. (ii) If M has a square S-presentation and dim_A M<q+1, the relation map is injective over the domain S by passing to its fraction field. For M≠0 its projective dimension is exactly 1 and depth_A M=q. If A is regular local of dimension q, the existing maximal-depth theorem makes M A-free. Full component support in case (i) is a separate R03.6 conclusion requiring its irreducibility hypotheses.

Hypotheses: Finite-dimensional Noetherian local rings; M≠0; module finite over both rings; map S→A local. Depth/dimension restriction-of-scalars is requested from R03.3; no equality for arbitrary infinite modules or nonlocal actions.

Proof or construction:

1. Use the requested finite-local-map depth and support-dimension comparison.
1. For S-free modules transfer the native regular parameter sequence (π,z₁,…,z_q), then use depth≤support dimension≤dim A.
1. For a square presentation tensor with Frac(S). Proper support dimension makes the cokernel vanish there, so the square matrix is bijective; its kernel is torsion-free as a submodule of S^d, hence zero.
1. M cannot be a nonzero free S-module with proper support, so AB gives projective dimension 1 and depth q; apply R03.3 maximal-depth freeness when A is regular.

Source: [Frank Calegari and David Geraghty](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~fcale/papers/CG.pdf), §2.2, Proposition 2.3 and proof, PDF pp. 11–14. The final proof passes from support dimension to an injective square relation map, then uses Auslander–Buchsbaum over both rings.

Source: [Richard Taylor](https://www.numdam.org/article/PMIHES_2008__108__183_0.pdf), §4, proof of Theorem 4.1, printed pp. 218–221, PDF pp. 36–39. The proof compares S-free depth with the separately computed deformation-ring dimension; irreducibility enters the support conclusion later.

Acceptance: For S=k[[u,v]], the residue field is unbalanced and has projective dimension two; it is not covered by the square codimension-one case. For A=O[[u,v]]/(uv), M=A/(u) is maximal Cohen–Macaulay supported only on one component; maximal depth does not supply full support.

### The balanced-module patching theorem

`R03.5/balanced-modules-and-module-patching` — theorem.

Let O be the integers of a finite extension of ℚ_p with p≥3, R∈CNL_O(k), and H a finite R-module finite over O. Let q≥1, A=O[[x₁,…,x_{q−1}]], S_N=O[(ℤ/p^Nℤ)^q]. For every N≥1 assume a continuous local surjection φ_N:A↠R and an A⊗_O S_N-module H_N such that: H_N is finite balanced over S_N; H_N/a_N^aug H_N≃H is A-linear through φ_N; image(S_N→End_O H_N)⊂image(A→End_O H_N); and image(a_N^aug→End_O H_N)⊂image(ker φ_N→End_O H_N). Then H is free over R. For H≠0 the patching constructs a nonzero A-free M_∞ and a local iota:S_∞→A, with ker φ_∞=iota(a)A, M_∞/aM_∞≃H and R≃A/iota(a)A. No complex patching theorem is an input.

Hypotheses: The two image containments and the A-linearity of specialization are mandatory. S_N is the group ring over O, not reduced modulo π; its residue field is k. H=0 is permitted and handled before constructing the d_N cutoffs.

Proof or construction:

1. For H=0, H is free of rank zero. Otherwise d=dim_k(H/πH)>0.
1. Use balanced-square presentations and the cofinal b_N,d_N cutoffs to form D_{M,N}; d is constant by the augmentation identification and Nakayama.
1. Extract while preserving the action, φ, augmentation quotient and square based presentation.
1. Construct M_∞, φ_∞ and iota; M_∞ is finite over A. As dim A=q<dim S_∞=q+1, apply the numerical square-presentation target to obtain A-freeness.
1. A nonzero finite free module is faithful, so augmentation containment improves to iota(a)⊂ker φ_∞. The quotient M_∞/iota(a)M_∞ is nonzero free over A/iota(a)A and also has action through R; this forces ker φ_∞=iota(a)A.
1. The generalized support and kernel-descent API stays in R03.6; this final paragraph is the elementary quotient argument for this exact source theorem, not a duplicate general descent node.

Source: [Frank Calegari and David Geraghty](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~fcale/papers/CG.pdf), §2.2, Proposition 2.3 and proof, PDF pp. 11–14. Retains the integrated target identifier and all hypotheses of Proposition 2.3, including the separate augmentation-kernel condition.

Acceptance: At q=1, A=O; a nonzero output is O-free before descent. Balanced H_N are not assumed free over S_N. The theorem gives integral freeness, stronger than the rational conclusion of Kisin’s criterion.

### Simultaneous ring and free-module patching

`R03.5/kisin-ring-module-patching` — theorem.

Assume the four finite-level conditions of the Kisin criterion stated below, with h+j>0. There exist compatible ring-module patch data and limits R_∞ and M_∞, a surjection C↠R_∞, maps S→R_∞↠R, and an R_∞-module M_∞ free of rank s>0 over S. With a=(y₁,…,y_h), R_∞/aR_∞≃R as B-algebras and M_∞/aM_∞≃M with their induced actions. Every map is continuous in the complete local topology. The cutoffs satisfy a uniform finite bound on the cardinality of D, independent of the large original level n≥m.

Hypotheses: O and B in the stated complete Noetherian finite-residue setting; h+j>0; s=rank_{S₀}M>0. For every n, R_n is a quotient of C and M_n is free over S/b_n with b_n⊂((1+y_i)^(p^n)−1)_{i≤h}.

### Kisin’s module patching criterion

`R03.5/kisin-patching-criterion` — theorem.

Let O be the integers of a finite extension of ℚ_p with fraction field E. Let B be a complete Noetherian local flat O-algebra with fixed finite residue field, a domain of dimension d+1, with B[1/p] formally smooth over E in the source’s complete-local sense. Let R be a B-algebra, M≠0 an R-module, h,j≥0 and h+j≥d. For each n≥0 assume O-algebra maps S=O[[y₁,…,y_{h+j}]]→R_n↠R and an R_n-linear surjection M_n↠M with: the ring map B-linear; ker(R_n→R)=(y₁,…,y_h)R_n; ker(M_n→M)=(y₁,…,y_h)M_n; b_n=Ann_S M_n⊂((1+y_i)^(p^n)−1)_{i≤h}; M_n finite free over S/b_n; and R_n a B-algebra quotient of C=B[[x₁,…,x_{h+j−d}]]. Then R is finite over S₀=O[[y_{h+1},…,y_{h+j}]] and M⊗_O E is finite projective and faithful over R[1/p]. Integral projectivity, freeness or faithfulness is not asserted.

Hypotheses: The source’s formal smoothness must provide regularity of C[1/p]; this interface is requested explicitly from R03.1 rather than silently replaced by algebraic Algebra.FormallySmooth. The S₀-action is obtained by specialization and must be independent of n; all residue labels and local maps are as in §3.3.

Proof or construction:

1. For h+j>0 apply simultaneous ring-module patching. S and C are Noetherian domains of the same dimension h+j+1, M_∞≠0 is S-free and has the C-action.
1. Lift the images of the S-variables from R_∞ to m_C through C↠R_∞. R03.1 local series evaluation gives a local O-algebra map S→C whose composite is the existing S→R_∞, so the C-action on M_∞ restricts to its S-free action.
1. Import the same-dimensional-domain finiteness lemma (Kisin Lemma 3.3.4) already contained in the integrated R03.3 depth/dimension target: C is finite over S. Faithfulness of the S-free module makes S→C injective. After inverting p the finite injective map still gives equal dimensions; the requested regularity of S[1/p] and C[1/p] makes M_∞[1/p] finite projective faithful over C[1/p].
1. Faithfulness kills ker(C[1/p]→R_∞[1/p]); C is O-flat, so ker(C→R_∞)=0. Therefore C≃R_∞.
1. A nonzero finite projective faithful module has positive rank at every prime; this property survives the quotient by a. Descend projectivity and faithfulness only after inverting p. Ring finiteness over S survives quotienting to S₀.
1. For h+j=0, d=0 and h=j=0. The hypotheses give b_n=0, M_n=M free over O and R_n=R a quotient of B. Apply the same-dimensional-domain finiteness lemma directly with O→B and M≠0 to obtain B finite over O. The domain B[1/p] is then a field and the nonzero O-free M gives a faithful B[1/p]-action. Flatness of B kills ker(B→R), so R=B. The generic-fibre module is a nonzero finite-dimensional vector space, giving the stated rational projectivity/faithfulness without the zero-exponent patch cutoff.

Source: [Mark Kisin](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v170-n3-p03-p.pdf), §3.3, Proposition 3.3.1 and proof, printed pp. 1157–1159, PDF pp. 75–77. Retains the integrated criterion identifier and the exact separation between integral finiteness and generic-fibre projectivity/faithfulness.

Source: [Mark Kisin](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v170-n3-p03-p.pdf), §3.3, Lemma 3.3.4, printed p. 1159, PDF p. 77. The domain finiteness and regularity lemma is imported from the existing R03.3 target, not re-planned here.

Acceptance: M≠0 is essential for the rank and faithfulness argument. B domain and the dimension equality are essential; maximal depth alone does not make an arbitrary reducible action faithful.

### Comparison of chosen patching constructions

Unrelated diagonal subsequences need not have identical action-ring maps or canonically isomorphic patched modules, and no independence of such choices is claimed.

Hypotheses: Actual coherent finite-level equivalences, not merely isomorphic underlying module types.

Proof or construction:

1. Apply the native limit universal property and transfer along PatchedModule.limitEquiv.
1. Check the inverse and all action/specialization identities by every finite projection.
1. Use cofinality only for a restriction of the already chosen tower.
