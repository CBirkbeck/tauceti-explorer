# Roadmap: logic and definability in number theory, Part II

**Summary.** Build uniform ring formulas for geometric prime divisors of finitely generated fields, use their intersections to define finite normalizations, and obtain parametric bi-interpretations with the integers. The end theorem assigns each finitely generated field a parameter-free ring sentence characterizing its isomorphism type among finitely generated fields. The only conditional case is characteristic two above Kronecker dimension three, where resolution above F₂ is required. This is a specification of the mathematical development; no theorem is claimed to have been formalized.

## Conventions

A finitely generated field means a field generated by finitely many elements over its prime field, represented by Mathlib `Field.FG`. It need not be finitely generated as a ring. For such a field K let κ be its relative algebraic closure of the prime field. Write δ(K)=trdeg(K/Q)+1 in characteristic zero and δ(K)=trdeg(K/F_p) in characteristic p. Thus δ(Q)=1, δ(F_q)=0, δ(Q(s, t))=3 and δ(F₂(s, t, u))=3. A global field has δ=1. In characteristic zero κ is a number field; in positive characteristic it is finite.

For fixed d≥3, H_d(K) includes finite field generation and δ(K)=d. If char K=2 and d>3 it also includes resolution above F₂ in its two-clause geometric sense: proper integral varieties admit smooth proper birational resolutions, and smooth affine varieties admit smooth projective compactifications with simple normal crossings boundary. Neither clause is replaced by an uninterpreted flag. No resolution assumption is added for d≤3 or for characteristic different from two.

A prime divisor has normalized discrete rank-one value group and a one-step drop in Kronecker dimension of the residue. A geometric prime divisor is trivial on κ. An ordinary finite place of a number field is arithmetic, not geometric. Additive values use the convention w(0)=∞ in prose. Tau Ceti's integer function `Place.ord` instead takes the junk value zero at zero; every translation to that function has an explicit zero branch or a nonzero hypothesis. Henselizations, rather than completions, are used in the formulas; standard local comparisons supply completion results when required.

For nonzero slots use q_{a_d,…, a₀} with outer binary factors ⟨1,−a_i⟩ in characteristic different from two. In characteristic two the outer factors are bilinear and the last quadratic factor is x²+xy+a₀y². A two-fold tail always has four variables and is nonsingular. All valuations in compactness range over arbitrary ranks; discreteness is obtained after coarsening and the one-variable comparison.

Uniformity means one natural parameter length and one ring formula on the entire specified class of fields. Parameter tuples may repeat the same subset. Every failed tuple must give the empty set; it must not accidentally give the whole field. An intersection over no valuation rings is K, and a union over no balls is empty. These two conventions are used explicitly.

All page numbers for Dittmann–Pop refer to the final author version dated 27 April 2023, not to the published Annals pagination. Poonen and AKNS locators also use the author copies named in the references. Pop 2017 locators use the published page numbers.

### 0.2 uniform definable families with empty fibers

For a fixed natural m and a ring formula Φ(x; y₁,…, y_m), the fiber in a field K at b∈K^m is {x∈K:K satisfies Φ(x; b)}. A family of subrings or valuation rings is uniformly definable on a class C if one such m and Φ give every member on every K∈C and every fiber is either an allowed member or empty. Formula and arity are independent of K; repetitions of the same subset are allowed.

The object variable has one field coordinate; parameter length m is fixed for the class.

Use the existing ring language and its semantics.

Admissibility can be conjoined to a raw fiber formula; failed tuples then give the empty set.

The interface consists of the following operations and comparisons.

- `UniformDefinableFamily.fiber`: Evaluate Φ at a tuple to obtain its subset of K.

- `UniformDefinableFamily.fiber_definable`: After parameters b are named, the fiber is Set.Definable in the ring language.

- `UniformDefinableFamily.guard`: Conjoin a parameter condition P; the resulting fiber equals the old one when P holds and is empty otherwise.

- `UniformDefinableFamily.transport`: Field isomorphisms transport parameter tuples and their fibers.

These serve Recipe 4.3 — filters raw stabilizers; Proposition 5.1 — quantifies over all valuation-ring parameters.

**Checks.**

- A formula that gives K on some failed parameter is not a family of proper valuation rings or empty.

- A false parameter guard gives the empty subset, which contains neither zero nor one.

- Naming a fixed tuple agrees with Set.Definable; letting the tuple length depend on K does not meet uniformity.

(Philip Dittmann and Florian Pop, §1 Theorem 1.3, p.2; Recipe 4.3, p.15.)

### 5.6 arithmetic flags detect nonintegral coefficients

For a number field κ, its integer ring A and an e-tuple T of independent variables, the intersection of all arithmetic composite rings in V_T inside κ(T) is A[T].

For e=0 this is the usual finite-prime intersection A=⋂_vO_v.

### 5.8 a uniform definable finitely generated normalization

For each fixed Kronecker dimension d there is a fixed-arity ring formula defining R_T inside every finitely generated K of that dimension, for each transcendence basis T over its constants, with the characteristic-two resolution hypothesis only when d>3. R_T is a normal finitely generated ring and Frac(R_T)=K.

The formula may name T and the fixed-length codes used by its definable subfield families. It is uniform in those parameters, not a uniform choice of a transcendence basis.

In positive characteristic use R_T=S_T and geometric-intersection.

In characteristic zero use arithmetic-intersection and interpret each finite-length residue flag; apply the global finite-place formulas in the terminal number field. At e=0 use the integer-ring formula directly.

At fixed d the characteristic-zero and positive-characteristic lengths of T are d−1 and d. Use a finite characteristic split or pad tuples with dummy parameters with a stated guard. No formula quantifies over polynomials of unbounded degree.

(Philip Dittmann and Florian Pop, Proposition 5.1 and Remark 5.4, pp.16–17.)

### Examples

For K=Q and empty T, the geometric normalization is Q and the arithmetic normalization is Z. The geometric flag is empty; appending every finite prime gives the usual intersection Z=⋂_p Z_(p). Thus 1/2 is detected at the terminal 2-adic place. Omitting the empty flag would incorrectly return Q.

For K=Q(s), T={s}, the geometric intersection is Q[s], whereas the arithmetic intersection is Z[s]. To exclude f=(s+2)/3, reduce its primitive numerator modulo three and choose s↦2 in F₃×. ## Layer 7: single sentences for finitely generated fields

### 7.1 finite-language arithmetic bi-interpretation implies qfa

Let M be a finitely generated structure in a finite first-order language. If M is parametrically bi-interpretable with Z, then a single parameter-free sentence characterizes M up to isomorphism among the finitely generated structures in that language.

Finite generation is as a structure in the specified language. This is not finite axiomatizability among arbitrary structures.

### 7.2 total inverse and finite generation

Expand the ring language by one unary inverse symbol, interpreted by inv(0)=0 and inv(x)=x⁻¹ for x≠0. A field is finitely generated as a field iff its total-inverse expansion is finitely generated as a structure. This expansion is definitional on fields, preserves parametric bi-interpretability and admits effective elimination of inverse terms from sentences on the class of fields.

The graph of inverse is (x=0 and y=0) or xy=1; it is functional and total on fields.

A substructure closed under the field operations and total inverse is a subfield; this proves the generation equivalence.

Interpretations and comparison maps transfer across this definitional expansion. No inverse symbol is added to the final ring sentence.

### 7.4 a single field axiom in every characteristic

For every finitely generated field K, if resolution above F₂ holds when char K=2 and δ(K)>3, there is a parameter-free sentence θ_K in the ring language such that for every finitely generated field L, L satisfies θ_K iff L≅K as fields. The comparison fields L carry no resolution assumption.

For char K≠2, and for char K=2 with δ(K)≤3, there is no conditional resolution premise.

For infinite K apply main-biinterpretability, transfer to the finite total-inverse language, and use qfa-transfer there.

The QFA assertion ranges over all finitely generated inverse-field structures, so no geometric or resolution hypothesis is imposed on the comparison field L.

Check in particular: The sentence distinguishes isomorphism classes among all finitely generated fields, not just fields already satisfying H_d. No claim of a unique arbitrary-field model is made.

## Downstream consumers

The uniform geometric-divisor family is the interface for defining subrings by intersections; it includes all-fibers control so later universal quantifiers cannot accidentally range over spurious subsets. The normalization formula exports a finitely generated normal domain with the original field as fraction field. Arithmetic bi-interpretability then exports definable comparison graphs, not just a pair of interpretations. The single-sentence theorem is the final consumer of those graphs and the finite-generation language bridge.

Functional transcendence for Shimura uniformization has a separate build:weakly-special geometry and definability, Hilbert families, pure Ax–Schanuel, fixed-arity jets and rigidity, jet Ax–Schanuel, differential uniformized loci, and mixed Ax–Lindemann. It consumes only the early o-minimal/counting prefix of LD.6 and early mixed foundations; its outputs feed the later arithmetic and mixed applications. It has no dependency back into the field-definability layers here.
