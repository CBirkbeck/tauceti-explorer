# Integral local models

This is the integral-local-model continuation of Geometric Satake over the Fargues–Fontaine curve. Its endpoint is a normal flat projective representative of the minuscule integral bound, with geometrically reduced special fibre. Two geometric applications are tangent spaces spanned by formal curves in the classical range, and Schubert curves through rational points without changing the residue field. General bounds have connected geometric tubes even when there is no scheme representative. The final arithmetic application identifies the tame unramified minuscule nearby-cycle test function in the parahoric Bernstein center.

The plan has nine layers. Every target is planned; it is not dependency-closed. The requests below are mathematical obligations, not assertions that a supplier already proves them. Nothing here is an implementation claim. The rational Gelfand proof of commutativity is an independent continuation, with all 29 routed items assigned in the final ledger.

## Conventions and boundaries

F is a finite extension of Q_p, O_F its ring of integers and k its residue field; E is the reflex field of the geometric cocharacter class. The integral level 𝒢 can be a full fixer, while 𝒢° is its connected parahoric. General-bound notation M^v denotes the v-closure; M^pol is a polynomial scheme closure; M^wn is the intermediate weakly normal representative; M^can is the canonical normal scheme. An EL/PEL naive determinant model is a fifth object and can have vertical components. Companion algebraic flags, perfected Witt flags and their diamonds are compared by maps, not conflated.

The KPZ convention uses a weight-one rank-d subspace of a rank-h representation. The SW convention uses a weight-one rank-d quotient, whose kernel has rank h−d. Opposite parabolics, inversion and duality must be transported explicitly. Highest weights belong to X*(T), cocharacters to X_*(T). Dominance differences use nonnegative integral coroot coefficients; rational-cone membership is insufficient. All central comparisons carry their reflex base extension.

General scheme tangent spaces already exist in Tau Ceti. The curve span is a submodule of that carrier. Being spanned by derivatives does not say every tangent vector integrates to one curve. The all-points tangent theorem excludes quaternionic D; the root tangent theorem and the minimal-stratum conclusion have the explicitly stated broader range. Rank-one unitary formulas using division by 2 require odd characteristic.

Scheme-theoretic nearby cycles on traits and diamond nearby cycles are separate inputs. Geometric Frobenius acts on Q_ℓ(1) by q^(−1). The unshifted constant-sheaf trace φ differs from the IC trace by (−1)^d q^(−d/2). The Bernstein function is normalized to have spherical image q^(−d/2) times the minuscule characteristic function. The plan does not prove global integral Shimura models, displays, very-good embeddings, shtuka tube comparisons, or the full fusion/AB equivalence.

### triple — Local model data and morphisms

A minuscule local model datum is (F,G,{μ},𝒢): F is a finite extension of Q_p, G/F is connected reductive, {μ} is a geometric minuscule conjugacy class, E/F is its reflex field, and 𝒢 is smooth affine with generic fibre G and connected component 𝒢° parahoric. Permit the full facet fixer as a quasiparahoric level. A morphism is a group morphism carrying {μ} to the indicated class and extending over the specified integral models; it is not, by definition, a closed immersion on local models.

### hodge-data — Hodge and abelian type; cocharacter conventions

A local Hodge representation ρ:G↪GL(V) is faithful, minuscule as a representation, contains scalar homotheties in its image, and carries μ to diag(z·1_d,1_(h−d)), h=dim_F V. A datum is of abelian type when its adjoint pair is isomorphic to that of a Hodge datum. The flag convention here selects the weight-one rank-d subspace. For SW quotient data the weight-one quotient has rank d and its kernel has rank h−d; use the opposite parabolic/dual representation to compare. KZ uses weights (0,−1); record inversion of μ and the resulting flag identification instead of identifying the two subspaces.

Proof route: 1. Attach the representation and its scalar and weight conditions to the datum. 2. Use kernels and annihilators to derive the rank and opposite-parabolic dictionary.

### bernstein-characterization — The unramified Bernstein characterization

Assume additionally G unramified and μ minuscule. Let d=〈2ρ,μ〉 and let z_(μ,q) be the unique parahoric central function whose image under the normalized Bernstein-to-spherical isomorphism is q^(−d/2)1_(K t^μ K), with vol(P′)=vol(K)=1 in their respective Hecke conventions and the standard idempotent conversion. Then φ_q=q^(d/2)z_(μ,q). The companion and mixed-characteristic coset bijection gives the corresponding function on G(E_r). The extra global abelian-type, p∤|π₁(G^der)| and no-D^H-or-hyperspecial-containment assumptions of KP4.7.11 are needed only for its Shimura application, which is not planned here.

Inputs: GeometricSatakeAndFusionPartII:LM.8/central-test-function, GeometricSatakeAndFusionPartII:LM.8/local-nearby-cycles, SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups:SR.4.

Proof route: 1. At hyperspecial level the minuscule model is smooth, so its unshifted trace is the characteristic function of the μ coset. 2. Use the Bernstein center isomorphism and proper parahoric pushforward to determine the central function by its hyperspecial image. 3. Apply the scalar q^(−d/2) normalization to define z, obtaining φ_q=q^(d/2)z. Keep the IC sign (−1)^d separate. 4. Use the root-data coset identification for the mixed-characteristic interpretation.

Source: **PZ**, Proposition 9.15 and Theorem 9.16, pp. 75–76 (v4). Determines the central trace by its spherical image. Source: **KP**, §4.7.9 and Theorem 4.7.11, pp. 214–215. Converts the unshifted trace into q^(d/2)z and matches the two local-field coset spaces.

Check: For GL₂ with μ=(1,0), the spherical image of z is q^(−1/2)1_(K t^μ K), and φ has spherical image1_(K t^μ K). At hyperspecial level the IC trace of the smooth minuscule model is (−1)^d q^(−d/2), not (−1)^d q^(d/2), on the support K t^μ K.

## Source corrections

The following descriptions are paraphrases, restricted to the inspected editions. They are not claims about an uninspected final text.

### E1 — Published Remark 4.1.5, p. 31

The hypersurface example x²+xyz=0 is described as normal. It is reduced and reducible, and is not normal at the origin; retain the tangent obstruction. The factorization x(x+yz) gives intersecting components and zero divisors in the local ring. Its formal-curve derivative calculation still forces x-coordinate0. Reach: nothing. Already recorded in PAPER-KISIN-PAPPAS-ZHOU-26/E8; no new erratum claim.

Review (REV-DESIGN-GeometricSatakeAndFusionPartII): confirmed. Read on the published p. 31: the remark calls X a normal surface (and prints the variables as x, y, x). x²+xyz=x(x+yz), so X is the union of the smooth surfaces x=0 and x=−yz, which meet along x=yz=0; the local ring at 0 has two minimal primes, so X is reduced but not normal. The tangent-cone and formal-curve computation of the remark is correct for this X, and normality is never used.

### E2 — Published proof of Proposition 4.2.10, p. 35

The proof treats a dual Weyl module as if it were the Weyl module of the dual highest weight in positive characteristic. Use both FM inclusions directly and restrict to the minuscule test modules in the stated classical abelian-type application. For SL₂ in characteristic p, Weyl and induced modules of weight p have different highest/lowest module structures; a generic-characteristic duality argument does not transfer. Minuscule root strings have unit coefficients. Reach: the proof. Existing reviewed extraction finding; the packet keeps the explicit modular-computation supplier gap.

Review (REV-DESIGN-GeometricSatakeAndFusionPartII): confirmed. Read the published proof of Proposition 4.2.10 on p. 35. It transports a weight vector with X_αv≠0 from V(ν) to V(−w₀ν) using only weights; in characteristic p, V(ν)^* is the induced module H⁰(−w₀ν), not the Weyl module, and the stated equivalence of §4.2.9 (X_α acts nonzero iff both weights occur) fails: for SL₂ and the Weyl module V(p) with divided-power basis, X_α kills the weight p−2 vector. For minuscule test modules every root string has length at most one with coefficients ±1, which is why the packet restricts the bound to S.

### E3 — Published Corollary 2.3.16, p. 162

The ordinary-Grassmannian conclusion is justified by reducing to a single hyperspecial lattice. Use the closed chain-incidence embedding into the product Grassmannian and the fixed-rank idempotent direct-sum embedding. Repeating a representation does not turn a nonvertex building point into a hyperspecial vertex. The product embedding proves the desired target without that reduction. Reach: the proof. Previously identified in the reviewed PAPER-KISIN-PAPPAS-18 extraction; its corrected product-map argument is used here.

Review (REV-DESIGN-GeometricSatakeAndFusionPartII): confirmed. Read KP §2.3.15 and Corollary 2.3.16 on p. 162: the corollary is reached by asserting that, after composing with the embedding into GSp(V′), the point y may be assumed hyperspecial, but Proposition 2.3.7 is proved for the toral image of x, and the lattice ⊕pΛ_i is not shown to be that image. The chain-incidence and fixed-rank direct-sum embeddings planned in LM.2 give the conclusion without this step.

### E4 — Author preprint Lemma 5.2.5, p. 52

The translation lift is required to be a positive loop. Require t^λ∈G(F_q((t))); only its rational conjugator is in 𝒢(F_q[[t]]). Nonzero cocharacters usually give Laurent powers with poles. ### E6 — Author preprint Lemma 3.1.10, p. 20; cited FHLR Lemma 5.27, pp. 33–34
