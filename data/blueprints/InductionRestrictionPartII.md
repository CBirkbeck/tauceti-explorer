## Conventions and the imported spine

Write M(G)=H₂(G,ℤ), using Mathlib's second homology of the trivial integral representation. This is an additive group carried by a ModuleCat object, with inhomogeneous cycles and their quotient map already available. It is different from TauCeti.schurMultiplier k G, which is the second *cohomology* of the units of k with their trivial G-action. The latter classifies factor-set classes and is used by the parent; it is not an integral homology carrier. Factor-set normalization, classification and the splitting criterion already exist in Tau Ceti and are never defined again in this plan.

Commutators use XYX⁻¹Y⁻¹. The oriented torus class is [x|y]−[y|x], with coefficient 1, and the positive ordered basis of ℤ². Swapping the two inputs negates its homology class and inverts its kernel commutator. Whenever the two conventions are compared, the UCT class map must have this orientation. Merely knowing that an abstract finite abelian group is isomorphic to M(G) is insufficient for interpreting lift commutators.

A reduced multiplier is canonical and cover-independent. A reduced cover retains the chosen ordinary cover and its kernel identification; different choices need not give isomorphic extensions. In particular, D₈ and Q₈ are two ordinary covers of C₂². For an empty c they remain distinct reduced covers. Quotienting by the relations generated by all nonidentity elements of C₂² kills the central kernel in either case. The universal marked presentation, rather than the reduced cover, is the choice-independent object used for comparing models.

The set c is conjugacy-invariant for the reduced quotient. Generation is imposed when claiming a central *surjection* and the marked universal property. Closure under invertible powers is additionally imposed for the power-action stages. These hypotheses serve different purposes and must not be silently copied into every definition. In the LWZB component notation c consists of nonidentity elements. The local normalization used in Wood's global invariant additionally requires one conjugacy class and a chosen c₀. The arithmetic lift, tame local conditions and global product remain with the arithmetic-fundamental-group consumer.

Put D=c/G and L=D→₀ℤ, equivalent to ℤ^D for finite D. The map δ:L→Additive(G^ab) sends a basis vector to the abelianized class of any representative. The universal degree map takes a marked generator to its class basis vector. Coordinates are integers: the inverse generator has negative degree. The subsets L_{n,≥M} impose nonnegative or positive lower bounds only when the consumer asks for them. Those bounded subsets are not groups.

In cover coordinates the universal marked extension is P=S_c×_{G^ab}Multiplicative L. Membership is an actual equality of the two abelianization images. The marking sends x to (x̂,e_[x]). The kernel K is central and commutative; in these chosen coordinates it identifies with M(G,c)×kerδ. This is not an identification of K with the finite multiplier: for the single involution class of C₂, U is ℤ and K is 2ℤ although the reduced multiplier vanishes.

## Proof contracts that affect every consumer

The reduced-cover homology image is Q_c, not merely a subgroup of it. Its proof uses exactness of the central-extension homological five-term sequence. The weaker zero composite used in Wood's companion proof is a consequence. UCT naturality and the diagonal splitting also prove that zero composite, but do not by themselves prove equality of the image. The packet keeps the stronger source target and names its extra supplier.

For the marked comparison, P→S_c is onto because the class-degree map is onto. It follows that [P,P]=[S_c,S_c]×0. The stem property then gives P^ab≅Multiplicative L. The presentation abelianizes to the same free lattice on conjugacy classes. To construct a marked map P→E, first split the pullback by arbitrary-kernel UCT: the Ext term vanishes because P^ab is free, and the homological class map is zero after reduction. If the split map sends a marked point to s(x)k_x, extend the discrepancies on the free degree basis to ψ. The corrected map is φ·ψ⁻¹. Multiplying by ψ would produce s(x)k_x² and fail already for a C₃ discrepancy. The ratio of two marked maps is a central-kernel homomorphism vanishing on the degree basis, so it is zero. This proves uniqueness without assuming that the chosen marked points generate P before the comparison has been established.

The unit-power action on a nonabelian finite group is a set permutation. Inversion in S₃ is the immediate acceptance test against an erroneous homomorphism claim. The class-degree action permutes coordinates: m^α(d^α)=m(d). It does not multiply the integer coordinates by α. The correction W_α(m) is a genuine homomorphism from the free degree lattice into the reduced central kernel. Its composition law is W_{αβ}(m)=W_β(m)^αW_α(m^β). This is the missing calculation that makes the coordinate formula an action. The discrete action on U need not preserve products. On K it does, because its first coordinate belongs to the abelian central kernel. The action restricts to group automorphisms of K. This distinction corrects the parenthetical assertion in both the LWZB preprint and the published article without changing the set-level applications.

The finite-level bound is |G|²: a residue modulo that integer determines the action even though U itself is infinite. The argument uses the multiplier exponent dividing |G|. A cyclotomic twist is a set of equivariant functions from a simply transitive unit torsor. Evaluation at one generator is a bijection, but depends on that generator. Replacing equivariant maps by all maps or by fixed points produces the wrong cardinality even for the regular C₂ torsor. The orbit set 𝔖 removes the choice of generator in the degree invariant. A q-fixed degree vector is constant on q-power orbits, and its total degree becomes a *weighted* sum of orbit coordinates.

For involution classes, all chosen lift squares are central. With g=1 or g∈c, write b(g,m)=Y²∏(X_i²)^−m_i. Compatibility δ(m)=[g] is checked first. If q is odd and r=(q−1)/2, the fixed-fiber equation is h^{q−1}=b(g,m)^−r. Its solvability is equivalent to b^r being a 2r-th power. The existing power-fiber/kernel equivalence then gives the torsion count. It gives no nonemptiness theorem by itself. The special parities of Proposition 4.1 make b a square and therefore prove nonemptiness. Other parities can fail the solvability test.

For finite abelian A, b^r is a 2r-th power precisely when b is a square times an r-torsion element. The image of A[2^t] in the elementary-2 quotient gives an increasing filtration B_t. Its exhaustion uses the finite primary decomposition; it is not the 2-torsion subgroup itself. In C₈ the image of A[2] is zero and the nonzero square class appears only at t=3. The threshold is the first such image containing b and the criterion is v₂(q−1)≥threshold+1.

The class images give a surjective map a:F₂^N→Additive(G^ab), and the lift-square columns define s:F₂^N→A/A². Only this free-vector-space map s is linearized; no nonabelian square map is used. Combine the abelianization and quotient obstruction in L_t. The surviving parities of a g-signature are v_g+kerL_t, with v_g=0 or the appropriate class basis vector. Rank-nullity counts them, while the finite weight enumerator retains their different degree profiles. Equal signature parity cardinalities do not imply equal weight polynomials or fixed-degree counts. The source's screened candidates with no nonzero obstruction are evidence about those candidates, not a proof that the general obstruction vanishes or that a disputed limit exists.

For Lemma 12.10, finite coprime H and Γ suffice for the cover construction. Separate M(H⋊Γ) into its H-primary and Γ-primary parts. The central extension over H splits uniquely by Schur–Zassenhaus and coprime uniqueness, giving a characteristic Hall preimage D and the quotient cover S/D of Γ. Naturality of the class map, its primary support and the split homology surjection identify the kernel with the H-primary part. Admissibility is needed for the equal-order inertia classes to generate H⋊Γ and for its abelianization to reduce to Γ^ab. The reduced multiplier kernel is a quotient of the unreduced H-primary kernel, not necessarily equal to it. A count of power roots across this map additionally needs its kernel prime to the power exponent; being prime to |Γ| does not imply prime to arbitrary q−1.

A finite central surjection cannot be passed to the reduction algorithm as a certified Schur cover merely because GAP calls it one. Tables and arithmetic embedding labels remain evidence until the exact group, marked c, map, maximality/class identification and generated relations are checked. For the order-96 A₄ type, the sum-kernel condition is ab(a)+ab(b)=0 and the involution swaps factors. Its kernel has order 48. A difference condition or arbitrary semidirect-product action specifies different input data. The reported multiplier C₂ requires a certified cover with the reported C₂³ kernel and a certified order-4 reduction subgroup.

### Finite power action

Node `InductionRestrictionPartII:RS.3/finite-power` · definition · unchecked.

For a central extension E→G, the subgroup generated by all κ_E(x,y) with x∈c,y∈C_G(x) equals that generated using one representative per class in c and any group-generating set of its centralizer.

Construction or proof: At fixed x the commutator-of-lifts map C_G(x)→A is a homomorphism because its values are central. Conjugating both arguments preserves the central result.

Prerequisites: InductionRestrictionPartII:RS.1/lift-commutator, InductionRestrictionPartII:RS.1/class-compatible-marking, InductionRestrictionPartII:RS.6/centralizer-commutator-hom.

Acceptance: Do not use arbitrary generators of G; they need not belong to the relevant centralizer.

Source: wood, §8.2 Table 2 p.419; §2.3 p.387; certified computation requirements are this plan’s derived interface.

### Finite reduced-cover certificate

Node `InductionRestrictionPartII:RS.6/reduction-certificate` · construction · unchecked.

A certificate for explicit finite groups E,G and π:E→G contains executable multiplication/inverse tables, a surjective homomorphism π, centrality of its kernel, an independently checked ordinary Schur-cover certificate (stem plus maximal kernel/class-map isomorphism), class representatives for c, generating sets for their centralizers, and the subgroup R generated by their lift commutators. Its conclusion identifies A=kerπ/R with M(G,c). Raw GAP output does not supply the cover certificate.

### Finite reduced-cover parity algorithm

### Odd-index-two homology reduction

Node `InductionRestrictionPartII:RS.6/odd-index-two-reduction` · theorem · unchecked.

For finite H normal in G with |H| odd and G/H=C₂, M(G)≅M(H)_{C₂} and M(G) has odd order. For any union c of involution classes, Q_c=0 and M(G,c)=M(G).

Construction or proof: The integral Lyndon–Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence collapses in degree 2 because higher C₂-homology on odd-order modules vanishes and M(C₂)=0. The homological commuting-pair class with an involution entry is killed by 2, so vanishes in the odd group.

Prerequisites: InductionRestrictionPartII:RS.1/homological-commutator, InductionRestrictionPartII:RS.1/reduced-multiplier, tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/RepresentationTheory/InductionRestriction#layer-7-projective-representations-factor-sets-and-the-schur-multiplier, InductionRestrictionPartII:RS.1/commutator-order.

Acceptance: This does not extend to even-order H.

Source: evw, Example 9.3.2 p.57; commuting-pair bilinearity follows from the oriented torus class.

### The order-96 A₄ arithmetic type

Node `InductionRestrictionPartII:RS.6/order96-type` · construction · unchecked.

Use the explicit embedding F⊆A₄≀C₂ and c its outside order-2 elements for the reduced multiplier, rather than an unmarked abstract SmallGroups label.

Construction or proof: The sum map A₄²→C₃ is onto and preserved by swap; form its kernel and restricted semidirect product. The finite embedded-type enumeration belongs to ST.3.

Prerequisites: mathlib:GroupExtension, mathlib:Abelianization.of.

- `TauCeti.ReducedSchur.order96_type_swap` (structure): Swap preserves K and squares to the identity.
- `TauCeti.ReducedSchur.order96_type_order` (example): |K|=48 and |F|=96.
- `TauCeti.ReducedSchur.order96_type_outside` (projection): The quotient F→C₂ is the swap coordinate; c is its outside involution set.
