# Arithmetic Galois representations: recognition by Frobenius polynomials

## R01.5 and its boundary

This document refines the recognition layer of the accepted [parent blueprint](../packets/ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations.json). That blueprint supplies 53 R01.5 target declarations, including Frobenius density, recognition of semisimplifications, coefficient descent, Haar-measure Chebotarev and the two GSp₄ semisimplicity criteria. Their IDs remain unchanged. The accompanying [part packet](../packets/ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations--R01.5.json) adds the separate carriers and image-algebra lemmas requested by the parent coverage record, the attached Brauer/index interfaces, and the rank-two Boston–Lenstra–Ribet bridge. Its [suggested file](../suggested/ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations--R01.5.lean) prototypes those additions; the parent suggested file retains the other signatures.

The layer starts with a continuous representation of an absolute Galois group and data at its unramified places. Its central recognition statement compares two representations over a common coefficient field: equality of characteristic polynomials at a density-one set of Frobenius places determines their semisimplifications. In characteristic zero, traces can replace characteristic polynomials. In residual characteristic, characteristic polynomials are retained. Recognition over an algebraic closure does not by itself give a model over the field generated by those polynomials. The image algebra and its Brauer class give the precise obstruction. The finite-field corollary removes that obstruction; the real quaternion example shows why the general theorem must retain it.

This is a complete planning pass, with R01.5 **planned**, rather than closed. Every target is a retained parent declaration or one of the additions below, and every missing mathematical input is bounded by a supplier request or a named gap. ## Conventions and hypotheses

A Frobenius lift is **arithmetic** Frobenius unless a curve statement explicitly chooses the geometric convention for both compared sheaves. For a place of a number field all places above it are allowed. Its conjugacy class is the intrinsic datum; choosing different places permits the conjugates needed in a basic open coset. A density-one set may omit infinitely many places. Removing a finite ramification or exceptional set preserves its density.

Algebraic recognition uses a group Γ and finite-dimensional representations over a field. The trace/image-algebra interface fixes fields k⊂Ω and a representation ρ:Γ→GLₙ(Ω). Write

- Z=k(tr(ρ(g)):g∈Γ), the intermediate trace field;
- B=Z[ρ(Γ)]⊂Mₙ(Ω), the unital image subalgebra;
- full span for the condition Ω·ρ(Γ)=Mₙ(Ω).

Z and B are defined without irreducibility, topology or a characteristic restriction. The algebra lemmas take full span explicitly. For an absolutely irreducible representation over an algebraic closure, the retained `ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.1/absolutely-irreducible` supplies it through Burnside/module density. Taking the conclusion as the interface also handles a representation over any ambient field for which it holds. A reducible representation need not satisfy it: the trivial representation of dimension two produces the scalar subalgebra.

The basis, dimension and canonical base-change assertions include n=0. The simple-ring, Brauer, index and coefficient-field comparison interfaces require n>0. The zero algebra is therefore never fed into a central-simple constructor. Central simplicity carries the existing predicates `FiniteDimensional Z B`, `Algebra.IsCentral Z B` and `IsSimpleRing B`, rather than a new combined predicate. The Brauer group is written multiplicatively, with neutral element 1. Thus β=1 means split; no additive zero convention is mixed into these signatures. All matrix and tensor modules are left modules, and conjugation means Pρ(g)P⁻¹.

For semisimple descent, the parent statements work over a **perfect** base field k, with finite trace fields for the irreducible constituents over its algebraic closure. Those conditions allow the separable embeddings of each centre to index the constituent orbit. The full-span algebra lemmas below need neither perfection nor finite trace-field degree. The abstract determinant quotient over an imperfect field has the separate qualifications of Chenevier Theorem 2.16; it is not silently substituted for the perfect-field constituent formula.

For the BLR input F is any field, including characteristic two. The source representation algebra is an onto map r:F[Γ]→M₂(F). A second representation σ has finite dimension N, including N=0. Its hypothesis concerns annihilation by the characteristic polynomial of each **group element**, without assuming semisimplicity of σ. This hypothesis is weaker than full polynomial-law Cayley–Hamilton annihilation in general degree. The rank-two kernel theorem is precisely the bridge that makes it sufficient here.

## How the algebraic proof reaches the baseline

The group matrices are closed under multiplication and contain the identity. Consequently their Z-linear span is already a unital subalgebra and equals B. Every b∈B then has trace in Z. Neither assertion needs a division by n or a semisimplicity assumption.

Assume full span and choose e₁,…,eₙ² from the group image forming an Ω-basis of Mₙ(Ω). The Gram matrix Q with entries Qᵢⱼ=tr(eᵢeⱼ) has entries in Z: each product is another group matrix. Its invertibility follows from the matrix trace pairing. Mathlib’s `Matrix.ext_iff_trace_mul_right` detects a matrix from all pairings with test matrices, so the pairing is nondegenerate in every characteristic. Since det(Q) is a nonzero element of Z, Q is invertible over Z itself.

For b∈B the vector with entries tr(beⱼ) lies in Zⁿ². Its coordinates in the eᵢ basis solve the Gram system, so they lie in Z. This proves B=ΣᵢZeᵢ. Ω-linear independence implies Z-linear independence and gives dim_Z B=n². The canonical multiplication map Ω⊗_Z B→Mₙ(Ω), x⊗b↦xb, is onto and has source and target dimension n². It is therefore an algebra isomorphism, fixed on pure tensors. An unspecified abstract splitting would not be enough for the matrix coefficient comparisons.

Centrality descends through the baseline `TauCeti.Algebra.isCentral_baseChange_iff`. Simplicity can be checked directly without constructing another generic descent theory. A nonzero two-sided ideal of B extends to a nonzero ideal in Mₙ(Ω), which is the whole matrix algebra. Extending a basis of the ideal to one of B shows that scalar extension preserves its injection and dimension. Its original Z-dimension is therefore n², and the finite-dimensional submodule equality criterion makes it all B. This argument explains both the n>0 hypothesis and why there is no new generic central-simple-algebra node.

There is a separate coefficient argument. Choose a splitting of B over an absolute separable closure Zˢ. Applying an automorphism of Zˢ/Z to that splitting and cancelling the semilinearity gives another matrix identification. Skolem–Noether makes the comparison inner. Hence its matrix characteristic polynomial is fixed by every automorphism of Zˢ/Z, and the Galois fixed-field theorem puts the coefficients in Z. To compare with the given Ω realization, embed Zˢ into an algebraic closure of Ω, extend the two splittings, and compare them there by Skolem–Noether. Matrix characteristic polynomials commute with coefficient maps and inner conjugacy. Their equality in the common extension and injectivity of Ω into it recover the original coefficient statement.

This route avoids Newton identities in characteristic dividing n!. It also avoids the incorrect use of all automorphisms of an algebraic closure to detect an **imperfect** base field: purely inseparable elements would survive that test. The separable closure has fixed field exactly Z. No generic reduced characteristic polynomial is defined here. A reusable reduced-polynomial/reduced-norm API is an extension of SemisimpleAlgebras, whose current layer 4 does not state it.

For n>0, the trace is the negative of the coefficient of Xⁿ⁻¹. Thus the coefficient field of all charpoly(ρ(g)) contains Z, while the preceding lemma puts it inside Z. The two fields agree under full span. This equality for a single absolutely irreducible factor is compatible with the warning that traces do not recognize arbitrary residual semisimple multiplicities: they can lose multiplicities divisible by the characteristic.

The determinant quotient comparison is likewise attached to B. The map φ:Z[Γ]→B is onto. If charpoly(bφ(x))=Xⁿ for all b∈B, its trace coefficient gives tr(bφ(x))=0 for every b. The Ω-span of B is the full matrix algebra, so the nondegenerate pairing forces φ(x)=0. Conversely a zero image passes every polynomial test after every scalar extension. This matches the imported universal determinant-law kernel and identifies the faithful quotient with B. Singular matrices alone are not an ideal kernel: the quantification over all left multipliers is essential.

The class β is now the existing Brauer-group class of B. If β=1, a Z-splitting identifies B with Mₙ(Z); scalar extension and Skolem–Noether give a matrix conjugator between it and the original realization. The converse uses the dimension of the Z-algebra generated by a Z-valued conjugated representation. The same reasoning over an intermediate L⊂Ω gives the extension-field criterion. Inverses are group images as well, so the conjugated representation lands in GLₙ(L), not just in matrices with L-valued entries. The index s is the existing index of B; a division presentation B≃M_a(D) gives n=a·s and s=deg_Z(D).

The source support for these attached steps is BLGGT, Lemma A.1.5 and its proof, printed p.85; Serre’s lecture written by Rouquier, Theorem 4 and its trace-Gram proof, p.3; and Chenevier arXiv v2, Definition–Proposition 2.18(iii) and the final kernel/trace-pairing criterion, pp.32–33. BLGGT’s stated descent theorem has characteristic-zero hypotheses. The characteristic-free basis argument here uses the read library trace-pairing statement and the Serre/Chenevier matrix algebra argument, rather than attributing the stronger generality to BLGGT’s lemma alone.

## Added declaration catalogue

Every name below is in `TauCeti.GaloisRep`. IDs have prefix `ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.5/`. API entries promoted to their own lemma keep a single declaration in the suggested file. The concrete tests are examples carrying the stated names in comments; they are not implementation claims.

### Trace field of a representation

**TauCeti.GaloisRep.traceField** — `trace-field` (definition). Z is the smallest intermediate field of Ω/k containing every matrix trace tr(ρ(g)). It is defined for every n, without absolute irreducibility, finiteness, continuity or a characteristic restriction.

Take the intermediate-field adjoin of the range of the trace function. Its order universal property supplies membership and minimality. Cyclic invariance of trace supplies conjugacy invariance.

API:

- **traceField_mem** (simp): For every g∈Γ, tr(ρ(g))∈Z.
- **traceField_le_iff** (universal-property): For any intermediate field L, Z⊂L iff tr(ρ(g))∈L for every g.
- **traceField_conjugate** (functoriality): If ρ′(g)=Pρ(g)P⁻¹ for a fixed P∈GL_n(Ω), then their trace fields agree as intermediate fields of Ω/k.

Unit tests:

- **traceField_trivial** (degenerate): The trivial one-dimensional representation has trace field k (the bottom intermediate field).
- **traceField_cubic_character** (computation): Over k=ℚ, Ω=ℂ, if a one-dimensional character has image exactly {1,ζ,ζ²} with ζ a primitive cube root, its trace field is ℚ(ζ), strictly larger than ℚ.
- **traceField_quaternion_real** (non-example): Over ℝ⊂ℂ the rank-two representation with image {±I,±A,±J,±AJ}, A=diag(i,−i), J=((0,1),(−1,0)), has trace field ℝ although some matrix entry is nonreal.

The recorded uses are BLGGT14, Lemma A.1.5, p.85 — The trace field is the scalar field of each absolutely irreducible image algebra.; ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.5/descent-obstruction-absolutely-irreducible — A field of moduli becomes a field of definition precisely when the attached Brauer class splits.; IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants:IHG.0/determinant-coefficient-subring — For a full-span field representation, compare this field with the field generated by all characteristic-polynomial coefficients..

Direct prerequisites: `mathlib:IntermediateField.adjoin`, `mathlib:IntermediateField.adjoin_le_iff`.

Source: BLGGT14, Lemma A.1.5, proof, printed p.85; SerreCarayol93, Theorem 4 and proof, p.3; ChenevierV2, Definition–Proposition 2.18(iii), p.32.

### Image algebra over the trace field

**TauCeti.GaloisRep.imageAlgebra** — `image-algebra` (construction). B is the unital Z-subalgebra of M_n(Ω) generated by the group matrices ρ(g). Its underlying Z-submodule is their linear span. The construction does not assert that B is central simple when the full-span hypothesis fails.

Adjoin the range of the group representation as a subalgebra over Z. Closure under multiplication follows from ρ(g)ρ(h)=ρ(gh), and the identity is ρ(1). The span comparison is promoted as a separate lemma.

API:

- **imageAlgebra_mem** (constructor): Each ρ(g) belongs to B.
- **imageAlgebra_le_iff** (universal-property): For any Z-subalgebra S of M_n(Ω), B⊂S iff every ρ(g) belongs to S.
- **imageAlgebra_span** (characterisation): The underlying submodule of B is span_Z{ρ(g):g∈Γ}.
- **imageAlgebra_trace_mem** (projection): For each b∈B, its matrix trace belongs to Z.

Unit tests:

- **imageAlgebra_trivial_rank_two** (degenerate): For the trivial rank-two representation, B is the scalar subalgebra Z·I (the bottom subalgebra), not all M₂(Ω).
- **imageAlgebra_one_dim** (compatibility): For every one-dimensional character, B is canonically Z-algebra isomorphic to Z.
- **imageAlgebra_cubic_not_base_span** (non-example): For the primitive cubic character over ℚ⊂ℂ, span_ℚ(ρ(Γ)) has ℚ-dimension 2, whereas B has dimension 1 over Z=ℚ(ζ).

The recorded uses are BLGGT14, Lemma A.1.5, p.85 — Its constituent image algebra identifies the descent obstruction and its multiplicity.; ChenevierV2, Definition–Proposition 2.18(iii), p.32 — This concrete image is compared with the faithful determinant quotient.; ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.5/simple-module-of-an-irreducible-representation — The central division algebra presentation controls constituent multiplicities and the Schur index..

Direct prerequisites: `ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.5/trace-field`, `mathlib:Algebra.adjoin`, `mathlib:Algebra.adjoin_le_iff`.

Source: BLGGT14, Lemma A.1.5, proof, printed p.85; SerreCarayol93, Theorem 4 and proof, p.3; ChenevierV2, Definition–Proposition 2.18(iii), p.32.

### Trace-dual descent of coordinates

**TauCeti.GaloisRep.imageAlgebra_traceDualBasis** — `trace-dual-basis` (lemma). If e_i=ρ(g_i) form an Ω-basis of M_n(Ω), then B.toSubmodule=span_Z{e_i}.

Form Q_ij=tr(e_i e_j)=tr(ρ(g_i g_j))∈Z. The matrix trace pairing is nondegenerate by Matrix.ext_iff_trace_mul_right, so Q is invertible over Ω and hence over Z (its determinant lies in Z and is nonzero). For b∈B, all coordinates of the vector (tr(b e_j)) lie in Z. Solving the Gram system gives the Ω-coordinates of b in Z. This gives the reverse inclusion; basis generators give the forward inclusion.

Acceptance: Over characteristic 2 in size 2, tr(I)=0 but tr(E₀₁E₁₀)=1. The pairing remains nondegenerate; no Newton identities or factorial inversions occur.

Direct prerequisites: `ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.5/image-algebra-span`, `ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.5/image-algebra-trace`, `mathlib:Matrix.ext_iff_trace_mul_right`.

Source: BLGGT14, Lemma A.1.5, proof, printed p.85; SerreCarayol93, Theorem 4 and proof, p.3; ChenevierV2, Definition–Proposition 2.18(iii), p.32.

### Simplicity of the image algebra

**TauCeti.GaloisRep.imageAlgebra_isSimple** — `image-algebra-simple` (lemma). Under full span and n>0, B is a simple ring.

For a nonzero two-sided ideal I of B, extend its Z-vector space to Ω. ### Faithful determinant quotient of the image algebra

**TauCeti.GaloisRep.imageAlgebra_faithfulDeterminantQuotient** — `image-algebra-faithful-determinant-quotient` (comparison). ### Polarization of rank-two group-element relations

**TauCeti.GaloisRep.rankTwo_polarized_relations** — `rank-two-polarized-relations` (lemma). There is an F-linear involutive anti-automorphism s of F[Γ], s([g])=det(r([g]))[g⁻¹]. For every x, x+s(x)−tr(r(x))·1∈J and x·s(x)−det(r(x))·1∈J. This one declaration collects the relations proved simultaneously in BLR Proposition 1.

The determinant character defines the anti-involution on the group basis and extends F-linearly. On 2×2 matrices it is the adjugate, a linear map satisfying adj(AB)=adj(B)adj(A). The relation for one group element gives x+s(x)≡tr(r(x)) on the basis and hence on every x. Apply it to the relevant group products and polarize to obtain the determinant relation for finite linear combinations. Follow the characteristic-free calculation of Proposition 1.

Acceptance: Works in characteristic 2. It extends relations from group elements, rather than assuming relations for every element of the group algebra.

### Kernel of a full rank-two image

**TauCeti.GaloisRep.rankTwo_quadratic_relations_kernel** — `rank-two-quadratic-ideal-kernel` (theorem). If r:F[Γ]→M₂(F) is surjective, then J=ker(r) as two-sided ideals.

Cayley–Hamilton gives J⊂ker(r). Work in F[Γ]/J with its induced onto matrix map. For x in its kernel, the polarized relations imply yx=xy* and show that the annihilator of x is a two-sided ideal whose matrix image contains matrix commutators. Since M₂(F) is noncommutative and simple, that image ideal is all matrices. Choose w annihilating x whose matrix image is I. The determinant relation gives w·w*=1, so w is invertible in the quotient and x=0. This proves the opposite kernel inclusion.

Acceptance: Surjectivity is the full-span/Burnside output; a reducible or scalar rank-two image is excluded. No characteristic restriction on F is used.

Direct prerequisites: `ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.5/rank-two-polarized-relations`, `mathlib:TwoSidedIdeal.ker`, `mathlib:IsSimpleRing.matrix`.

### Rank-two annihilation implies isotypy

**TauCeti.GaloisRep.rankTwo_annihilation_isotypic** — `rank-two-annihilation-isotypic` (theorem). Let r:F[Γ]→M₂(F) be onto and let σ:Γ→GL_N(F), with N finite. If charpoly(r([g])) evaluated on σ(g) vanishes for every g∈Γ, then there exist m≥0 and a Γ-equivariant F-linear isomorphism F^N≃F²⊗_F F^m, where Γ acts through r on F² and trivially on the multiplicity space. Thus N=2m and σ is semisimple and isotypic.

Extend σ to F[Γ]. The annihilation hypothesis kills each generator of J, hence kills ker(r) by the preceding kernel theorem. The module therefore factors through M₂(F). The full matrix action kills CH(det r), including all polarized relations: apply matrix Cayley–Hamilton after adjoining formal scalar variables, not only to group matrices. This verifies the imported module-recognition hypothesis. Apply the imported full matrix-module equivalence: the multiplicity space is E₁₁F^N and the module is F²⊗E₁₁F^N. Choose a basis of that finite-dimensional space to obtain F^m; the matrix action gives equivariance.

Acceptance: For N=0 take m=0. For a standard rank-two representation, take m=1. A nonsplit extension satisfying these relations cannot survive. The theorem is rank-two-specific. BLR §5, pp.327–328 gives a rank-three A₄ example where elementwise annihilation does not force isotypy; this is not replaced by an unrestricted-degree Cayley–Hamilton assertion.
