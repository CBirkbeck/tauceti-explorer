# Finite-dimensional representations of the classical groups, Part II: Rational symplectic-similitude characters

This Part II starts with the connected complex classical-group theory of
[Classical Groups](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/blob/main/TauCetiRoadmap/RepresentationTheory/ClassicalGroups/README.md),
especially its standard representation, highest weights and integral formal
characters. It adds the central-similitude variable and the comparison with
rational algebraic representations over Q. The motivating target is Hongjie
Yu's Remark 7.1.1, in §7.1 on printed page 64 of
[arXiv:1807.04659v5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.04659v5).

For g≥1 the two endpoints are the canonical ring identifications

\[
R(\mathrm{GSp}_{2g}/\mathbb Q)
\cong \mathbb Z[t^{\pm1},z_1^{\pm1},\ldots,z_g^{\pm1}]^{W_g},
\qquad
C_g(\mathbb Q)\cong
\mathbb Z[z_1,\ldots,z_g,t/z_1,\ldots,t/z_g]^{W_g}.
\]

Here W_g permutes pairs and exchanges z_i with t/z_i. The full invariant ring
is freely polynomial in g primitive exterior characters over Z[t±1]. The
second ring is freely polynomial over Z in those same g characters and t.
C_g is generated by the classes of the simple constituents of all tensor
powers of the standard representation, including the degree-zero power.
It is a ring of virtual representations, so its integer coefficients may
be negative. The adjective positive below refers solely to monomial exponents.

Arithmetic Statistics owns the general matrix similitude group in
`ArithmeticStatistics:ST.5/symplectic-similitude-group`. This document imports
that group and gives its coordinate presentation in characteristic zero.
Reductive Groups owns Hopf/comodule foundations, quotients, descent and
semisimplicity. Classical Groups owns the connected complex Sp classification,
character theory, exterior-power trace computations and dimension formula.
Lie Highest Weight supplies the Cartan-component theorem used by that theory.
This document adds the GSp central lattice, rational models, and the two
integral character-ring identifications; those general suppliers are imported.

The elementary invariant-ring development uses only explicit Laurent
monomials, reciprocal swaps and integral symmetric polynomials. Thus the
counting continuation routed as
`GlobalShtukasAndFunctionFieldLanglandsCountingPartII` can use the exponent
cone without using the classification or the representation-ring isomorphism.
Its route has no finer packet node in this snapshot, so no speculative
dependency on an unbuilt counting node is introduced.

## Conventions and reusable interfaces

All ranks satisfy g≥1. All coefficient fields have characteristic zero. Q is
the initial coefficient field; K/F denotes a field extension when scalar
extension is considered. No choice of an embedding K into C is assumed.
Representations are finite-dimensional rational algebraic representations:
equivalently, finite right comodules over the coordinate Hopf algebra. An
arbitrary representation of the abstract group GSp(Q) is a different object.
The split Grothendieck group agrees with the usual representation Grothendieck
group here because semisimplicity splits every short exact sequence.

Use the ordered basis e_1,…,e_g,f_1,…,f_g and the form
J=[[0,I],[-I,0]]. The source and pinned symplectic coordinate algebra use
hJhᵀ=ν(h)J. Since J²=−I and h is invertible, this is equivalent to
hᵀJh=ν(h)J, the convention used for the alternating pairing on vectors.
For the diagonal torus the first block has entries z_i and the second block
t/z_i. The multiplier coordinate is t, and a scalar cI has t=c² and z_i=c.
In particular scalar degree and multiplier exponent differ by a factor of two.

The additive character lattice is X_g=Z×(Fin g→Z), with (m,u) representing
t^m∏z_i^u_i. MonoidAlgebra uses the multiplicative wrapper of this additive
group. Denote the multiplier weight by e_t=(1,0), and the first standard
weight in pair i by e_i=(0,δ_i). The second standard weight is e_t−e_i.
The chosen simple roots are e_i−e_(i+1) and 2e_g−e_t. Their coroot pairings
cut out u_1≥⋯≥u_g≥0; they impose no condition on m. We call this subtype Dom_g.
The scalar degree is d(m,u)=2m+Σu_i, and every root has scalar degree zero.

Write E_k for the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree k in the 2g
letters z_i,t/z_i. Its index is an integer: E_k=0 for k<0 or k>2g and E_0=1.
For 1≤k≤g put F_k=E_k−tE_(k−2). Consequently F_1=E_1, without a correction
term. Primitive modules P_k are defined only for 0≤k≤g, with P_0=1 and P_1=V.
When an expression in the representation ring has a negative exterior index,
its value is zero; ordinary natural-number subtraction cannot encode this case.
The suggested file indexes F_(i+1) by i in Fin g and P_k by k in Fin(g+1).

Every construction below includes its direct inputs, the proof route, its
proposed API, and tests distinguishing the intended convention from tempting
alternatives. Each packet node is at target level. The tests are acceptance
statements for a future implementation, and all implementation statuses remain
unchecked. The reader is definitive; the accompanying suggested file supplies
unchecked signatures against the pinned vocabulary.

## CG.0. Algebraic similitudes, diagonal torus and central weights

Represent the imported matrix GSp group by its explicit finite-type coordinate Hopf algebra. Construct the diagonal torus and central-similitude lattice Z×Z^g, register its reciprocal Weyl action, and prove the central isogeny Sp₂g×G_m→GSp₂g with μ₂ kernel, its parity/descent criterion and the split connected reductive root datum. Check rank one, the multiplier-one fibre and non-surjectivity of the cover on Q-points.

The torus and the central cover do different jobs. The independent torus
coordinates give an integral group algebra, while the central cover explains
which Sp representations acquire algebraic GSp structures. On Sp×G_m the
map (a,c)↦ca pulls (m,u) back to (u,2m+Σu_i). Its kernel is the diagonal μ₂.
The scalar −1 and the Sp centre −I therefore act together by
(−1)^(d+Σu_i), giving exactly the displayed parity condition. Negative m is
allowed because an invertible multiplier coordinate has algebraic inverse.

The cover is an fppf quotient. For a point with invertible multiplier s,
adjoining a root of C²−s gives a finite faithfully flat algebra: it is free
of rank two and C is a unit. Dividing the matrix by C gives a symplectic
matrix. Over Q the point diag(I_g,2I_g) has multiplier 2 and no lift through
Sp(Q)×Q×. Surjectivity on rational points is therefore not an acceptance
condition. The scheme quotient and its representation descent belong to the
requested Reductive Groups interfaces, not to a new local theory of quotients.

### The integral central-similitude weight lattice

Node: `ClassicalGroupsPartII:CG.0/similitude-weight-lattice`. Proposed declaration: `TauCeti.GSpCharacter.Weight`.

Use X_g=Z×Z^g, with (m,u) representing t^m∏z_i^u_i. The multiplier weight is e_t=(1,0), z_i has weight e_i=(0,δ_i), and the second standard weight in pair i is e_t−e_i. The central scalar degree is d(m,u)=2m+Σu_i. Dominant weights are exactly u_1≥⋯≥u_g≥0, with m any integer; denote this subtype Dom_g. Pullback along (a,c)↦ca from Sp₂g×G_m sends (m,u) to (u,d), with image precisely d≡Σu_i (mod 2).


Atlas planet: **Central-similitude weight lattice**.


Direct inputs: the displayed integer lattice and elementary arithmetic.


Proof route:

1. Expand the diagonal character monomials: the torus coordinates t,z_i are independent invertible variables.
2. On scalar matrices z_i=c and t=c²; hence d=2m+Σu_i.
3. Simple roots e_i−e_(i+1) and 2e_g−e_t pair with coroots by u_i−u_(i+1) and u_g. Recover m=(d−Σu_i)/2 exactly when the parity condition holds.


Sources: yu-2022, §7.1, equation (7.1.1) and Remark 7.1.1, printed p.64; milne-2022, Chapter 22 §a, Theorem 22.2 and complements 22.3–22.6, pp.464–466.

- ClassicalGroupsPartII:CG.3/tensor-constituent-criterion: Tests nonnegative m and computes the exact tensor degree.


API:

- `TauCeti.GSpCharacter.multiplierWeight` (data): e_t=(1,0).

- `TauCeti.GSpCharacter.DominantWeight` (constructor): Subtype of X_g cut out by antitonicity and nonnegativity of u, with unrestricted integer m.

### Reciprocal Weyl action on the Laurent character algebra

Node: `ClassicalGroupsPartII:CG.0/weyl-action`. Proposed declaration: `TauCeti.GSpCharacter.invariantSubring`.

### The central cover and its parity constraint

Node: `ClassicalGroupsPartII:CG.0/central-cover`. Proposed declaration: `TauCeti.GSpCharacter.centralCover`.

For g≥1 in characteristic zero, (a,c)↦ca defines a faithfully flat central isogeny Sp₂g×G_m→GSp₂g with kernel μ₂ embedded by ε↦(εI,ε). Its pullback on diagonal character lattices is coverWeight. A rational representation of the product descends exactly when this diagonal μ₂ acts trivially. In particular the Sp highest weight u paired with scalar degree d descends iff d−Σu_i is even. ## CG.2. Integral torus characters and the full representation ring

Construct the integer formal torus character using existing weight spaces and SplitK0.liftRingHom. Prove algebraic highest-weight injectivity, define the fundamental exterior polynomials with the signed-degree convention, construct the integral full invariant polynomial presentation, and identify the primitive characters. Prove Yu’s canonical rational character ring isomorphism and the full polynomial generators over Z[t±1].

Here is the integral fixed-ring argument in detail. Put A=Z[t±1] and
x_i=z_i+t/z_i. In one reciprocal pair, an invariant is a sum of terms
a_0(t) and a_n(t)(z_i^n+(t/z_i)^n), n>0. Set p_0=2, p_1=x_i, and
p_n=x_i p_(n−1)−t p_(n−2). This recurrence expresses every pair orbit sum
as a polynomial in x_i with coefficients in A. The orbit at exponent zero
is the single monomial 1; it is not p_0. Repeating this decomposition for
each pair proves the fixed ring for independent flips is A[x_1,…,x_g].

Injectivity can be checked in the Laurent monomial basis: a polynomial in
the x_i has a unique leading all-positive z exponent for its maximal
multi-index, so its coefficient cannot cancel with those of smaller indices.
Permutations now act by permuting the x_i. The pinned integral fundamental
theorem of symmetric polynomials gives the fixed ring
A[e_1(x),…,e_g(x)], with algebraically independent elementary generators.
There is no averaging over W and no division by its order.

The generating polynomial is ∏(1+x_i U+tU²). To obtain U^k, choose j
quadratic factors and k−2j linear factors. Thus, for 0≤k≤g,

\[
E_k=\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}
\binom{g-k+2j}{j}t^j e_{k-2j}(x).
\]

It follows that F_k=e_k(x) plus integer-polynomial combinations of t and
earlier e_r(x), r<k. This triangular change of generators has diagonal
coefficient one. Successively solving it is an integral polynomial inverse,
so F_1,…,F_g are algebraically independent over A and generate the full
fixed ring. For g=2, E_2=x_1x_2+2t and F_2=x_1x_2+t; this small case detects
both the coefficient and the primitive correction.

The formal character takes a representation to the finite sum of its
integer weight dimensions. Tensor products multiply characters because
the weight of a pure tensor is the sum of the two weights; biproducts add
them. The existing SplitK0 lifting interface makes this a ring map. Weyl
normalizer representatives identify weight spaces, so the character is fixed.
The multiplicity-one highest line and strictly lower other weights give
injectivity: in a finite relation select a maximal highest weight in the
root order and inspect that coefficient. Primitive decomposition identifies
F_k with the actual character of P_k, and ν gives t with inverse ν⁻¹.
Since these generate the full invariant ring, the map is surjective as well.

## CG.3. Tensor constituents and the positive character algebra

Define the elementary reciprocal Laurent subring by its monomial generators and prove the precise exponent-support cone, independently of representations. Construct its integral invariant polynomial presentation. Define the ring generated by simple algebraic tensor-power constituents, prove the exact highest-weight occurrence criterion m≥0 at degree 2m+Σu_i, and identify that ring with the positive invariants. Establish integral generation by ν and the primitive exterior expressions and recover the full rational ring by inverting ν.

The exponent cone has an elementary normal form. Put w_i=t/z_i.
A product ∏z_i^a_i w_i^b_i has m=Σb_i and u_i=a_i−b_i, hence
h=m+Σmin(u_i,0)=Σmin(a_i,b_i)≥0. Conversely a monomial of exponent
(m,u) with h≥0 equals t^h times the product of z_i^u_i for u_i>0 and
w_i^(−u_i) for u_i<0. Since g≥1, t=z_i w_i is among the generated
elements. This proves the support criterion, using the Laurent monomials
as a free integer basis. The function h is not additive; its superadditivity,
or the equivalent family m+Σ_(i∈S)u_i≥0 for every subset S, shows that
the exponent set is closed under addition. Pair flips and permutations
preserve h, so the subring is Weyl-stable.

As a Z[t]-module the elementary subring has basis the products choosing
one of z_i^n or w_i^n for each n>0, and the single choice 1 for n=0.
The independent-flip invariants are generated by the products of their
orbit sums. The same recurrence as above expresses them in Z[t,x_i].
Only nonnegative powers of t occur. Integer symmetric-polynomial theory
and the same triangular F_k substitution now give
B_g^W=Z[t,F_1,…,F_g], freely polynomial over Z. This proof uses no
representation category and can be consumed by the counting continuation.

For a dominant highest weight (m,u), the support inequality on its highest
monomial is simply m≥0. Every weight of V^⊗d is a product of d standard
letters, so any simple summand has m≥0 and central degree d. Conversely,
P_k splits out of V^⊗k by antisymmetrization and semisimplicity, and ν
splits out of V^⊗2 via the alternating pairing. The Cartan component of
∏P_k^⊗a_k then splits out of degree Σk a_k=Σu_i. Tensor with ν^m for
m≥0 to obtain L(m,u) in precisely degree 2m+Σu_i. This is an exact
occurrence criterion, stronger than existence in an unspecified tensor power.

The character of every such constituent belongs to B_g. Therefore the
character-ring isomorphism restricts to an injective map C_g→B_g^W.
Its image contains t and F_k because ν and P_k are constituents; the
pure integral presentation supplies surjectivity. It follows that C_g is
freely generated by [ν] and [P_k] as a polynomial ring over Z. Negative
integer coefficients are allowed, but [ν⁻¹] is excluded. In rank one,
ν is a summand of V⊗V, yet [ν] does not belong to the ring generated by
[V] alone: its character t is independent of x=z+t/z. Finally inverting
[ν] recovers the full ring, since every finite Laurent polynomial in t
can have its denominators cleared by a sufficiently high nonnegative power.

### The exact highest-weight criterion for tensor occurrence

Node: `ClassicalGroupsPartII:CG.3/tensor-constituent-criterion`. Proposed declaration: `TauCeti.GSpCharacter.tensorConstituentCriterion`.

For λ=(m,u)∈Dom_g, L(λ) occurs in a tensor power V^⊗d iff m≥0. More precisely, it occurs in V^⊗d iff m≥0 and d=2m+Σu_i. Every torus weight of V^⊗d lies in the support cone of B_g. Thus ch(L(λ)) belongs to B_g whenever m≥0; ν⁻¹ is excluded despite being a rational algebraic representation.


Atlas planet: **Tensor occurrence highest-weight criterion**.


Direct inputs: [The subring generated by tensor-power constituents](#tensor-constituent-subring); [Primitive exterior decomposition and fundamental highest weights](#primitive-exterior-decomposition); [Rational models of the GSp highest-weight modules](#irreducible-rational-model); [Highest-weight classification over Q and its extensions](#rational-highest-weight-classification); [The positive reciprocal Laurent subring](#positive-laurent-subring); [The integral formal torus character](#formal-torus-character).


Proof route:

1. A tensor basis chooses either z_i or t/z_i in each factor. Write its counts as a_i,b_i≥0: m=Σb_i and u_i=a_i−b_i. For a dominant highest weight this forces m≥0; scalar matrices also force d=2m+Σu_i. All such weights obey the support-cone inequality.
2. For sufficiency write u=Σ_(k=1)^g a_k(1^k), with a_k=u_k−u_(k+1). The preceding rational Cartan-product construction realizes L(0,u) as a summand of the tensor product of the P_k^⊗a_k, hence of V^⊗Σu_i.
3. For m≥0 tensor with ν^m, a summand of V^⊗2m by the pairing split. Semisimplicity and the central degree fix the exact tensor degree. Subrepresentation weights give the positive-character conclusion.


Sources: yu-2022, §7.1, equation (7.1.1) and Remark 7.1.1, printed p.64; sternberg-2004, §7.9, pp.131–132.
