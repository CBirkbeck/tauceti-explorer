# Explicit K₃ and Bloch groups: V.3 convention comparisons

This document supplies the convention targets left open by the accepted
`BP-K3BlochGroups` packet. Its scope is `K3BlochGroups:V.3`. The
[packet](../packets/K3BlochGroups--V.3.json) records the declarations and direct
dependencies; the [suggested signatures](../suggested/K3BlochGroups--V.3.lean)
prototype their carriers and interfaces. All declarations are plans with
implementation status unchecked. The pass is complete and the stage is
planned. The canonical geometry needed for curve specializations and the
injectivity of the all-curve comparison remain explicit gaps.

The base packet provides the Suslin presentation, the distinction between
antisymmetric and exterior tensors, the angle symbols, the element c and its
torsion, the repaired older CGZ presentation, and the small-field conventions.
Those declarations keep their existing ids. This part adds the full-tensor
lecture object of Bloch, Goncharov's two weight-two presentations, the
published CGZ modification, and the exact maps between them. Each comparison
specifies the integral correction before changing coefficients. A group with
the word “Bloch” in its name can be a free-cycle kernel, a pre-Bloch quotient,
or a quotient of a boundary kernel. Its name alone does not determine which.

## Conventions and inherited inputs

Let F be a field and let U be its multiplicative unit group, regarded as an
additive abelian group when forming tensor products. Set T = U ⊗ℤ U. The
inherited antisymmetric tensor quotient is

\[
 Q_s=T/\operatorname{im}(1+\tau),
 \qquad \tau(u\otimes v)=v\otimes u.
\]

The notation u ∧ v in this document denotes the image of u ⊗ v in Q_s,
unless an ordinary exterior square is explicitly named. In Q_s, twice a
diagonal tensor vanishes; the diagonal tensor itself need not vanish.
`K3BlochGroups:V.3/antisym-exterior-comparison` supplies the exact sequence
from U/2U into Q_s and then onto the ordinary exterior square. In particular
the two targets agree after rationalization, but substituting one for the
other in an integral definition can change its kernel.

Write A₀ = ℤ[F − {0,1}]. A symbol at 0 or 1 means zero in A₀. The inherited
five-term element is

\[
 R(x,y)=[x]-[y]+[y/x]
 -[(1-x^{-1})/(1-y^{-1})]+[(1-x)/(1-y)],
\]

where x,y avoid 0,1 and x ≠ y. Let R₅ be the subgroup they generate. Then
A₀/R₅ is canonically the inherited P(F), whose original presentation uses
F − {0} and kills [1]. Let q denote its quotient map. The inherited boundary
is ∂[x] = x ∧ (1−x), and B(F) is its kernel. The exterior-boundary kernel
inside P(F) is denoted B̃; this tilde is local notation for the inherited
`exterior-kernel-bloch-group`, not the published CGZ group's typography.

For |F| ≥ 4, the inherited constant c = [x]+[1−x] lies in B and is
independent of the nondegenerate x. The angle homomorphism h:U→P has
h(u)=[u]+[u⁻¹]. Its image H is killed by 2, and ∂h(u)=u∧(−u).
Thus H belongs to P and is not generally a subgroup of B. The relevant
Bloch subgroup is B ∩ H. The inherited identities give 3c=h(−1) and 6c=0.
The first identity also explains why removing the angle image can remove
the 2-primary part of c without removing its 3-primary part.

The projective line is the set F ∪ {∞}. In the extended CGZ presentation,
zeros and poles are actual free generators before relations. Projective
division permits its ordinary determinate values; 0/0 and ∞/∞ are forbidden.
Let C_ext be generated by all determinate extended five-term elements, with
diagonal pairs allowed when all displayed fractions exist. For |F| ≥ 4,
the inherited `cgz-degenerate-relations` identifies ℤ[P¹(F)]/C_ext with P/H,
sending [0] to c, [1] to zero and [∞] to −c. The older repaired group is the
image of B̃ in this common presentation. The inherited id
`K3BlochGroups:V.3/cgz-bloch-group` retains that meaning throughout.

## The comparison ledger

Every entry uses the indicated canonical symbol map. “Kernel zero” refers
to that map, rather than to a possible abstract isomorphism of groups.

| Object and map | Hypotheses | Exact correction | Coefficients giving an isomorphism |

| Published CGZ B_new → older repaired B_old | at least four elements | Kernel zero; cokernel B̃/(B+(B̃ ∩ H)), killed by 2 | ℤ[1/2], ℚ, and every positive odd modulus |

Here λ[x]=(1−x)⊗x takes values in the **full** tensor product T. It does
not descend through the ordinary five-term quotient: over F₅, R(2,3)=[4]
in A₀ but λ[4] is nonzero. This is why the lecture object needs a relation
kernel before its rational comparison is injective. Over ℚ the nonzero
free cycle 4[−1] belongs to that kernel and has infinite order. Killing
bounded torsion does not remove it.

For the lecture cokernel bound one can use an explicit lift instead of an
unspecified comparison theorem. If qα=β∈B, set σ[x]=[1−x] and let deg send
each nondegenerate symbol to 1. Then λ(α−σα)=0 and
q(α−σα)=2β−deg(α)c. The raw cycle 2[−1] maps to 3c, with the raw symbol
interpreted as zero in characteristic 2. Therefore

\[
 \gamma=3(\alpha-\sigma\alpha)+\deg(\alpha)\,2[-1]
 \quad\text{satisfies}\quad q\gamma=6\beta.
\]

The published negative tensor target is Q_neg=T/⟨u⊗(−u)⟩. The minus is
multiplication by the field unit −1. Expanding the relation at the product
uv shows that u⊗v+v⊗u vanishes in this target. Hence Q_s surjects onto
Q_neg, and the kernel is exactly ∂H when |F| ≥ 4. The published cycle
condition inside P/H is then represented by B+H. This gives
B_new=(B+H)/H=B/(B∩H), proving the published surjectivity. In contrast,
B_old is the image of the larger exterior-cycle subgroup B̃ in P/H.
The natural inclusion B_new→B_old is constructed inside this common
presentation. There is no general tensor projection from Q_neg to the
ordinary exterior square that could construct it instead.

The fields F₂ and F₃ require direct calculation. For F₂ the complete
extended relations give [1]=0, [∞]=2[0], and 3[0]=0. Its published group
is ℤ/3 since Q_neg=0; the Suslin group is zero. For F₃ the relations give
[0]=[1]=[∞]=0 and 2[−1]=0. Its extended quotient is ℤ/2, and the published
boundary to Q_neg=ℤ/2 sends [−1] to the nonzero element. Thus B_new(F₃)=0,
whereas the Suslin group is 2ℤ in the free group on [−1]. These calculations
exhibit both failures of an unrestricted Lemma 2.2: F₂ has a nonzero
cokernel and F₃ has a kernel of infinite order. They also prevent the
generic Goncharov group, which is ℤ over F₃, from being mistaken for
either Bloch kernel.

## Curve specializations and the exact open boundary

The all-curve object uses all smooth connected curves over F with two
F-rational points. It is a rational vector-space quotient. This is the
weight-two reading of Goncharov's §1.9, interpreted consistently with the
rational left side of (1.25a) and Corollary 1.19. It differs from the
generic five-point presentation and from the F(t)-only inductive definition
owned by `Polylogarithms:P.4`. A proof for the latter relation set cannot
be substituted for the all-curve comparison.

The geometric input is assigned to
`tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/AlgebraicCurves#layer-12-the-dictionary--function-fields--curves-and-the-comparison-contracts`.
At an F-rational point of an integral curve smooth of relative dimension
one, that dictionary must supply a local DVR, its fraction-field
identification with the scheme function field, its residue-field
identification with F, and the resulting projective specialization.
Regular values, constants, zeros, poles, projective-line coordinates and
change of field must agree with these identifications. This part requests
that interface and builds on it; it does not develop another theory of
curves. `Polylogarithms:P.4/specialization-and-delta` supplies the rational
degree-two valuation calculation once these geometric data are present.

The suggested signatures record actual scheme and function-field carriers.
They parameterize the evaluation family while its canonical construction
is missing. The free symbol quotient and its universal property exist
for any such family. Boundary descent explicitly takes R_curv^ℚ≤ker d;
the generic comparison explicitly takes R_red≤R_curv^ℚ; the comparison
on kernels takes both containments. These are the concrete inputs to
ordinary quotient descent. Neither containment is claimed for an
arbitrary family of functions. The projective-line examples display their
evaluation equalities as hypotheses; the requested canonical dictionary
must supply those equalities. Successful elaboration checks the types of
these conditional interfaces, not the missing geometry or proofs.

With that input, [t]+[1−t] evaluated at 0 and ∞ puts [1] into the curve
relations. Rationally [t]+[t⁻¹] is also a cycle; evaluation at x and 0
gives inversion antisymmetry. The rational coefficient restriction matters:
the integral angle boundary can be nonzero 2-torsion. The free cycle
R(1+t(x−1),y) specializes at t=1 to R(x,y) and at t=0 to [1]. This proves
the inclusion of the ordinary relations into the curve relations.
Boundary closure follows from the supplied degree-two valuation formula.
The quotient map and its exact kernel then require only linear algebra.

For F₃, inversion antisymmetry at x=−1 gives 2class(−1)=0; the
rational coefficient field then makes class(−1)=0. Together with the three
degenerate classes this kills every generator, so G₂^curv(F₃)=0 and
K_curv≅ℚ. In contrast, the generic presentation is ℚ after rationalization
because no injective five-tuple exists. Over F₂ both rational groups and
the correction are zero. Thus an all-field isomorphism is false for these
literal presentations.

For fields with at least four elements, the remaining injectivity assertion
is K_curv=0, the weight-two instance of Conjecture 1.20 in the source. This pass establishes the quotient map,
its surjectivity, its exact kernel and its restriction to cycles; it has
not established that the kernel vanishes. This records what the read
source proves, rather than asserting a claim about the current general
status of that conjecture. An applicable source proving the precise
all-curve statement must be read before the gap can be closed. If the
rational kernel is nonzero, multiplying it by 2, 3 or 6 cannot kill it.

### 1. Bloch’s full-tensor lecture kernel

Node `K3BlochGroups:V.3/bloch-lecture-kernel` (definition).

For every field F put A₀(F)=ℤ[F−{0,1}], T(F)=Additive(Fˣ)⊗ℤ Additive(Fˣ), and λ:A₀(F)→T(F), [x]↦(1−x)⊗x. Define B_raw(F)=ker λ as a subgroup of A₀(F), before imposing any five-term relations. Symbols at 0 and 1, when used in formulas, mean zero in A₀. Its inclusion into A₀ is part of the interface. This is Bloch’s lecture object, not the inherited Suslin kernel inside P(F).

**Hypotheses.** F is a field; no field-size hypothesis.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Use FreeAbelianGroup on the subtype x≠0,1, and its lift to extend the displayed generator assignment. The codomain is the full tensor product, not either wedge quotient.
2. Take the additive kernel. Restricting the free-symbol field-embedding map gives functoriality, because embeddings preserve units and commute with λ.

**Uses.**

- Bloch §6.1 and §7.2: specifies the source of the full-tensor regulator construction without moving its K₃ comparison from V.4/V.6.
- V.3 convention ledger; V.6 consumers: prevents replacing a full-tensor cycle by an arbitrary five-term class.

**API.**

- `LectureBloch.lambda_symbol` (simp): λ([x])=(1−x)⊗x for x≠0,1.
- `LectureBloch.mem_iff` (characterisation): α lies in B_raw exactly when λ(α)=0; equality in B_raw is equality in A₀.
- `LectureBloch.inclusion_injective` (compatibility): The kernel inclusion B_raw→A₀ is injective, using the Mathlib additive-kernel subtype.
- `LectureBloch.map_symbol` (functoriality): For a field embedding f:F→E, the free map [x]↦[f(x)] commutes with λ via the tensor map of units, restricts to B_raw, and has identity/composition laws.

**Unit tests.**

- `LectureBloch.test_F2` (degenerate): B_raw(F₂)=0 because A₀(F₂)=0.
- `LectureBloch.test_F3` (computation): A₀(F₃)≅ℤ on [−1], T(F₃)≅ℤ/2, and λ([−1]) is its nonzero generator; B_raw(F₃)=2ℤ.
- `LectureBloch.test_F5_relation` (non-example): Over F₅ the admissible relation R(2,3) equals [4] in A₀, and λ([4]) is nonzero of order 2. The ordinary relation subgroup is not contained in B_raw.

**Acceptance.** The F₃ kernel is 2ℤ inside ℤ[−1]. Five-term elements need not be λ-cycles.

**Direct prerequisites.** `mathlib:FreeAbelianGroup`, `mathlib:FreeAbelianGroup.lift`, `mathlib:TensorProduct`, `mathlib:MonoidHom.ker`, `mathlib:Additive`.

**Source locators.** Bloch2000, §6.1, p. 43, (6.1.2).

### 2. The map from lecture cycles to Suslin classes

Node `K3BlochGroups:V.3/bloch-lecture-comparison` (construction).

Let R₅≤A₀ be generated by the inherited admissible five-term elements, q:A₀↠P(F) the reduced-generator presentation map, and π:T↠Q_s the inherited antisymmetric tensor quotient. Then ∂q=−πλ. Hence q restricts to μ:B_raw→B(F). Define K_raw=R₅∩ker λ, regarded as a subgroup of B_raw. The induced map μ_rel:B_raw/K_raw→B(F) is injective and has image μ(B_raw). No quotient A₀/R₅→T with generator assignment λ exists in general.

**Hypotheses.** F is a field; all symbols and signs as in bloch-lecture-kernel and the inherited bloch-boundary.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Identify A₀/R₅ with the inherited free-on-F−0 presentation killing [1], by the two inverse generator maps.
2. For each nondegenerate generator π((1−x)⊗x)=−x∧(1−x); extend by the free universal property.
3. Restrict q to ker λ, identify its kernel with R₅∩ker λ, and use the additive quotient to obtain μ_rel.

**Uses.**

- V.3 rational convention comparisons: specifies which relation quotient has a rational identification.
- V.6 explicit cycle recipes: tests whether a representative really is a full-tensor cycle.

**API.**

- `LectureBloch.boundary_compare` (compatibility): ∂∘q=−π∘λ as homomorphisms A₀→Q_s.
- `LectureBloch.mu_coe` (simp): For α∈B_raw the image of μ(α) in P(F) is q(α).
- `LectureBloch.mu_ker` (characterisation): μ(α)=0 iff the underlying free element α belongs to R₅.
- `LectureBloch.muRel_injective` (universal-property): μ_rel is the unique map from B_raw/K_raw composing with the quotient map to μ, and is injective.

**Unit tests.**

- `LectureBloch.test_F5_kernel` (non-example): The nonzero free element 2[4] in A₀(F₅) is a lecture cycle and maps to zero under μ; K_raw is not finite torsion.
- `LectureBloch.test_F5_generator` (computation): The lecture cycle 4[3] over F₅ maps to 4[3]=2c, a nonzero generator of B(F₅)≅ℤ/3.
- `LectureBloch.test_Q_kernel` (non-example): Over ℚ, 4[−1] is a nonzero lecture cycle in ker μ and has infinite additive order. Thus μ⊗ℚ itself is not injective.

**Acceptance.** The sign is negative before kernels are taken. The kernel of μ is a relation kernel, without a torsion bound.

**Direct prerequisites.** `K3BlochGroups:V.3/bloch-lecture-kernel`, `K3BlochGroups:V.3/pre-bloch-group`, `K3BlochGroups:V.3/five-term-relation`, `K3BlochGroups:V.3/bloch-boundary`, `K3BlochGroups:V.3/bloch-group`, `K3BlochGroups:V.3/antisymmetric-tensor-quotient`, `mathlib:QuotientGroup.mk'`.

**Source locators.** Bloch2000, §6.1, p. 43, (6.1.2).

### 3. The exact obstruction to lifting a Suslin class

Node `K3BlochGroups:V.3/bloch-lecture-obstruction` (theorem).

For every field F set S=range(1+τ)≤T, E=im λ∩S and L=λ(R₅)≤E. There is a canonical obstruction o:B(F)→E/L: choose α with qα=β and take λ(α) mod L. It is independent of the lift and is surjective. The sequence 0→K_raw→B_raw→B(F)→E/L→0 is exact. In particular ker μ=K_raw and coker μ≅E/L, not merely an unspecified torsion discrepancy.

**Hypotheses.** F is a field. τ is the tensor flip; R₅, q, λ and K_raw are the preceding definitions.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Since ∂R₅=0 and ∂q=−πλ, λ(R₅) lies in ker π=S, proving L≤E.
2. A lift of a boundary-zero class has λ-image in E. Changing the lift by an element of R₅ changes λ by an element of L, so the additive quotient lift is well-defined.
3. Every e∈E has e=λ(α), and πe=0 implies qα∈B(F); this proves surjectivity. The obstruction vanishes exactly when subtracting a relation from α produces a lecture cycle.
4. Identify the first kernel using the preceding comparison and translate range=kernel into Function.Exact.

**Acceptance.** Over F₅, E=L≅ℤ/2, so μ is surjective even though it has an infinite kernel. The formula remains meaningful over F₂ and F₃, where the six-torsion identities need not hold.

**Direct prerequisites.** `K3BlochGroups:V.3/bloch-lecture-comparison`, `K3BlochGroups:V.3/antisymmetric-tensor-quotient`, `mathlib:Function.MulExact`, `mathlib:QuotientGroup.mk'`.

**Source locators.** Bloch2000, §6.1, p. 43, (6.1.2).

### 4. Six kills the lecture comparison obstruction

Node `K3BlochGroups:V.3/bloch-lecture-six-torsion` (theorem).

For a field F with at least four elements, 6·(E/L)=0. Consequently μ_rel:B_raw/K_raw→B(F) is injective with cokernel killed by 6, and becomes an isomorphism after tensoring with ℤ[1/6] or ℚ. Explicitly, if qα=β∈B(F), let σ[x]=[1−x], let deg([x])=1, and interpret [−1]=0 in characteristic 2. Then γ=3(α−σα)+deg(α)·2[−1] belongs to B_raw and μγ=6β. No such conclusion identifies B_raw itself rationally with B(F).

**Hypotheses.** F is a field with at least four elements.

**Construction or proof.**

1. The full tensor flip sends λ(α) to λ(σα). Since λ(α)∈range(1+τ), applying 1−τ gives λ(α−σα)=0.
2. The inherited constant relation gives q(σα)=deg(α)c−β, hence q(α−σα)=2β−deg(α)c.
3. The symbol 2[−1] is a lecture cycle since the second tensor factor has order dividing 2. Its image is h(−1)=3c, by the inherited three-c-angle-minus-one. The displayed γ therefore maps to 6β.
4. Apply exactness and flat localization; the quotient by K_raw must precede the coefficient change. The coefficient-exports node also treats coprime moduli.

**Acceptance.** The lift is explicit and compatible with the sign convention. The nonzero rational kernel 4[−1] over ℚ survives if K_raw is omitted. For F₂/F₃ use direct presentations, not the |F|≥4 constant and angle identities.

**Direct prerequisites.** `K3BlochGroups:V.3/bloch-lecture-obstruction`, `K3BlochGroups:V.3/element-c`, `K3BlochGroups:V.3/three-c-angle-minus-one`, `mathlib:TensorProduct.comm`, `mathlib:Module.Flat.lTensor_exact`.

**Source locators.** Bloch2000, §6.1, p. 43, (6.1.2).

### 5. Goncharov’s generic configuration group B₂

Node `K3BlochGroups:V.3/goncharov-generic-b2` (definition).

For every field F define G₂^gen(F)=A₀(F)/R_conf, where R_conf is generated by ∑_{i=0}^4(−1)^i[cr(x₀,…,x̂ᵢ,…,x₄)] for pairwise distinct points x₀,…,x₄∈P¹(F). Use exactly the cross-ratio of K3BlochGroups:V.4/cross-ratio, cr(0,∞,1,z)=z. The generators are P¹(F)−{0,1,∞}; degenerate symbols are zero by notation. No relations from coinciding points are imposed. Its boundary δ_gen is [x]↦(1−x)∧x in Q_s, with descent proved in goncharov-generic-comparison.

**Hypotheses.** F is a field, possibly F₂ or F₃. Only injective five-tuples occur in R_conf.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Import the cross-ratio and its five normalized face values from V.4, rather than reconstructing configurations or PGL₂.
2. Form the free additive relation subgroup and quotient. The reduced generators are explicit on p. 218; p. 203’s projective-line shorthand does not authorize free degenerate generators.
3. The map induced by a field embedding respects cross-ratios, so it takes R_conf into the corresponding relation subgroup; the universal property is the ordinary additive quotient lift.

**Uses.**

- Goncharov §1.8 Bloch–Suslin complex: places the generic B₂ in degree one before taking its boundary kernel.
- Polylogarithms:P.3/polylogarithmic-complex and P.4/explicit-to-inductive-comparison: imports the algebraic weight-two presentation and boundary sign.
- K3BlochGroups:V.4 configuration homology: shares one cross-ratio convention and relation set.

**API.**

- `GoncharovB2.class` (constructor): For x≠0,1, class(x) is the quotient class of [x]; symbols 0,1,∞ evaluate to zero.
- `GoncharovB2.eq_iff` (characterisation): The classes of α and β agree iff α−β belongs to R_conf.
- `GoncharovB2.lift` (universal-property): Any assignment into an abelian group annihilating all injective five-point relations extends uniquely to G₂^gen.
- `GoncharovB2.map_symbol` (functoriality): A field embedding maps class(x) to class(f(x)); identity and composition maps agree with those on the free symbols.

**Unit tests.**

- `GoncharovB2.test_F2` (degenerate): G₂^gen(F₂)=0; the projective line has only three points and there are no nondegenerate generators.
- `GoncharovB2.test_F3` (computation): G₂^gen(F₃)≅ℤ on [−1], since its projective line has only four points and no relation five-tuple.
- `GoncharovB2.test_F5` (non-example): G₂^gen(F₅)≅ℤ/6 on [3], while its boundary kernel is ℤ/3. In particular G₂^gen is not the Bloch kernel.

**Acceptance.** A five-point relation is the inherited admissible five-term relation after normalization. Reducing the generator set does not kill the inherited constant c.

**Direct prerequisites.** `mathlib:FreeAbelianGroup`, `mathlib:QuotientGroup.mk'`, `K3BlochGroups:V.4/cross-ratio`.

**Source locators.** Goncharov1995, pp. 202–203 and §2.3 pp. 253–254; Goncharov1995, §1.8, p. 218, definition before the Bloch–Suslin complex.

### 6. Integral identification of the generic B₂ presentation

Node `K3BlochGroups:V.3/goncharov-generic-comparison` (comparison).

For every field F the identity on nondegenerate symbols gives e_gen:G₂^gen(F)≃P(F), integrally, and δ_gen=−∂∘e_gen into Q_s. Consequently ker δ_gen≃B(F), with zero kernel and zero cokernel. If one instead composes with the ordinary exterior-square projection, its kernel corresponds to the inherited B̃(F); the exact discrepancy is exterior-kernel-discrepancy. Tensoring with ℚ makes both wedge targets coincide. Neither G₂^gen itself nor a free projective-line presentation is identified with B(F).

**Hypotheses.** F is a field; the boundary uses the antisymmetric tensor quotient specified on p. 218.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Normalize any injective five-tuple by the imported V.4 three-point normalization. Its five face cross-ratios give exactly [x]−[y]+[y/x]−[(1−x⁻¹)/(1−y⁻¹)]+[(1−x)/(1−y)].
2. Conversely each admissible pair x,y gives the injective tuple (0,∞,1,x,y); hence R_conf=R₅. The additive quotient yields the inverse generator maps.
3. Extend the displayed negative boundary identity by the universal property; transfer kernels, and apply the inherited antisymmetric/exterior exact sequence for the alternative wedge target.

**Acceptance.** Over F₅, [3] has nonzero boundary, c=[2] has order 3 and lies in the kernel. Over F₁₁, c has order 6: the notation [0]=0 in the reduced free group does not imply [x]+[1−x]=0. The rational degree-one cohomology identification with K₃^ind is imported from V.4, not proved a second time.

**Direct prerequisites.** `K3BlochGroups:V.3/goncharov-generic-b2`, `K3BlochGroups:V.4/cross-ratio`, `K3BlochGroups:V.3/pre-bloch-group`, `K3BlochGroups:V.3/bloch-boundary`, `K3BlochGroups:V.3/bloch-group`, `K3BlochGroups:V.3/antisym-exterior-comparison`, `K3BlochGroups:V.3/exterior-kernel-discrepancy`.

**Source locators.** Goncharov1995, pp. 202–203 and §2.3 pp. 253–254; Goncharov1995, §1.8 p. 218, displayed Bloch–Suslin complex.

### 7. Goncharov’s rational smooth-curve group

Node `K3BlochGroups:V.3/goncharov-curve-b2` (definition).

For every field F let V_F=ℚ[P¹(F)] and W_F=ℚ⊗ℤ Q_s(F), and define d_F:V_F→W_F by [x]↦1⊗((1−x)∧x) for x≠0,1,∞, with degenerate values zero. For each smooth connected curve X/F and F-rational points u,v, projective specialization sends rational functions in F(X) to F∪{∞}. Let R_curv^ℚ(F) be the ℚ-span of [0], [∞], and sp_u(α)−sp_v(α), for every such curve and every α∈ker d_{F(X)}. Define G₂^curv(F)=V_F/R_curv^ℚ(F). This is the rational version of §1.9, (1.25a), with the rationalization on both sides. Smooth connected curves over a field are integral; the suggested carrier uses integral schemes smooth of relative dimension one, not arbitrary fields with unexplained evaluation maps.
