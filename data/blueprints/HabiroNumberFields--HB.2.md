## 1. Scope, conventions and imports

The cyclic dilogarithm is the parent's finite polynomial

\[
D_\zeta(u)=\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}(1-\zeta^k u)^k.
\]

No root of this polynomial is chosen in the finite identities below. Power classes mean \(K^\times/(K^\times)^n\), represented by Tau Ceti's `powerClassQuotient` and `powerClassHom`. They are distinct from classes of units of the ring of integers and from Selmer classes. The pinned `kummerClassMap` uses the cocycle \(\sigma(\alpha)/\alpha\), where \(\alpha^n=a\); its injectivity is available, its surjectivity is not a baseline claim. The full Kummer identification and its compatibility with cup products are imports of `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ProfiniteCohomology#layer-9-the-galois-interface-hilbert-90-and-kummer-theory`, including that layer's predecessors. Mathlib's `IsPrimitiveRoot` supplies the order convention, and `IsPrimitiveRoot.geom_sum_eq_zero` supplies the geometric-sum compatibility test.

Hutchinson 2013 §6.4 uses the **left** periodic resolution of a cyclic group generated by \(t\): the odd differential is \(t-1\), the even differential is \(1+t+\cdots+t^{N-1}\). The imported parent API follows Hutchinson 2024 §2.1, p.2, whose **right** bar resolution has odd differential \(1-t\). Their degree-three coinvariant chains coincide, and their degree-two classes agree by the positive Bockstein calculation in §4; the resolutions themselves are not identified. The Bott class has Bockstein \(\partial\beta=\zeta\). The twist isomorphism sends \(\zeta\) to \(\zeta\otimes\zeta\). These choices, the Bloch-convention comparison, and the sign of the Chern map must be carried by the maps themselves.

## 2. The cyclic hypergeometric sum

New construction: `HabiroNumberFields:HB.2/cyclic-hypergeometric-sum`, proposed declaration `TauCeti.HabiroNF.cyclicHypergeom`.

For any field \(K\), any \(n\geq0\), and \(\zeta,x,y,z\in K\), define

\[
f_{n,\zeta}(x,y\mid z)=
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
\frac{\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-\zeta^{j+1}y)}
     {\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-\zeta^{j+1}x)}z^k.
\]

This is a total finite expression using field division. The zero-length product is one, so the first summand is one when \(n>0\). The cyclic interpretation requires \(n>0\), \(\zeta\) primitive of order \(n\), \(x^n\ne1\), \(y^n\ne1\), and

\[
(1-y^n)z^n=1-x^n.
\]

Under these conditions each denominator is nonzero. The summand recurrence extends periodically because the ratio over one period is \((1-y^n)z^n/(1-x^n)=1\). The finite q-Pochhammer construction belongs to `QSeriesPartitionsAndMockModularForms:QM.0/q-pochhammer`; the formula above specifies only this specialized sum. Its arbitrary-ring finite-product extension is requested there.

CGZ §2.5 uses \(f^n\) as the explicit nth-power witness for the five-term identity. GZ Appendix A obtains \(f\) as the sum of the leading constants of the residue classes of a bilateral series. These uses require the following API; all names have prefix `TauCeti.HabiroNF.`.

| Declaration | Required statement |
| --- | --- |
| `cyclicHypergeom_eq_sum` | The displayed finite sum for arbitrary field arguments. |
| `cyclicHypergeom_zero` | \(f_{0,\zeta}(x,y\mid z)=0\). |
| `cyclicHypergeom_one` | \(f_{1,\zeta}(x,y\mid z)=1\). |
| `cyclicHypergeom_two` | \(f_{2,\zeta}=1+(1-\zeta y)(1-\zeta x)^{-1}z\), including the total-division case. |
| `cyclicHypergeom_map` | Every field homomorphism commutes with the sum; identity and composition follow from this equation. |
| `cyclicHypergeom_diagonal` | If all denominator products are nonzero, \(f_{n,\zeta}(x,x\mid z)=\sum_{k<n}z^k\). |
| `cyclicHypergeom_diagonal_root` | Under that condition, a primitive \(z\) of order \(n>1\) gives zero, agreeing with Mathlib's geometric-sum theorem. |
| `cyclicHypergeom_shift_z` | Under the cyclic hypotheses, \((1-z)f(x,y\mid z)=(x-\zeta yz)f(x,y\mid\zeta z)\). |

The shift equation follows by summing the recurrence over one period. Cancellation in the diagonal equation requires its denominator hypothesis: total field division alone does not make a zero numerator divided by itself equal to one.

Four unit tests discriminate the indexing and boundary conventions:

- `cyclicHypergeom_empty`: order zero gives zero over any field.
- `cyclicHypergeom_singleton`: order one gives one over any field.
- `cyclicHypergeom_rational_two`: over \(\mathbb Q\), \(f_{2,-1}(2,3\mid5)=23/3\). Starting the product at \(y\) instead of \(\zeta y\) fails this test.
- `cyclicHypergeom_diagonal_three`: over \(\mathbb C\), if \(\zeta\) is primitive of order three, \(f_{3,\zeta}(0,0\mid\zeta)=0\), exactly by `IsPrimitiveRoot.geom_sum_eq_zero`.

## 3. The odd-order KMS identity

New theorem: `HabiroNumberFields:HB.2/kms-odd-order-proof`. Let \(K\) be a field, \(n\geq3\) odd and invertible in \(K\), and \(\zeta\) primitive of order \(n\). For nonzero \(x,y,z\), put \(X=x^n,Y=y^n,Z=z^n\). Assume

\[
X\ne1,\qquad Y\ne1,\qquad X\ne Y,\qquad (1-Y)Z=1-X.
\]

Writing \(T=n(n-1)/2\), the identity is

\[
f_{n,\zeta}(x,y\mid z)^n
D_\zeta(1/x)D_\zeta(\zeta y)D_\zeta(\zeta/z)
=(\zeta y)^T D_\zeta(1)D_\zeta(\zeta y/x)D_\zeta(x/(yz)).
\tag{KMS}
\]

The three dilogarithm factors on the left are nonzero under these hypotheses: their nth-power arguments are \(1/X,Y,1/Z\), none equal to one. Thus division gives the form quoted by CGZ §2.5 as (C.7). For \(n=1\) the empty-product identity is one equals one. The odd-order statement is exactly the input needed by the parent five-term descent; no even-order extension is asserted.

The proof route is the independent proof in GZ Appendix A, Proposition 8.1. Four corrections to its published calculation are recorded as `HabiroNumberFields/EHB2.1`–`EHB2.4` in the packet. They concern the proof, not the final KMS identity. A fifth finding, `EHB2.5`, corrects the second reference to (56) in the rational-function paragraph on p.236 to (55), the identity being proved. All five findings were confirmed by `REV-HabiroNumberFields--HB.2`.

### 3.1 Bilateral series and Gaussian limit

Work first over \(\mathbb C\) on a sufficiently small simply connected complex neighborhood of \((X,Y)=(1/5,2)\). Put \(Z=(1-X)/(1-Y)\) and choose compatible analytic nth roots \(x,y,z\). Shrink the neighborhood so that \(|X|<1<|Y|\), \(|X/Y|<|Z|<1\), and \(\operatorname{Re}S>0\), where \(S=(1-X)(1-Y)/(X-Y)\). At its center, \(Z=-4/5\), \(|X/Y|=1/10\) and \(S=4/9\), so all these inequalities are strict. Put \(q=\zeta\exp(-\varepsilon/n)\), \(\varepsilon\to0^+\), and write \(P_q(a)=(a;q)_\infty\). On the generic nonreal subopen with nonvanishing q-product factors, Ramanujan's bilateral identity gives

\[
\Psi=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}\frac{(qy;q)_k}{(qx;q)_k}z^k
=\frac{P_q(q)P_q(qyz)P_q(1/(yz))P_q(x/y)}
       {P_q(qx)P_q(1/y)P_q(z)P_q(x/(yz))}.
\]

The convergence domain is \(|x/y|<|z|<1\), \(|q|<1\), with nonzero denominator factors. Taking nth roots of the chosen magnitude inequalities gives exactly this domain. The inequalities \(0<X<1<Y\) alone are insufficient: \(X=9/10,Y=2\) gives \(Z=-1/10\) but \(|X/Y|=9/20>|Z|\).

Continue the summands across removable singularities in the separate negative-index q-Pochhammer expressions by the backward recurrence

\[
A_{k-1}=A_k\frac{1-q^k x}{z(1-q^k y)},\qquad
A_0=1.
\]

For \(k\leq0\), \(|q^k y|>1\), so this recurrence has no denominator zero on the chosen neighborhood. For nonnegative indices, \(|x|<1\) keeps the forward denominators nonzero. This continuation preserves the generic identity; it does not replace the uniform convergence estimates required below.

For residue class \(k+nj\), the local Gaussian exponent is

\[
\frac{Y-X}{(1-X)(1-Y)}\frac{j^2n\varepsilon}{2}.
\]

Its real part is negative. The numerator is \(Y-X\), whereas the printed unnumbered display on p.236 has \(X-Y\). With

\[
S=\frac{(1-X)(1-Y)}{X-Y},
\qquad
\sqrt{\varepsilon}\Psi\longrightarrow
\sqrt{2\pi/n}\sqrt S\,f_{n,\zeta}(x,y\mid z).
\]

The lattice Gaussian mass includes the factor \(1/\sqrt n\) missing from printed (59). This is an additive limit, so it does not require \(f\ne0\). A proof must control both infinite tails uniformly on the chosen open set, as well as the central Taylor expansion. `QM.0` is requested to supply this precise analytic contract; a pointwise expansion alone is insufficient.

### 3.2 Product limit, parameter shifts and phase

The common leading q-product limit requested from `QM.0` is

\[
P_q(a)^{-n}\sim
D_\zeta(a)(1-a^n)^{-n/2}
\exp(\operatorname{Li}_2(a^n)/\varepsilon),
\]

with compatible branches on the slit domain. Before applying it, use the exact identities
\(P_q(qx)=P_q(x)/(1-x)\) and \(P_q(qyz)=P_q(yz)/(1-yz)\). Replacing \(qx\) by its limit \(\zeta x\) inside an asymptotic with a \(1/\varepsilon\) exponent loses a finite constant. The corrected nth-power product asymptotic is

\[
\Psi^n\sim (2\pi/\varepsilon)^{n/2}\mu^n C^{n/2}
\frac{D(x)D(1/y)D(z)D(x/(yz))}
     {D(yz)D(1/(yz))D(x/y)}
\left(\frac{1-x}{1-yz}\right)^n\exp(-B/\varepsilon),
\]

where \(D=D_\zeta\),

\[
C=\frac{(1-YZ)(1-1/(YZ))(1-X/Y)}
        {(1-X)(1-1/Y)(1-Z)(1-X/(YZ))},
\]

\[
\begin{aligned}
B={}&-\operatorname{Li}_2(X/(YZ))+\operatorname{Li}_2(YZ)
+\operatorname{Li}_2(1/(YZ))+\operatorname{Li}_2(X/Y)\\
&+\operatorname{Li}_2(1)-\operatorname{Li}_2(X)
-\operatorname{Li}_2(1/Y)-\operatorname{Li}_2(Z)=0.
\end{aligned}
\]

The last equality is a **full complex** classical five-term identity on the specified neighborhood, requested from `Polylogarithms:P.1/classical-polylogarithm` with \(\operatorname{Li}_2(1)=\pi^2/6\). A Bloch–Wigner identity supplies only its imaginary part. All variable dilogarithm arguments at the chosen center lie in \(( -\infty,1)\), so their principal branches continue on a sufficiently small neighborhood. Direct algebra gives \(C/S=((1-YZ)/(1-X))^2\); fix its positive square root at \((X,Y)=(1/5,2)\), where \(C/S=169/16\) and \((1-YZ)/(1-X)=13/4\).

The printed (60) needs the exponent \(n/2\) on \(2\pi/\varepsilon\), and its displayed definition of \(B\) yields \(-B/\varepsilon\). Relative to the printed shifted-D ratio, the parameter-shift correction contributes \((1-X)/(1-YZ)\). These constants are fixed before any cancellation.

For \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i h/n}\), the eta transformation law `QM.1/eta-transformation-law` gives \(\mu=\exp(-\pi i s(h,n))\), with \(s\) the Dedekind sum. Directly multiplying the arguments of \(1-\zeta^k\) gives

\[
D_\zeta(1)=n^{n/2}\exp(\pi i n s(h,n)),
\qquad \mu^n=\frac{n^{n/2}}{D_\zeta(1)}.
\]

The printed phrase identifying the phase \(\mu_{24}\) with \(\zeta^n\) cannot hold: for \(n=3,h=1\), \(\mu^n=e^{-\pi i/6}\), while \(\zeta^n=1\). The needed phase is \(\mu^n\).

### 3.3 Finite algebra and integral specialization

Use the parent's dilogarithm shift relation and the finite-product identity

\[
D(u)D(1/u)=a u^{-T}\left(\frac{1-u^n}{1-u}\right)^n,
\quad a=(-1)^T\zeta^{\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}k^2},
\quad D(1)^2=a n^n.
\]

The first formula is used away from its displayed denominators; the second follows by the product limit at \(u=1\), or directly from the root-of-unity product. Since \(n\) is odd, \(\zeta^T=1\). Equate the corrected Gaussian and product limits, insert \(B=0\), \(C/S\), and the explicit phase, then apply these finite identities to obtain (KMS).

To transfer to positive characteristic, clear the denominators and Laurent powers. Work over \(A=\mathbb Z[t]/\Phi_n(t)\). The relation
\(x^n=1-z^n+y^nz^n\) is monic in \(x\), so reduced polynomials have basis \(1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}\) over \(A[y,z]\). Over \(\mathbb C(y,z)\), the right side has a simple zero along a divisor in \(y\); it is not a dth power for any divisor \(d>1\) of \(n\). The binomial is therefore irreducible. Equality on the complex open set implies that all reduced coefficients vanish. Injecting \(A\) into \(\mathbb C\) proves that those integral coefficients are zero. Specialize \(t\) to a primitive \(n\)th root in \(K\), and cancel only the nonzero factors in the hypotheses. This argument never embeds a positive-characteristic field in \(\mathbb C\).

Acceptance includes all admissible nonzero triples in \(\mathbb F_{19},\mathbb F_{31},\mathbb F_{43},\mathbb F_{73}\), at respective orders \(3,5,7,9\), for a fixed primitive root in each field. The counts are 108, 500, 1372 and 4374. Varying \(x,y,z\) covers every root choice of their powers in those fields. The analytic route imports no Nahm-sum theorem.

## 4. The image of the positive cyclic bar generator

New comparison: `HabiroNumberFields:HB.2/eta-bar-bloch-specialization`. Let \(N\geq3\) be odd and let \(\zeta\in\mathbb C\) be a primitive Nth root of unity. Put \(E=\mathbb Q(\zeta)\), \(F=\mathbb Q(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})\), and

\[
t=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_2(F).
\]

The positive generator is represented by \(\alpha_3(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}[t\mid t^j\mid t]\). Under the actual map from homology to the Suslin Bloch group and its CGZ convention comparison modulo \(N\), its image is

\[
\eta_\zeta=\sum_{k\bmod N}
\left[1-\left(\frac{\zeta-\zeta^{-1}}{\zeta^k-\zeta^{-k}}\right)^2\right]
\in B_{\rm CGZ}(F)/N,
\]

where the term at \(k=0\) is \([\infty]\). This is not an integral identification of Bloch conventions.

The left resolution of Hutchinson 2013 §6.4, pp.32–33, gives the degree-two chain \(\sum_j[t^j\mid t]\), while the parent's right resolution gives \(\sum_j[t\mid t^j]\). After trivial coinvariants, both integral boundaries are \(N[t]\): in \(d[a\mid b]=[b]-[ab]+[a]\), the other terms telescope. For the coefficient sequence with multiplication by positive \(N\), both Bocksteins therefore equal \([t]\). Since \(H_2(C,\mathbb Z)=0\), this coefficient Bockstein is injective, so the two finite-coefficient Bott classes agree. Their degree-three coinvariant chains are both the displayed \(\alpha_3(t)\). This gives the compatibility needed to use the parent API in the left homogeneous calculation.

`K3BlochGroups:V.4` is requested to supply the generic configuration calculation of Hutchinson 2013 §§6.3–6.4 and its actual homology map. The contracting homotopy on left homogeneous bar chains prepends 1, producing \(\sum(1,t,t^{j+1},t^{j+2})\). Choose \(y\in\mathbb P^1(F)\) outside the orbit of \(\infty\), with \(y\ne t(y)\); the field is infinite. After forgetting the refined squareclass coefficients, Hutchinson's expression is

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{N-3}\left[\frac{t\infty-t^{j+1}\infty}{t\infty-t^{j+2}\infty}\right]
+[1-A]-[1/A]+[B]+[1/B],
\]

where \(A=(t\infty-y)/(t^{-1}\infty-y)\), \(B=(t\infty-y)/(t\infty-t(y))\). Import `K3BlochGroups:V.3/cgz-convention-comparison` and `cgz-degenerate-relations`. The inversion symbols \([u]+[1/u]\) vanish in the CGZ quotient, giving the correction \([1-A]+[A]=[0]\). It must be retained.

Put \(u_k=(\zeta^k-\zeta^{-k})/(\zeta-\zeta^{-1})\). Matrix powers give \(t^k\infty=u_{k-1}/u_k\), and the second-order recurrence gives

\[
\frac{u_j u_{j+2}}{u_{j+1}^2}=1-\frac1{u_{j+1}^2}.
\]

The internal sum is therefore exactly the eta sum at \(k=2,\ldots,N-2\). Its other three terms are \([\infty]+2[0]=[0]\), matching the correction. For \(N=3\), the internal sum is empty and both expressions equal \([0]\). For \(N=5\), the two internal terms correspond to \(k=2,3\).

The diagonalizing matrix \(A_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\zeta&\zeta^{-1}\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) has determinant \(d=\zeta-\zeta^{-1}\). Use \(A_0\operatorname{diag}(d^{-1},1)\), whose determinant is one and whose conjugation of \(\operatorname{diag}(\zeta^{-1},\zeta)\) is unchanged. This supplies the required SL₂ conjugacy, rather than assuming a GL₂ conjugacy suffices.

The comparison is an input to the parent's `eta-is-zeta-times-bott`, not a prerequisite of its own proof. Restriction to \(E\) takes place in K-theory: the map \(B(F)/N\to B(E)/N\) can kill the cyclotomic class. Use the parent's restriction/corestriction and odd-coefficient Hurewicz interfaces to obtain \(\bar\eta_\zeta=\zeta*\beta\) in \(K_3(E;\mathbb Z/N)\), as in Hutchinson v4 §4, Proposition 4.6, p.7.

## 5. The signed Chern evaluation

New theorem: `HabiroNumberFields:HB.2/eta-chern-signed-evaluation`. Let \(N=\ell^m\geq3\), with \(\ell\) an **odd prime** and \(m\geq1\), and keep the fields and generators above. With the **raw Soulé finite Chern class**, the positive Bott Bockstein, the standard Kummer map and the stated untwisting, the product formula is

\[
\bar c_{2,1}(a*b)=-\bar c_{1,1}(a)\cup\bar c_{1,0}(b),
\qquad \bar c_{1,1}=\delta,\quad \bar c_{1,0}(\beta)=\zeta.
\]

Consequently

\[
\bar c_{2,1}(\bar\eta_\zeta)=-\delta(\zeta)\cup\zeta,
\qquad c_{{\rm raw},\zeta}(\eta_\zeta)=[\zeta^{-1}].
\]

The cohomological bidegrees are \((1,0)\), so moving these factors contributes no additional graded sign. Additive negation in cohomology corresponds to inversion of the power class.

Independently of \(\eta\), define \(c_{+,\zeta}\) by restriction, the negative of the raw degree-(2,1) Chern map, untwisting by cup with \(\zeta\), and inverse Kummer identification. This gives

\[
c_{+,\zeta}(\eta_\zeta)=[\zeta].
\]

This definition specifies which map is negated before evaluating any class. It does not identify the fixed CGZ/GSWZ map with this normalization. That comparison requires the actual étale-K-theory edge or boundary map, the finite Chern class, and the Suslin/Hurewicz signs. The parent source issue `HabiroNumberFields/E14` remains a gap: Hutchinson's printed step from \(-x\cup\zeta\) to \(x\otimes\zeta\) is incompatible with the stated standard Kummer identification and positive Bott normalization.

Hutchinson v4 §2.3, Corollary 2.13, p.5 gives the negative product coefficient. The explicit equality \(\bar c_{1,0}(\beta)=\zeta\) is in its §4 Bott-element paragraph before Lemma 4.1, p.6. The author-hosted Soulé thesis, Proposition 2.2.3.3, p.49, also gives the negative coefficient for an integral K-class times a finite-coefficient class, which suffices for the integral unit \(\zeta\) times \(\beta\). The thesis explicitly distinguishes this from the general product of two finite-coefficient classes; it is not claimed as an independent proof of Hutchinson's full Theorem 2.12. Its §2.2.4.3, pp.51–54, corroborates the degree-one Kummer normalization; the explicit Bott-value citation is the Hutchinson passage above. The precise supplier contract is the requested early finite-Chern prefix of `MotivicEtaleKTheory:M.8`, built from `M.7`. Unsplit M.8 has Borel-regulator and p-adic-regulator inputs and is not an HB.2 prerequisite.

Acceptance must distinguish the signs. In \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)\), \(\zeta_3\) is not a cube: a cube root would have order nine, impossible in this quadratic field. Hence \([\zeta_3]\ne[\zeta_3^{-1}]\) modulo cubes. The concrete baseline power-class test in \(\mathbb F_7\) uses \(\zeta=2\), with \([2]\ne[2]^{-1}\). The Chern evaluation alone does not determine the universal scalar of the regulator comparison.
