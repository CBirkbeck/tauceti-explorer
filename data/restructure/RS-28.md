# RS-28 — Higher class field theory as an extension

## Decision and scope

Make `HigherLocalFieldsAndHigherClassFieldTheory` an extension of
`tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ClassFieldTheory`, titled **Class field theory, Part II:
higher local fields and higher reciprocity**. Record Class field theory as the
declared base (`extends`, with its declared roadmap edge), which is how the atlas
carries section 15's first prerequisite; the build sorts prerequisite lists
alphabetically, so no list position is claimed. Retain all eight stable stage identifiers HL.0–HL.7.
Narrow HL.0–HL.6 to the new constructions and the comparison theorems listed in
the result; keep HL.7. No target is retired, no layer is dropped, and no Tau Ceti
roadmap changes.

The extension starts from the ordinary local Artin maps, the correspondence in
the exact ranges supplied by Class field theory, and number-field global
reciprocity. It adds residue towers and their higher topologies, topological
Milnor groups, higher invariants and duality, higher local reciprocity and
existence, explicit formulas, Parshin chains, higher global reciprocity and
arithmetic comparison maps. The base's explicitly excluded
**equal-characteristic p-primary local existence** is imported from
FunctionFieldArithmetic FA.4, which owns it; RS-28 does not move mathematics out
of a roadmap outside its family (red-team fixes below). An extension of this
base therefore cannot be described simply as “dimension at least two.”

The JSON is a structural proposal, not a blueprint or implementation claim.
Named anchor exports below are roadmap contracts, not declarations claimed to
exist in the pinned libraries. The scope of a supplier is part of every import.

## The anchor boundary

ClassFieldTheory §1 distinguishes several ranges that must survive integration.
Layer 5 constructs the local invariant before any local class formation and
exports local duality in its specified coefficient and characteristic range.
Layers 6 and 7 construct finite and absolute local Artin maps, including the
arithmetic Frobenius normalization. Layer 8 gives the full correspondence for
finite extensions of the p-adics and a prime-to-residue-characteristic
correspondence in equal characteristic. Its general norm-kernel description does
not by itself prove equal-characteristic injectivity.

Thus HL.2 imports the ordinary invariant and existing duality, then proves the
higher-field and missing wild statements. HL.3 imports the ordinary maps in all
their stated ranges. Its one-dimensional comparison identifies its Milnor-degree
one map with the existing Artin map; it does not construct an alternate map.
The equal-characteristic p-primary existence theorem and consequent
injectivity/completion statement are imported from FunctionFieldArithmetic FA.4,
which owns them (red-team fix RT-RS-28/2). On mixed-characteristic fields,
and on the equal-characteristic prime-to-p part, the norm-subgroup correspondence
is imported from Layer 8. No assertion enlarges the scope of that layer.

Arithmetic Frobenius remains primary. The n=1 comparison must carry a
uniformizer to the existing arithmetic Frobenius, identify the unramified
coordinate, compare finite restrictions, and match norm subgroups. Reversing the
Frobenius convention is an explicit inversion comparison. Density of an Artin
image does not identify an uncompleted source group with its profinite target.

Layers 10–12 of ClassFieldTheory supply the number-field global branch. The
function-field branch belongs to FunctionFieldArithmetic FA.4. HL.6 proves the
comparison appropriate to the scheme, including unramified and boundary
conditions and the treatment of real places. It must not identify the full
number-field idele class group with the reciprocity object for the ring of
integers without taking the relevant quotient. Nor can the number-field theorem
be used as evidence for a finite-field curve's reciprocity theorem.

## Ownership outside the anchor

The family evidence was not an exhaustive overlap list. AUDIT-03 identifies the
all-degree Milnor presentation and transfers already planned by K2SymbolsBrauer;
HL.1's phrase “extend ... to all Milnor degrees” would duplicate them. T.4 owns
construction/transitivity of transfers; T.3:localization-comparison supplies the
residue and projection compatibilities. HL.1 owns their higher-topology and
tower-specific assembly. These imports retain the degree-two sign convention
and do not replace integral antisymmetry by alternation.

ProfiniteCohomology owns the generic cohomology carrier, all-degree maps, cups and
dimension definitions. HL.2 owns the arithmetic computations and invariants on
that machinery. Similarly CR.4 owns ordinary de Rham–Witt, while M.5d supplies
only its prime-power Bockstein induction and reductions; it keeps the
Bloch–Gabber–Kato differential theorem separate, and that theorem is requested
(red-team fixes). The extension owns the higher-field wild pairings still needed
by its higher-field duality.

The audit also flags KTheoryFiniteLocalFields L.5, which owns the
logarithmic/DVR de Rham–Witt comparison for complete discrete valuation fields.
That comparison is now L.5's in the JSON (owners row and link L.5 → HL.2,
RT-RS-28/3). L.5 is not the supplier of all higher-field wild coefficients or
local duality: HL.2 keeps the higher-field Artin–Schreier–Witt pairings built on
the comparison. If a blueprint needs the comparison under different
characteristic or ramification hypotheses, that extension is requested from L.5,
not re-proved in HL.2.

FA.4's local setup specializes ordinary reciprocity before building the global
function-field theorem, and FA.4 owns full equal-characteristic local and global
function-field reciprocity, including the p-primary local existence (RS-04).
RS-28 therefore adds no link into FA.4, since that is the function-field
family's decision. Instead HL.3 imports the p-primary existence/injectivity from
FA.4 (link FA.4 → HL.3). FA.4's Artin–Schreier–Witt extension descriptions in
FA.3 are not confused with a higher-local reciprocity existence theorem. The
result makes no external roadmap or layer mutation.
