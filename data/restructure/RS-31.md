# RS-31 — Reductive algebraic groups and their local extension

## Decision

Retain `ReductiveGroupsPartII` as an extension, with the exact base title:
**Reductive algebraic groups, Part II: local structure and arithmetic models**.
Its first roadmap prerequisite is `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ReductiveGroups`.
Retain all seven stable stages: RG2.0, RG2.0a and RG2.1–RG2.5. Narrow RG2.1,
RG2.4 and RG2.5 to import existing algebraic constructions. Keep RG2.0, RG2.0a,
RG2.2 and RG2.3. No layer is dropped or moved, and the anchor is unchanged.

The extension starts with the anchor's group-scheme/points vocabulary, tori,
reductivity, relative-root structure, abstract Bruhat theory and explicit pinned
split integral group construction. It adds topologies on local points, affine
Weil restriction, valued roots, buildings, connected parahoric models,
nonarchimedean decompositions and the functorial integral dual/L-group interface.
An upstream predicate or generic construction does not supply its local
application, comparison theorem or geometric hypotheses automatically.

## Disposition of the four family leads

1. **RG2.1 versus anchor Layer 7: a genuine overlap in the unvalued part.**
   The anchor explicitly includes the relative root system of a nonsplit group,
   not only the split/absolute root datum. RG2.1 therefore imports that system
   and relative Weyl data. It retains the local root-group realization,
   valuation sets and filtrations, multipliable roots, nonsplit cases, valued
   commutator estimates, torus conjugation, unramified compatibility, affine
   roots and apartment. The local comparison with the imported algebraic data
   is itself required. Calling the extension “relative roots” without this
   boundary would duplicate Layer 7.
2. **RG2.3 versus Layer 6: a supplier and its consumer.** Reductivity of a
   group over a field is an upstream notion. Constructing a parahoric model
   and its connected special-fiber reductive quotient, then proving that the
   quotient has that property, remains RG2.3. Neither the reductivity predicate
   nor the general unipotent-radical theory constructs the model or its
   reduction map. Keep the whole layer, adding explicit imports of the
   group-scheme, quotient, radical and reductivity interfaces.
3. **RG2.4 versus Layer 7: shared abstract theory, new local theorems.**
   Import the generic BN-pair and Bruhat results. RG2.4 must still construct
   the applicable local Tits system and show how its subgroup sits in the
   chosen ambient group, identify affine/extended Weyl parameters and their
   stabilizers, and prove Iwasawa and Cartan decompositions in their exact
   nonsplit/ramification ranges. In particular a BN-pair for a subgroup
   generated by local root groups must not be silently declared a BN-pair
   for every full group of rational points.
4. **RG2.5 versus Layer 9: generic construction and its dual application.**
   Anchor Layer 9 already constructs a pinned split integral group for a root
   datum and the pinned isomorphism theorem. Apply it to the dual datum.
   RG2.5 retains the identification, the finite-image action induced by the
   field form, the semidirect L-group, projection/action laws, change of
   pinning, Levi inclusions and functoriality. Satake's Tannakian reconstruction
   remains a separate consumer construction with an identification theorem.
   No coefficient square root is inserted into the integral dual group.

The six ownership rows in the JSON separate these overlaps and two shared
interfaces: the points dictionary and the reductivity predicate. The latter
rows do not transfer the point topology or parahoric quotient to the anchor.

One phrase requires an explicit mathematical reading: “finiteness of
compact-open double coset decompositions” cannot mean finiteness of the whole
Cartan double-coset set. Already the multiplicative group of the local field,
modulo its unit group on each side, has the valuation's infinitely many integer
classes. The retained claim is finiteness of the decomposition of an individual
compact double coset into single cosets, with its subgroup-index formula; any
bounded-union strengthening requires its stated bounds. This records and
corrects an ambiguous target, rather than deleting the local index calculation.

## Source limits and review questions

and [Tag 05YF](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/05YF) were read. The latter

scheme-representability or inseparable reductivity theorem is inferred.

readiness; they do not transfer those unproved constructions to the anchor.

projective-parameter-space contract must not be misreported as an already
