# RS-27: Algebraic moduli, building on Modular curves

Claude Code, session cc-fb70e5, 2026-09-29. Refs #854.

## The family

- **One proposed member:** *Algebraic moduli and representability for arithmetic geometry*
  (`AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry`, "AM"). Its layers are:
  - R09.1–R09.6: projective parameter spaces, Hilbert and Quot schemes, algebraic spaces, stacks, coarse spaces and
    deformation comparisons;
  - R09.7 with R09.7a–R09.7d: characteristic-zero resolution;
  - A0-extension: higher-dimensional moduli prerequisites.
- **Two Tau Ceti anchors, which never change:**
  - *Modular curves, following Katz–Mazur* (MC);
  - *Stable reduction of curves and stable maps* (SR).

The family file lists 28 evidence records. They show that AM, as written, plans in general form several things the
anchors already construct:

- the Grassmannian;
- finite quotients and faithfully flat descent;
- affine Weil restriction;
- the fibred category of elliptic curves, rigidifiers and coarse moduli of elliptic moduli problems;
- local deformation comparisons;
- relative ampleness and relative Proj, polarised étale descent, and cohomology and base change;
- the Rees-algebra blowup.

I read AM's document and all its layer descriptions in full, together with the anchors' layers named in the evidence:

- MC 0C, 0E, 0F, 0G, 1E, 4A–4C, 7B–7D, 9A, 9D and 9E;

## Decision for the roadmap: keep

AM stays a roadmap in its own right, with its title unchanged.

- **It is general theory with many consumers.** It supplies projective parameter spaces, Hilbert schemes, algebraic
  spaces, stacks, coarse spaces, deformation theory and characteristic-zero resolution. Its consumers include
  AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli, PELModuli, ShimuraVarieties, FaltingsFiniteness, LocalGaloisDeformationRings,
  SchemeKTheoryOperations, ComplexComparisonPartII, AdicSpacesPartII and MordellLawrenceVenkatesh.
- **It is not "Modular curves, Part II".** It does not continue Modular curves in that roadmap's own direction, and
  ModularCurvesPartII already exists for that direction (generalised elliptic curves and compactifications).
- **The PROTOCOL rule that applies is §15's rule for general theories.** A general theory that subsumes an existing
  roadmap's special cases builds on them: it cites them, proves its general statements compatible with them, and does
  not construct them again. AM's own R09.3 already asks for exactly this ("Compare with the finite étale quotient
  construction of #81 instead of replacing it").

## Layer decisions

Seven layers are narrowed. R09.7, R09.7b, R09.7c and R09.7d are unchanged. Each narrowed layer names its suppliers, and
every consumer that relied on a moved piece gets a link from the new owner.

### R09.1, projective parameter spaces

- **Imported:**
  - MC 0G: the relative Grassmannian of locally free rank-N quotients of a finite locally free module, with charts,
    gluing, universal property, base change and projectivity, and the closedness of invariant-subbundle equations;
  - SR layer 2: relative ampleness, projective morphisms and relative Proj.
- **Kept:**
  - P(E) with O(n) and the cohomology of O(n), identified with the rank-one Grassmannian and with relative Proj;
  - smoothness of Grassmannians and the Plücker immersion, since 0G states only projectivity;
  - flag schemes, relative very ampleness, Hilbert polynomials and Castelnuovo–Mumford boundedness;
  - the semilinear, Frobenius-stable lattice conditions of R05 and L7;
  - the incidence and rank loci that MordellLawrenceVenkatesh requests.

### R09.2, Hilbert and Quot functors

- **Imported:** MC 0F's Hom functors from a finite locally free source, and MC 0G's Grassmannian.
- **Kept:**
  - the general projective Hilbert and Quot schemes, including the flatness warning and the dual-number example;
  - Hom and Isom schemes of projective flat families, proved to agree with 0F on finite sources;
  - the Chow-lemma and coherent-dévissage package for proper GAGA.

### R09.3, algebraic spaces and descent

This layer had the most flagged overlaps. Imported from MC:

- 0C: finite locally free equivalence-relation quotients, free finite group actions and torsors;
- 0E: faithfully flat descent for affine and finite locally free schemes, sections, morphisms, polarised curves, group
  objects, subgroups, level structures and torsors;
- 0F: affine Weil restriction;
- 1E: elliptic descent;
- 4C and 9A: finite étale quotients of elliptic moduli.

Also imported, from SR layer 2: polarised étale descent through relative Proj.

Kept:

- the general algebraic-space theory, with its comparison theorem on the anchors' quotients;
- Weil restriction as an algebraic space for arbitrary targets (Stacks 05YC and 05YF), which AbelianSchemes A6 consumes;
- fppf descent for polarised projective schemes in all dimensions and for polarisations of abelian schemes.

### R09.4, algebraic stacks

- **Imported:** MC 4A's fibred category Ell/R, with representability relative to it.
- **Kept:** the general theory of stacks. 4A itself says it is not a general theory of stacks. The comparison with Ell/R
  is added to what this layer keeps.

### R09.5, coarse spaces and rigidification

- **Imported:**
  - MC 4C: elliptic rigidifiers (KM 4.7.0);
  - MC 9D–9E: coarse moduli of affine problems over Ell, and the coarse j-line.
- **Kept:**
  - the finite-inertia coarse-space theorem;
  - auxiliary level for abelian schemes and generalised elliptic curves;
  - normalisation and closure for Deligne–Mumford stacks;
  - the recovery of 9D and 9E.

This is the split the family's link-rescope evidence already records.

### R09.6, formal deformation

- **Imported:** MC 7B and 7D, the elliptic special cases.
- **Kept:** the general stack-level comparison, algebraisation and Artin's criterion.

### R09.7a, blowups and marked ideals

- **Imported:** SR layer 4's Rees-algebra blowup package.
- **Kept:** the marked-ideal machinery of the characteristic-zero algorithm.

SR layer 4's own resolution theorem, for arithmetic surfaces, is a different theorem and is not affected.

### A0-extension

- **Imported:** SR layer 2's cohomology and base change, Grauert's theorem and semicontinuity over locally Noetherian
  bases. That layer states them for proper morphisms in general and specialises to curves only afterwards.
- **Kept:**
  - the finite-presentation, Tor-amplitude and perfect-complex extension;
  - Picard representability through Artin's criterion;
  - étale-presentation analytification;
  - finiteness of normalisation.

## Points of uncertainty

- **Generality of MC 0G.** 0G builds the relative Grassmannian to parameterise subgroup schemes, as quotients of a Hopf
  algebra. Its construction (charts, gluing, universal property for a finite locally free module) and the reviewed
  audit both treat it as the general relative Grassmannian, so I made 0G the owner. If the Tau Ceti implementation
  specialises it to Hopf algebras, R09.1 must still import it and state the general module version as a thin
  generalisation, not a second construction.
- **Generality of SR layer 2's cohomology and base change.** The layer lists it among general statements over locally
  Noetherian bases and derives the curve statements afterwards. If its implementation turns out to be curve-only,
  A0-extension would take back the locally Noetherian higher-dimensional theorems, which is a one-line change of
  `keeps`.
- **A duplication outside this family.** ReductiveGroupsPartII RG2.0a says it "owns" restriction of scalars for affine
  finite-type schemes along a finite locally free base map. That is MC 0F's affine Weil restriction. I added the link
  MC 0F → RG2.0a but did not change RG2.0a, which belongs to another family. That family's restructure should narrow
  it.
- **Blueprint requests still pointing at AM.** SchemeKTheoryOperations S.5 requests blowups from R09.7a, and
  MordellLawrenceVenkatesh and LocalGaloisDeformationRings request Grassmannians from R09.1. When those blueprints next
  change, they should cite SR layer 4 and MC 0G through the new links, and keep AM for the parts AM keeps.
- **Nothing is dropped.** Every narrowed target is either kept, with an explicit `keeps`, or supplied by a named anchor
  layer, and each consumer keeps a path to what it used.
