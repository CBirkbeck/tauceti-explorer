## 2. The mathematical division

Write H for `HilbertModularVarietiesAndShimuraCurves`, M for `PELModuli`, D for `ShimuraData`, and C for `ShimuraCompactifications`. All identifiers in the JSON are the full stable identifiers.

**Keep M as the generic PEL supplier.** Its core is an integral/rational PEL datum, a fibered moduli problem with exact polarization and level conventions, representability, complex comparison on the correct components, and higher-level normalization over a specified good base. Its Siegel/unitary examples and arithmetic rational-family interface remain useful. They are not a replacement for the more specialized Hilbert theory.

**Keep H as the arithmetic specialization.** It supplies ordered polarization modules, integral trace-lattice comparisons, the geometric/arithmetic Hilbert-group distinction, arbitrary-prime models and loci, unit and level actions, prescribed torsion twists, and the separate quaternionic branch. This is not merely a restatement of M2 at a different dimension. Retitling the whole roadmap as a PEL Part II would incorrectly suggest that the quaternionic bad-reduction and arithmetic-group branches start after, or are covered by, all of M.

The shared construction order is:

1. D5 supplies the rational Hilbert groups, data and rational embedding. H0 refines their lattice and convention interfaces.
2. M0–M3 supply the generic PEL datum, moduli, representability and complex comparison. H1 supplies the polarization-module instance and proves that the imported statements apply.
3. H2–H4 supply the extra integral/locus, quotient and level geometry. Their stronger assertions keep their own hypotheses and proofs.
4. M5 consumes the Hilbert example from H0/H1. It is not a late producer on which H1 depends.
5. M6 consumes the rigidified moduli object and family, but retains arbitrary-level stack algebraicity as well as its Hodge-line and arithmetic descent statements. It remains independent of the Hilbert example and of the later integral quasi-projectivity theorem in its early algebraic-space part.

A rational symplectic embedding, a lattice-valued trace pairing and a polarization-module moduli problem are three different outputs. Likewise, generic representability, good-prime smoothness and a ramified ordinary-neighborhood theorem are not interchangeable.

### The M5 direction

The original M5 asks for a polarization-module trace-pairing datum and then to “hand its Hilbert moduli problem to H1–H2.” H1 already consumes M3, and H2 consumes M4. The generic M0–M4 engine must not depend on the late example that illustrates it. Assigning the specific polarization-module construction to H1 and adding H0/H1 → M5 resolves the duplicated example without feeding M5 back into those prerequisites.

The existing graph has no outgoing edge from M5. No present consumer loses a prerequisite when this part of M5 becomes an import. M5 still verifies its instance and retains every non-Hilbert example.
