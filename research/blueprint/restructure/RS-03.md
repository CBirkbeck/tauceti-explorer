## Decision

Keep all eight roadmaps and all 52 member stage identifiers. Make 21 scope narrowings, explicitly keep the elementary CA.4 boundary, record 25 target owners, and add 21 prerequisite/comparison edges. There is no retirement, merge, move or live-roadmap edit. Unlisted layers retain their entire original contracts. The issue's older count of 49 is not used: the family input and eight atlas extracts enumerate 52 stages.

This is not eight independent requests to rebuild number theory. Built arithmetic, cyclic Fourier and height infrastructure stay upstream. Missing generic foundations have one owner, and algorithmic specializations consume that owner. The elliptic-curves anchor is immutable, including its **general** discrete-module Selmer framework and its **existing algorithms**. Nothing in this proposal changes an anchor's stage, title, scope or carrier.

No Part II renaming is proposed. CA, FF, AC, ES and DT have distinct mathematical targets; CN is cross-cutting exact computation, ED is a cross-cutting certified-application programme, and RP concerns general varieties, abelian varieties and obstructions. They are not eight extensions of the same upstream elliptic-curves topic. Within them, the genuinely elliptic subtargets are imports from the existing anchor rather than a second elliptic-curves roadmap.

## Ownership and import decisions

### 1. Arithmetic, presentations and matrix algorithms

CA.3 owns the missing general matrix normal-form theorem contracts. A purported normal form must reconstruct the original matrix by the appropriate invertible transformations, satisfy the correct diagonal/divisibility or triangular conditions, and carry the termination/correctness argument demanded by the original layer. CN.0 owns comparison maps from algorithmic presentations to intrinsic algebraic carriers, checker contracts and cost models. It consumes the matrix results instead of proving a competing theorem.

FF.0 owns specialized finite-field constructors and their Frobenius/subfield/normal-basis comparison certificates; CN.0 imports their output. FF.3 owns finite-field factorization, Hensel lifting and certified counting algorithms; CN.1 imports these for its integer/rational/primality-facing orchestration. A product identity alone is not an irreducible factorization certificate. A probabilistic running-time analysis is not a deterministic correctness theorem.

The certified integral-basis, ideal, unit and class-group algorithms belong to CN.2. CA.5 retains the comparison from these certified outputs to the intrinsic quadratic/cyclotomic and number-field invariants. This adds CN.2 → CA.5, not CA.5 → CN.2. Mere termination of a search for units or ideal representatives does not prove that the returned list is complete; completeness/stopping evidence remains in CN.2.

CA.4 is a false blanket duplicate. Keep its elementary proof routes and effective special cases, including Pell, squares, elementary descent, scoped exponential equations, linear systems, semigroups and Egyptian fractions. DT/ED supply general methods, not a replacement owner of all elementary proofs. They are not added as prerequisites of CA.4.

### 2. Characters, Fourier analysis and analytic counting

AC.0 imports the audited additive-energy, Plunnecke–Ruzsa and cyclic-Fourier results and owns only the missing general finite-abelian comparison/interface work. FF.1 specializes that interface using field trace characters and owns the finite-field Gauss/Jacobi normalization comparisons used by CA.1. Existing Gauss/Jacobi identities are still imported from the audited baseline; “owner of comparison” does not mean “reprove every identity”.

A concrete interface test fixes, for a finite abelian group G, normalized averaging and Fourier coefficients

`hat(f)(chi) = (1/|G|) sum_x f(x) conjugate(chi(x))`.

Then normalized convolution and Parseval must use that same convention. Finite-field Gauss sums are commonly unnormalized sums, so the comparison must exhibit the factor |G| rather than silently identify the two conventions. Trivial characters must be separate cases. This preserves the higher-reciprocity gap in CA.1 instead of pretending finite-field character identities prove it.

ES.1 is **not** AC.0 again. Its exact counting identity uses Haar integration on the torus and integral-frequency orthogonality, before analytic major/minor-arc estimates. A finite cyclic transform is a related but different statement, especially when reducing frequencies modulo a modulus. Keep both target contracts, with a comparison only when the actual application requires one.

FF.2 remains the finite-field Weil/Deligne estimate handoff. ES.0 adds analytic completion, differencing and stationary-phase estimates; FF.5 packages applications. These are three outputs, not three proofs of the same estimate. Preserve main-term/top-cohomology and degenerate-character exceptions. Likewise ES.3's local densities and AC.5's prime-pattern local factors require different weighting/normalization data: this proposal does not identify their Euler factors by name alone.

AC.5 and ES.4 also stay separate. Finite-complexity systems of linear prime patterns do not subsume optimized Waring/circle-method ranges or prime-weighted Type I/II and major-arc hypotheses. Neither layer may advertise the other's scope as a consequence without the missing comparison and analytic inputs.

### 3. Heights, ineffective finiteness, effective bounds and enumeration

The normalized absolute-height/Northcott foundation has one upstream owner. DT.0 retains approximation exponents, continued fractions and geometry-of-numbers arguments; CA.6 retains special algebraic-number lower bounds and dynamics comparisons; RP.0 retains line-bundle/divisor functoriality and general-abelian-variety height theory. RP.0 must compare with the elliptic anchor's normalization, including the factor of two between conventions based on the x-coordinate height and conventions with a half-factor. No silent equality of differently normalized heights is acceptable.

DT.1 and DT.2 retain the full ineffective Roth/Subspace/finiteness proof targets. DT.3 owns explicit archimedean and p-adic logarithmic-form lower bounds. DT.4 converts those into bounds for the supported equation classes. **Add DT.4 → ED.2**: the present ED.2 input list contains DT.3, but the DT.4 README explicitly exports equation bounds to ED.2. Importing only the lower-bound theorem leaves that intermediate equation-specific proof unassigned in the dependency graph.

ED.2 then certifies numerical evaluation, reduction and exhaustive residual enumeration. Its contract has three independent parts: every solution satisfies the imported bound; all finite-precision comparisons have certified error margins; every remaining candidate is searched and checked exactly. Nonvanishing and logarithm/embedding conventions must travel with the bound. An ineffective finiteness theorem cannot supply an executable search cutoff, and a large finite search is not a completeness proof.

CN.4 owns generic analytic certification and precision propagation. ED.0 keeps exact algebraic-number isolation and local input adapters; analytic logarithm evaluation remains deferred to ED.2. In particular, no CN.4 → ED.0 edge is introduced. ED.1 consumes the verified LLL foundation in GN.5 but owns the required bounded-region exclusion/reduction application; it does not claim an unrestricted shortest/closest-vector algorithm merely from LLL.

DT.5's functional transcendence and RP.5's arithmetic distribution results are not duplicates. Preserve the respective Mahler/E/G-function/differential-difference and Mordell–Lang/Manin–Mumford/Bogomolov proof targets. RP.4's Parshin/Faltings and Siegel finiteness inputs remain distinct from certified effective solution methods.

### 4. Protected elliptic anchor and general descent

The anchor already owns explicit isogeny formulas, elliptic finite-field trace/Hasse statements, elliptic Mordell–Weil and two-descent algorithms. CN.3 and ED.3 may add presentation adapters, precision, saturation and completeness certificates, but must not relabel the existing algorithms as new work. FF.3 imports the elliptic trace/Hasse contract while retaining genuine algorithmic complexity and counting certificates.

More importantly, Layer 7 of the anchor already specifies **Selmer structures for general discrete Galois modules**. RP.1 must instantiate that interface and add general-abelian-variety Kummer geometry, relevant flat/fppf inputs and finite-generation comparisons; it cannot claim abstract Selmer structures as a new RP definition. ED.3 consumes RP.1 and the existing elliptic algorithm. CN.3 then consumes ED.3's new certified local-image/saturation/descent service. No reverse CN.3 → ED.3 edge is added.

The anchor's pointed elliptic forms are classified using automorphisms of (E,O). This is not a replacement for RP.3's general unpointed torsors and geometric descent. The anchor's Weil–Chatelet cohomological interface is an input to the geometric comparison, not a proof that all that geometry is finished.

The anchor's selected Q-specific database adapters include a **bounded** integral-point search. They do not give Siegel finiteness or an exhaustive all-integral-points bound. CN.5 owns generic certificate schemas and reproducibility; ED.6 and FF.5 instantiate them for their actual examples. Neither a database row nor a numerical approximation becomes a theorem merely by using that schema.

For CN.3's modular-form side, preserve the existing Hecke/character and symbol-to-form interfaces. Operator action on periods is by precomposition, so a computational matrix must be compared with the correct dual action. A Sturm equality certificate must state its level, weight, character, coefficient and cusp-width hypotheses; truncation length alone is not a theorem. The existing modular-curve dependencies are retained, not displaced by an informal appeal to modular symbols.

## Consumers and graph safety

The atlas records external consumers of CA.0/CA.2/CA.3/CA.4 in analytic number theory, q-series, arithmetic statistics, geometry of numbers and definability; DT.0/DT.5 in periods; FF.1 in analytic number theory; and RP.0/RP.1/RP.6 in arithmetic statistics and nonabelian Chabauty. The outside consumers are checked here through the atlas's stage links and descriptions, not through a claim to have reviewed their entire programmes.

The new dependency direction is explicit: CA.3 → FF.0 → CN.0; AC.0 → FF.1 → CA.1; FF.3 → CN.1 → CN.2 → CA.5; DT.3 → DT.4 → ED.2; RP.1 → ED.3 → CN.3. Anchor stages are only suppliers.
