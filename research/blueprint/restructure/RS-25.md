## 4. Hypotheses and comparisons that must survive integration

**Models and base change.** A Néron model is tested against all smooth test schemes
in its stated base category, not merely against sections over unramified extensions.
The arithmetic model is smooth and separated, usually nonproper; a proper regular
curve model is not the same construction. R11.1 retains excellent DVR/local existence
and arithmetic Dedekind gluing. SR's general-DVR curve theorem is not silently
weakened to that excellent setting. R11.1's permitted unramified base changes do not
justify commuting arbitrary ramified base change with Néron models.

**Reduction predicates.** `HasNodalReduction`, the genus-restricted semistable/stable
family predicates, and semiabelian reduction of an abelian variety are different
statements. SR7's curve–Jacobian comparison carries its own extra source hypotheses
and is not the premise of the Artin–Winters curve-reduction proof. This proposal
adds no reciprocal SR7/R11.3 dependency. A later theorem-level decomposition can
expose the comparison's primitive inputs without making either whole proof circular.

**Picard and component data.** The graph for a nodal curve retains loops and multiple
edges. The simple adjacency graph of an abstract numerical type does not. Weighted
Pic(T) uses relations a_ij / w_j, whereas Coker(A) uses the unweighted matrix. Even
a comparison of these abelian groups is not representability of Picard or a Néron
component-group theorem. R11.4 retains the exact geometric, regularity, multiplicity,
residue-field and integral hypotheses of that latter theorem.

**Elliptic invariants.** The NOS prime is different from the **residue**
characteristic. EC4's finite-level good-reduction criterion assumes potential good
reduction. EC4's Tate curve is a compatible point-level uniformisation over finite
extensions of a complete rank-one field; R11.3 still constructs the formal/analytic
objects it needs. The conductor bridge is split between EC8's semistable special
case and R01.3's general statement; wild contributions at 2 and 3 cannot disappear.
The global differential lattice is still R11.1, and R11.6 preserves public equation
invariants rather than redefining them through a model chosen by a consumer.

**Relative Proj and descent.** Reusing SR2 does not weaken the general SF.0 target
to finitely generated algebras. The general construction must agree on affine
restrictions and on the common domain; projectivity/properness and invertibility of
twists require their own hypotheses. Polarized étale descent carries the actual
ample bundle and descent datum. The broader SF.1 theorem must identify its effective
object class: subcanonical topology, descent of morphisms, effectivity of objects,
representability, and properness are not interchangeable accomplishments.

### Concrete anti-regression checks for the later blueprint

These are interface acceptance obligations, not claims of new Lean tests in this PR.

1. A split multiplicative elliptic curve has a toric identity component; its Néron
   model must not be asserted to be a proper elliptic scheme. For a nonsplit I_3
   example over a finite residue field, keep the geometric component count distinct
   from the rational component count.
2. Over an algebraically closed field, the one-self-node rational curve has a loop
   in its dual graph and a rank-one toric character lattice. A loop-free simple
   graph must fail this test.
3. Preserve SR's weighted example m=(1,1), w=(2,2), genera=(1,1),
   A=[[-2,2],[2,-2]]: weighted Pic(T) is Z, while the unweighted cokernel is
   Z plus Z/2. Do not introduce its spurious two-torsion into the geometric argument.
4. A smooth genus-one curve does not acquire an unpointed stable-curve structure
   merely because it has good reduction; pointed stabilization retains 2g-2+n>0.
5. Comparing a general relative-Proj construction to the finite-generated one must
   yield the specified canonical isomorphism and commute with base change, not
   merely identify dimensions. A descent datum without the required effectiveness
   hypotheses must not type-check as an application of polarized descent.

            assert all((supplier, c) in new | base for c in clients)

def acyclic(edges):
    adj, degree = defaultdict(set), defaultdict(int)
    for u, v in edges:
        degree.setdefault(u, 0)
        if v not in adj[u]:
            adj[u].add(v)
            degree[v] += 1
    todo = deque(v for v, d in degree.items() if not d)
    seen = 0
    while todo:
        u = todo.popleft()
        seen += 1
        for v in adj[u]:
            degree[v] -= 1
            if degree[v] == 0:
                todo.append(v)
    return seen == len(degree)
