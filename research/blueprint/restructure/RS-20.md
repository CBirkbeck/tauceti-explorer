## 3. Mathematical contracts which must survive

### 3.1 Two period domains, not one

For the anchor, form the complete Huber pair with the **two-generator** `(p,[varpi])` topology before taking the open `Y_F = D(p) intersect D([varpi])`. Neither the union nor p-adic completeness alone describes it. Its Frobenius windows and strong-noetherian interval rings belong to A6.

For general `E` with residue `F_q` and `S = Spa(R,R^+)`, construct

`curly-Y_S = Spa(W_OE(R^+)) minus V([varpi])`, then `Y_S = curly-Y_S minus V(pi)`.

The integral product is `curly-Y_S^diamond = S x Spd(O_E)`. Only the generic restriction has `Spd(E)`. The coefficient ring in the integral charts is `R^+`, not an unspecified field of fractions or a replacement perfectoid field. Keep all completed chart rings, their actual plus subrings, root-extension/tilt arguments, the split topological-module retraction, and arbitrary-base gluing. A pointwise bijection or an algebraic direct summand does not establish these topological comparisons.

F1 remains an independent, useful theorem over the fixed field, proved on **all test perfectoid spaces** through marked untilts, bounded theta maps and compatible plus rings. Generalizing to an integral product requires new work; conversely, the fixed theorem should not wait for relative bundle theory.

### 3.2 Quotients and coefficients

F2 must identify the selected generator under F1, prove the actual coproduct-of-translates orbit relation and its effectiveness, and compare the structure maps over `Spd(Q_p)`. The action is on the S factor in that presentation. Neither the slogan “quotient by Frobenius” nor a relation between underlying topological spaces identifies all the quotient functors in the documents.

There is no ordinary structural map `X_S -> S` to invent. The relative construction is functorial in the perfectoid test base; its diamond presentation and its Frobenius action are spelled out. F3 instantiates the site equivalence before coefficients. F5 remains late: C2/L0 provide the permitted categories and finite-coefficient/derived-completion construction. No new properness, lower-shriek, dualizing-complex or smoothness conclusion is inferred from the name of the curve.

### 3.3 Closed Cartier divisors are analytic theorems

FS II.1.4, printed p. 50, requires more than a principal algebraic kernel. On the specified rational neighbourhood, multiplication by a primitive equation satisfies a lower norm bound of the form `|xi a| >= q^(-n)|a|`, with the normalization and the neighbourhood fixed. This supplies **both injectivity and closed image**; the completion comparison then gives the exact quotient. The proof uses the Shilov boundary, root extension, the tilt and approximation/maximal-modulus argument, with all the actual descent hypotheses.

F4 retains the fixed-Q_p-field proof. Its extra generalization is not smuggled into that owner. The integral-divisors stage owns the all-E/all-S degree-one theorem needed to multiply ordered equations in VI.1.2, and must prove its additional relative/ramified steps. Its generic restriction is imported by RF2:untilts. This fixes an important proof-order issue: the old refined packet places the all-base integral closed-Cartier theorem under `RF2:untilts`, even though `RF2:integral-divisors -> RF2:untilts` is the correct stage order.

The reordered proof retains characteristic-p integral untilts, repeated legs, the product ideal on the ordered cover and descent of an **invertible ideal** on the symmetric v-sheaf quotient. It does not choose a global generator after unordered descent. The integral divisor has the source's affineness; the curve case is only locally affinoid on S. The degree criterion, ordinary vector-bundle descent and infinitesimal-thickening argument are explicit proof obligations, not consequences of a declaration that a ring is a v-sheaf.

### 3.4 Period rings and the base of Div^1

Construct completion along the invertible divisor ideal and its punctured localization once in RF2:integral-divisors. RF2:untilts proves the **generic degree-one interpretation** as `B_dR^+` and `B_dR`, with the relative residue, filtration/graded-line and geometric-point comparisons. In the matching mixed-characteristic p-typical affinoid case it imports R06.1/bdr-plus-of-perfectoid-affinoid-algebras after identifying the completion, theta/residue map and generator. That supplier already works over any characteristic-zero perfectoid K. RF2 retains the all-E coefficient comparison, arbitrary-base descent, integral/equal-characteristic construction and nonsupplied DVR comparisons; prove coefficient-change compatibility before extending reuse. Keep independence of local generators and all completion/base-change hypotheses. At an integral characteristic-p point this is not a characteristic-zero de Rham period ring; the ramified Witt specialization must remain a separate test.

A source-verified correction is essential. On `Perf_Fq`, FS II.1.20 gives

`Div^1 = Spd(E)/phi^Z`.

After the specified change to `Perf_k`, with `k` the algebraic closure of `F_q`, printed p. 61 uses

`Div^1 = Spd(breve E)/phi^Z`.

The second formula is not an unqualified replacement on `Perf_Fq`. Record the base change and Frobenius convention. Neither formula identifies Div^1 with the diamond of a fixed curve. The moduli definition stays early. Its properness/spatial-representability/cohomological-smoothness theorem over Spd(F_q), FS II.1.21, moves to the precise existing late owner VectorBundlesAndIsocrystals:VB3:general-BC/div1-proper-smooth, with RF2:untilts, C4 and S5 inputs. Its proof retains the finite-extension and equal-characteristic argument and the open/closed topological family map. The later positive Banach–Colmez comparisons keep their own scope. No property theorem is assumed in the early constructor.

### 3.5 Line bundles and two different patching theorems

RF3 owns O(1)/O(n), the pi-normalized Frobenius descent, tensor/dual/section comparisons, the sign of degree under the isocrystal convention, the graded section algebra P, the scheme Proj(P), and the chartwise morphism on U = union of the nonvanishing loci D(g). VB2:ampleness owns three things, in this order:
1. positive generation with cohomology vanishing (FS Theorem II.2.6), which gives U = X_S;
2. the global morphism X_S -> Proj(P), the tautological twists and f^*O(1) = O(1) (round 3; see section 8);
3. GAGA.

None of these results is a premise of the definitions it uses.

RF4:vector-bundles proves **linear** Beauville–Laszlo gluing on the actual open/completed/punctured rings, retaining finite-local-freeness and every descent/effectivity hypothesis. It supplies the BKF comparison in AI.2 without waiting for G-torsors, Bun_G uniformization or affine Grassmannians. Completion is not automatically fpqc.

BG0 first proves the appropriate three descriptions of G-torsors and their exact tensor/faithful-representation comparison. RF4:G-torsors imports that theorem and transfers the actual linear patching equivalence, proving effectivity, choice independence, change of group and relative v-local statements. It then supplies BG2:uniformization, GS0:loop-geometry and the Hecke/shtuka consumers. **Never insert RF4 -> BG0 or GS0 -> RF4** to prove the very constructions that those consumers import.
