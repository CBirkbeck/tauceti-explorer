## 3. Why the retained geometry cannot be collapsed into the anchors

### Relative abelian schemes and Picard representability

A1 works in `Over S`, not with a field-valued point group or globally chosen Weierstrass equation. Relative dimension is locally constant and may vary between components. The proof must handle a nonreduced base; agreement of geometric fibres does not establish equality of scheme morphisms. A homomorphism and a translated scheme map remain different types of data. The relative-dimension-one comparison must identify the group law and differential bundle on the actual existing elliptic scheme.

A0 is the reexport boundary for general representability and cohomology, not a second construction. A2 must check its abelian-specific Picard hypotheses and prove the abelian-algebraic-space-to-scheme step with the actual source base assumptions. Neither field-curve Picard representability, properness of an algebraic space, nor an assumed locally chosen polarization proves that step. The Poincaré bundle needs its rigidification and base-line correction, and biduality must identify evaluation, not just the underlying scheme.

The elliptic sign is a concrete acceptance condition. With translations `t_P(Q)=Q+P`, the expression `t_P^* O([0]) tensor O([0])^(-1)` has divisor class `[-P]-[0]`. The anchor's identification using `[P]-[0]` therefore requires the corresponding inversion when compared to that convention for `phi_L`. Select and prove the convention conversion instead of identifying two principal-polarization maps by their names. The proposal retains the original positivity and pairing targets; it does not change the immutable elliptic convention.

### Quotients, ranks and pairings

A3 must construct a nonaffine quotient with its **fppf universal property**. Generic quotient sheaves and affine torsor quotients are inputs, not its representability proof. Preserve effective descent and every required linearization, norm/ample-bundle or alternative source argument; characteristic dividing the group order does not permit averaging by a nonunit.

The retained multiplication theorem has `n != 0` and rank `n^(2g)` on each relative-dimension component. The README already qualifies the étaleness condition as the appropriate one; the family paraphrase is less careful. Make the positive-dimensional condition explicit. On a dimension-zero abelian component the group is the identity object and multiplication is an isomorphism even when n is not a unit. This is an acceptance clarification, not a claim that the careful README stated a false universal criterion.

Pair `A[n]` with `A-dual[n]` first. Pulling through a polarization gives a self-pairing whose radical/perfectness must be computed. A nonprincipal polarization is an essential test against accidental unimodularity. Alternation in characteristic two is not proved merely by calling a pairing skew-symmetric. Keep the Tate twist and distinguish the invertible-prime Tate local system from p-primary group schemes in characteristic p; geometric torsion points cannot replace the latter.

### Arithmetic endomorphisms and restriction of scalars

A6's finitely generated, torsion-free Hom groups, Poincaré complete reducibility, rational semisimplicity, Rosati positivity and degree formulas remain substantial new theorems. The pinned basic field End ring is not their proof. These results supply DWP.1's initial Rosati/Weil argument and the later Faltings finiteness route: do not import the later Tate isogeny theorem or Faltings finiteness to establish their own A6 prerequisites.

R09.3 owns the general restriction functor and its algebraic-space representation for finite locally free bases. A6 proves the additional abelian preservation theorem for **finite étale** bases by split-product descent and its actual scheme criterion. Its finite-separable-field Tate-module induction theorem needs embeddings, equivariance and their independence. It does not imply good reduction at a ramified integral place. Preserve the dual-number example: restriction from `k[epsilon]/(epsilon^2)` gives the elliptic tangent bundle, identified by translation with `E times Lie(E)`, and is not proper.

PEL owns the actual polarized moduli stacks, fine covers and universal families. A6 exports maps from real families to those objects; a coarse moduli point and a descended family are not interchangeable.

## 4. Integral classification and the deformation split

### Existing finite groups, genuinely new towers

Anchor 0B's scope and the inspected affine Cartier anti-equivalence rule out a second general duality carrier in R07.1. Finite flatness must carry the presentation hypotheses needed for finite local freeness. Quotients, kernels and flat closures retain their own hypotheses rather than being deemed automatically flat.

R07.1 imports that finite-level theorem and proves coherent arbitrary-height tower statements, duality, exact transition maps and the permitted Tate-limit comparisons. The elliptic tower is an explicit compatibility test. Raynaud local classification, its source bounds and its residual inertia consequences remain new integral work; 7E expressly declines to supply the general order-p classification programme.

### Rational isocrystals are not integral Dieudonné theory

VB0 is the independent owner of general rational isocrystal algebra and the full slope/classification theorem in its stated coefficient and algebraically closed field setting. Its later vector-bundle classification, Lubin–Tate and Bun_G developments are not prerequisites of R07.2. The inspected Mathlib one-dimensional theorem is only an initial case.

R07.2 constructs the group-to-module/crystal functor, integral classification, covariance and duality, F/V linearization, height/dimension formula and rationalization comparison. Tensor operations are used on the appropriate crystal/isocrystal objects or in explicitly admitted structured cases; this is not a claim that arbitrary tensor products stay in the Barsotti–Tate category. It keeps any perfect-but-not-algebraically-closed descent/forms work not supplied by VB0. Generic crystals and PD sites remain CR.0/CR.1 objects; CR.7 is the later common-object compatibility application, not an input needed to define its own R07 supplier.

### One generic Grothendieck–Messing owner

The old wording makes R07.2 promise deformations over nilpotent thickenings, while the R07.6 footer and A4 explicitly assign the generic filtration equivalence to R07.6. A naive new `R07.6 -> R07.2` edge would run backward: R07.2 is already an input to R07.6, directly and through R07.4–R07.5.

The existing `R07.6 -> A4` link is preserved for those additional local comparisons; its explanation must be updated during integration so it no longer announces a second generic owner.

For the next blueprint, split A4 into an **early Serre–Tate proof prefix** and its later degree-one/Hodge-filtration calculations. This is a proof-order instruction inside the retained A4 stage, not a claim that new fine nodes already exist. The early theorem compares actual abelian and p-divisible deformation categories. The later calculation invokes the PD-filtration theorem under its exact additional hypotheses. Endomorphism/polarization lifting is checked on morphisms and structures, not by counting dimensions of deformation spaces.

Ordinary nilpotence of an ideal, nilpotence of its divided powers and nilpotence of p must remain distinct predicates. A source statement over one such category does not establish the equivalence over every nilpotent thickening, especially in the dyadic branch.

### Small-prime and ramification outputs are unchanged

R07.3 retains the unramified Fontaine–Laffaille construction, unrestricted torsion full faithfulness in the common shifted safe interval `[0,p-2]`, and the separately restricted endpoint `[0,p-1]` statement. R07.5 retains full extension data in the ordinary/supersingular and peu/très ramification cases. The local calculations do not define a weight by assuming the representation is modular.

`G_Fontaine^(u) = G_standard^(u-1)`.
