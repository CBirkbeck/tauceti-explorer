## 2. The resulting architecture

**Discrete, compact, derived and arithmetic are separate steps.** The immutable
ProfiniteCohomology roadmap owns its canonical discrete carrier, finite-quotient
comparison, exact/change-of-groups/Shapiro/cup maps, finite Kummer and discrete
cohomological dimension. R02.1 owns the common module/complex Milnor and
Mittag–Leffler statements and their actual compact-cochain comparison. R02.2 adds
the full continuous Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence; the anchor's five-term
sequence is not that theorem. R02.3 owns arithmetic finiteness and R02.4 the actual
Poitou–Tate proof. P7 is local perfect-complex algebra over complete Noetherian local
rings, importing R02.1's limit lemmas. D7 proves arithmetic perfection and derived
duality using these inputs. This agrees with the generic-module/spectrum distinction
in RS-33 and the local-ring/derived-specialization scope of RS-16 and RS-18.

A distinguished-triangle definition of a homotopy limit does not by itself provide
canonical maps for all the arithmetic operations. R02.1 must construct the comparison
maps using its chosen complex/system model and prove their independence/coherence.
D7 does not identify cohomology with an inverse limit until the relevant lim^1 term
vanishes. The inverse-limit statements for spectra remain distinct from these module
and arithmetic comparisons.

**Selmer formalism is built once.** Selmer L2 owns local-condition maps, the semilocal
kernel and the mapping-fibre complex. Their first cohomology agrees only after the
stated H0 correction, not by definition. R02.5 and D8 retain their deformation-specific
tangent, obstruction and duality identifications. L1's pairing lemmas are parametric
in supplied subcomplex maps, so L2 can instantiate named conditions without an L2→L1
cycle. L3 owns actual arithmetic Iwasawa/corestriction/control maps, and L4 owns the
Hodge-defined local conditions with lattice/rational/quotient compatibility. K-theory
M.1/M.2 and regulator M.8 import these constructions but retain their comparison maps,
coefficient restrictions, arithmetic local factors and real-place corrections.

**General algebra is not an arithmetic deformation theorem.** R03.1 owns general
coefficient categories and R03.2 general representability algebra. Galois R04.1 owns
the generic representation deformation recipe; Local R08.1 and Global R04.2 are
separate actual representability applications. Local R08.1 does not import arithmetic
G_F,S representability. ModularCurves 7D/7F supply their Witt-vector elliptic special
cases and geometric deformation equivalence; 7B/4D supply their local-algebra and
regularity results. None supplies arbitrary Schlessinger pro-representability. These
special cases must be recovered, not reconstructed or overgeneralized.

Global R04.3 constructs an actual constrained completed-tensor presentation. R02.6
supplies the conditional dual-Selmer calculation; R04.5 proves the existence of primes
meeting it. The order is not reversed. G7 and G8 keep distinct polarized and unpolarized
variable-determinant functors and counts. The ACC+ enormous-image, auxiliary-prime,
framing, determinant, coefficient-prime and field-splitting hypotheses survive. In
particular, the variable-determinant count `g = qn - n^2[F^+:Q]` must not be reused
unchanged after fixing a multiplier/determinant, and the unpolarized framing expression
`n^2|T| - 1` must retain its nonempty framing set and residual hypotheses.

**Local moduli versus local components.** L7 owns the general bounded-height/flag
moduli and proper-image construction. R08.4 imports it and proves the rank-two
Barsotti–Tate component theorem. A proper connected parameter space does not prove
that its image is a specified whole deformation-ring component; image coverage and
scheme-theoretic structure are separate obligations. ModularCurves 0G gives the
Grassmannian special case, not the height equations or Kisin image theorem. L8's
ordered ordinary determinant identities and an invariant full flag are not identified
on arbitrary nonreduced coefficient rings without the comparison theorem's hypotheses.
Away-p local compatibility retains N, not just semisimplified inertia.

**Patching modules does not wait for patching complexes.** R03.5 and R03.6 are the
abstract module/support suppliers for classical R22 and ordinary R21.4. P8/P9 add the
complex-valued and derived-action theorems. R22.3 must construct the arithmetic modules
and verify bounds, depths and presentations; a reference to P8 does not discharge that
work. R22.4 must match the right arithmetic components. A module being nonzero on one
component proves neither faithfulness nor support on all components.

**Small-prime modularity retains separate proofs.** R21.4 supplies the exact ordinary
overlap with R22.5, not every Barsotti–Tate/crystalline branch. Classical dyadic R22.6
and modern regular-de-Rham R32.3 have different scope. R32.4 is Pan's p≥5 branch;
R32.5 imports the p=3 Skinner–Wiles/BLZ input from R21.5. The inherited sequential
R32.4→R32.5 atlas edge is not evidence that Pan proves the p=3 case. The next
fine-grained blueprint must separate that organizational ordering from the proof DAG;
this §15 format does not delete existing edges. No new reverse edge or false proof
substitution is introduced.

**Symbols, periods and families.** ModularForms 8 supplies homological modular symbols,
the Hecke/coefficient-field construction and its period-map result. The full
cohomological/Eichler–Shimura comparison remains Symbols L0. Symbols L1 owns signed
period lines and saturated integral lattices, including Gauss and Euler-factor
normalizations. A coefficient field is not a canonical integral period basis. Symbols
L2 owns chosen-root stabilization and small-slope distribution-symbol control.
Families L1/L2 retain the new family variation, control and generic-machine application.
F/L0a owns finite/profinite factorial projectors, with the existing Fitting theorem as
an input; Ordinary R21.1 applies them to arithmetic modules.

Rational/totally-real cuspidal Hida control is F/L0/L5. R21.2 retains its additional
nearly ordinary/Eisenstein/residually reducible branches, with their actual integral
and small-prime assumptions. R21.3 owns ordinary big Galois/determinant data; F/L4
imports it and keeps family Selmer/regulator/big-Kato reciprocity. F/L3 is the single
owner of critical/theta-critical/secondary theory, supplying Symbols L3. There is no
reverse critical-slope edge, no Kato-reciprocity prerequisite for the analytic symbols,
and no ordinary-arithmetic-export-to-symbol-foundation edge from R21.6.

**Motives retain three different correspondence constructions.** SF.5 supplies ordinary
Chow intersection operations. Motives MC.0 forms rational-equivalence correspondences
on smooth projective schemes, and MC.1 their pure Karoubi/Tate category. K-theory M.4
owns higher Chow complexes; M.5a owns finite correspondences, transfers and effective
Nisnevich/A1 motives. These are not interchangeable carriers. For example, the rational
points 0 and infinity of P1 define distinct generators of the free finite-correspondence
group from Spec(Q), while their degree-one zero-cycles agree modulo rational equivalence.
A bridge to the chosen Chow-motive variance needs the appropriate cycle class, transpose
and duality conventions, not a renaming of hom-sets.

MC.4 imports M.4 and the **fine stage M.5a**, not the completed norm-residue summit, and
keeps geometric mixed/Tate-stabilization and motivic-Hom comparisons. MC.2's realizations
need not factor through numerical equivalence; MC.3's semisimplicity is numerical, not
Chow semisimplicity. M.3's Tate degree-two route remains independent of the all-weight
M.5a–d theorem. M.6a retains its K-theoretic layer-to-cycle comparison and M.6b its
filtered convergence/Adams statements. The seven KU checkpoints retain provenance and
aggregation; they are not seven extra mathematical theories.

## 6. Regression checks and integration limits

The accompanying local audit performed structural checks and these elementary
mathematical tests, not Lean proofs:

1. On `(Z/8)^2`, U=diag(2,3), the factorial powers at n=4,5,6 are all diag(0,1),
   the intended ordinary projector, with idempotence and commutation checked. On the
   rank-one complex vector space, U=2 has powers 2^(n!) which do not stabilize; finite
   generation alone is not the L0a hypothesis.
2. In characteristic 2, the identity matrix lies in sl2. On the basis I,E12,E21,
   the trace-pairing Gram matrix has rank 2, not 3. D8/G7 cannot identify the
   trace-zero adjoint with its dual using that degenerate pairing.
3. For O=Z_3, the two-term complex O --3→ O in degrees 0,1 has only H1=F3; derived
   tensor with F3 has a zero differential and nonzero cohomology in both degrees.
   Ordinary tensoring of H1 loses the extra term. Inverting 3 instead makes the
   complex acyclic, so generic-fibre vanishing does not imply integral vanishing.
4. The residue field F3 is finite over Z_3 but has no characteristic-zero point:
   a unital map to a characteristic-zero field would send 3=0 to 3≠0. This tests
   R03.4's non-torsion/characteristic-zero-locus hypotheses.

The first two were checked by explicit finite arithmetic; the last two are displayed
algebraic calculations and their elementary matrix/rank reductions, not numerical
experiments purporting to prove a general theorem. The P1 correspondence example in
§2 is a mathematical non-example, not an assertion that such a scheme computation
was run in Lean.
