## Decision and extension boundaries

Keep all three mathematical developments, with two explicit extensions:

| Roadmap | Decision | Starting boundary and residual work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EllipticKTheory | Extend **Elliptic curves** as **Elliptic curves, Part II: scheme K-theory and arithmetic symbol classes** | Import equation/function-field elliptic theory and the existing scheme/Picard dictionaries. Build their K-theory comparisons, curve K₀–K₃, arithmetic integral images and certified symbol classes. |
| KTheoryLowDegrees | Extend **Grothendieck groups, Cartan maps, and Euler forms** as **Grothendieck groups, Cartan maps, and Euler forms, Part II: explicit ring and curve K₀ and stable-matrix K₁** | Import ordinary categorical K₀, exact structures, finite resolutions and Cartan maps. Add explicit projective/rank/determinant/Steinitz calculus, general curves and stable-matrix/arithmetic K₁. |
| SchemeKTheoryOperations | Keep | Import categorical K-theory, enhanced derived sheaves, early ring K₀ and intersection theory. Own general scheme K/G maps, supports, descent, coniveau, geometric formulas and higher operations. |

For each extension the named base becomes its **first roadmap prerequisite**.
The present introductions already call E and Z/U Part II developments; the
proposal makes that relationship precise and removes the remaining duplicate
constructions. The scheme roadmap is an independent general theory, not an
elliptic application. A merge would blur the early explicit ring theory with the
later spectrum/scheme comparisons on which only some of its stages depend.

All **36 stages** remain, including **nine readiness checkpoints**. Narrow
E.1–E.7, Z.1–Z.2, Z.5–Z.6, U.5, S.1–S.3 and S.5–S.7: **18 stages**. The other

## Upstream ownership and generality safeguards

**E.1–E.2.** ModularCurves 1A/1B/1D owns the projective model, points dictionary
and scheme group law; 2A/2B owns multiplication and finite-flat isogenies; 2D
owns relative elliptic Picard duality with descent. Its multiplication-map
function-field bridge is deliberately narrow. AlgebraicCurves 12 owns the
broad regular-projective-curve/function-field and arbitrary-isogeny dictionary.
E.1 retains their compatibility with the actual K-theory carriers and treats
zero separately. JacobianChallenge A/B supplies line bundles and coherent
Euler characteristic, not an identification of every invariant geometric
class with a line bundle over the ground field. E.2 must reconcile the existing
function-field point equivalence and the scheme Picard package before applying
Z.5; the right side of its K₀ formula is not a product ring by fiat.

**Z.1–Z.2 and Z.5.** GrothendieckEulerForms owns the categorical presentations,
exact structures and Cartan inputs. General ring idempotent/stable-equality,
scalar-extension and Morita comparisons remain early Z.1 work. Higher K.2/K.7
uses and compares with these; it cannot supply a prerequisite needed to define
its own ring input. Z.5's general regular noetherian curve target is broader
than JacobianChallenge's smooth proper field-valued curve scope or
AlgebraicCurves' regular projective dictionary. Those cases are imported;
additional nonproper/arithmetic dictionary and resolution arguments remain
explicitly in Z.5, not silently discharged or removed.

**S.1–S.3.** EnhancedDerivedSheaves E1 supplies generic derived sheaves and
enhancement. P7 supplies only complete-Noetherian-local perfect-module
constructions. S.1 retains general affine comparison, local perfectness, qcqs
smallness and the global resolution-property theorem. Singular schemes remain
valid inputs. S.2's higher Cartan equivalence retains its regular, noetherian,
finite-dimensional hypotheses. S.3 keeps support/G-theory when a closed
subscheme cannot be substituted by K(Z). K2SymbolsBrauer T.3:symbols and
T.3:localization-comparison supply the tame formula and its ring comparison;
S.3 supplies the scheme boundary comparison, with the same signs.

**S.4–S.7.** Ordinary codimension coniveau, homotopy-coniveau layer-to-cycle
comparison and motivic convergence are not one theorem: their owners are S.4,
M.6a and M.6b. K.6 owns the Laurent ring fundamental theorem, including singular
Nil terms. S.5 retains the geometric extension, projective bundles and blowups.
K.7 owns generic external products. SchemeAndStackFoundations SF.5 owns
geometric Chow/Chern/intersection/Gysin/excess and source-scoped GRR;
S.7 owns their realization and normalization on these K/G maps. SF.5 is a
source-acquisition task, not an available unrestricted GRR theorem. Any further
generality needed for M/ER must be proved explicitly, without a second

**E.6–E.7.** StableReduction 4/5 supplies local DVR blowups, resolution, common
