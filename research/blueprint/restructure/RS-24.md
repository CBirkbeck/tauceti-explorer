## Adjudication of all four overlap leads

### 1. IHG.2 versus TC.2: generic ghost theorem versus geometry

This is a supplier–consumer relation, not two proofs of the same geometric result. IHG.2 owns the abstract comparison of chain, homotopy/derived and cohomological Hecke images and the amplitude-dependent nilpotence theorem for the ideal of derived endomorphisms acting trivially on cohomology. Keep the ideal-level composition theorem: individual endomorphisms being nilpotent without a uniform product bound is not enough.

TC.2 must first construct the actual finite-level/completed/perfectoid-Cech/section comparison, its action on one bounded object and the relevant amplitude. It then applies IHG.2 and obtains the geometric error ideal and compatible quotient maps. A generic perfect complex, or separate equalities on each cohomology group, does not supply the arithmetic correspondence diagram.

The original IHG.2 → TC.2 link is preserved. TC.2 is narrowed to import, not reprove, the generic theorem. Its four existing CC.1/2/4/8 suppliers remain explicit. CC owns the general tower, completion, limit order, finite-level chain models and Shimura adapter; TC proves the additional geometry and all instance hypotheses. A complete tower need not have vanishing lim1 without the required control argument. The older GL2/Qp local–global theorem is not a general tower constructor.

### 2. IHG.3 versus TC.3: normalized GLn polynomial versus factor extraction

IHG.3 owns the determinant-facing integral GLn polynomial

`P_v(X) = sum_i (-1)^i q_v^(i(i-1)/2) T_(v,i) X^(n-i)`, with `T_(v,0)=1`,

for the stated unramified place, coefficients, arithmetic Frobenius and `vol(K)=1` convention. SR.4, not IHG.3, owns the local spherical algebra, Satake transform and its coefficient/q-half conventions. The existing SR.4 → IHG.3 link is retained and the latter's wording narrowed accordingly.

TC.3 needs that polynomial and its conversion laws, but it must still construct and compare the actual symplectic/unitary boundary and Levi data. The added IHG.3 → TC.3 and ALS.4 → TC.3 links identify the correct suppliers. ALS.4 owns the general boundary complex, induced Hecke action and modulus/Tate shifts; TC.3 proves the particular ambient-to-Levi, contragredient and character-twist identities.

The abstract factor-separation lemma stays in TC.3. It needs actual auxiliary characters satisfying the separation condition, coefficient-ring endomorphisms, identities of determinant laws, change-of-ring and independence of the permitted choices. Pointwise factorization of polynomials does not construct a continuous determinant. Nor can the GLn polynomial simply be renamed as the polynomial of an unspecified representation of a symplectic or unitary L-group. This is why the alleged overlap does not justify dropping or moving the factor-extraction work.

### 3. IHG.5 versus TC.2: generic schema versus a proved premise

Keep IHG.5 as a theorem schema on an actual supplied comparison and quantified nilpotent error. TC.2 constructs that premise in its arithmetic setting. The ownership ledger makes both halves explicit; it does not turn the schema's premise into a universally valid assertion about arithmetic spaces.

**Do not insert a TC.2 → IHG.5 construction edge.** The generic theorem must be available independently of a particular application. Instead, TC.3/TC.4 consume IHG.5 alongside their TC.2 input. Their application combines the generic theorem with the geometrically proved premise. This avoids imposing a spurious whole-roadmap cycle or requiring completed geometry before the algebra can be developed.

IHG.5 currently has no recorded consumers despite being the named quotient-descent supplier. The added IHG.5 → TC.3/TC.4 links expose the use at the correct stage. IHG.4 → TC.4 similarly records the generic compatible-limit theorem, while TC.4 still proves the source's actual uniformity and compatibility hypotheses. A quotient-valued determinant is not silently promoted to a determinant over the full Hecke algebra.

### 4. IHG.6 versus TC.4: reject the false positive

IHG.6 is the independent integral Ribet theorem with residual coincidence allowed; TC.4 is a four-route comparison theorem and validation suite. Neither proves the other's endpoint. No ownership is transferred between them, and no dependency is created merely because the evidence uses the words “integral Hecke/determinant compatibility.”

Preserve **both** Ribet versions in IHG.6. The first constructs a finite T-module and a continuous cohomology class with the stated generation and Fitting bound. Here “finite T-module” means finitely generated, not necessarily a finite discrete set. The local-condition version retains the inclusion of coefficient rings, completeness, reduced-factor irreducibility and congruence assumptions, local triangularizations and the Sigma/P partition. It also retains the coboundary generators in addition to the cocycle image and the extended-ideal containment **in Ttilde** with the product of local factors. Neither an annihilator bound nor dropping those factors preserves this theorem.

The matrix-invariant argument, rational algebraic-group cohomology, and the grade/exactness and obstruction calculations for the Koszul/Buchsbaum–Rim complexes remain explicit proof work. Rational algebraic-group cohomology here is not cohomology of discrete GL2(Z). IHG.1's residually multiplicity-free reconstruction cannot supply the residual-coincidence branch. IHG.6 → IntegralIwasawaTheory:I.7 stays unchanged: the latter verifies the actual Hilbert/Eisenstein and ramification hypotheses and proves the arithmetic Fitting comparison.
