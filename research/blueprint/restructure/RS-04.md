### 2.1 Adelic geometry, automorphic functions and spectral theory

AA owns reductive adelic points, quotient measures, Tamagawa normalization, reduction and qualified approximation over number fields. Its AA.0 restricted-product construction has a genuinely characteristic-independent topological/measure component. GN owns the number-field adele and idele carriers, additive discreteness/cocompactness and norm-one idele compactness. AA must compare with those carriers, not manufacture alternative copies.

AF owns real representation-theoretic globalizations, relative Lie cochains, the automorphic-function category, constant terms, cuspidal rapid decay and cuspidal multiplicity. AA.3 supplies its invariant height comparisons. AF.1a supplies van Est and invariant forms; it does not itself prove the arithmetic quotient comparison in R.2 or the weighted automorphic comparison in AS.5.

AS retains the hard analysis: measurable Hilbert fields/direct integrals, Eisenstein continuation, intertwining operators, truncation, residual and continuous spectra, surjectivity/completeness, weighted comparison, and the general trace formula. Importing AF.3's cuspidal theorem does not turn this into a completed spectral theory. In particular, MF Layer 11's level-one holomorphic Eichler–Selberg formula cannot supply the invariant trace formula for general reductive groups. MF Layer 3's Petersson/old-new theory cannot supply a full residual/continuous spectral decomposition either.

AA.5, AF.5 and V8 have different comparison endpoints: respectively the **underlying quotient**, the **functions/weights/Hecke actions**, and the **canonical algebraic model with determinant, Weil pairing, cusps and compactification**. Keep all three and connect the underlying comparison to its consumers. The classical normalization of Hecke operators is not replaced by a second adelic convention; AF.5 must prove the conversion.

### 2.2 Borel regulators

R.1 already imports reduction and Borel–Serre/finite-complex infrastructure. Its order/division-algebra specialization, block maps and finite-type homotopy consequences remain. R.2 imports van Est but owns the arithmetic restriction and block-inclusion comparison. R.3's stable calculation and its rational K-rank consequence are not the same theorem as finite generation. R.4 still proves the regulator image is a full lattice, with integral basis and orientation conventions. R.5 retains analytic orders of vanishing and rational proportionality; it does not claim the full integral Lichtenbaum formula. R.6 imports generic measures but still proves Bloch's pairing and every conversion factor. R.7 retains the Beilinson/Deligne scalar comparison and explicit tests.

Measure reuse is conditional on matching the actual group, category and measure normalization. AA.2 is not a license to assume a Tamagawa-measure theorem for an arbitrary K-theoretic or non-reductive object. Any Bloch-specific measure construction outside that supplier's hypotheses remains an R.6 obligation, along with the comparison proving that the imported measure is the intended one.

The `KU-arithmeticgroups`, `KU-borelranks` and `KU-borelreg` nodes are preserved readiness checkpoints, not duplicate proof owners. Their original source-unit aggregation edges remain.

### 2.3 CM algebra, reciprocity, explicit generators and Heegner points

HE.0 then constructs the actual conductor-change tower, norm maps, kernel and degree formulas, unit corrections and conjugation action. CM.3 proves which explicit CM values generate those fields and why the class polynomials are integral. Neither theorem replaces the other.

### 2.4 General descent versus its arithmetic hypotheses

All ES stages are kept. Existing import boundaries in HE.4–HE.8 are clarified by owner entries and direct links, not by deleting those arithmetic stages.

ES.1 proves the finite/singular comparison with the cyclic tensor factor and the simultaneous localization/core-vertex prime-supply theorem. HE.5 must apply this to the actual residual representation and classes. ES.3 proves corrected derivative construction in the source-qualified setting; HE.4 must construct and compare the actual Heegner/Howard system. Raw derivatives are not automatically genuine Kolyvagin systems.

ES.4's generic error-tolerant inequalities are conditional on **proved** finite error bounds. HE.5 and HE.7 prove the arithmetic errors and uniformity. Clean odd-prime large-image descent in HE.6 does not dispose of dyadic, bad, exceptional or CM branches in HE.7. Separate finiteness at each prime is still not finiteness of the entire Sha without the almost-all vanishing step. At p=2, an integral proof cannot use division by 2 in the conjugation projectors.

ES.5's primitivity theorem is not proof that a particular nonzero Heegner point is primitive. The factor two/square-index bound in the self-dual Heegner application is not a general formula for every representation. ES.6–ES.7 retain the exterior-bidual, Gorenstein and higher-Fitting machinery with their original reflexivity, cohomological and small-prime hypotheses. ES.8's control interface does not prove geometric nonvanishing or a main-conjecture equality.

The integrated Heegner source decomposition records corrected Howard classes and explicitly limited theorem hypotheses. It also says HE.0, HE.7, HE.7s and HE.8b are not read there. This proposal neither promotes them to source-complete nor discards their targets. The Gross norm/reduction-congruence proof and the classical CM/dyadic source obligation remain real work. HE.8's early nonvanishing route, HE.8c's source-qualified Cornut–Vatsal assertions and HE.8b's later main-conjecture comparison remain separated. In particular, preserve the existing `L5a/L5w -> HE.8b -> L5b` orientation rather than using the desired main-conjecture equality to construct its own input.

### 2.5 Function fields really extend the curve foundation

AC0/1/2/6/8/12 supply FA.0's general carriers and model dictionary. Finite exact constants and the chosen arithmetic presentation still require explicit specialization/comparison. Over the exact finite constant field, smoothness/geometric connectedness have the intended hypotheses; a constant extension viewed over the smaller field fails that contract.

AC3/4/5/9 supply FA.1's divisors, product formula, Riemann–Roch, residues and finite degree-zero class group. Riemann–Roch does not by itself give a certified basis algorithm for a chosen input representation. That algorithm and its correctness have a single owner, FA.1; FA.7 imports it and validates assembled arithmetic certificates. The rational `L(n infinity)` calculation belongs to AC3, not AC1 alone.

AC4's repartitions have entries in the function field; they are not silently identified with the completed local-field restricted product. AC5/9 supply local completion/filtration and residue comparisons. FA.2 retains the completed-adele comparison, residue characters, self-dual measures, global annihilator statement and Poisson summation. It must prove the change-of-differential normalization and compactness of the degree-zero idele quotient. AA.0 supplies only generic restricted-product topology/measure machinery.

AC6–8 and AC10 supply FA.3's general finite-extension, lower-ramification, different/Hurwitz and basic Kummer/Artin–Schreier material. They do not supply the full upper-numbering and Witt cyclic p-power theory. The latter remains FA.3, with the original local-ramification supplier to be specialized at its actual hypotheses. Purely inseparable extensions remain separate from separable Galois covers.

FA.4 retains the full equal-characteristic p-primary and global reciprocity proof. The number-field ClassFieldTheory endpoint cannot replace it. FA.5 retains zeta/Artin factors and Chebotarev with constants-induced degree congruences and genus/conductor-dependent errors. No archimedean Harish–Chandra argument is renamed as a proof of FA.6. Its exports to global shtukas and excursion operators stay intact.
