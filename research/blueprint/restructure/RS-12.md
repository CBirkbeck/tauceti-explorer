## 5. Branch and interface contracts

**Existence versus strength.** Keep separate packages for: (i) good-prime
characteristic-zero GL_n existence; (ii) polarized systems with the available
full WD and coefficient-prime results; (iii) cohomological unitary
 discrete-parameter sums; and (iv) lattice/residual polynomial comparison.
A nonselfdual HLTT package does not acquire the other packages by coercion.
New stronger results require their own source and exact hypotheses.

**Uniqueness.** Frobenius-polynomial recognition concerns semisimple
representations up to isomorphism. It does not produce a unique matrix conjugacy,
a canonical basis or a canonical integral lattice. Compare coefficient embeddings
and arithmetic/geometric Frobenius and twists before applying recognition; prove
any coherence actually needed by a consumer separately.

**Compatible systems.** The common carrier contains representations at the
coefficient places, not only common traces. Weak, almost-strict and strict
conditions are separate predicates. The label “strict” in a source proving
away-from-coefficient-prime compatibility does not supply another source's
coefficient-prime assertions. Purity, regularity, polarization and admissibility
are proved properties with preservation hypotheses, not unconditional fields.

**Hecke rings.** R19.6 must construct an actual integral determinant with the
uniform congruences and continuity required by IHG.4. Its reduced characteristic-
zero points do not see nilpotents. The inherited Chenevier Theorem B statement
uses a henselian local ring with algebraically closed residue field; applying a
suitable reconstruction theorem to a finite-residue-field Hecke algebra requires
the appropriate split/descent theorem. Do not erase that source gap or conclude
an actual representation from every residually reducible determinant.

**Geometric special cases.** In weight two, retain the restriction-of-scalars
comparison for `V_ell(A_f)`: total Q_ell dimension `2[K_f:Q]`, rank two over each
completed eigenvalue field. In higher weight, retain symmetric-power/parabolic
cohomology, not an invented abelian quotient. The weight-one Artin case has its
own hypotheses and repeated Hodge weights; it is not discarded by a regularity
restriction imposed in Part II.

**Genericity.** AG2.7 supplies unramifiedness and the no-ratio-p condition at each
place over the stated completely split auxiliary prime. IG.5 supplies the
separate dual-Hecke-normalized Igusa obstruction, including its inverse-root and
Tate-twist comparison. G7 owns enormous-image and other global residual-image
hypotheses. Pairwise distinctness, local no-ratio genericity, global
irreducibility, adequacy and enormity are not interchangeable conditions.

### A directly checked source-consistency problem

The inherited R19.1 node
`geometric-construction-and-the-eichler-congruence-relation` says that a geometric
characteristic-p fibre has only one subgroup scheme of order p, the Frobenius
kernel. The sentence also appears in the scanned Deligne passage on printed
p. 157, so this is **not** being attributed merely to a faulty PDF extraction.
As a universal assertion about all order-p subgroups it fails:

```text
E / F_5 : y^2 = x^3 + 3x + 2
4*3^3 + 27*2^2 = 1 (mod 5), so E is nonsingular.
E(F_5) = {O, (1,1), (1,4), (2,1), (2,4)}.
```

Thus these rational points form a reduced constant subgroup scheme of order five.
It is not the Frobenius kernel: Frobenius fixes the displayed nonzero rational
points rather than sending them to O. Together with the degree-five Frobenius
kernel this gives at least two order-five subgroup schemes. Over the algebraic
closure one may choose prime-to-five fine level, so the level datum does not
remove the example. Direct enumeration and the discriminant calculation were
run in this pass.

The later blueprint must correct or properly qualify that fibre-count assertion
and check the ordinary/supersingular distinction. The finite-correspondence target
is retained; the argument needs finite fibres, not this incorrect universal count.
No claim is made here to have repaired the complete source proof.

### Additional acceptance obligations

These are checks for the future blueprint, not claims of new Lean proofs.
The finite-field ratio and matrix identities below were checked computationally.

1. **Monodromy is not determined by the underlying semisimplification.** Set
   `F=diag(1/2,1)` and `N=[[0,1],[0,0]]`. Then `F N F^-1 = N/2` and `N^2=0`.
   Both N and zero satisfy the WD relation for an unramified Weil action with
   modulus 1/2 on this Frobenius. Their underlying semisimple action agrees, but
   their monodromy ranks differ. A semisimplified comparison cannot identify them.
2. **Ratio versus distinctness.** In F_5 at an auxiliary prime 7, roots `(1,1)`
   satisfy the no-ratio-7 condition, while the distinct roots `(1,2)` fail it.
   At auxiliary prime 11 repeated roots fail. The reducible trivial rank-two
   representation at 7 also satisfies the local ratio condition; it does not
   become globally irreducible. Unramifiedness remains a separate requirement.
3. **Nilpotents.** Over `Q_p[epsilon]/(epsilon^2)`, the representations of the
   additive group Z_p given by `diag(1+epsilon*x,1)` and the identity have identical
   field-valued specializations but different determinant laws. This toy test
   concerns loss of nilpotent information, not the residual-irreducible
   reconstruction theorem. An argument using only those specializations fails it.
4. **Coefficient dimensions and weight ranges.** Test weight two with a quadratic
   eigenvalue field, both split and nonsplit coefficient primes, and compare the
   resulting Q_ell dimension four. Test higher weight with parabolic cohomology
   and weight one via Deligne–Serre instead of assigning either an A_f by fiat.
5. **Early/late dependency fence.** The raw AG2.1a signature must not mention an
   already attached general automorphic Galois representation, established `rec`,
   torsion concentration or a potential-automorphy endpoint. Separately test n=1
   normalization and n=2 comparison after the late dictionary is available.

Other inherited gaps include Carayol's auxiliary-place/bad-reduction cases,
normalization conversion across sources, finite-residue-field reconstruction,
the full approximation/descent and discrete-parameter proofs, and source-complete
coefficient-prime/ordinary/endpoint interfaces. Restructuring does not close these
proof gaps and does not remove their mathematical targets.
