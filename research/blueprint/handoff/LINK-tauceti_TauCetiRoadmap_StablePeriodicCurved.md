## Nine-stage input/output audit

**Layer 0.** Input: a preadditive category, additive two-sided morphism ideals, and then an intrinsic Quillen exact structure. Output: additive quotients and universal properties; relative projective/injective predicates; Frobenius exact categories; generic finite-dimensional Frobenius-functional and self-injectivity interfaces. The ideal generated by factorizations is its additive closure: no biproduct-closure hypothesis is needed to form it. Closure under zero, finite biproducts and isomorphisms is needed to represent each ideal element through one object. No arbitrary ideal quotient is declared triangulated.

**Layer 1.** Input: Frobenius exactness and admissible pushout/pullback calculus. Output: suspension, syzygy and their choice-independent equivalence, Happel triangles, and descent of conflation-exact functors preserving projective-injectives. The exact-structure supplier is upstream, not a consumer of this triangulation.

**Layer 2.** Input: appropriate finite-dimensional self-injective or two-sided noetherian Iwanaga–Gorenstein hypotheses, with finitely generated totally acyclic projective resolutions. Output: stable Gorenstein-projective and singularity comparisons. Keep small finite-module and large all-module categories distinct. The general exact-category comparison first needs the construction of `D^b(E)` by exact-acyclic localization; GrothendieckEulerForms explicitly does not supply this construction. Finite resolutions in that supplier are not bi-infinite total acyclicity here.

**Layer 3.** Input: additive categories for degreewise-split exact structures, and the stated abelian/localization hypotheses for homology. Output: positive-period cyclic complexes, differential modules, homotopy and periodic-derived categories. Contractibility, zero homology and zero in the derived category are different predicates; parity-forgetting has no canonical inverse.

**Layer 4.** Input: bounded and periodic complexes plus the DG pretriangulated-envelope interface. Output: compression, the additive orbit category with actual direct-sum Hom groups, the gradable image and triangulated hull. The orbit/hull comparison assumes a finite-dimensional algebra of finite global dimension. The hull is generated under shifts and cones before Karoubi completion; `tria` cannot silently become `Perf`. Relative projectivity does not imply homotopical projectivity without further hypotheses.

**Layer 5.** Input: signed graded-algebra and DG-category interfaces. Output: right-convention curved algebras and modules, same-curvature Hom DG categories, shifts/cones and duplexes. The equations are `d_A²(a)=aw-wa` and `d_M²(m)=mw`; the graded opposite has curvature `-op(w)`. Left-module signs need an explicit opposite bridge. It is the Hom differential that becomes square-zero by common-curvature cancellation, not the individual curved module differential.

**Layer 6.** Input: the curved homotopy category and the necessary products/coproducts. Output: absolute/co/contra closures, Verdier quotients and comparison functors. The abstract closure tool is reusable, but the curved-module instantiation and its exact hypotheses remain visible. No canonical co-to-contra comparison is asserted. Finite graded homological dimension and the additional degree restrictions for comparison with ordinary acyclicity are not dropped; natural localisation comparisons are not strict equalities.

**Layer 7.** Input: finite-projective duplexes and separately stated hypersurface hypotheses. Output: matrix-factorization homotopy categories and Eisenbud, Buchweitz and Orlov comparisons. Keep regular-local/nonzerodivisor assumptions distinct from the affine regular/flat-superpotential theorem. Neither zero potential nor idempotent completion is silently inserted. Duality changes potential sign and tensor constructions specify the resulting potential.

**Layer 8.** Input: the constructed categories and named equivalences. Output: explicit comparison functors with checked structures, hypotheses, kernels/images and natural coherence, not definitional identification of different quotients.
