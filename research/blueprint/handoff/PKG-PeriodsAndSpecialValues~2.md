3. **Give a rational projective comparison route.** Park's published 2024 paper supplies explicit cochain maps and Frobenius compatibility. PS.8.17 specializes its map to S=tφ and six-forms, gives the two Euler contractions and the geometric residue, and describes the compatible-radius adapter to the usual charge-zero dagger complex. Corollary 4.12 compares polynomial and radius complexes for a smooth complete-intersection fibre; filtered colimits and RD.4.31 handle radii and finite coefficient extensions. This is a fixed smooth finite-field fibre argument, not an assertion about the formal boundary.
4. **Derive the normalization.** Park Proposition 5.2 has ρ(qF_raw)=Frρ, with a factor q. Each Euler contraction contributes q when commuting past coordinate Frobenius, and the geometric residue contributes the (−1) Tate twist. Consequently (Res T)F_raw=q²Fr_V(Res T). This supplies the rational good-fibre q⁻² normalization route missing from the previous checkpoint; it does not establish formal λ specialization, the log boundary comparison or integral cohomology.
5. **Expose the signed factorials.** SH Lemma 4.8 yields the column matrix D from derivative classes to monomial classes, with det D=1. Park's normalized residue sends u_s to (−1)^s s!r_s. Therefore the derivative-to-residue matrix is R=diag(1,−1,2,−6)D, with det R=12. At p=3 its reduction has rank three; at p=7 it is invertible. This is a rational coordinate calculation. If the residue classes were a geometric W-basis over an unramified Witt ring, the derivative quotient would have W-length one at 3; its cardinality need not be 3 when the residue field is larger. No such integral-basis premise is assumed or proved here. In particular R is not the missing geometric lattice matrix C.
