## Eight-stage input/output worksheet

**Layer 0 — exact structures.** Inputs are additive categories and genuine kernel-cokernel universal properties; outputs are Quillen exact structures, admissible squares, exact functors, opposites, split/canonical structures and induced structures on replete full additive extension-closed subcategories. Split and canonical exact structures must not be conflated. Relative projective/injective ownership remains R-RELATIVE, rather than a silent reverse dependency.

**Layer 1 — presentations.** Essential smallness and free abelian groups supply object classes and relation quotients. Outputs include object-level induction/lift principles, uniqueness, independence of skeleton choices, products and biadditivity. A small model is not a claim that the entire unrestricted module category is small.

**Layer 2 — Grothendieck groups.** The common presentation engine produces split, exact, abelian and triangulated K0 and the graded shift action. Functoriality carries the correct additive, conflation-exact or triangulated hypothesis. The bounded comparisons have their stated domains; a general exact-derived category is expressly outside this layer. Laurent coefficients belong to Layer 6.

**Layer 3 — resolutions.** Inputs include the specified resolving subcategory and finite resolutions. Outputs are Euler-class independence, the K0 resolution theorem and graded versions. Projective comparison/horseshoe arguments require their stronger lifting hypotheses. Relative duality and Koszul complexes are not supplied by a numerical matrix identity; R-KOSZUL restricts the imported criterion.

**Layer 4 — Cartan theory.** A finite-dimensional algebra over a field, actual projective covers, finite bases and the stated split/endomorphism conditions produce the projective/simple pairing and Cartan map. Columns are projectives, with entry [P_j:S_i]. Finite global dimension supplies the full comparison only under the stated finite-resolution conditions. Determinant plus/minus one is not a claim about its positive sign or arbitrary rings.

**Layer 5 — finite Euler descent.** Explicit support and dimension bounds permit alternating sums and exact-sequence additivity. Long exact Ext sequences and closure of the selected exact categories support bilinear descent. Pairwise eventual vanishing is distinct from finite global dimension. Graded cohomological and internal support are separate; geometric finiteness proofs are not replaced by the finite-sum identity.

**Layer 6 — Laurent coefficients.** A compatible invertible shift action supplies the Laurent-module structure. Finite Laurent support and degree conventions produce q-Euler sesquilinearity. Algebraic q-specialization is not automatically comparison with an ungraded category: the forgetful functor and graded/ungraded Ext decomposition must be supplied and checked.

**Layer 7 — numerical quotients.** Left and right radicals and quotient universal properties produce two numerical groups. Identification needs additional duality. Maps require radical preservation, not only preservation on the image of a pair of maps. R-RADICAL makes this requirement explicit.

## Mathematical safeguards

### R-RADICAL: pairing preservation alone is insufficient

Layer 7 correctly first requires radical preservation, but its following sentence about pairing-preserving pairs must carry those hypotheses explicitly. Let L=R=Z with zero pairing. Let L'=R'=Z^2 with

`B((a,b),(c,d)) = a*d + b*c`,

and let both maps send n to (n,0). The maps preserve the source pairing since B((n,0),(m,0))=0. Both source radicals are all of Z. The target matrix is `[[0,1],[1,0]]`, with determinant -1, so both target radicals are zero. In particular, B((1,0),(0,1))=1. Neither map preserves its source radical and neither descends compatibly from the zero source numerical quotient to Z^2.

Thus the repair is to retain the two explicit radical containments. For one-sided descent, surjectivity on the opposite side is a sufficient additional condition: every opposite target argument is then the image of an original argument, so the pairing-preservation equation tests against all of them. The counterexample does not reject the valid quotient construction or the preceding correct hypothesis; it rules out reading the next sentence as an independent sufficient criterion. This elementary calculation was also checked locally.
