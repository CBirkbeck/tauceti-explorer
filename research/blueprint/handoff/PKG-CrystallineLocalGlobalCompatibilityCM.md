## Adversarial mathematical pass

Read and checked every accepted statement, hypothesis, definition, convention,
API and negative control against its README expression. The layer counts are
10, 13, 6, 16, 5, 8, 13, 7, 7 and 12 targets. The audit covered degenerate
ranks, coefficient denominators and shifts, source restrictions, genericity,
residual hypotheses and interfaces; it is not a proof of the planned theorems.

- **CL.0:** empty/rank-one block exchange is defined; rank two distinguishes
  exchange from full reversal. Positive block-central exponents are descending
  integers, permitting negative common shifts. Lowest-weight scaling uses the
  full unitary weight, a nonzero uniformizer and inverse scalar rescaling.
  Integral coefficient preservation and evaluation remain genuine lattice
  assertions. Both Hecke polynomials retain their X powers.
- **CL.1:** smooth monoid restriction is distinct from a group-category
  equivalence. Ordinary localization uses the central positive operator and
  exactness/acyclicity contracts. Finite-index transfer uses trace, not division
  by a p-power index. Completed and finite-level objects retain their actual
  coefficient and transition maps.
- **CL.2:** the evaluation kernel, not an arbitrary quotient, carries the
  strict weight inequality that ordinary localization kills. Dual coefficients
  and derived linear duals have different shifts and actions. A two-term
  coefficient complex detects an incorrect unshifted duality formula.
- **CL.3:** functions are compact modulo P and Bruhat strata use p-adic
  topology; classical algebraic closure is a supplier input. Open-cell
  acyclicity, top cohomological shift and inverse determinant unit character
  are preserved. Abelian unipotent cohomology is the coefficient-valued dual
  exterior power with contragredient action, including p=2. Artin–Rees retains
  the chosen submodule and one shift uniform before choosing a quotient or
  coefficient depth; p-powers in the ℤ_p example are not confused with
  ramified O-uniformizer powers.
- **CL.4:** Q-ordinarity inverts all partial block operators. Strictly ordered
  slopes isolate the identity geometric-lemma term. Crystallinity uses the
  absence of maximal-compact invariants in a nonzero-monodromy Steinberg
  factor; semistability alone would be too weak.
- **CL.5:** the completed localized boundary retract uses the specified
  stratum, Hecke localization and positive-operator control. General selected
  weights give a cohomological bound; exact nonvanishing in every allowed
  degree is asserted only for zero selected weights, as required downstream.
- **CL.6:** deep level continues to contain U₀ and is not substituted by a
  principal congruence level. Both duality inversions and conjugated Satake
  maps have their prescribed orientations. The complementary degree bound
  allows zero rank and odd dimension. The incoming spectral-sequence
  differential has source `(q−r,d−q+r−1)`. Nilpotence exponent uniformity is
  distinguished from a congruence depth that may depend on coefficient depth.
  Hecke images come from a commutative Hecke algebra's actual action.
- **CL.7:** CTG twisting separates **residual** local constituents and
  reconstruction retains the local corner, generic fiber and continuity.
  Torsion Hecke targets are allowed; they are not assumed flat. Local
  semistable-ordinary quotients are tested on all finite E-algebras and the
  fixed-determinant tensor comparison requires p∤n. The local supplier's
  cyclotomic Hodge–Tate sign is converted to HT(ε_p⁻¹)=+1 by negating and
  reordering weights; determinant and extension orientations are retained.
  The characteristic-zero endpoint keeps residual genericity and uses
  auxiliary extensions disjoint from the residual field, with selected local
  fields unchanged.
- **CL.8:** the two complexes share the stated residual fiber, dimension and
  amplitude hypotheses. Generic support and propagation through a special
  fiber are different assertions; neither is an unsupported intersection of
  characteristic-zero components. Non-neat perfectness retains ζ_p∈F and the
  localized residual hypotheses.
- **CL.9:** all fifteen prepared hypotheses remain, including the strict
  complementary-degree inequality, two distinct auxiliary residue
  characteristics and the nontrivial-residue-field condition. Mixed local
  flags are oriented before taking partitions; the excluded flags (0,1) need
  a change of chosen place. Auxiliary pro-v Iwahori and χ⁻¹ are retained.
  Projective image uses the ambient GL₂ scalar quotient, not the center of
  the image. The invariant object has rank one, so the zero submodule fails.
  The finite-image criterion retains the cubic-tetrahedral exception. Solvable
  preparation preserves the full residual-plus-cyclotomic field, hence both
  projective image and cyclotomic degree. At p=3,5 the extra restriction is
  automatic because the cyclotomic degree divides p−1.
