## Delivered

The family construction uses the actual root Picard object over a test
scheme, coherent vanishing and arbitrary base change at
`d >= deg(R) + max(2g-1,1)`. It identifies the scheme open in the quadratic
evaluation fibre product. The removed ideal is generated by the kernel
coordinates and the squares of the root coordinates: its support, rather
than its possibly nonreduced closed subscheme, equals the zero section's
support. The chart retains the root coordinate; changes of evaluation
splitting are quadratic and respect weights two and one.

The effective descent argument uses a proper smooth tame DM scalar quotient
of each family chart. Its projective square-coordinate cover is finite
locally free of rank `2^a`; its geometric simple connectivity imports the
parent ramification proof. At finite coefficients, degree-zero proper base
change constructs the chart descent line. Smooth local sections prove
lissity. Full faithfulness on the actual Abel–Jacobi scheme charts gives
unique overlap comparisons, their cocycle and effective atlas gluing. This
replaces a requirement for a general nonproper-fibration descent theorem.
The normalized pair has unique comparison; a bare line still has scalar
automorphisms. Empty ramification uses ordinary weight-one scalar action
and the ordinary projective bundle throughout.

The map from the scalar quotient to the section stack is essential, but its
composite to the Picard stack is generally a scalar-line twist of the fixed
Picard object. Do not treat that quotient square as Cartesian or even
2-commutative over the Picard stack. The checking square is the one for the
actual family chart. No simple-connectivity assertion is made for the
punctured affine fibre.
