### CAII.1 Division orders, their lattices and finite modules

For a finite-dimensional central division algebra Δ_F over a nonarchimedean local field F, construct the unique maximal O-order Δ, its integer-normalized division valuation, a uniformizer Π, and radical J=ΠΔ=ΔΠ. Every nonzero one-sided fractional Δ-ideal is J^a, a∈ℤ. Every finitely generated torsion-free left or right Δ-module is free. Every finite-length right Δ-module is a finite direct sum of Δ/J^{a_i}, a_i≥1; the left version uses the opposite ring. If the module is killed by J^e, all a_i≤e. Include the zero module as the empty sum, rather than demanding a positive number of factors.

Proof route: use the valuation induced by the reduced norm, with its value-group index, and completeness to get Δ and its unique radical. An ideal contains an element of least valuation; divide by it on the appropriate side to prove the ideal classification. For a finite presentation, choose a matrix entry of least valuation, clear its row and column by invertible operations and continue on the smaller rectangle. This gives the torsion decomposition and the free torsion-free case. Right and left divisions are kept distinct, although J is bilateral. Nakayama gives the alternative lattice-basis proof.

API: `divisionOrder`, `radical_eq`, `fractionalIdeal_pow`, `lattice_basis`, `finiteModule_cyclic`, `cyclicHom_card`. For the last item Q=Δ/J^e and W a finite right Q-module, #Hom_Δ(W,Q)=#W: on Δ/J^a, the dual is J^{e-a}/J^e, of cardinality (#Δ/J)^a. This is a cardinality theorem, not an unsupported assertion of canonical self-duality.

Checks: Δ=O gives the ordinary DVR statements; Δ/J^0=0 and a=e give respectively zero and the whole dual; for the quaternion division algebra, Π need not be a central uniformizer and the exponent of p is v_Δ(p), rather than one. A module Δ/J⊕Δ/J² has cardinality (#Δ/J)^3 and needs two cyclic summands; replacing it by a single length-three cyclic module changes its annihilator.

Source inspected: Chia-Fu Yu, [On the existence of maximal orders](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.2897v1), §4.3, Lemma 4.4 pp.12–13. The cyclic-module proof above is the explicit additional diagonal-reduction argument needed by Nekovář §6.4 pp.39–40; neither Yu's ideal classification alone nor a citation to commutative PID decomposition supplies it.

### CAII.2 Matrix maximal orders and projective lattices

For n≥1, M_n(Δ) is maximal in M_n(Δ_F), and every maximal order there is conjugate to it. Every lattice over a maximal order is projective on its specified side. In particular a surjection of such lattices onto a lattice admits a module-linear section. Products of simple components obey the componentwise version.

Proof route: construct a full right Δ-lattice stable under an arbitrary order by applying its finite O-generators to a chosen lattice. Its endomorphism order contains the given order. A Δ-basis identifies the endomorphism order with M_n(Δ); matrix units and the division valuation prove maximality, hence equality when the starting order is maximal. For Λ=M_n(Δ), apply the full e₁₁ corner to a Λ-lattice, which is a free Δ-lattice by CAII.1, and reconstruct it by the imported Morita equivalence. This proves projectivity and the splitting assertion. A lattice need not be free as a Λ-module: the column lattice Δ^n is projective and is not Λ-free for n>1.

API: `matrixOrder_isMaximal`, `maximalOrder_conjugate_matrix`, `maximalOrder_lattice_projective`, `latticeSurjection_section`, with equality of the section composite and the identity. Checks: n=1 is CAII.1; the column lattice for n=2 detects confusion between projective and free; the order [[O,O],[πO,O]] is excluded from the maximal-order assertion even though it is hereditary. A surjection onto the zero lattice has its unique section.

Source inspected: Yu, Theorem 4.5 pp.13, and Lemma 5.2 p.14; its hypotheses are complete DVR/central simple algebra. The local-field setting here supplies these. Maximal-order existence is imported, not planned again.

### CAII.3 Double centralizers with multiplicity

Let B⊆End_F(V) be semisimple, V finite-dimensional and faithful, D=End_B(V)^op, and R_max a maximal O-order in D. Every full right R_max-lattice L⊂V has maximal left stabilizer Λ_max={b∈B:bL⊆L}, and End_{Λ_max}(L)^op=R_max. The centres Z(B) and Z(D) identify by their common action on V. This assertion includes nonsimple B and non-simple V over a simple B.

Proof route: write B=∏ M_{n_i}(D_i), V=⊕ M_{n_i,m_i}(D_i), m_i≥1, and D=∏ M_{m_i}(D_i). Use CAII.2 to conjugate R_max and the full matrix corner to identify each lattice with M_{n_i,m_i}(Δ_i). Its left stabilizer is M_{n_i}(Δ_i), and its right stabilizer M_{m_i}(Δ_i). Verify all left/right actions before passing through the imported Morita equivalence. This proves maximality and the centre identity. The invalid reduction “B simple implies End_B(V)^op division” is never used.

API: `centralizerLatticeOrder_isMaximal`, `centralizerLatticeOrder_commutant`, `centralizer_center_equiv`, `rectangularBimodule_normalForm`, and compatibility with reduction by a central scalar. Checks: B=F, V=F² gives D=M₂(F), not F; B=M₂(F), V=M_{2,3}(F) gives the two stabilizers M₂(O), M₃(O); B=F×F requires the two central idempotent components. A zero multiplicity is forbidden by faithfulness; V=0 is dealt with separately as the zero algebra/module convention, not a spurious division factor.

Source inspected: Nekovář, [The Euler system method for CM points on Shimura curves](https://web.archive.org/web/20240000000000id_/https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~jan.nekovar/pu/euler.pdf), 6.4.1 and Proposition 6.4.3 pp.38–40; Yu §4.1 p.11 and Theorem 4.5 p.13 give the order normal forms used to repair its multiplicity reduction. Curtis–Reiner is cited by Nekovář but was not read; it is neither a cleared source nor needed as an unchecked sole reference here.

### CAII.4 Central conductors and reduction error

For full orders R⊆R_max in D and Λ⊆Λ_max in B as above, there are common central non-zero-divisors c,d with cΛ_max⊆Λ and dR_max⊆R. They can be chosen as powers of the base-field uniformizer, hence work on every simple factor at once. For full lattices T⊆L with dL⊆T, prove

```text
(T ∩ aL)d ⊆ aT
```

for every central a∈O. Thus the kernel of T/aT→L/aL is killed by d, uniformly in a. In particular, if T is stabilized by R and L=T R_max, then dL⊆T follows from dR_max⊆R. Zero coordinates in a product of centres are excluded: “nonzero” alone does not mean a non-zero-divisor there.

Proof route: finite O-index of the two overorders bounds denominators; multiply by one power of the base uniformizer. If t=aℓ lies in T∩aL, then td=a(ℓd)∈aT. API: `centralConductor_exists`, `overLattice_mul_conductor`, `reductionKernel_annihilator`. Checks: identical orders allow c=d=1; T=πO⊂L=O has reduction kernel killed by π for every a=π^N; c=(1,0) in O×O is rejected. Sources: Nekovář Proposition 6.4.3 p.39, with the product-centre non-zero-divisor condition made explicit.

### CAII.5 Morita comparison for finite separated pairings

This is the transport interface, not the Euler-system evaluation theorem: for the rectangular integral bimodule M_{n,m}(Δ)/a, a finite pairing X×W→M_{n,m}(Δ)/a, left M_n(Δ)-linear and right M_m(Δ)-linear, transports under the two full matrix corners to a pairing eX×We′→Δ/aΔ. Both separatedness maps correspond to the corner maps. Their surjectivity is equivalent before and after Morita transport. Preserve finite modules, both multiplicities, and arbitrary nonzero central a.

Proof route: identify the evaluation maps through the unit/counit of the existing full-idempotent equivalence; the two corners are on different sides. CAII.1's cyclic dual cardinality shows that two separated finite corner maps have equal source/target cardinality and are bijective. Reconstruct with matrix units. This provides a shorter maximal-order case for EulerSystemsAndKolyvaginSystems 4.8 than a lattice induction and does not require V to be a simple B-module.

API: `pairingCorner`, `pairingCorner_eval_square`, `pairingCorner_separated_iff`, `pairingCorner_surjective_iff`. Checks: n=m=1 is the division-order pairing; n=2,m=3 retains both multiplicities; the infinite coordinate pairing has injective maps but direct-sum image in a product, so finiteness cannot be dropped. Source: Nekovář Proposition 6.4.3 pp.39–40, with the explicit two-corner repair above, and the imported Tau Ceti corner equivalence.

The consumer 4.8, rather than this Part II, builds the tensor extension and its separated pairing images. It proves d₀² kills the tensor kernel and combines this with CAII.4 to obtain c₀d₀³·Coker(j)=0. Thus it gives the required uniform bound with c=c₀, d=d₀³. The sharper source claim c₀d₀ for every conductor pair remains unverified, as explained in 4.8. The all-prime square-index **length** theorem independent of the imaginary quadratic field is a separate Heegner-point application target; this order-theoretic brief supplies a uniform annihilator only and does not purport to solve that length theorem.
