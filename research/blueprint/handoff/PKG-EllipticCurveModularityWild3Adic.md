## Mathematical points for the reviewer

- **6.5 is implied by Kisin's theorem.** BCDT's lifting theorems have hypotheses containing those of GL2ModularityLifting R22.5 *Kisin's potentially Barsotti–Tate theorem over ℚ*, so 6.5's proof sketch deduces it from there and records BCDT's own Taylor–Wiles proof (which uses admittance and weak acceptability). Consequently the end theorem does not logically need Layers W3.3–W3.5 once R22.5 exists; the roadmap still owns BCDT's explicit local computations. CDT 7.2.1 and 7.2.2 are likewise deduced from Kisin's theorem and ordinary lifting (OrdinaryAutomorphicFormsAndModularityLifting R21.4).
- **6.12 (CDT 7.2.4).** When `ρ̄_{E,5}|_{G_{ℚ(√5)}}` is not absolutely irreducible, the mod-5 image lies in a Borel subgroup (order 80) or has order 16, in either case prime to 3, so the conductor of `E` is prime to 27 and 6.9/6.11 apply; no Hilbert-irreducibility switch is needed in this branch.
- **6.11 exceptional classes.** CDT says the conductor-50 and conductor-338 classes are "known to be modular" from tables. The package plans this as an explicit nonconstant `X₀(N) → E` certificate (bound as in ComputationalNumberTheory CN.5) plus EllipticCurveModularity R29.6's converse; ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations has no Faltings–Serre test-set target, contrary to a remark in the EllipticCurveModularityImaginaryQuadratic package. The specific curve equations are not fixed in the README.
- **Checked here:** all explicit field formulas of 1.3–1.7 (roots, conjugates, `γ₂γ₃ = γ₃²γ₂`, the positive-quadratic `γ₃(α)`, `π¹²`, the norm and valuations of `(α − 1)/β`) by an exact-arithmetic script; `c_π = −φ(G_π)` and the admittance rules of BCDT §1.3 on page images (the text layer drops minus signs); Manoharmayum's lists for the five test curves (pp. 746–753); the point count of `Y² = X³ − X − 1`; the discriminants of the six test curves (also as Lean `example`s).
