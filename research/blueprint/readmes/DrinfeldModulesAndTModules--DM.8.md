# DM.8 — Fixed vectors and the Betti comparison

This is a partial blueprint for **Periods, logarithms and algebraic independence**.
It closes the dependency graph of nine declarations in the linear-algebra argument
underlying Papanikolas's Betti realization. The source is
[Matthew A. Papanikolas, arXiv math/0506078v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0506078v2),
29 June 2007. All 39 pages, including the proofs of the period and Carlitz-logarithm
theorems, were read. The actual analytic fields, motive category, difference-Galois
group and specialization theorem still need their declaration-sized decomposition.
The end-to-end DM.8 target is consequently **not closed**.

The component deliberately uses objects already in the pinned libraries. A fixed
space is `LinearMap.fixedSubmodule`; its inclusion is `Submodule.subtype`; its
comparison map is the existing scalar-extension map obtained from
`LinearMap.liftBaseChangeEquiv`. Bases and their scalar extensions are also native.
There are no new definition or construction nodes in this component, so there are
no new definition APIs or definition unit tests. The nine new proof declarations
carry acceptance conditions, and the suggested file includes twelve concrete
acceptance examples. Their proofs are placeholders, not implementations.

## Fields, operators and the actual intended application

For the closed component, let F and L be fields and fix an embedding F→L. Let V be
an L-vector space with the induced F-vector-space structure. Let σ be an
F-algebra automorphism of L. Let f be an F-linear endomorphism of V satisfying

\[
f(av)=\sigma(a)f(v)\qquad(a\in L,\ v\in V).
\]

The crucial constant-field hypothesis is **equality**: every σ-fixed scalar of L
comes from F. It is stronger than the assertion that σ fixes the image of F.
Neither F nor L is required to have characteristic zero or to be perfect. The
extension L/F can be infinite and σ can have infinite order. The independence
and comparison-injectivity declarations do not require V to be finite-dimensional,
and they do not require f to be invertible. Finite-dimensionality over L is added
only for the dimension statements. In particular, finite-dimensionality over F of
the whole ambient V is never inserted.

Write W for the native F-submodule of f-fixed vectors. Its scalar-extension
comparison is

\[
\kappa:L\otimes_F W\longrightarrow V,\qquad a\otimes w\longmapsto aw.
\]

The formula on pure tensors, uniqueness as an L-linear extension, and the equality
of its range with the L-span of W already belong to Mathlib. This blueprint proves
that κ is injective using the semilinear operator and exact constants. When V is
finite-dimensional over L it proves that W is finite-dimensional over F, establishes
the dimension bound, and characterizes bijectivity by equality of dimensions.

In the Papanikolas application the fields are different from the generic names
above. Fix q=p^m with p prime and m≥1, k=F_q(θ), and an algebraic closure bar(k)
inside C_infinity, the completion of an algebraic closure of F_q((1/θ)). Normalize
|θ|_infinity=q. The analytic variable t is independent of θ. Let T be the
one-variable restricted series algebra C_infinity{t}, and let L=Frac(T).
Coefficient twisting is **inverse Frobenius**: σ fixes t and sends a coefficient
c to its unique q-th root. The fixed field required for the application is
F=F_q(t). The fact L^σ=F_q(t) is Papanikolas Lemma 3.3.2, an analytic divisor
argument; it is not supplied by the generic theorem proved here.

For a pre-t-motive P over bar(k)(t), the intended ambient vector space is
P†=L⊗_{bar(k)(t)}P with the diagonal σ-semilinear operator, and the Betti space
is P^B=(P†)^σ. Rigid analytic triviality means that the actual comparison
L⊗_{F_q(t)}P^B→P† is an isomorphism. The new dimension criterion applies only
after DM.4 supplies the real motive and scalar-extension interfaces and DM.8
proves the analytic fixed-field statement. It does not create these objects by
assuming their desired conclusions.

The campaign's DM.4 uses contravariant τ-motives. Papanikolas uses dual Anderson
motives with inverse Frobenius σ. The request to DM.4 requires the conversion,
including its effect on morphisms and the Carlitz example. The category R of all
rigid analytically trivial pre-t-motives and the category T generated by the
rigid-trivial Anderson image (Definition 3.4.10) must also remain distinct.

### Coefficients of a fixed linear combination

`DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/fixed-coefficients` — `TauCeti.Difference.FixedVectors.fixed_coefficients`

For a finite index type I, let v_i∈V be fixed by f and L-linearly independent. If c_i∈L and the vector Σ_i c_i·v_i is fixed by f, then σ(c_i)=c_i for every i. Equality of the constant field with F is not needed for this lemma.

The proof is divided as follows:

1. Apply f to the finite sum and use additivity, semilinearity and fixedness of each v_i to get Σ_i σ(c_i)·v_i=Σ_i c_i·v_i.
2. Subtract the two sums. Apply the finite-relation criterion for L-linear independence to the coefficients σ(c_i)−c_i.

Dependencies: `mathlib:linearIndependent_iff'`.

Acceptance:

- With the singleton vector 1 in C and conjugation, a fixed coefficient is real.
- The empty family gives a vacuous conclusion.
- Independence is necessary: with two copies of 1, the zero combination i·1+(−i)·1 is fixed although neither coefficient is fixed.

Source: Papanikolas, Lemma 3.3.7, pp.12–13.

### Descent of the span of an independent fixed family

`DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/fixed-span-descent` — `TauCeti.Difference.FixedVectors.fixed_mem_span_iff`

Assume exact constants. For a finite L-linearly independent family v_i fixed by f and a fixed vector w, w lies in the L-span of the v_i if and only if it lies in their F-span.

The proof is divided as follows:

1. For the forward implication use Submodule.mem_span_range_iff_exists_fun to choose L-coefficients of w. The fixed-coefficients lemma makes each coefficient σ-fixed.
2. Use exact constants to choose coefficients in F. Scalar-tower compatibility turns the same finite sum into an F-linear expression for w.
3. For the reverse implication, map the F-coefficients into L and use scalar-tower compatibility.

Dependencies: `DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/fixed-coefficients`, `mathlib:Submodule.mem_span_range_iff_exists_fun`.

Acceptance:

- Conjugation on C with the singleton family 1 gives precisely the real axis.
- With the empty family, a fixed w belongs to either span exactly when w=0.
- For σ=id on C with F=R and v=1, w=i is fixed and in the C-span but outside the R-span, demonstrating the exact-constants requirement.

Source: Papanikolas, Lemma 3.3.7, pp.12–13.

### Independence descent for finite fixed families

`DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/finite-fixed-independence` — `TauCeti.Difference.FixedVectors.linearIndependent_fin_of_fixed`

Assume exact constants. If v:Fin(n)→V is F-linearly independent and each v_i is fixed by f, then v is L-linearly independent, for every n≥0.

The proof is divided as follows:

1. Induct on n. The empty family has no nonzero relation.
2. Use linearIndependent_finSucc over F to separate the tail and its first vector. By induction the fixed tail is L-linearly independent.
3. If the first vector were in the L-span of the tail, fixed-span descent would put it in the F-span, contradicting the F-independence criterion. Apply linearIndependent_finSucc over L.

Dependencies: `DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/fixed-span-descent`, `mathlib:linearIndependent_finSucc`.

Acceptance:

- n=0 and n=1 are included; in the singleton case independence means the vector is nonzero.
- The pair (1,1) over Q is rejected at the independence premise.
- The R-independent pair (1,i) is not a fixed family for conjugation, and must not pass as a C-independent family.

Source: Papanikolas, Lemma 3.3.7, pp.12–13.

### Linear independence of fixed vectors

`DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/fixed-independence` — `TauCeti.Difference.FixedVectors.linearIndependent_of_fixed`

Assume exact constants. For any index type I, an F-linearly independent family v:I→V whose members satisfy f(v_i)=v_i is L-linearly independent.

The proof is divided as follows:

1. Use linearIndependent_iff_finset_linearIndependent to reduce the conclusion to an arbitrary finite subfamily. Restrict F-independence and fixedness to it.
2. Reindex that finite set by Fin(card) and apply finite-fixed-independence. Transport independence back along the bijection.

Dependencies: `DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/finite-fixed-independence`, `mathlib:linearIndependent_iff_finset_linearIndependent`.

Acceptance:

- The theorem has no finite-dimensional hypothesis on V and no finite-order hypothesis on σ.
- Exact constants and the fixed-vector premise are independently necessary: σ=id on C fails the former for F=R; conjugation fixes the latter field but not i.
- The theorem supplies independence of an arbitrary F-basis of the fixed submodule before its finiteness is known.

Source: Papanikolas, Lemma 3.3.7, pp.12–13.

### Injectivity of the Betti comparison map

`DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/betti-comparison-injective` — `TauCeti.Difference.FixedVectors.bettiComparison_injective`

Assume exact constants. Set W=f.fixedSubmodule, using the native F-submodule. The native L-linear map κ=(W.subtype).liftBaseChange L:L⊗_F W→V, given by κ(a⊗w)=a·w, is injective. No finite-dimensional hypothesis is required.

The proof is divided as follows:

1. Choose an F-basis b of W using Module.Free.chooseBasis. Its image in V is F-linearly independent by the native injective inclusion and LinearIndependent.map'. Every image vector is fixed.
2. Apply fixed-independence to conclude that the image family is L-linearly independent.
3. Use the native scalar-extended basis b.baseChange L of L⊗_F W. Its i-th vector is 1⊗b_i, whose κ-image is the same image vector.
4. Identify κ with the linear map prescribed on that basis and apply Module.Basis.injective_constr_of_linearIndependent.

Dependencies: `DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/fixed-independence`, `mathlib:LinearMap.fixedSubmodule`, `mathlib:LinearMap.mem_fixedSubmodule_iff`, `mathlib:Submodule.subtype`, `mathlib:Submodule.subtype_injective`, `mathlib:LinearIndependent.map'`, `mathlib:Module.Free.chooseBasis`, `mathlib:Module.Basis.linearIndependent`, `mathlib:Module.Basis.baseChange`, `mathlib:Module.Basis.baseChange_apply`, `mathlib:LinearMap.liftBaseChangeEquiv`, `mathlib:LinearMap.liftBaseChange_tmul`, `mathlib:Module.Basis.injective_constr_of_linearIndependent`.

Acceptance:

- For conjugation on C the map C⊗_R R→C is bijective.
- For f=2·id on Q the fixed space is zero and κ is injective but not surjective.
- For the zero vector space κ is the map between zero spaces.

Source: Papanikolas, Lemma 3.3.7 and Proposition 3.3.8, pp.12–13.

### Finite dimensionality of the fixed submodule

`DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/fixed-space-finite` — `TauCeti.Difference.FixedVectors.finite_fixedSubmodule`

Assume exact constants and that V is finite-dimensional over L. Then the native fixed submodule W=f.fixedSubmodule is finite-dimensional over F; V itself is not assumed finite-dimensional over F.

The proof is divided as follows:

1. Choose an F-basis b of W; include it into V as in betti-comparison-injective.
2. Use fixed-independence to make it an L-independent family in the finite-dimensional L-space V. LinearIndependent.finite makes its index type finite.
3. Apply Module.Basis.finiteDimensional_of_finite to the original F-basis of W. This establishes finiteness before any finrank equality criterion is used.

Dependencies: `DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/fixed-independence`, `mathlib:LinearMap.fixedSubmodule`, `mathlib:LinearMap.mem_fixedSubmodule_iff`, `mathlib:Submodule.subtype`, `mathlib:Submodule.subtype_injective`, `mathlib:LinearIndependent.map'`, `mathlib:Module.Free.chooseBasis`, `mathlib:Module.Basis.linearIndependent`, `mathlib:LinearIndependent.finite`, `mathlib:Module.Basis.finiteDimensional_of_finite`.

Acceptance:

- Conjugation on C has fixed space of real dimension 1.
- Multiplication by 2 on Q has zero-dimensional fixed space.
- Finite-dimensionality is proved, not inferred from the numerical value of finrank, which is zero for infinite-dimensional spaces.

Source: Papanikolas, Proposition 3.3.8, pp.12–13.

### The dimension bound for fixed vectors

`DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/fixed-dimension-bound` — `TauCeti.Difference.FixedVectors.finrank_fixedSubmodule_le`

Assume exact constants and finite-dimensionality of V over L. Then dim_F(f.fixedSubmodule)≤dim_L(V).

The proof is divided as follows:

1. Use betti-comparison-injective and the native dimension bound for an injective map into V.
2. Rewrite the dimension of L⊗_F W using Module.finrank_baseChange. The fixed-space-finite lemma separately certifies that this is the dimension of a finite space.

Dependencies: `DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/betti-comparison-injective`, `DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/fixed-space-finite`, `mathlib:LinearMap.finrank_le_finrank_of_injective`, `mathlib:Module.finrank_baseChange`.

Acceptance:

- Conjugation on C realizes equality 1≤1.
- The invertible operator 2·id on Q realizes the strict inequality 0<1.
- For the zero space both sides are zero.

Source: Papanikolas, Proposition 3.3.8, pp.12–13.

### The dimension criterion for the Betti comparison

`DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/betti-comparison-dimension` — `TauCeti.Difference.FixedVectors.bettiComparison_bijective_iff`

Assume exact constants and finite-dimensionality of V over L. The native comparison κ:L⊗_F f.fixedSubmodule→V is bijective if and only if dim_F(f.fixedSubmodule)=dim_L(V). In the pre-t-motive application this is the numerical criterion for rigid analytic triviality, once the actual scalar field and operator are supplied.

The proof is divided as follows:

1. Establish finite-dimensionality of W by fixed-space-finite; a finite F-basis extends to a finite L-basis of L⊗_F W.
2. The forward implication packages κ as a linear equivalence and uses finrank invariance and Module.finrank_baseChange.
3. For the reverse implication, base-change dimension makes source and target have equal finite dimension. Use betti-comparison-injective and LinearMap.injective_iff_surjective_of_finrank_eq_finrank to prove surjectivity.

Dependencies: `DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/betti-comparison-injective`, `DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/fixed-space-finite`, `mathlib:Module.Basis.baseChange`, `mathlib:Module.Basis.finiteDimensional_of_finite`, `mathlib:Module.finrank_baseChange`, `mathlib:LinearEquiv.finrank_eq`, `mathlib:LinearMap.injective_iff_surjective_of_finrank_eq_finrank`.

Acceptance:

- Identity on Q and conjugation on C have bijective comparison maps.
- The zero-dimensional object is admitted.
- The invertible operator 2·id on Q has no nonzero fixed vector and fails surjectivity; invertibility alone does not imply triviality.
- The actual DM.8 applicability check still needs L^σ=F_q(t) and the supplied analytic field; this generic criterion does not establish either.

Source: Papanikolas, Proposition 3.3.8, pp.12–13.

### Constant coordinates in a fixed basis

`DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/fixed-basis-coordinates` — `TauCeti.Difference.FixedVectors.fixed_iff_existsUnique_coordinates`

Assume exact constants. Let b:I→V be an L-basis indexed by a finite type, with f(b_i)=b_i. For w∈V, f(w)=w if and only if there is a unique family c:I→F with Σ_i algebraMap(c_i)·b_i=w.

The proof is divided as follows:

1. A basis spans V, so express w with L-coefficients. If w is fixed, fixed-coefficients and exact constants lift each coefficient uniquely to F, the latter uniqueness using injectivity of a field embedding.
2. L-independence of b makes the lifted coefficient family unique by subtracting two expansions.
3. Conversely, an F-coefficient combination of fixed vectors is fixed by F-linearity.

Dependencies: `DrinfeldModulesAndTModules:DM.8/fixed-coefficients`, `mathlib:Module.Basis.linearIndependent`, `mathlib:Module.Basis.span_eq`, `mathlib:Submodule.mem_span_range_iff_exists_fun`, `mathlib:linearIndependent_iff'`.

Acceptance:

- The singleton basis 1 of C with conjugation has exactly real fixed coordinates.
- For the empty basis and zero space the unique coefficient function is empty.
- This is the coordinate step in the fundamental-solution argument; no analytic matrix or general solution space is newly defined here.

Source: Papanikolas, Lemma 4.1.6, p.22, with Lemma 3.3.7.

## Concrete acceptance examples

The suggested file contains these twelve examples, each using native types and
operations. They test theorem hypotheses and the proposed comparison statements;
there are no replacement definitions hidden in the examples.

| Example | Required outcome |
| --- | --- |
| `complex_conjugation_constants` | The fixed scalars of complex conjugation are exactly the image of R. |
| `complex_conjugation_fixed_space` | Its native fixed submodule has underlying set {z : Im(z)=0}. |
| `complex_conjugation_dimension` | The real dimension of that submodule is 1. |
| `complex_conjugation_comparison` | The actual C-linear map C⊗_R W→C is bijective. |
| `identity_comparison` | The analogous comparison for id on Q is bijective. |
| `zero_dimensional_comparison` | The zero object Q^(Fin 0) has bijective comparison. |
| `invertible_operator_without_fixed_vectors` | Multiplication by 2 on Q has fixed submodule zero. |
| `invertibility_does_not_imply_triviality` | The preceding comparison is not surjective, although the operator is invertible. |
| `smaller_constant_field_fails` | The real basis (1,i) of C is not C-linearly independent. |
| `exact_constants_required` | For σ=id on C the fixed field is not the image of R. |
| `fixed_vector_hypothesis_required` | Complex conjugation does not fix i. |
| `dependent_pair_is_rejected` | The pair (1,1) over Q is linearly dependent. |

The two uses of (1,i) diagnose different hypotheses. With σ=id and F=R the
vectors are fixed but the claimed constant field is too small. With complex
conjugation, F=R is the correct constant field but the second vector is not fixed.
Thus neither failure can be hidden by a numerical dimension assertion.

The multiplication-by-2 example establishes the distinction between an invertible
semilinear operator and a trivialized one in the generic linear-algebra component.
It does not purport to construct a nontrivial analytic pre-t-motive over the
Papanikolas fields; that stage-level example is an explicit remaining obligation.
Likewise the dependent vector pair is a prototype acceptance check, not a proof
of any Carlitz logarithm theorem.

## Period and logarithm targets still to close

Papanikolas Theorem 5.2.2 is the selected period theorem. Its input is a t-motive
in the specified Tannakian category, a representing matrix
Φ∈GL_r(bar(k)(t))∩Mat_r(bar(k)[t]) with det(Φ)=c(t−θ)^s, c≠0 and s≥0,
and Ψ∈GL_r(T)∩Mat_r(E) satisfying Ψ^(−1)=ΦΨ. Here E consists of entire
series whose coefficients lie in a finite extension of k_infinity. The theorem
identifies the transcendence degree over bar(k) of the field generated by the
entries of Ψ(θ) with the dimension of Γ_M over F_q(t). The determinant,
analyticity, specialization point and choice of constant field are substantive
hypotheses. An arbitrary invertible semilinear object is not an eligible input.

Theorem 6.4.2 assumes λ_i∈C_infinity with exp_C(λ_i)∈bar(k) and linear
independence of the λ_i over k=F_q(θ). Its conclusion is algebraic independence
over bar(k). These are different base fields, and the logarithms need not begin
in the principal convergence disk. The proof uses the auxiliary series L_α,
the explicit extension motive X(α_i), the calculation of its group, the
period theorem and a division-point reduction. The period case exp_C(λ)=0
must be treated explicitly when invoking the reduction lemma, whose stated
hypothesis excludes it. No arbitrary higher-rank special-value statement is
inferred from this theorem.
