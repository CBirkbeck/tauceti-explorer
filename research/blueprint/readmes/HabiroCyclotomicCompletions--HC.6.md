The comparison with the arithmetic Habiro ring attached to the number field **Q** is supplied by `HabiroNumberFields:HB.6/ring-operations-and-the-classical-comparison`. ## Objects and exported API

Let R be a commutative ring and S a set of positive integers. Let Φ*ₛ be the multiplicative monoid generated by the cyclotomic polynomials Φₙ(q), n ∈ S, including their powers and the empty product 1. Define

\[
R[q]^S=\varprojlim_{f\in\Phi^*_S}R[q]/(f),
\qquad
P_N(q)=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(1-q^i),\quad P_0=1.
\]

The convention S = ∅ gives the zero ring. For S the set of all positive integers, the ideals (Pₙ) are cofinal, so this is the classical factorial completion. The topology is the inverse limit of the discrete quotient topologies. It is a complete separated topological ring with dense polynomial image; its universal property is for continuous ring maps to complete separated topological rings. It is generally not completion with respect to powers of one fixed ideal.

For an R-module M, the ordinary module is

\[
M[q]^S=\varprojlim_{f\in\Phi^*_S}M[q]/fM[q].
\]

Monic division makes each finite quotient a finite direct sum of copies of M; a cofinal chain gives a product description. Consequently M ↦ M[q]ˢ is exact. This assertion concerns polynomial coefficient modules. Completion of arbitrary R[q]-modules with torsion uses `HabiroRings:HR.2`. The comparison with M ⊗ᵣ R[q]ˢ is an isomorphism for finitely presented M; no unrestricted tensor/inverse-limit interchange is part of this interface.

## Finite precision and factorial arithmetic

Use the compatible representatives

\[
F_N=\sum_{0\le n<N}P_n,\qquad
U_N=\sum_{0\le n<N}q^nP_n.
\]

For M ≥ N, Pₙ divides Fₘ − Fₙ. Thus F is a well-defined element of the integral Habiro ring with every normalized digit equal to 1. At N = 0 the quotient by P₀ = 1 is the zero ring, with representative F₀ = 0. At N = 1 the representative is F₁ = 1. These cases distinguish empty precision from evaluation at q = 1.

The telescoping identity qUₙ = 1 − Pₙ holds as a polynomial identity for every N, including N = 0. It supplies the finite inverse of q and hence the imported identity q⁻¹ = Σₙ qⁿPₙ. Multiplication gives

\[
Fq^{-1}=\sum_{n\ge0}(n+1)q^nP_n,
\]

and the first four normalized digits of F² are checked by

\[
F_4^2\equiv
1+(3-q)P_1+(6-3q-q^2)P_2
+(7-4q-4q^2+2q^3)P_3\pmod{P_4}.
\]

The coefficient of Pₙ must have degree at most n. A multiplication procedure that keeps oversized digits fails this test. Addition and negation need no carries, while multiplication carries towards higher indices.

Mathlib's `Polynomial.modByMonic` computes the intended remainder only with a monic divisor. Since Pₙ has leading coefficient (−1)ᴺ, use its monic associate (−1)ᴺPₙ. The suggested multiplication test uses P₄, already monic, and explicitly checks that P₃ has leading coefficient −1. The parent HC.2 node also supplies the executable coefficient-list algorithm and its agreement with Mathlib from Tau Ceti's polynomial API; this part imports that declaration rather than restating it.

## Values and Taylor coefficients

Let ζ be a primitive d-th root in a characteristic-zero field, d > 0. In Pₙ(ζ + x), exactly ⌊n/d⌋ factors have zero constant coefficient, and the corresponding zeros are simple. Hence Pₙ(ζ + x) is divisible by \(x^{\lfloor n/d\rfloor}\). Values stabilize for N ≥ d, and coefficients through degree k stabilize for N ≥ d(k + 1). Divisibility by that power still holds over any commutative ring when ζᵈ = 1; the exact multiplicity assertion uses the characteristic-zero field and primitivity.

The integral cyclotomic quotient `AdjoinRoot (Polynomial.cyclotomic d ℤ)` provides the small root calculations without choosing complex embeddings:

| Order d | Value of F at ζ_d, using F_d |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 3 |
| 3 | 5 − ζ₃ |
| 4 | 8 − 3ζ₄ |
| 5 | 9 − 5ζ₅ − 3ζ₅² |
| 6 | 17 − 13ζ₆ |

At q = 1, with x = q − 1, the Taylor series begins

\[
1-x+2x^2-5x^3+15x^4-53x^5+217x^6
-1014x^7+5335x^8-31240x^9.
\]

The displayed coefficients are obtained from F₁₀. At q = −1, with y = q + 1, it begins 3 + 11y + 72y² + 635y³ + 7085y⁴; F₁₀ suffices through degree 4. At a primitive cube root ω, the first three coefficients are

\[
(5-\omega)+(49+40\omega)(q-\omega)
+(128+693\omega)(q-\omega)^2.
\]

In GSWZ's multiplicative coordinate q = ζₘ(1 − u), the additive variable x = q − ζₘ is −ζₘu. Coefficient comparisons must include this change of variable. Mathlib's `Polynomial.taylor` uses additive coordinates; `Polynomial.taylor_coeff` identifies its coefficients with Hasse derivatives, so no division by k! is used over **Z**.

F and the constant 1 have the same value at q = 1 but different first Taylor coefficients, −1 and 0. The full integral Taylor map at 1 is injective; its constant coefficient alone is not. Root automorphisms carry 5 − ω to 5 − ω², and coefficient change commutes with every displayed finite calculation.

The two full integral Taylor maps at 1 and −1 are injective by Habiro's Theorem 5.2, using its separation hypotheses. They are not surjective. For the map at 1, the formal inverse of 3 − 2q is Σₖ 2ᵏxᵏ. If this were a global element, its 2-adic re-expansion at q = −1 would have constant coefficient 1/5. A global integral element has an integer value at −1, a contradiction. This is the obstruction in Habiro's Proposition 7.4(1), already owned by HC.4.

The p-adic translation used here is a convergent operation over a complete separated coefficient ring. A nonzero p-adically small shift in a domain is not nilpotent. The construction therefore uses the HC.3 continuous evaluation API, rather than applying the formal power-series substitution API with a constant it does not permit.

Evaluation on all roots determines an integral Habiro element. For smaller infinite order sets, use the precise adjacency hypothesis of `HC.4/evaluation-uniqueness-and-its-exact-hypothesis`. Injectivity on every arbitrary infinite set of roots is Habiro's Conjecture 6.1, and is not exported as a theorem.

## Units and non-examples

The polynomial 1 − q is P₁, with digits (0, 1, 0, …), and has value 1 − ζ at a root ζ. It is not a unit in the full integral Habiro ring, since its value at 1 is zero. It is also not a unit in **Z**[q]^{\{2\}}, where its value at −1 is 2. It is a unit in **Z**[q]^{\{6\}}: (1 − q)q = 1 − Φ₆(q), giving inverse qΣₖ Φ₆(q)ᵏ. More generally, the integral unit criterion is that S contain neither 1 nor a prime-power order; the requisite cyclotomic value is Φₙ(1) = 1 for the remaining orders.

The partial sums \(\sum_{0\le n<N}q^n\) have value N at q = 1. Since this evaluation lands in the discrete ring **Z**, these sums are not Cauchy in the Habiro topology. An algorithm must not treat this geometric series as a factorial series or assign a global inverse to 1 − q.

## Inverting 2, rational coefficients and reduction modulo 2

Over R = **Z**[1/2], the order sets Sₐ = {n : v₂(n) = a}, a ≥ 0, are the Chinese remainder components. Let e₀ be 1 on the odd-order component and 0 on all the others. Its first representatives are

\[
e_0\equiv\frac{3+2q-q^2}{4}\pmod{P_2},\qquad
e_0\equiv\frac{7+2q-q^2+q^3-2q^4+q^5}{8}\pmod{P_3}.
\]

The second reduces to the first. Modulo P₃ it equals 1 on the (q − 1)³Φ₃ component and 0 on the Φ₂ component; its square equals itself. The element 1 − e₀ is nonzero, since its value at −1 is 1, and its whole Taylor series at 1 vanishes. Thus the single Taylor map loses injectivity when 2 is inverted, while the all-roots evaluation remains injective. In the order-2 factor, 1 − q becomes a unit with inverse Σₖ Φ₂(q)ᵏ/2ᵏ⁺¹.

The suggested file states these finite identities for any commutative algebra S satisfying `IsLocalization.Away (2 : ℤ) S`, using the existing inverse of 2. It does not assume a field or write polynomial division by 8. This tests the localized coefficients themselves.

With coefficient ring **Q**, the full classical completion is the product of the single-order completions. Let e₁ be 1 on the order-1 factor and zero elsewhere. Modulo P₃,

\[
e_1\equiv\frac{47+42q+7q^2-23q^3-18q^4+17q^5}{72},
\]

and t = (q − 1)e₁ has representative

\[
t\equiv\frac{-5-2q+3q^2+5q^3+2q^4-3q^5}{12}.
\]

Its Taylor series on the order-1 factor is x, so t is nonzero. Its value at every root is zero: at 1 the factor q − 1 vanishes, and every other component is zero. At precision P₃ the representative has Taylor coefficients (0, 1, 0) at 1, vanishes modulo Φ₂Φ₃, and is not divisible by P₃. These are finite consequences of the imported product description; a finite representative by itself does not establish an infinite component decomposition.

Reduction modulo 2 uses the HC.5 coefficient-module exactness to identify the full integral Habiro ring modulo 2 with the completion over **F**₂. At precision 3,

\[
P_3=(q+1)^4\Phi_3(q),\qquad e=q^5+q+1,
\]

and e² − e is divisible by P₃, e − 1 by (q + 1)⁴, and e by Φ₃. The corresponding component idempotent in the full completion separates the orders 2ᵏ from 3·2ᵏ, and gives zero divisors. Thus the ideal generated by 2 in the integral Habiro ring is not prime. The finite test checks the first representative; the global idempotent comes from the HC.5 decomposition, not from this polynomial being globally idempotent.
