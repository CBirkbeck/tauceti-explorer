This document completes the target-level planning pass for
`HabiroNahmSeries:HB.3`. It continues the accepted
[parent blueprint](../packets/HabiroNahmSeries.json) and its
[reader](HabiroNahmSeries.md). The parent supplies the analytic and formal Nahm
data, the equations, the distinguished solution, the discriminant, the
coordinate-field and Bloch-class targets, and the regulator and torsion
criteria. Those declarations retain their identifiers. This part supplies the
missing algebraicity argument, makes the denominator-clearing equations and
their Jacobian precise, and fixes the field and normalization interfaces needed
to apply the parent constructions.

The pass is complete and HB.3 is **planned**. Closure requires four specified
supplier interfaces: the real Rogers dilogarithm, Borel regulator injectivity,
unique divisibility over algebraically closed fields, and the exact integral
convention of the signed formal class. The statements and dependencies of those
interfaces appear below. Every declaration is a plan; none is claimed
implemented.

The two outputs of this layer have different uses. For root-of-unity
asymptotics in HB.4, the distinguished positive real solution fixes the real
branches, its algebraic coordinate field supplies embeddings, and its Rogers
value controls the leading exponential. For the formal Habiro constructions in
HB.8 and HB.9, a symmetric integral matrix supplies signed equations even when
it is not positive definite. Their algebraic nondegeneracy and their integral
Bloch or K-theory convention must be specified independently of analytic
convergence.

## Conventions and ownership

Let the index set be `Fin n`; an empty system is permitted in the auxiliary
algebraic interfaces. Analytic Nahm data have a symmetric positive-definite
matrix (A\in\operatorname{Mat}_n(\mathbb Q)). The positive real equations are

\[
  1-x_i=\prod_j x_j^{A_{ij}},\qquad 0<x_i<1,
\]

where rational powers use the positive real branch. The parameters (B,C) of
the Nahm sum do not affect these equations. Formal Nahm data have a symmetric
integral matrix (M) and use the diagonal signs of GSWZ:

\[
  1-z_i=(-1)^{M_{ii}}\prod_j z_j^{M_{ij}}.
\]

There is no positive-definiteness hypothesis on a formal datum. Symmetry allows
the row notation above to agree with the column notation in GSWZ (41).

Choose an integer (d>0) such that (M=dA) is integral. For the positive
solution put (y_i=x_i^{1/d}). Two fields occur:

\[
  F=\mathbb Q(x_1,\ldots,x_n),\qquad
  E=\mathbb Q(y_1,\ldots,y_n),\qquad F\subseteq E.
\]

Both are represented by Mathlib's existing `IntermediateField.adjoin`.
The root field (E) includes the choices needed to interpret rational powers
coherently. Equality (E=F) is not assumed. In examples they can coincide,
but none of the general conclusions may depend on that coincidence. A field
embedding preserves polynomial and integer-power equations; its conjugate
coordinates need not satisfy a newly chosen positive or principal branch of a
rational power.

The boundary used for the CGZ convention takes values in the **true exterior
square** (\Lambda^2_{\mathbb Z}(\operatorname{Additive} K^\times)). It is
alternating: (a\wedge a=0). The exact antisymmetric-square convention of
K3BlochGroups V.3 can retain diagonal elements of order two. The accepted parent
`suslin-obstruction-of-the-nahm-element` and
`K3BlochGroups:V.3/antisym-exterior-comparison` specify that distinction.
Passing to a true exterior square or tensoring with (\mathbb Q) does not
justify an assertion about an integral torsion order in the other convention.

Here (B_{\rm CGZ}(K)) means the **corrected** convention in
`K3BlochGroups:V.3/cgz-bloch-group`. If (Z(K)) is the free group on
\(\mathbb P^1(K)\), (A(K)) is the exterior-boundary kernel, and (C') is
the subgroup generated by the projective five-term expressions, this is
\(A(K)/(A(K)\cap C')\). The intersection is part of the supplier's correction
of CGZ's degenerate projective-relation presentation. This part imports that
correction and the rational comparison with the exact Suslin convention; it
does not define another Bloch group.

The real Rogers normalization is CGZ (42), with values in
\(\mathbb R/(\pi^2/2)\mathbb Z\). On the positive interval,

\[
 L_{\rm CGZ}(x)=\frac{\pi^2}{6}-\operatorname{Li}_2(x)
                  -\frac12\log x\log(1-x)
                =\frac{\pi^2}{6}-L_{\rm std}(x).
\]

The value of an integral Bloch class under this circle-valued map is meaningful.
A rationalized Bloch class has no canonical circle-valued Rogers evaluation
obtained by dividing an integral value. Bloch–Wigner values, whose target is a
real vector space, do extend rationally. The difference between those two
targets controls the denominator arguments below.

## Polynomial denominator clearing

The new definition is
`HabiroNahmSeries:HB.3/clearing-polynomial-system`, with declaration name
`clearingPolynomial`. For an integral matrix (M), an integral sign vector
\(\varepsilon\), and natural (d), set

\[
  P_i(Y)=
  (1-Y_i^d)\prod_jY_j^{\max(-M_{ij},0)}
     -\varepsilon_i\prod_jY_j^{\max(M_{ij},0)}.
\]

This is a polynomial with rational coefficients even when some entries of
\(M\) are negative. There is no hidden assumption that a positive-definite
matrix has nonnegative entries. Multiplying the Laurent equations by the
negative-exponent monomial gives (P_i(y)=0\). The converse cancels that
monomial and therefore requires each (y_j\ne0\). For denominator clearing we
also impose (d>0\). The polynomial itself is defined for every natural (d\),
so its degenerate value can be tested without inventing a positive-natural
wrapper.

Its uses determine the API. The algebraicity proof needs an actual polynomial
system and its derivatives; embeddings need evaluation to commute with field
maps; changes of index order must not alter the solution set. The formal system
is the specialization (d=1\),
\(\varepsilon_i=(-1)^{M_{ii}}\), with no analytic assumptions.

| API name | Mathematical interface |
| --- | --- |
| `clearingPolynomial.eval` | Existing `MvPolynomial.aeval` gives exactly the displayed polynomial expression at (y\). |
| `clearingPolynomial.zero_iff` | For (d>0\) and every (y_j\ne0\), (P_i(y)=0\) iff (1-y_i^d=\varepsilon_i\prod_jy_j^{M_{ij}}\). |
| `clearingPolynomial.nonnegative` | When all (M_{ij}\ge0\), the clearing monomial is one and (P_i=1-Y_i^d-\varepsilon_i\prod_jY_j^{M_{ij}}\). |
| `clearingPolynomial.map` | For a rational-algebra field map \(\varphi\), \(\varphi(P_i(y))=P_i(\varphi y)\). |
| `clearingPolynomial.reindex` | Renaming variables by a permutation (e\) sends (P_i(d,M,\varepsilon)\) to (P_{e(i)}(d,M\circ(e^{-1},e^{-1}),\varepsilon\circ e^{-1})\). |

The five unit tests are part of the definition's specification:

- `clearingPolynomial_rank_one`: (d=1,M=(2),\varepsilon=1\) gives
  (1-Y-Y^2\).
- `clearingPolynomial_negative_entry`: (d=1,M=(-1),\varepsilon=1\) gives
  \((1-Y)Y-1\). This distinguishes clearing negative exponents from discarding
  their signs.
- `clearingPolynomial_zero_denominator`: (d=0,M=(0),\varepsilon=1\) in rank
  one gives the constant (-1\). The definition still has a value, while zero
  cannot serve as a denominator or root index.
- `clearingPolynomial_boundary_zero`: for the rank-two matrix with diagonal
  (-1\) and off-diagonal (1\), both polynomials vanish at the origin. The
  origin is excluded from the Laurent system. This is a test against dropping
  the unit condition.
- `clearingPolynomial_aeval_compatibility`: in the first rank-one example,
  Mathlib's rational evaluation gives (1-y-y^2\), literally, rather than an
  independently defined evaluation operation.

These tests do not assert positive-cube existence for arbitrary (M\).
They test the polynomial interface on its full algebraic domain.

## Exact Jacobian and the coordinate-field argument

The theorem `HabiroNahmSeries:HB.3/cleared-system-jacobian`, named
`clearingJacobian`, supplies the normalization factors needed by the parent
algebraicity proof. Let (y\) be a zero of the cleared system in a
\(\mathbb Q\)-algebra field, with (y_i\ne0\) and (1-y_i^d\ne0\). Put

\[
 x_i=y_i^d,\quad
 c_i=(1-x_i)\prod_j y_j^{\max(-M_{ij},0)},\quad
 B=M+d\operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{x_i}{1-x_i}\right).
\]

Writing (J_{ij}=(\partial P_i/\partial Y_j)(y)\), the exact equality is

\[
 J=-\operatorname{diag}(c)B\operatorname{diag}(y^{-1}),\qquad
 \det J=(-1)^n\left(\prod_i c_i\right)
                   \left(\prod_j y_j^{-1}\right)\det B.
\]

To prove it, differentiate both monomials. At a zero, replace the positive
monomial term by the equal negative monomial times (1-x_i\). The derivatives
of the clearing factor cancel against each other, and the remaining entry is

\[
 -\frac{c_i}{y_j}
   \left(M_{ij}+d\delta_{ij}\frac{x_i}{1-x_i}\right).
\]

All row and column factors in the determinant are nonzero. Thus
\(\det J\ne0\) exactly when (\det B\ne0\). If (M=dA\), then
\(B=d(A+\operatorname{diag}(x/(1-x)))\). At the distinguished analytic solution
this is nonsingular by positive definiteness and the existing
`Matrix.PosDef.det_pos`. The factor (d\), the clearing monomials and the
inverse roots are all retained. GSWZ (36) uses the corresponding logarithmic
Jacobian for its discriminant; a bare identification of that determinant with
the polynomial Jacobian would omit units and powers of (d\).

Acceptance checks include (P=1-y-y^2\), whose derivative is (-1-2y\), and
\(P=1-y^2-y\), whose derivative is (-2y-1\). The empty determinant is one,
in agreement with the formula's empty products.

The next theorem is
`HabiroNahmSeries:HB.3/coordinate-field-differential-bridge`, named
`coordinateFieldUnramified`. Let (k\subseteq L\) be fields and let the finite
tuple (p_1,\ldots,p_n\) generate (L\) **as a field** over (k\). Suppose
\(f_i\in k[Y_1,\ldots,Y_n]\), (f_i(p)=0\), and the square Jacobian at (p\)
is invertible. Then

\[
 \Omega_{L/k}=0,\qquad L/k\text{ is formally unramified},\qquad
 \dim_k L<\infty.
\]

This is the missing proof input to the accepted parent
`nondegenerate-points-are-algebraic`. It gives an algebraic argument over
arbitrary fields, with no assertion that analytic isolation alone establishes
algebraicity.

Apply the universal derivation (D:L\to\Omega_{L/k}\) to each equation.
Leibniz and induction on polynomial monomials give

\[
 \sum_j(\partial_j f_i)(p)D(p_j)=0.
\]

Invert the Jacobian matrix over (L\); all (D(p_j)\) vanish. The elements
annihilated by (D\) contain (k\) and the tuple (p\). They are closed under
addition and multiplication. For a nonzero (a\), differentiating
\(aa^{-1}=1\) gives (D(a^{-1})=-a^{-2}D(a)\), so they are also closed under
inversion. Mathlib's `IntermediateField.adjoin_induction` therefore implies
that (D\) vanishes on all of (L\). The range of (D\) spans the differential
module by `KaehlerDifferential.span_range_derivation`, which proves
\(\Omega_{L/k}=0\). This is exactly the definition of
`Algebra.FormallyUnramified`, also equivalent by Stacks tag 00UO.

The finiteness step needs field generation, rather than finite generation as an
algebra. The finite set of coordinates makes the generated intermediate field
finitely generated by `IntermediateField.fg_adjoin_of_finite`.
`IntermediateField.essFiniteType_iff` identifies this with an essentially
finite-type algebra. Every vector space over (k\) is free. The pinned theorem
`Algebra.FormallyUnramified.finite_of_free` now gives a finite (k\)-module.
Its three hypotheses—formal unramifiedness, essential finite type and
freeness—have all been supplied. Applying this argument to (\mathbb Q(p)\)
inside \(\mathbb C\) proves algebraicity of each coordinate, as required by
the existing parent target.

For acceptance, (f=Y^2-2\) at (p=\sqrt2\) gives the finite extension
\(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\). The zero polynomial evaluated at a transcendental
coordinate has zero Jacobian and gives no conclusion. These examples prevent
replacing the nonsingularity hypothesis by the assertion that there are as
many equations as unknowns. CGZ's generic dimension observation motivates the
target; the differential proof supplies the exact nonsingular case needed here.

## Coherent roots and their field

The construction
`HabiroNahmSeries:HB.3/positive-coherent-root-lift`, named `positiveRootLift`,
uses the existing real power function:

\[
  y_i=x_i^{1/d},\qquad E=\mathbb Q(y_1,\ldots,y_n).
\]

For (d>0\) and (x\) in the positive cube, the roots are positive and less
than one, and (y_i^d=x_i\). If (A=M/d\), taking logarithms gives
\(y_j^{M_{ij}}=x_j^{A_{ij}}\) even for negative entries. Hence the roots
satisfy the unraised equations (1-x_i=\prod_jy_j^{M_{ij}}\) and the cleared
polynomials with \(\varepsilon=1\).

The exact Jacobian and the coordinate-field theorem prove that (E\) is
finite over \(\mathbb Q\). Since (x_i=y_i^d\), the adjoin universal property
gives (F\subseteq E\); (F\) is consequently a number field too. This supplies
the parent distinguished-coordinate algebraicity argument and preserves its
original field (F\). The construction does not replace (F\) by (E\) wherever
the parent uses coordinate embeddings.

| API name | Mathematical interface |
| --- | --- |
| `positiveRootLift.eq_rpow` | Its ith coordinate is the existing real power (x_i^{1/d}\). |
| `positiveRootLift.pow` | For (d>0\), (x_i\ge0\), the dth power of the lift is (x_i\). |
| `positiveRootLift.mem_cube` | A positive-cube input has a positive-cube lift. |
| `positiveRootLift.one` | For nonnegative inputs, denominator one gives the original tuple. |
| `positiveRootLift.unique` | The lift is the unique nonnegative tuple whose dth powers are the prescribed nonnegative input. |
| `positiveRootLift.equations` | Under the rational-power Nahm equations, (1-x_i=\prod_jy_j^{M_{ij}}\) exactly. |
| `positiveRootLift.field_le` | For nonnegative inputs and (d>0\), the coordinate field is a subfield of the root field. |
| `positiveRootLift.finite` | For positive-definite (A=M/d\) and a positive-cube solution, the root field is a finite rational vector space and hence a number field. |

The tests are:

- `positiveRootLift_rank_one_half`: for (A=(1/2),d=2,M=(1)\), put
  (x=(3-\sqrt5)/2\). Then (y=(\sqrt5-1)/2\), with (y^2=x\) and (1-x=y\).
- `positiveRootLift_denominator_one`: the lift of (1/2\) with (d=1\) is
  (1/2\).
- `positiveRootLift_empty`: the empty root tuple adjoins precisely the bottom
  intermediate field \(\mathbb Q\).
- `positiveRootLift_negative_branch`: the lift of (1/4\) with (d=2\) is not
  (-1/2\). An arbitrary algebraic square root would fail this test.

Zagier II.3A requires coherent determinations for rational exponents. CGZ
§7.1 also explicitly adjoins positive roots when constructing its Kummer
extension. These uses motivate the root choice and field distinction. The
full cyclotomic Kummer extension used for radial asymptotics is an HB.4
construction; it is not replanned in HB.3.

## Integral boundaries and the signs

The theorem `HabiroNahmSeries:HB.3/coherent-root-exterior-boundary`, named
`coherentRootBoundary`, is an exact statement in the existing exterior square.
Let (K\) be a field and (M\) symmetric integral. Let (y_i,t_i\in K^\times\)
satisfy

\[
  t_i=1-y_i^d=\prod_j y_j^{M_{ij}}.
\]

Then

\[
 \sum_i y_i^d\wedge t_i
   =d\sum_{i,j}M_{ij}y_i\wedge y_j=0.
\]

Off-diagonal terms cancel in pairs; alternatingness kills the diagonal terms.
The statement needs symmetry and the exact unraised equations. It needs no
analytic positivity once those hypotheses hold. In the root field (E\), it
certifies the integral class \(\beta=\sum_i[x_i]\) in the corrected CGZ
convention. Its passing to a Bloch class uses the imported V.3 definition, not a
new construction of a Bloch group.

In (F=\mathbb Q(x)\), the unraised rational-power equation cannot be used as
an integral bilinear identity. Raising to the dth power gives

\[
 (1-x_i)^d=\prod_jx_j^{M_{ij}},\qquad
 d\sum_i x_i\wedge(1-x_i)=0.
\]

Thus (c_d\), represented by (d\sum_i[x_i]\), is an integral class in
\(B_{\rm CGZ}(F)\), and the parent's rationalized class is
\(\xi_F=c_d/d\). This argument proves neither that the unmultiplied exterior
boundary vanishes in (F\) nor that diagonal squares vanish in the exact
antisymmetric convention. The familiar unsigned rank-one example
\(A=(1),x=1/2\) separates the latter two assertions: the true exterior boundary
is zero while the parent's antisymmetric-square obstruction can retain
two-torsion.

The signed equations need another calculation. The theorem
`HabiroNahmSeries:HB.3/signed-exterior-boundary-obstruction`, named
`signedBoundaryObstruction`, assumes (z_i,t_i\in K^\times\), symmetric integral
\(M\), and

\[
 t_i=1-z_i=(-1)^{M_{ii}}\prod_jz_j^{M_{ij}}.
\]

Expansion and symmetry give

\[
 \sum_i z_i\wedge(1-z_i)=\sum_i M_{ii}z_i\wedge(-1),\qquad
 2\sum_i z_i\wedge(1-z_i)=0.
\]

The second equality uses the order of (-1\). It certifies the doubled CGZ
class (2\sum_i[z_i]\) and the rationalized class. Even diagonal entries, or
characteristic two, kill this obstruction outright. With odd diagonal entries,
symmetry alone does not kill it.

The parent `general-nondegenerate-class` imports the source's unmultiplied
integral class for the Habiro module. The local boundary calculation does not
identify that class in the corrected CGZ convention or lift it canonically to
the required integral K-theory group. That identification is the fourth
supplier request. Applying a statement about an integral torsion order or an
integral Habiro twist before it is supplied would lose precisely the torsion
information this stage is required to preserve. The discriminant ring and its
square-root involution remain the parent's constructions; they are independent
of this unresolved class interpretation.

## Embeddings, rational classes and Rogers values

The comparison
`HabiroNahmSeries:HB.3/regulator-field-and-normalization-comparison`, named
`regulatorConventionComparison`, assembles the field and convention interfaces
for the existing regulator targets. For the positive solution and its fields,
the map (F\hookrightarrow E\) sends

\[
 c_d\longmapsto d\beta,\qquad
 \xi_F=c_d/d\longmapsto\beta\otimes1.
\]

Changing the positive clearing denominator does not change (\xi_F\). Indeed,
after rationalization the kernel presentation embeds in the rational
pre-Bloch group, where each candidate represents the same unmultiplied symbol
sum. This is the denominator independence of the parent's rational class, not
an integral division operation in (B_{\rm CGZ}(F)\).

For every embedding \(\tau:E\to\mathbb C\), the Bloch–Wigner value of
\(\beta\) is

\[
  \sum_i D(\tau(x_i)).
\]

It equals the value of (\xi_F\) at the restricted embedding of (F\): the
integral class (c_d\) evaluates to (d\) times this sum. Every embedding of
\(F\) extends across (E/F\) because (E/F\) is algebraic and \(\mathbb C\)
is algebraically closed. The exact pinned extension theorem is
`IsAlgClosed.surjective_domRestrict_of_isAlgebraic`. One therefore tests all
embeddings of (F\), or all embeddings of (E\), with equivalent zero
conditions.

The parent torsion criterion uses the rational Suslin/Bloch comparison and
`Polylogarithms:P.2/borel-comparison`. A nonzero rational comparison scalar
suffices for detecting zero. Its precise scalar and sign, owned by
BorelRegulators R.7, are not needed for this conclusion. The still-requested
injectivity in BorelRegulators R.4 yields

\[
 \xi_F=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \sum_iD(\sigma x_i)=0\text{ for every }\sigma:F\to\mathbb C
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\beta\text{ is torsion}.
\]

Unique divisibility of the algebraically closed Bloch group, supplied by the
V.4 request, implies that torsion maps to zero over \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\).
Conversely, extending embeddings and evaluating after passage to
\(\mathbb C\) gives every regulator value zero, hence torsion by the same
number-field criterion. This is the parent's algebraic-closure target. It does
not assert an integral injection of every number-field Bloch group into an
algebraically closed one.

At the preferred real embedding, define the actual real sum
\(S=\sum_i L_{\rm CGZ}(x_i)\) using the positive-interval formula. The
requested Rogers descent gives

\[
 L(c_d)=dS\pmod{(\pi^2/2)\mathbb Z}.
\]

If (m c_d=0\), then (mdS\in(\pi^2/2)\mathbb Z\), so (S\in\mathbb Q\pi^2\).
This proves the required rationality consequence with integer arithmetic.
It neither divides a circle element canonically nor assigns a circle-valued
homomorphism to (B_{\rm CGZ}(F)\otimes\mathbb Q\).

CGZ's continuation is (L(x)=-L(1/x)\) for (x>1\), and
\(L(x)=\pi^2/6-L(1-x)\) for (x<0\). Its projective endpoint values are
\(L(0)=\pi^2/6\), (L(1)=0\), and (L(\infty)=-\pi^2/6\), all modulo
\(\pi^2/2\). That period preserves the source's order-three endpoint class.
Replacing it by \(\pi^2/6\) changes the integral normalization.

The discriminating rank-one values are:

| Matrix (A\) | Distinguished (x\) | (L_{\rm std}(x)\) | (L_{\rm CGZ}(x)\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| (1/2\) | ((3-\sqrt5)/2\) | (\pi^2/15\) | (\pi^2/10\) |
| (1\) | (1/2\) | (\pi^2/12\) | (\pi^2/12\) |
| (2\) | ((\sqrt5-1)/2\) | (\pi^2/10\) | (\pi^2/15\) |

These agree with the parent's (A=(2)\) Rogers test. Vanishing of Bloch–Wigner
at the preferred real embedding is an inadequate torsion test, because it
vanishes at every real argument. All complex embeddings are essential.
