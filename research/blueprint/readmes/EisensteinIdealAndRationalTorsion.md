# Roadmap: Eisenstein ideal and rational torsion of modular Jacobians

**Summary.** Develop prime-level Eisenstein arithmetic as a library: the full integral Eisenstein eigensystem, its cuspidal image ideal, the exact cuspidal and Shimura subgroup schemes, the component group at the level prime, odd and dyadic multiplicity one, winding homomorphisms and Ogg’s rational torsion theorem. Construct the Eisenstein quotient as an abelian variety and prove that its rational points are exactly the cuspidal image. Use its Néron model, torsion specialization and the level-subgroup meaning of the cusps to exclude rational prime torsion on elliptic curves over ℚ. The prime 13 has its own genus-two, norm-19 descent; together with explicit examples this gives precisely the prime orders 2, 3, 5 and 7.

## Scope and ownership

This roadmap owns the arithmetic specific to prime-level Eisenstein ideals and the two torsion descents. Its prime-level statements quantify over every prime P. The positive-genus condition appears exactly where nonzero cuspidal classes or a nonconstant map from X₀(P) are needed. The genus-zero levels are included in the index, torsion and quotient theorems. The resulting consumer interface distinguishes a rational torsion point from a rational cyclic isogeny: an invariant vector is excluded above 7, while a stable line with a nontrivial character can still occur.

`TauCetiRoadmap.ModularCurves` and `ModularCurvesPartII` own the curves, compactifications, generalized elliptic curves, Hecke correspondences, modular Jacobians and cohomological comparisons. `TauCetiRoadmap.ModularForms` owns integral cusp forms, q-expansions, modular symbols and the classical integral Hecke algebra. `AutomorphicFormsOnReductiveGroups` owns the full Hecke-algebra action and its normalization. `FiniteFlatGroupsAndIntegralPadicHodgeTheory` owns generic finite-flat groups, admissibility, purity, extensions and their cohomology. `NeronModelsAndSemistableAbelianVarieties` owns the Néron model, components, monodromy and integral cohomological comparisons. `HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions` owns Mordell–Weil and isogeny Kummer theory. `TauCetiRoadmap.EllipticCurves` owns local elliptic reduction, Tate uniformization and elliptic torsion representations. General connected abelian quotients belong to `JacobianChallenge`; cyclotomic class-field eigenspaces belong to `ClassFieldTheory`.

The supplier extensions stated below are Parts II in those existing directions. They are exact mathematical inputs, and the corresponding gaps remain open until those extensions supply them. The level-13 cusp/intersection computation and the fixed cyclotomic class-number certificate are arithmetic targets here. The full composite-order classification, arbitrary squarefree-level Eisenstein ideals, an explicit list of all rational isogenies and general residual irreducibility are outside this roadmap’s statements. Place this roadmap after `HeightsRationalPointsAndObstructions` in dependency order, respecting the other supplier prerequisites as well.

## Conventions

P is the prime level, ℓ is a coefficient or residual prime, and l is an auxiliary Hecke prime. They are separate variables. Put X=X₀(P), J=Pic⁰(X/ℚ), 𝒥 the Néron model over ℤ, S=Spec ℤ and S′=Spec ℤ[1/P]. The positive integer n is the numerator of (P−1)/12, equivalently (P−1)/gcd(P−1,12). Set c=[0]−[∞]. A cyclic subgroup generated by this rational point is first defined on the generic fibre; its integral closure is identified only after its order and flatness have been proved.

The forgetful map is (E,H)↦E. The cusp ∞ is the width-one Tate cusp with μ_P in the identity direction, while 0 has width P and its ample level subgroup runs through the components of the P-gon. The Fricke involution w exchanges these cusps. Thus w(c)=−c. Hecke operators have the weight-two normalization T_l(c)=(1+l)c for l≠P; U_P=−w on the prime-level cuspidal new part. The full integral Hecke character has ε(w)=−1 and ε(U_P)=1. Its kernel I_full and its image I in the cuspidal algebra T are different ideals in different rings.

A quotient of J in this document is an actual quotient abelian variety with its specified map. An optimal quotient has connected kernel. The Eisenstein quotient A_Eis is J/γ_I J, where γ_I is the kernel of I-adic completion and γ_I J is the connected sum of the endomorphism images. In particular A_Eis is neither the finite group J[I] nor J/IJ. For n=1 the quotient and J are zero. An Eisenstein maximal ideal m_ℓ=(ℓ,I) exists precisely for ℓ|n, so ℓ≠P.

For ℓ≠P, over S an admissible ℓ-group is separated, quasi-finite, flat and finitely presented, finite flat over S′, with an admissible filtration there by ℤ/ℓ and μ_ℓ. Retain its specified fibre at P; a generic Galois module alone does not retain that information. At ℓ=2 a constant generic module need not have the constant integral model. All subgroup intersections involving C, Σ or D are scheme-theoretic intersections. A μ-type group has constant Cartier dual, a stronger requirement than merely being of multiplicative type.

For integral homology H=H₁(X(ℂ),ℤ), σ denotes complex conjugation, H⁺=ker(σ−1), and H_co+=H/(σ−1)H. The natural map from the invariant lattice to the invariant quotient is multiplication by 2 after compatible rank-one identifications. The normalized winding map divides that map by 2 using the group-ring freeness theorem; it does not invert 2 in the coefficients.

At level 13 write B=J₁(13) and S₁₃=Spec ℤ[1/13]. The coefficient prime is 19. The diamond action of 2 generates the order ℤ[ζ₆]; choose the norm-19 factors π, πbar so that πbar kills the rational cuspidal subgroup T₁₉. This labeling fixes which kernel contains the twisted nonconstant line.

## Layer 0: Integral Eisenstein arithmetic

Fix n, the full integral Eisenstein eigensystem and its kernel, distinguish its image I in the cuspidal Hecke ring T, prove the weight-two integral congruence criterion and T/I ≅ Z/n. These statements include the zero Jacobian cases.

### 0.1 The cuspidal index

Define `cuspidalIndex`. For prime P define cuspidalIndex(P)=n=(P−1)/gcd(P−1,12), the positive numerator of (P−1)/12 in lowest terms.

Reduce the rational fraction using the integer gcd. Use P≥2 to prove positivity and the numerator comparison.

The API exposes the following operations and relations.

- `cuspidalIndex_pos`: n>0 for every prime P.
- `cuspidalIndex_num`: n equals the natural numerator of the reduced rational (P−1)/12.
- `cuspidalIndex_dvd`: n divides P−1 and P−1=n·gcd(P−1,12).
- `cuspidalIndex_eq_one`: For prime P, n=1 iff P∈{2,3,5,7,13}.

In II 9.7 and 11.1, normalizes both T/I and the exact order of c. In III §5, detects the positive-genus contradiction n>1.

**Checks.**

- `index_eleven`: n(11)=5, not 10.
- `index_seventeen`: n(17)=4; its dyadic part is not discarded.
- `index_thirteen`: n(13)=1, although P>7.

The denominator is gcd-reduced, with no rounding or factor-of-two ambiguity.

### 0.3 The weight-two congruence criterion

## Arithmetic certificates and source corrections

The full one-dimensional residual-constituent criterion includes residual characteristic equal to the level. Its prime-to-level proof uses unipotent inertia and finite-flat prolongation under the hypothesis ℓ≠P. The ℓ=P boundary needs a separate local argument, recorded as a gap. A rational invariant constituent still forces T_l=1+l directly by Eichler–Shimura in every residual characteristic; that special case is not a proof of the stronger general character criterion.

The level-13 target `level_thirteen_cusp_data` includes three concrete computations: the order-19 cusp subgroup, the bound on the full rational torsion and the intersection of the Abel–Jacobi curve with that nineteen-element subgroup. Mazur–Tate cite Ogg’s unpublished calculation. An implementation must provide an explicit curve model, cusp coordinates, divisor-class relations and the full intersection certificate. Rank zero by itself does not prove the intersection assertion. The target `level_thirteen_global_cohomology` also requires a certificate that 19 does not divide the full class number of ℚ(ζ₁₃); class number one is sufficient. This numerical input is independent of the inverse-character vanishing used at general prime level. These two certificates are the stated arithmetic gaps in Layer 8.

The published errata in [MazurWiles1984Errata] §8.I, pp. 187–188 correct three steps used here. Corollary 16.3 needs the ordinary Weil duality between the multiplicative and étale halves. The P≡9 mod 16 dyadic construction needs a Siegel-function square calculation to identify its two order-2 cusp classes. The lifted Hecke correspondence on X₁(P) must transport the marked generator through the quotient isogeny. The plan uses these corrections in the corresponding targets; it does not reuse the erroneous intermediate divisor manipulation or unmarked Hecke formula.

There is also a cusp-label inconsistency in [Mazur1977], III §5 Step 3, p. 159, relative to II §1, p. 64. With the fixed forgetful map, the Tate μ_P direction is the width-one cusp ∞ and the component direction is 0. Thus the component-escape argument compares specialization to 0 at 3 and to ∞ at the hypothetical identity-direction prime. The contradiction is unchanged, but its geometric interpretation depends on using these labels consistently. Deligne–Rapoport VII §§1–2 gives the moduli comparison. This correction is derived from those conventions; no published erratum for this particular label reversal has been located in the primary texts examined.
