## Scope, conventions and suppliers

Throughout, (F) is a number field, (n>0), (\zeta) is a specified primitive (n)-th root, (L=F(\zeta)), and (G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)). Write (U_L=(\mathcal O_L)^\times). The modular cyclotomic character is determined by

\[
 \sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^{\chi(\sigma)},\qquad
 \chi:G\longrightarrow(\mathbb Z/n)^\times.
\]

The parent declaration `HabiroNumberFields:HB.1/cyclotomic-character-and-eigenspaces` uses Mathlib’s `IsPrimitiveRoot.autToPow`. The character is injective because (L) is generated by the root; it need not be surjective without an additional arithmetic argument. Changing the primitive root changes the trivialization of the Tate twist, but does not change this character.

For a module killed by (n), an eigenspace means the simultaneous equations

\[
 M^{\chi^j}=\{x\in M:\sigma x=\chi(\sigma)^j x\text{ for every }\sigma\in G\}.
\]

For multiplicative power classes, the same equation reads (\sigma[x]=[x]^{\chi(\sigma)^j}). An exponent is a residue modulo (n), and any integer lift gives the same class. In particular a class in the inverse-character eigenspace need not have a representative satisfying the eigenrelation as an equality of units. The class is the object used by (c_\zeta).

The published integral Bloch convention comes from **K3BlochGroups V.3**. Its owner supplies the extended (\mathbb P^1(F)) symbols, the negative-tensor target, the boundary, the kernel presentation (B_{\mathrm{new}}(F)), published Lemma 2.2 and the comparison maps. The exact imports are:

- `K3BlochGroups:V.3/cgz-published-bloch-group`;
- `K3BlochGroups:V.3/cgz-published-lemma-two-two`;
- `K3BlochGroups:V.3/cgz-published-to-older`;
- `K3BlochGroups:V.3/convention-coefficient-exports`.

For number fields, the specified maps (B(F)/n\to B_{\mathrm{new}}(F)/n\to B_{\mathrm{old}}(F)/n) are isomorphisms for odd (n). The integral groups can differ. The parent was written using the repaired exterior-kernel convention of the preprint, whereas V.3 now distinguishes that convention from the published one. Assembly must use these actual coefficient maps, together with the parent’s separate restrictions for the Suslin–(K_3) comparison. No integral identification, removal of a degenerate symbol, or disappearance of 3-primary torsion follows just from agreement after inverting 2. HB.1 consumes these conventions along the direct V.3-to-HB.1 interface; it owns none of their presentations or comparison proofs.

Generic finite-coefficient (K)-theory comes from **StableHomotopyKTheory H.6**. The relevant sequence is

\[
 0\longrightarrow K_3(F)/n\longrightarrow K_3(F;\mathbb Z/n)
 \longrightarrow K_2(F)[n]\longrightarrow0.
\]

The left group is a subgroup of the middle one, not automatically all of it. HB.1 retains the parent `finite-coefficient-K3-and-the-chern-class` interface and its coefficient compatibility requirements.

Generic Soulé finite étale Chern classes and their product formula belong to **MotivicEtaleKTheory M.8**, under the accepted RS-08 decision. An early prefix requiring M.7 must export coefficient reduction, restriction, the (K_1) Kummer normalization and the product formula. The current whole M.8 also uses D.2 and R.7 and cannot simply be put upstream of HB.1. The packet records the precise early-prefix request and a split proposal; it invents neither a stage ID nor an edge from the whole late M.8. HB.1 keeps the degree-three specialization (c_\zeta) and its inverse-character identification. HB.2 and D.2 consume the same generic product theorem from the approved prefix.

## The retained finite Chern interface

The parent’s `the-chern-class-map-c-zeta` composes the Soulé class with reduction modulo (n), restriction to (L), the root trivialization and Kummer theory. For weight (m), the trivialization sends (\zeta\) to (\zeta^{\otimes m}), and the resulting power class has character (\chi^{1-m}). Weight two therefore lands in the inverse-character part. The tensor exponent (n) printed before equation (31) in the published paper is the inherited misprint E1.

The degree-three map is

\[
 c_\zeta:K_3(F)/n\longrightarrow
 (L^\times/L^{\times n})^{\chi^{-1}}.
\]

Its change-of-root formula is (c_{\zeta^k}(x)=c_\zeta(x)^{k^{-1}}), for (k) prime to (n). Finite-extension base change commutes with this map when the root and coefficient identifications are transported compatibly. These normalization requirements are inherited, not additional generic Chern constructions in this packet.

The explicit excluded integers remain

\[
 M_F=6|\operatorname{disc}F|\,#K_2(\mathcal O_F),\qquad
 M'_F=2|\operatorname{disc}F|\,#K_2(\mathcal O_F).
\]

The second integer is used with (9\nmid n). The parent `the-excluded-primes` and `injectivity-of-c-zeta` separate the square-prime condition (p\nmid w_2(F)) for (p^2\mid n) from the conditions giving ordinary units. One must not replace that condition by (\gcd(n,w_2(F))=1), since this would wrongly discard the admissible case (n=3). The retained definition API is still owned by the parent. `cycloChar_spec`, `cycloChar_injective`, `cycloChar_independent` and `cycloChar_eq_modularCyclotomicCharacter` pin the character. `mem_charEigenspace_iff` and `charEigenspace_map_le` give the element and map interfaces. `unitClassesToPowerClasses_injective` identifies unit classes inside field power classes. `cgzExcludedInteger`, `cgzExcludedIntegerNotNine` and their prime-divisor and coprimality lemmas encode the excluded orders. `twistTrivialization_equivariant`, `twistTrivialization_pow`, `chernClassMap_mem_eigenspace`, `chernClassMap_pow_root` and `chernClassMap_baseChange` pin the Chern normalization. Their definitions, API declarations and tests are referenced in the accepted parent rather than copied into this follow-up.

The inherited tests retain their purpose. For (F=\mathbb Q,n=3), the inverse-character unit classes have dimension one despite (r_2(F)=0). ## Finite Picard obstructions

`HabiroNumberFields:HB.1/finite-endomorphism-obstruction-criterion` proposes `TauCeti.HabiroNumberFields.finiteEndomorphism_torsionKernel_eq_zero`.

Let (A) be a finite abelian group, (f:A\to A) an additive endomorphism and (n\) a natural number. If every (x\in A) can be written (x=f(y)+nz), then

\[
 \ker f\cap A[n]=0.
\]

For a prime (q\mid n), restrict to the finite (q)-primary subgroup. Iterating (A_q=f(A_q)+nA_q) gives (A_q=f(A_q)+n^kA_q). For large (k), the last summand is zero, so (f) is surjective and hence injective there. All components of an element killed by (n) lie in these primary groups. At (n=0), the hypothesis is already surjectivity of (f), and finiteness again gives injectivity.

For a cyclic group (G=\langle\gamma\rangle) acting on a finite class group, take (f=\gamma-a), where (a) lifts (\chi(\gamma)^{-1}\). On (A/n), the relation for the generator generates all twisted coinvariant relations. On (A[n]), its eigenrelation implies the relations for every group element. Consequently zero twisted coinvariants of (A/n) imply zero twisted eigenvectors of (A[n]). This proves the particular vanishing implication needed in CGZ. It does not identify (A[n]) with (A/n), nor construct a canonical isomorphism between invariants and coinvariants.

The finite hypothesis matters. Multiplication by 3 on (\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z) is surjective and has a nonzero kernel killed by 3. On (\mathbb Z/9), multiplication by 2 passes the criterion at (n=9), while the zero endomorphism fails it at (n=3). These acceptance cases distinguish the actual argument from a false claim for arbitrary torsion groups.

`HabiroNumberFields:HB.1/keune-picard-eigen-obstruction` proposes `TauCeti.HabiroNumberFields.picard_inverseCyclotomicEigen_torsion_eq_zero`.

Assume (p\) odd, (m\ge1), (n=p^m), (p\nmid\operatorname{disc}(F)) and (p\nmid\#K_2(\mathcal O_F)). Let (A=\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_L[1/p])). Keune’s supplier contract is the injection

\[
 (A/p^m)_{\chi^{-1}}\hookrightarrow K_2(\mathcal O_F)/p^m.
\]

It is owned by `ArithmeticKTheory:N.6/keune-cyclotomic-picard-injection`, already present in `ArithmeticKTheory--N.1.json`. The finite (K_2) target is zero under the stated order condition. The pinned `NumberField.RingOfIntegers.instFintypeClassGroup` and Tau Ceti’s `finite_integer_classGroup` supply finiteness: extension of ideal classes to S-integers is surjective. The cyclotomic ramification calculation below gives a cyclic (G\cong(\mathbb Z/p^m)^\times). The preceding finite-endomorphism argument therefore yields

\[
 \bigl(\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_L[1/p])[p^m]\bigr)^{\chi^{-1}}=0.
\]

This last group, rather than (A/p^m), is the Kummer obstruction. The distinction is part of the contract.

The published CGZ application has been read. Keune’s original article, *On the structure of the (K_2) of the ring of integers in a number field*, K-Theory 2 (1989), 625–645, [DOI 10.1007/BF00535049](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00535049), was not obtained through the publisher or as a public author copy. The N.6 packet also explicitly leaves its original proof and exact hypothesis translation open. This follow-up does not turn that open input into a verified theorem. It plans the downstream argument conditional on that exact contract and records the propagated gap. The M.3 étale description of (K_2) alone does not replace the injection.

## Cyclotomic ramification and the actual valuation lattice

`HabiroNumberFields:HB.1/cyclotomic-prime-valuation-action` proposes `TauCeti.HabiroNumberFields.cyclotomic_primePrimes_fixed`.

For (p\) odd, (m\ge1) and (p\nmid\operatorname{disc}(F)), the extension (L=F(\zeta_{p^m})) has degree (\varphi(p^m)). Every prime (\mathfrak p\mid p) of (F) has a unique prime (\mathfrak P) of (L) above it, with

\[
 e(\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p)=\varphi(p^m),\qquad
 f(\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p)=1.
\]

Mathlib’s discriminant criterion makes the completed base field (F_{\mathfrak p}/\mathbb Q_p) unramified. Its uniformizer is therefore (p). The pinned theorem `cyclotomic_prime_pow_comp_X_add_one_isEisensteinAt` states that (\Phi_{p^m}(X+1)) is Eisenstein at (p) over the integers. Its coefficients remain Eisenstein over the completed integer ring: the constant coefficient has valuation one and the other non-leading coefficients have positive valuation. The existing LocalFieldsRamification Layer 3 criterion gives a totally ramified completed extension of degree (\varphi(p^m)).

The global degree is at most this polynomial degree. The NumberFieldArithmetic Layer 5 completion product formula forces it to equal the degree of this single completed factor and leaves no other factor above (\mathfrak p). This proves the unique-prime and relative (e,f) statements. Faithfulness of the modular character, together with equality of finite orders, gives (G\cong(\mathbb Z/p^m)^\times). This proof does not assume a product decomposition involving the Galois closure of (F).

Write (S_p) for the finite set of primes of (L) above (p), (U_p=(\mathcal O_L[1/p])^\times), and

\[
 D=\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{ord}_{S_p}:U_p\to\mathbb Z^{S_p}\bigr).
\]

This is a free finite-rank subgroup of the integer valuation group. The unique-prime result says that (G) fixes every coordinate, and hence acts trivially on (D). The exact sequence to reduce is

\[
 0\to U_L\to U_p\to D\to0,
 \qquad
 0\to U_L/n\to U_p/n\to D/n\to0.
\]

The second sequence is exact because (D) is free. It is not justified by replacing (D/nD) with the full coordinate group (\mathbb Z^{S_p}/n): the inclusion of a sublattice need not remain injective on reduction. For example (D=3\mathbb Z\subset\mathbb Z) gives a nonzero (D/3D) mapping to zero in (\mathbb Z/3\mathbb Z). This is an acceptance test for the proof’s choice of target, not a claim that this particular lattice occurs under every arithmetic hypothesis.

At (F=\mathbb Q,n=9), the relative degree and ramification index are 6. The valuation action remains trivial although 3 divides the group order. The theorem’s discriminant condition is essential to the Eisenstein argument over the completed base.

## Lifting eigenclasses without averaging

`HabiroNumberFields:HB.1/injective-exact-map-eigenclass-lift` proposes `TauCeti.HabiroNumberFields.eigenclass_existsUnique_lift`.

Let (R) be a commutative ring, (G) a group, and (U,M,C) linear (G)-representations. Suppose (i:U\to M) is injective and equivariant, (\delta:M\to C) is equivariant, and (\operatorname{im}i=\ker\delta). For a character (\eta:G\to R^\times), assume (C^\eta=0). Every (x\in M^\eta) then has a unique preimage in (U^\eta).

Indeed, equivariance makes (\delta(x)) an eigenvector, so it is zero. Exactness gives a lift (u). The expression (\rho_U(g)u-\eta(g)u) maps to zero under (i), so injectivity forces it to vanish. Injectivity also gives uniqueness. The argument needs neither surjectivity of (\delta) nor a finite group, and uses no inverse of the group order. It does not assert right exactness of an eigenspace functor.

`HabiroNumberFields:HB.1/ordinary-unit-eigenclass-lift` proposes `TauCeti.HabiroNumberFields.etale_inverseCyclotomicEigen_unitLift`.

Under the Keune application’s hypotheses, every inverse-character class in (H^1_{\mathrm{ét}}(\mathcal O_L[1/p],\mu_n)) has a unique ordinary-unit class as preimage. Apply the lifting lemma to the equivariant Kummer sequence

\[
 0\to U_p/n\to H^1_{\mathrm{ét}}(\mathcal O_L[1/p],\mu_n)
 \to\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_L[1/p])[n]\to0.
\]

The Picard eigenpart vanishes by the preceding application, producing an inverse-character (p)-unit class. Apply the same lemma to the reduced valuation-image sequence. Its right group (D/n) has trivial action. Since (G\cong(\mathbb Z/p^m)^\times), choose an element whose character is not one modulo (p). The difference (\chi(\sigma)^{-1}-1) is a unit modulo (p^m). It annihilates an inverse-character vector of the trivial module only when that vector is zero. Thus the right eigenspace vanishes, and the (p)-unit class lifts uniquely to an ordinary-unit class.

This explicitly covers (m>1). For (n=9), division by (|G|=6) would be invalid; the difference between a nontrivial residual character value and one is still invertible. That is the scalar used in the proof.

Tau Ceti’s pinned `selmerGroup.fromSUnitLift_injective`, `ker_toClassGroup` and `range_toClassGroup` already supply the algebraic unit/Selmer/class-group sequence. Their actual statements were read. They do not by themselves identify that algebraic sequence with the displayed étale Kummer sequence. The packet requests the equivariance and compatibility at M.3 and the field Kummer naturality from ProfiniteCohomology Layer 9.

The inherited (c_\zeta) factors through this lift because its weight-two class restricts to precisely this inverse-character étale group. The parent’s prime-power and field-extension compatibility assembles composite good orders. For arbitrary orders the inherited S-unit statement permits (S=S(F,n)); no uniform set independent of (n) is deduced.

## The direct logarithmic character calculation

`HabiroNumberFields:HB.1/odd-cyclotomic-unit-multiplicity` proposes `TauCeti.HabiroNumberFields.oddCharacter_unitMultiplicity`.

Let (L/F) be finite Galois, with (L) totally complex. Let (\eta:G\to\mathbb C^\times) be a genuine nontrivial one-dimensional character. At every infinite place (w) over a real place of (F), assume (\eta(c_w)=-1), where (c_w) is the nonidentity element of its stabilizer. Then

\[
 \dim_{\mathbb C}(\mathbb C\otimes_{\mathbb Z}U_L)^\eta=r_2(F).
\]

The proof starts from existing Dirichlet theory. Mathlib’s `NumberField.Units.logEmbedding` omits a chosen coordinate and is not itself an equivariant target. `sum_logEmbedding_component` restores that coordinate as the negative sum of the others. `logEmbeddingEquiv` identifies the integral unit quotient by torsion with the lattice, and `unitLattice_span_eq_top` gives full real span. Together these identify the real tensor of units with the hyperplane

\[
 H_L=\{f:S_\infty(L)\to\mathbb R:\textstyle\sum_w f(w)=0\}
\]

using the full weighted logarithm (\lambda(u)(w)=\operatorname{mult}(w)\log|u|_w). The baseline `instZLattice_unitLattice` gives a discrete full lattice, and `Module.Basis.ofZLatticeBasis` turns an integral lattice basis into a real basis. This supplies injectivity as well as surjectivity after scalar extension; `unitLattice_rank` confirms the rank comparison. Finite torsion disappears under tensoring.

The action convention is decisive. Mathlib uses (\sigma\cdot w=w\circ\sigma^{-1}), so

\[
 \lambda(\sigma u)(w)=\lambda(u)(\sigma^{-1}\cdot w).
\]

Weights are constant along an automorphism orbit. Checking this equality coordinate by coordinate makes the full logarithmic isomorphism equivariant. It does not pretend that the omitted-place coordinate system is invariant.

After complexification the full permutation module is (H_{L,\mathbb C}) plus the constant line. The sum map splits equivariantly in characteristic zero; a nontrivial character contributes nothing to the constant line. This splitting is used only here, in characteristic zero.

The pinned `orbitRelEquiv` indexes the orbits by infinite places of (F). On the orbit (G/G_w), an eigenfunction is determined by one value. That value is free when the character is trivial on (G_w), and forced to zero otherwise. Over a complex place, `IsUnramified.stabilizer_eq_bot` gives trivial stabilizer. Over a real place the `IsConj` stabilizer formula gives ({1,c_w}), where oddness kills the eigenfunction. Each complex base place therefore contributes one dimension and each real base place contributes zero.

This proves the needed character multiplicity without planning a second generic Dirichlet or Herbrand unit theorem. [Sutherland’s Lecture 24](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.785/2021fa/LectureNotes24.pdf), Theorem 24.6, supplies an independent source for the permutation-module description. The actual proof interface here is the pinned Dirichlet lattice and place action.

For (L=F(\zeta_p)), (p\nmid\operatorname{disc}(F)), use the inverse Teichmüller character, which is nontrivial and odd. An embedding of its finite roots of unity into (\mathbb C) gives the complex character in the theorem. For composite (n), a homomorphism to ((\mathbb Z/n)^\times) is not automatically a complex-valued character. The assertion must name an actual character, not silently treat a residue unit as a complex eigenvalue.

The acceptance cases include (F=\mathbb Q,L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_5)), with inverse-character multiplicity zero, and (F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2),L=F(\zeta_3)), with sign multiplicity one. In the latter case the Galois closure already contains (\zeta_3), invalidating the product step in the published proof but not this relative calculation. The trivial character is deliberately excluded: its multiplicity is the ordinary unit rank of the base, with the constant-line subtraction retained.

## Integral comparison and the torsion correction

`HabiroNumberFields:HB.1/prime-unit-torsion-exact-sequence` proposes `TauCeti.HabiroNumberFields.unit_inverseCyclotomicEigen_torsionSequence`.

Let (p) be odd and unramified in (F), (L=F(\zeta_p)), (U=U_L), and (T=\operatorname{torsion}(U)). The free integral quotient (U/T) gives

\[
 0\to(T/T^p)^{\chi^{-1}}\to(U/U^p)^{\chi^{-1}}
 \to((U/T)/(U/T)^p)^{\chi^{-1}}\to0.
\]

The quotient (U/T) has no (p)-torsion, which justifies reduction of the original torsion sequence. Here (|G|=p-1) is a unit in (\mathbb Z_p) and (\mathbb F_p), so the inverse-Teichmüller projector is defined and exact. This is the legitimate specialization of the parent projector node, not a claim about the group at order (p^m).

To compare the complex multiplicity with the residual rank, use the integral representation on (U/T). Its matrices and traces are integral. Evaluate the character idempotent over the field of ((p-1))-st roots of unity and transport its trace under an embedding into the (p)-adic coefficient field. The resulting projector rank is the same integer (r_2(F)). Over (\mathbb Z_p), its image is a direct summand of a finite free module and is free; reduction modulo (p) has that same dimension. This integral argument is required: an abstract complex dimension does not on its own determine a residual eigenspace, and no field isomorphism (\mathbb C\simeq\mathbb Q_p) is used.

The cyclotomic completion degree also proves (\mu_{p^\infty}(L)=\mu_p). If (L) contained (\zeta_{p^2}), its completion of degree (p-1) over the unramified (F_{\mathfrak p}) would contain a cyclotomic extension of degree (p(p-1)), a contradiction. Thus the (p)-primary part of (T) contributes one line with character (\chi). It lies in the inverse-character part precisely when (\chi^2=1). For the full group ((\mathbb Z/p)^\times), this means (p=3). Hence

\[
 \dim_{\mathbb F_p}(U/U^p)^{\chi^{-1}}=r_2(F)+[p=3].
\]

For (F=\mathbb Q,p=3), the units of (\mathbb Z[\zeta_3]) are (\mu_6), and their cube classes form a one-dimensional inverse-character space. The torsion-free quotient has rank zero. For (p=5), the inverse-character space is zero. If the base already contains (\zeta_p), the full-group hypothesis fails and this formula cannot be applied.

This supplies the unit-side count for the parent `cgz-theorem-1-5`. The parent’s imported (K_3) torsion comparison gives the matching (p)-component count. Consequently the square-free isomorphism conclusion survives, including admissible (3\mid n), with the torsion term included. The printed uniform count ((\mathbb Z/n)^{r_2(F)}) in published §3.5 is the inherited error E2.
