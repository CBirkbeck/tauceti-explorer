# P-adic differential equations, rigid cohomology and p-adic weights — RD.6, second part: conductors, rank-one coefficients, Tannakian groups, Kloosterman coefficients and the remaining weight inputs

This document completes layer RD.6 (trace formula, Fourier transform and p-adic weights) of the roadmap. The first part of RD.6 (the parent RD.6 nodes, cited by id below) states and proves the rigid Lefschetz trace formula, the p-adic Fourier transform, the Grothendieck–Ogg–Shafarevich formula, determinantal weights, Rankin squaring, Kedlaya's rigid Weil II and its consequences, following Kedlaya's *Fourier transforms and p-adic 'Weil II'*. This part supplies what those statements rest on and what the layer still owes its consumers:

1. **Conductors.** Christol–Mebkhout's index theorems and their global p-adic Euler–Poincaré formula, and the equality Irr_x = Swan_x for overconvergent F-isocrystals at the points at infinity of a curve (the local comparison of differential and ramification breaks is planned in RD.2). Together they prove the p-adic Grothendieck–Ogg–Shafarevich formula `RD.6/grothendieck-ogg-shafarevich-formula`.
2. **Rank-one coefficients.** Constant F-lines, the isoclinic twist, the unit-root correspondence of Katz, Crew and Tsuzuki, Katz–Lang finiteness, and Tsuzuki's theorem that a rank-one overconvergent F-isocrystal has finite order up to a constant twist; hence every rank-one coefficient is ι-pure. This is the input of `RD.6/rank-one-isocrystals-are-pure` and of determinantal weights.
3. **Tannakian input.** The neutral Tannakian recognition theorem and the neutral Tannakian category generated by a coefficient, which the global monodromy group `RD.6/global-monodromy-group` needs.
4. **Coefficients over finite fields and their conventions.** Dwork's additive character, the Kummer F-isocrystal, one dictionary of signs and directions for Dwork and Kummer coefficients across the literature, closed points and zeta functions over F_q, and descent of rationality of power series.
5. **Kloosterman coefficients.** The Kloosterman F-isocrystal Kl_{n+1}, its local monodromy and purity, purity of middle rigid cohomology, and the rigid cohomology of its symmetric powers as used by Fresán–Sabbah–Yu.
6. **The remaining proof steps of the first part.** The Frobenius on the p-adic Fourier transform, normalisation of real weights, weight–monodromy purity at the points at infinity of a curve, nonnegative slopes for constant coefficients, and the passage of nuclear traces from the de Rham complex to cohomology.

## Conventions

- k = F_q with q = p^a; K is complete for a discrete valuation, of characteristic 0, with residue field F_q, and σ\_K = id, so Frobenius on cohomology is the K-linear q-power Frobenius F of `RD.6/linear-q-frobenius`. For statements over a perfect field k (the Christol–Mebkhout and Irr = Swan theorems) σ\_K lifts the q-power map of k.
- A Frobenius structure is an isomorphism F : σ^\*E → E from the Frobenius pullback to the module. The linear Frobenius F_x at a closed point x (`RD.6/frobenius-at-closed-point`) plays the role of **geometric** Frobenius in ℓ-adic cohomology: O(−1) has F = q and weight 2.
- π ∈ K satisfies π^{p−1} = −p whenever Dwork coefficients occur, and p is odd for the identity ψ\_{−π} = ψ\_π^{-1}. The Dwork isocrystal L_π has ∇e = π e ⊗ dx and F(σ^\*e) = exp(π(x^q − x)) e; its Frobenius trace at x_0 ∈ F_{q^r} is ψ\_π(Tr x_0)^{-1}, where ψ\_π(1) ≡ 1 + π mod π². The Kummer isocrystal K_a (a ∈ (q−1)^{-1}Z) has ∇e = a e ⊗ dt/t and F(σ^\*e) = t^{(q−1)a} e, with trace χ\_a(Norm x_0), χ\_a(u) = ũ^{(q−1)a}. Other conventions in the sources are translated by `RD.6/dwork-kummer-sign-dictionary`.
- Weights are ι-weights for a field embedding ι : K^alg → C, imported from DeligneWeightsAndPurity DWP.0 (`DWP.0/iota-weight`, `DWP.0/weil-q-number`, `DWP.0/endomorphism-weights`); this layer defines only pointwise purity and mixedness of overconvergent F-isocrystals (`RD.6/pointwise-iota-weights`).
- Irr_x(E) is the differential Swan conductor (sum of differential breaks) of E ⊗ R_x over the Robba ring at x (`RD.1/differential-swan-conductor`); Swan_x(E) is the Swan conductor of the local monodromy representation (`RD.6/swan-conductor-of-isocrystal`, built on ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations R01.3, which covers equal-characteristic local fields κ(x)((t)) with perfect residue field).
- Slopes are normalised by v_q = v_p/v_p(q), so O(−1) has slope 1 (`RD.6/slope-interval-of-isocrystal`).

## A. The p-adic Grothendieck–Ogg–Shafarevich formula

Kedlaya's Theorem 4.4.1 combines two theorems: Christol–Mebkhout's Euler–Poincaré formula with irregularities, and the equality of irregularity and Swan conductor (Crew, Matsuda, Tsuzuki). The local index theorems are statements about differential modules over the Robba ring and belong to RD.1; the global formula needs rigid cohomology and its finiteness, so it sits here, after RD.4 and RD.5. The local comparison of breaks and of conductors is planned in RD.2 (`RD.2/matsuda-highest-break-theorem`, `RD.2/irregularity-equals-swan-conductor`) on the Swan conductors of ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations R01.3; here it is applied at the points at infinity of a curve. With these, `RD.6/grothendieck-ogg-shafarevich-formula` is proved: its step (a) is the global formula and its step (b) is Irr_x = Swan_x.

#### Christol–Mebkhout local index theorems on the Robba ring

`RD.1/christol-mebkhout-local-index` (theorem)

**Statement.** Let K be complete for a discrete valuation, of characteristic 0, with perfect residue field k of characteristic p, let R be the Robba ring over K in the variable x (germs at the boundary |x| = 1 of analytic functions on annuli 1 − ε < |x| < 1), A the ring of analytic functions on the open unit disc and, for small ε, A(I_ε) = A ⊕ H_ε the functions on the annulus I_ε = ]1 − ε, 1\[ split into the parts with nonnegative and negative powers of x. Let M be a finite free differential module over R that is solvable at the boundary (generic radius tending to 1) and whose exponent is non-Liouville in the sense of Christol–Mebkhout (Définitions 10.2–10.9, 13.1). Write the derivation on a basis of a model M_ε over A(I_ε) as D − G. Then: (a) for ε small enough the operator x(D − G) has a generalized index (Robba's notion: the composite of the operator with the projection to the A-part, resp. the H_ε-part, has finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel) and χ(M, A) = −χ(M, H_ε) equals the height of the differential Newton polygon of M, i.e. the irregularity Irr(M), which is the differential Swan conductor of RD.1/differential-swan-conductor; (b) ∇ acting on M itself has finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel, and χ(M, R) := dim ker ∇ − dim coker ∇ = 0. Every solvable module with a Frobenius structure has non-Liouville exponent, so (a) and (b) hold for every (σ, ∇)-module over R.

**Hypotheses.**

- K complete discretely valued of characteristic 0 with perfect residue field of characteristic p.
- M finite free over R, solvable at the boundary, with non-Liouville exponent (automatic in the presence of a Frobenius structure).
- Indices in (a) are generalized indices in the sense of Christol–Mebkhout Définition 14.1; in (b) the index is the ordinary one on R.

**Proof outline.**

1. Additivity: generalized indices add in short exact sequences of solvable modules (CM Proposition 14.10), so it suffices to treat the graded pieces of the slope (break) decomposition (CM Théorème 9.10 and Corollaire 9.20; RD.1/break-decomposition).
2. Slope-zero pieces: by CM Théorème 12.1 an irreducible solvable module of slope 0 with non-Liouville exponent is x^a·R with a non-Liouville; the index is computed directly and is 0 on A, on H_ε and on R.
3. Pieces of slope λ > 0: take a Frobenius antecedent near ρ < 1 with radius below |π|ρ (CM Théorème 7.5, the transfer of RD.1), use the cyclic vector theorem (CM Théorème 6.2) and invariance of the index under small perturbation (CM Théorème 14.3, c-compact perturbation of operators between LF spaces) to reduce to multiplication by a nonzero analytic function on an annulus, whose generalized index counts its zeros (CM Proposition 14.8); pass to the limit ρ → 1 by Mittag-Leffler. The result is λ times the rank, i.e. the height of the slope-λ side of the Newton polygon.
4. (b) is (a) for both halves: χ on A(I_ε) is the sum of the generalized indices on A and H_ε (CM Proposition 14.6), which cancel; independence of ε gives the index on R (CM Théorème 14.13, compare Poineau–Pulita V Corollary 2.4.3 for the colimit over annuli).
5. Frobenius structure ⇒ hypotheses: solvability is RD.1/frobenius-structure-forces-solvability; the slope-zero part of a module with Frobenius structure is quasi-unipotent and its exponent lies in Z_p ∩ Q, which is non-Liouville (CM Corollaire 11.9 and its introduction, p. 127).

**Acceptance.**

- The Dwork module ∇e = πe dx at ∞ (coordinate t = 1/x): Irr = 1, so the generalized index on the disc part is 1 while H^0 and H^1 over R both vanish (compare Kedlaya Weil II Proposition 4.4.5, where H^0_loc = H^1_loc = 0 for such twists).
- The trivial module R: H^0 = K and H^1 = K·dx/x, index 0, Irr = 0.
- Kummer module x^a·R with a ∈ (q − 1)^{-1}Z − Z: H^0 = H^1 = 0 over R.

**Prerequisites.** `RD.1/differential-swan-conductor`, `RD.1/break-decomposition`, `RD.1/solvable-at-boundary`, `RD.1/frobenius-structure-forces-solvability`, `RD.1/highest-break`.

**Sources.** Christol et al., *Équations différentielles p-adiques et coefficients p-adiques sur les courbes*, Définition 14.1, Proposition 14.6, Proposition 14.10, pp. 170-172: Robba's generalized index of an operator on the annulus split into its disc and outer parts, its additivity and its relation with the ordinary index on the annulus. Christol et al., *Équations différentielles p-adiques et coefficients p-adiques sur les courbes*, Théorème 14.11, p. 172: A solvable module with non-Liouville exponent has a generalized index on the disc part and on the outer part; the two are opposite and equal the height of the Newton polygon of the module. Christol et al., *Équations différentielles p-adiques et coefficients p-adiques sur les courbes*, Théorème 14.13, p. 173: Such a module has an index on the Robba ring, equal to 0. Christol et al., *Équations différentielles p-adiques et coefficients p-adiques sur les courbes*, Introduction to Chapter I and Corollaire 11.9, pp. 126-127 and 165: A slope-zero module with Frobenius structure is quasi-unipotent with rational exponents, hence non-Liouville. Poineau et al., *The convergence Newton polygon of a p-adic differential equation V: local index theorems*, Corollary 2.4.3 and (2.98), p. 39: The de Rham cohomology over the Robba ring is the colimit of that over thin annuli at the boundary germ, finite when the annulus indices stabilise; used as a public cross-check of (b).

#### Christol–Mebkhout global p-adic Euler–Poincaré formula with irregularities

`RD.6/christol-mebkhout-global-euler-characteristic` (theorem — planet: *p-adic Euler–Poincaré formula*)

**Statement.** Let k be perfect of characteristic p, C a smooth geometrically connected affine curve over k with smooth compactification C̄, and E ∈ F-Isoc†(C/K) of rank r. For each x ∈ C̄ − C let R_x be the Robba ring over the unramified extension K_x of K with residue field κ(x) at the parameter of x, and Irr_x(E) := Swan(E ⊗ R_x) the differential Swan conductor of the solvable module E ⊗ R_x (RD.1/differential-swan-conductor, computed over K_x). Then H^0_rig(C/K, E) and H^1_rig(C/K, E) are finite-dimensional and χ(C/K, E) := dim H^0_rig − dim H^1_rig = r·χ(C/K, O_C) − Σ\_{x ∈ C̄ − C} \[κ(x) : k]·Irr_x(E), with χ(C/K, O_C) = 2 − 2g(C̄) − Σ\_x \[κ(x) : k].

**Hypotheses.**

- k perfect; C smooth geometrically connected affine curve with smooth compactification C̄ (when C̄ has several geometric components, apply the formula after a finite extension of k).
- E carries a Frobenius structure: this gives solvability and non-Liouville exponents at every x ∈ C̄ − C (RD.1/christol-mebkhout-local-index), which is what Christol–Mebkhout's hypothesis MLCSNL asks for. Without Frobenius structure the formula can fail (p-adic Liouville exponents, Kedlaya, Notes on isocrystals Remark 8.5).

**Proof outline.**

1. Finiteness is RD.5/finiteness-of-rigid-cohomology (curve case RD.5/crew-finiteness-and-duality-on-curves). Rigid cohomology of E is the de Rham cohomology of the dagger module (RD.4/monsky-washnitzer-comparison). For constant coefficients the index computation RD.5/principal-parts-index-computation gives χ(C/K, O_C) = 2 − 2g(C̄) − Σ\_x \[κ(x) : k].
2. Reductions (Kedlaya Weil II §4.4 proof sketch, pp. 33–34): enlarging K and k changes neither side; removing a rational point of C lowers both sides by r, the irregularity at the removed point being 0 (Kedlaya's text says 'increases'; see sourceIssues); after shrinking, C admits a finite étale map to A^1 (RD.3/etale-cover-of-affine-space, Kedlaya Proposition 6.6.1), and the formula for E follows from the formula for its pushforward (RD.3/finite-etale-pushforward-of-isocrystals, RD.4/finite-etale-pushforward-cohomology), the irregularities of the pushforward being computed through RD.6/irregularity-equals-swan and the induction formula ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.3/local-induction-formula.
3. Algebraization (Christol–Mebkhout Théorème 1.1, p. 127, and the first part of Théorème 1.2, p. 129): after a finite extension of K, each E ⊗ R_x has a lattice over the bounded functions near x carrying a meromorphic connection with its only singularity at x; gluing with E gives a vector bundle with meromorphic connection on the analytification of C̄\_K, which is algebraic by proper rigid GAGA (AdicSpacesPartII:R3/proper-gaga-coherent-equivalence).
4. Index comparison: the snake lemma for the dagger functions modulo algebraic functions near the points at infinity (Kedlaya Weil II (4.4.3), p. 34) expresses χ\_rig(C, E) − χ\_dR,alg(C_K, F) as a sum of local generalized indices; by RD.1/christol-mebkhout-local-index (a) each local term is a difference between the algebraic (Malgrange) irregularity and the p-adic irregularity Irr_x(E); the algebraic Euler–Poincaré formula χ\_dR,alg(C_K, F) = r·χ(C) − Σ Irr^alg_x(F) then gives the stated formula. This deduction is Christol–Mebkhout \[CM IV, Corollaire 5.0-12], whose detailed proof is not freely available; it is recorded as a gap of this packet.

**Acceptance.**

- C = A^1, E = O: χ = 1 = 1·1 − 0.
- C = A^1, E = L_π (Dwork): Irr_∞ = 1, so χ = 0, matching H^0 = H^1 = 0 (parent RD.6/dwork-isocrystal).
- C = G_m, E = K_a (Kummer, a ∉ Z, RD.6/kummer-isocrystal): Irr_0 = Irr_∞ = 0, χ = 0, matching H^0 = H^1 = 0.
- C = A^1, E = L_{x^d}, p ∤ d: χ = 1 − d, so dim H^1 = d − 1.

**Prerequisites.** `RD.1/christol-mebkhout-local-index`, `RD.1/differential-swan-conductor`, `RD.5/finiteness-of-rigid-cohomology`, `RD.5/crew-finiteness-and-duality-on-curves`, `RD.4/monsky-washnitzer-comparison`, `RD.4/rigid-cohomology`, `RD.3/overconvergent-f-isocrystal`, `RD.3/finite-etale-pushforward-of-isocrystals`, `RD.4/finite-etale-pushforward-cohomology`, `AdicSpacesPartII:R3/proper-gaga-coherent-equivalence`, `RD.6/irregularity-equals-swan`, `ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.3/local-induction-formula`, `RD.3/etale-cover-of-affine-space`, `RD.5/principal-parts-index-computation`.

**Sources.** Christol et al., *Équations différentielles p-adiques et coefficients p-adiques sur les courbes*, Théorème 1.2, p. 129: For a proper smooth curve over the valuation ring and a solvable module with non-Liouville conditions on the complement of the singular residue classes, the p-adic de Rham cohomology is finite-dimensional and its Euler–Poincaré characteristic is rank times χ of the open curve minus the sum of the irregularities at the singular points (stated with rational points; proof referred to \[CM IV]). Christol et al., *Équations différentielles p-adiques et coefficients p-adiques sur les courbes*, Théorème 1.1, p. 127: Algebraization of solvable Robba modules with non-Liouville conditions: a lattice with a meromorphic connection singular only at 0, after a finite extension of K. Kedlaya, *Fourier transforms and p-adic 'Weil II'*, Theorem 4.4.1 (a) and the proof sketch, pp. 33-35: Cites Christol–Mebkhout IV Corollaire 5.0-12 for the formula with Irr_x, and sketches the reductions, the algebraic extension at infinity, the snake-lemma identity (4.4.3) and the use of Mebkhout's complex Euler–Poincaré formula. Kedlaya, *Notes on isocrystals*, Theorem 8.6, p. 21: States the Euler characteristic formula for overconvergent F-isocrystals on a curve over an algebraically closed field with Swan conductors, attributing it to Christol–Mebkhout, Crew, Matsuda and Tsuzuki.

#### Irregularity equals Swan conductor for overconvergent F-isocrystals

`RD.6/irregularity-equals-swan` (theorem)

**Statement.** Let k be perfect, C a smooth affine curve over k with smooth compactification C̄, E ∈ F-Isoc†(C/K) and x ∈ C̄ − C. Then the p-adic irregularity of E at x equals its Swan conductor: Irr_x(E) = Swan_x(E), where Irr_x(E) is the differential Swan conductor of E ⊗ R_x (RD.1/differential-swan-conductor) and Swan_x(E) is the Swan conductor of the local monodromy representation of E at x (parent RD.6/swan-conductor-of-isocrystal, built on ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.3/swan-from-breaks for the equal-characteristic local field κ(x)((t))). In particular Irr_x(E) is a nonnegative integer.

**Hypotheses.**

- k perfect of characteristic p (k finite in the applications of RD.6).
- The local field κ(x)((t)) has finite residue field when k is finite, so Swan_x(E) itself lies in tier (F) of ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.3. The local comparison it is matched with (RD.2/matsuda-highest-break-theorem, Kedlaya overview Theorem 5.4.10) is proved after passing to an algebraically closed residue field, so this node uses R01.3 in its tier (P) even for finite k, and rests on that roadmap's recorded gap for perfect infinite residue fields.

**Proof outline.**

1. E ⊗ R_x is a (σ, ∇)-module over R_x, hence quasi-unipotent (RD.2/quasi-unipotence-and-local-monodromy-statement) with monodromy representation V_x (RD.2/monodromy-representation-equivalence), whose inertia action on the semisimplification has finite image: this is the local monodromy representation of RD.6/swan-conductor-of-isocrystal.
2. By RD.2/irregularity-equals-swan-conductor (a) (whose highest-break case is RD.2/matsuda-highest-break-theorem, Kedlaya overview Theorem 5.4.10 and Corollary 5.4.11) the differential break decomposition of E ⊗ R_x corresponds to the ramification break decomposition V_x = ⊕\_b V_x(b), with rank E(b) = dim V_x(b). This node adds only the identification of V_x with the local monodromy representation that parent RD.6/swan-conductor-of-isocrystal attaches to E at x.
3. Irr_x(E) = Σ\_b b·rank E(b) by definition of the differential Swan conductor as the sum of the breaks; Swan_x(E) = Σ\_b b·dim V_x(b) by ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.3/swan-from-breaks; the two sums agree term by term.
4. Integrality follows from ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.3/hasse-arf-integrality (compare RD.1/swan-conductor-integrality, the differential Hasse–Arf statement).

**Acceptance.**

- E unipotent at x (e.g. E = O): both sides are 0.
- E = L_{x^d} on A^1, p ∤ d, at ∞: Irr_∞ = d (Dwork module) and Swan_∞ = d (ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.3/artin-schreier-swan-conductor).
- E = K_a on G_m (RD.6/kummer-isocrystal): tame at 0 and ∞, so both sides vanish.
- Additivity check: for 0 → E' → E → E'' → 0 both sides are additive (RD.1/differential-swan-conductor and ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.3/swan-additive).

**Prerequisites.** `RD.2/irregularity-equals-swan-conductor`, `RD.2/matsuda-highest-break-theorem`, `RD.6/swan-conductor-of-isocrystal`, `RD.1/differential-swan-conductor`, `RD.1/swan-conductor-integrality`, `RD.2/quasi-unipotence-and-local-monodromy-statement`, `RD.2/monodromy-representation-equivalence`, `ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.3/swan-from-breaks`, `ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.3/hasse-arf-integrality`, `ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.3/swan-additive`, `ArithmeticGaloisRepresentations:R01.3/artin-schreier-swan-conductor`.

**Sources.** Kedlaya, *Fourier transforms and p-adic 'Weil II'*, Theorem 4.4.1, part (b) of the proof, p. 33: Cites Crew (Theorem 5.4), Matsuda (Theorem 8.6) and Tsuzuki (Theorem 7.2.2) for the equality of irregularity and Swan conductor at each point at infinity. Kedlaya, *Local monodromy of p-adic differential equations: an overview*, Corollary 5.4.11 and Remark 5.4.13, p. 36: Break decompositions match under the monodromy equivalence; the comparison of irregularity with the Swan conductor is attributed to Matsuda, Tsuzuki and Crew. Kedlaya, *Swan conductors for p-adic differential modules, I: A local construction*, Proposition 3.5.5 (p. 23, proof p. 24) and Remark 3.5.14 (p. 25): When ρ becomes unramified over a finite separable extension whose residue field is separable over k (always so for perfect k), the differential break multiset and Swan conductor computed with respect to the uniformizer agree with the usual ones (Proposition 3.5.5); the differential Swan conductor does not change when the coefficients are enlarged (Remark 3.5.14).

## B. Rank-one coefficients

Determinantal weights (`RD.6/determinantal-weights`) are weights of top exterior powers, so they need every rank-one coefficient to be ι-pure. Following Crew 1992 and Abe, the proof twists a rank-one F-isocrystal to slope 0, identifies it with a p-adic character with finite local monodromy, and applies Katz–Lang finiteness on the compactification. The constant F-lines L_c introduced here are also the twists used for weight normalisation and for slopes.

#### Constant rank-one F-isocrystals (twists)

`RD.6/constant-f-line` (definition)

**Statement.** Let X be a separated F_q-scheme of finite type with structure map f : X → Spec F_q, K with residue field F_q and σ\_K = id, and c ∈ K^×. The constant F-line L_c on X is f^\* of the rank-one object (K, c) on Spec F_q: the unit isocrystal O_X with Frobenius structure c·Φ\_O, where Φ\_O : F^\*O_X ≅ O_X is the canonical one. For E ∈ F-Isoc†(X/K) write E(c) := E ⊗ L_c (Abe's 'twist by χ'). Tate twists are the special case L_{q^{-n}} = O_X(n) (parent RD.6/tate-twist-of-f-isocrystals). For c in a finite extension K' of K, L_c is defined after extending scalars to K'.

**Hypotheses.**

- σ\_K = id (so the linear q-Frobenius is defined, parent RD.6/linear-q-frobenius); c ∈ K^× or in a finite extension K'.

**Proof outline.**

#### A rank-one F-isocrystal is isoclinic and a constant twist makes it unit-root

`RD.6/rank-one-isoclinic-twist` (lemma)

**Statement.** Let X be a connected smooth F_q-scheme and E a convergent or overconvergent F-isocrystal of rank 1 on X/K (σ\_K = id). There is s ∈ Q such that at every closed point x the eigenvalue of F_x has q-adic valuation v_q = v_p/v_p(q) equal to s·deg(x). Hence, after replacing K by a finite extension containing an element c of q-adic valuation s, E(c^{-1}) = E ⊗ L_{c^{-1}} is unit-root (slope 0 at every point).

**Hypotheses.**

- X connected and smooth over F_q (rank-one specialisation needs only connectedness and the constancy of the endpoint of the Newton polygon).

**Proof outline.**

1. Grothendieck specialisation: the Newton polygon at a specialisation lies above that at the generisation, with the same endpoint (RD.2/special-polygon-above-generic in the local form; Crew 1992 Proposition 1.2 globally). For rank 1 the polygon is a single segment whose endpoint is its slope, so the slope is the same at every point.
2. Choose c in a finite extension of K with v_q(c) = s (a root in K^alg of a suitable power of q, so a finite extension suffices); then F_x(E(c^{-1})) = F_x(E)·c^{-deg x} has valuation 0 (RD.6/constant-f-line).

**Acceptance.**

- The Tate object O(−1) has slope 1; O(−1)(q^{-1}) = O is unit-root.
- The Dwork isocrystal L_π is unit-root (Frobenius at points is a root of unity), s = 0.

**Prerequisites.** `RD.2/special-polygon-above-generic`, `RD.6/slope-interval-of-isocrystal`, `RD.6/constant-f-line`, `RD.1/isoclinic-frobenius-module`.

**Sources.** Crew, *F-isocrystals and their monodromy groups*, Proposition 1.2, p. 433: If the generic fibre of an F-isocrystal on a geometrically connected scheme is unit-root, so is every fibre, by specialisation of Newton polygons with fixed endpoint. Kedlaya, *Notes on isocrystals*, Remark 3.5, p. 6: Every rank-one F-isocrystal is isoclinic of a single slope. Abe, *Langlands program for p-adic coefficients and the petites camarades conjecture*, Proof of Lemma 6.1 (i), p. 7: Since E has rank 1 there is a twist making it unit-root.

#### Unit-root F-isocrystals and p-adic representations (Katz, Crew, Tsuzuki)

`RD.6/unit-root-isocrystals-and-p-adic-representations` (theorem)

**Statement.** Let X be a smooth geometrically connected F_q-scheme with K of residue field F_q and σ\_K = id, and fix a geometric point x̄. (a) (Katz, Crew; RD.3/crew-unit-root-correspondence with its Q_q-coefficients extended to K) There is an equivalence of tensor categories between continuous representations ρ : π\_1(X, x̄) → GL_n(K) (each stabilises an O_K-lattice, π\_1 being compact) and unit-root convergent F-isocrystals (RD.3/unit-root-f-isocrystal) on X/K; under it, at a closed point x the linear Frobenius F_x is conjugate to ρ(Frob_x) for the geometric Frobenius Frob_x. (b) (Tsuzuki) When X is a curve with smooth compactification X̄, a unit-root convergent F-isocrystal is overconvergent along X̄ − X if and only if ρ has finite local monodromy, i.e. ρ(I_y) is finite for the inertia group I_y at every y ∈ X̄ − X; for rank one this is due to Crew. The functor in (b) is fully faithful and its essential image is closed under subquotients.

**Hypotheses.**

- X smooth and geometrically connected over F_q; in (b) X is a curve.
- Inertia groups at the points at infinity and the arithmetic/geometric fundamental-group sequence are taken from InverseGaloisAndArithmeticFundamentalGroups:IG.1.

**Proof outline.**

1. (a) For K = Q_q this is RD.3/crew-unit-root-correspondence (k = F_q, q-power Frobenius structures, which are Q_q-linear): V ↦ (V ⊗ O, 1 ⊗ σ), with quasi-inverse the Frobenius-fixed horizontal sections (Katz; Crew 1992 Theorem 1.3). For a finite extension K of Q_q with σ\_K = id, apply it to the underlying Q_q-objects with their K-action, as in Kedlaya's Notes Theorem 9.4. Normalisation: over Spec F_q the quasi-inverse is V = (M ⊗ K̂^ur)^{F ⊗ σ = 1}, on which the arithmetic Frobenius acts as 1 ⊗ σ = F^{-1} ⊗ 1, so F_x is ρ of the geometric Frobenius; the cited sources do not state this normalisation, and the review checked it on this case.
2. (b) is local at the points at infinity: on the Robba ring at y, a unit-root (σ, ∇)-module over the bounded Robba ring has finite local monodromy by RD.2/tsuzuki-unit-root-finite-monodromy, and RD.2/tsuzuki-equivalence-finite-local-monodromy (b) identifies such modules with the representations of the local Galois group with finite local monodromy; conversely finite local monodromy means the module is quasi-constant at y, hence overconvergent by RD.2/quasi-unipotent-implies-overconvergent.
3. Full faithfulness of (b) follows from (a) since overconvergent morphisms are in particular convergent ones and the restriction functor is faithful (RD.3/full-faithfulness-overconvergent-to-convergent covers the unit-root case).

**Acceptance.**

- The trivial representation corresponds to O_X.
- The Artin–Schreier character of u^p − u = x on π\_1(A^1) (finite image, wildly ramified at ∞ with finite inertia image) corresponds to the Dwork isocrystal L_π, which is overconvergent.
- A p-adic character of π\_1(G_m) of infinite image on inertia at ∞ (e.g. from the Z_p-extension of Artin–Schreier–Witt type) gives a unit-root convergent F-isocrystal that is not overconvergent.

**Prerequisites.** `RD.3/overconvergent-f-isocrystal`, `RD.3/restriction-to-convergent`, `RD.3/full-faithfulness-overconvergent-to-convergent`, `RD.2/tsuzuki-unit-root-finite-monodromy`, `RD.2/quasi-unipotent-implies-overconvergent`, `RD.1/isoclinic-frobenius-module`, `InverseGaloisAndArithmeticFundamentalGroups:IG.1`, `RD.3/crew-unit-root-correspondence`, `RD.3/unit-root-f-isocrystal`, `RD.2/tsuzuki-equivalence-finite-local-monodromy`.

**Sources.** Crew, *F-isocrystals and their monodromy groups*, Theorems 1.3 and 1.4, p. 434: For X smooth geometrically connected, continuous p-adic representations of π\_1 are equivalent to unit-root F-isocrystals; for curves, representations with finite local monodromy embed fully faithfully into unit-root overconvergent F-isocrystals, and every rank-one unit-root object is in the essential image. Kedlaya, *Notes on isocrystals*, Theorems 3.7 and 3.9, pp. 6-7, and Theorem 9.4, p. 22: Katz–Crew equivalence for unit-root convergent F-isocrystals and Tsuzuki's characterisation of the overconvergent ones as the potentially unramified local systems; the version with coefficients in a finite extension of Q_p.

#### Katz–Lang finiteness of the geometric abelian fundamental group

`RD.6/katz-lang-geometric-finiteness` (theorem)

**Statement.** Let X̄ be a smooth proper geometrically connected scheme over a finite field F_q. Then the image of π\_1(X̄ ⊗ F̄\_q)^ab in π\_1(X̄)^ab is finite. In particular, every continuous character of π\_1(X̄) is of finite order on the geometric fundamental group.

**Hypotheses.**

- X̄ smooth, proper and geometrically connected over F_q (Katz–Lang allow the perfection of an absolutely finitely generated field).

**Proof outline.**

1. For a curve X̄ with Jacobian J (Tau Ceti JacobianChallenge, Layer F: Abel–Jacobi and its universal property), abelian étale covers of X̄ ⊗ F̄\_q are pulled back from isogenies onto J; a character of the arithmetic group π\_1(X̄) restricts on the geometric part to a Frobenius-invariant character, which factors through the Frobenius coinvariants of the geometric abelian group, and these are finite because 1 − Frobenius is an isogeny of J with kernel J(F_q) (Lang).
2. The general case is Katz–Lang, Theorem 1, as restated by Crew 1992 Theorem 1.6; the curve case above is the one used in RD.6/rank-one-finite-geometric-monodromy. The identification of geometric abelian covers with isogenies of the Jacobian (geometric class field theory) is not planned by any roadmap of tier at most 12 and is recorded as a gap of this packet.

**Acceptance.**

- For X̄ = P^1, π\_1(P^1 ⊗ F̄\_q) is trivial and the image is trivial.
- For an elliptic curve E/F_q the image of the geometric abelian fundamental group in π\_1(E)^ab is isomorphic to E(F_q) (the kernel of Lang's isogeny 1 − Frobenius), a finite group of order q + 1 − a_q.

**Prerequisites.** `InverseGaloisAndArithmeticFundamentalGroups:IG.1`, `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/JacobianChallenge#layer-f-abeljacobi-and-the-universal-property`.

**Sources.** Crew, *F-isocrystals and their monodromy groups*, Theorem 1.6 and the proof of Corollary 1.5, pp. 434-435: Restates Katz–Lang: for smooth proper X_0 over an absolutely finitely generated field the image of the geometric abelian fundamental group in the arithmetic one is finite, and reduces the perfect-field case to it. Abe, *Langlands program for p-adic coefficients and the petites camarades conjecture*, Proof of Lemma 6.1 (i), p. 7: Uses Katz–Lang Theorem 2 in place of class field theory on a smooth compactification with normal crossings boundary.

#### Rank-one overconvergent F-isocrystals have finite order up to a twist

`RD.6/rank-one-finite-geometric-monodromy` (theorem — planet: *Finite monodromy of rank-one F-isocrystals*)

**Statement.** Let X be a smooth geometrically connected curve over F_q and E ∈ F-Isoc†(X/K) of rank 1. After a finite extension of K there are c and n ≥ 1 with E(c)^{⊗n} ≅ O_X (RD.6/constant-f-line). Equivalently, the geometric monodromy group of E (RD.6/coefficient-category-is-neutral-tannakian) is finite, contained in μ\_n, and for every closed point x, F_x(E)^n = c^{−n·deg x}. For an arbitrary scheme X of finite type over F_q the same holds (Abe, Lemma 6.1 (i)); that extension uses de Jong alterations, Shiho's logarithmic extension and Berthelot's restriction to a dense open, and is recorded here as an imported statement whose inputs are not planned in this packet.

**Hypotheses.**

- X a smooth geometrically connected curve over F_q (for the proved case); E of rank 1 with Frobenius structure.

**Proof outline.**

1. By RD.6/rank-one-isoclinic-twist there is a constant c_1 with E(c_1) unit-root.
2. By RD.6/unit-root-isocrystals-and-p-adic-representations, E(c_1) corresponds to a character ρ : π\_1(X) → O_{K'}^× with finite local monodromy at each point of X̄ − X; let m be the lcm of the orders of the inertia images, so ρ^m is unramified and extends to π\_1(X̄).
3. By RD.6/katz-lang-geometric-finiteness, ρ^m has finite order N on the geometric fundamental group; the restriction of ρ^{mN} to the arithmetic group factors through Gal(F̄\_q/F_q) = Ẑ, i.e. it is the character of a constant F-line L_d.
4. Hence E(c_1)^{⊗mN} ≅ L_d and E(c_1 d^{−1/(mN)})^{⊗mN} ≅ O_X after adjoining an mN-th root of d to K; the fibre formula follows from RD.6/constant-f-line.

**Acceptance.**

- L_π on A^1: L_π^{⊗p} ≅ O_X, n = p, c = 1.
- K_a on G_m with a = b/(q − 1): K_a^{⊗(q−1)} ≅ O, c = 1.
- O(−1) = L_q: twist by c = q^{-1}, n = 1.

**Prerequisites.** `RD.6/rank-one-isoclinic-twist`, `RD.6/unit-root-isocrystals-and-p-adic-representations`, `RD.6/katz-lang-geometric-finiteness`, `RD.6/constant-f-line`, `RD.3/overconvergent-f-isocrystal`, `RD.6/coefficient-category-is-neutral-tannakian`.

**Sources.** Crew, *F-isocrystals and their monodromy groups*, Corollary 1.5 and its proof, pp. 434-435: For a smooth geometrically connected curve over the perfection of an absolutely finitely generated field, some tensor power of a rank-one unit-root overconvergent F-isocrystal is constant, via the character with finite local monodromy and Katz–Lang. Abe, *Langlands program for p-adic coefficients and the petites camarades conjecture*, Lemma 6.1 (i) and proof, p. 7: For X of finite type, a rank-one overconvergent F-isocrystal has a twist with a trivial tensor power; proof via Crew's argument on a smooth compactification with normal crossings, an alteration and restriction to a dense open. Kedlaya, *Notes on isocrystals*, Lemma 9.14, p. 25: A twist of the determinant has finite order; the reference for curves is Crew, for general X Abe. Kedlaya, *Fourier transforms and p-adic 'Weil II'*, Proposition 6.1.2 and Corollary 6.1.3, p. 41: Proposition 6.1.2 (a special case of Tsuzuki's Proposition 7.2.1; for curves due to Crew, with a finite étale cover): a rank-one overconvergent F-isocrystal on a smooth variety becomes constant after pullback along a proper dominant generically finite étale morphism. Corollary 6.1.3 deduces that some tensor power is constant on the variety itself.

#### Rank-one overconvergent F-isocrystals are ι-pure

`RD.6/rank-one-purity-via-curves` (lemma)

**Statement.** Let X be a smooth irreducible F_q-scheme, E ∈ F-Isoc†(X/K) of rank 1 and ι : K^alg → C a field embedding. Then E is ι-pure of a single weight w: |ι(F_x(E))| = q^{w·deg(x)/2} for all closed points x. If X is a geometrically connected curve, w = −2·log_q|ι(c)| for the twist c of RD.6/rank-one-finite-geometric-monodromy.

**Hypotheses.**

- X smooth irreducible over F_q; ι arbitrary.

**Proof outline.**

1. Curves: by RD.6/rank-one-finite-geometric-monodromy, F_x(E)^n = c^{−n deg x}, so |ι(F_x(E))| = |ι(c)|^{−deg x} for all x.
2. General X: by Abe's Lemma 6.1 (i) (imported in RD.6/rank-one-finite-geometric-monodromy) the same identity holds on X. Alternatively, through any closed point x pass a smooth curve i : C → X (Abe, proof of Corollary 6.3), and the weights at x and at a fixed base point agree once a single irreducible curve contains both; the statement in all dimensions is taken from Abe.
3. If X is irreducible but not geometrically connected, with constant field F_{q^h}, apply the curve argument over F_{q^h}; every degree is a multiple of h and the weight is unchanged (parent RD.6/rank-one-isocrystals-are-pure, second step).

**Acceptance.**

- L_π: |ι(ψ(Tr x))| = 1, weight 0.
- O(−1): weight 2.
- K_a: weight 0.

## C. Tannakian input

The global monodromy group G_M of the first part is the automorphism group of the fibre functor at a rational point on the tensor category generated by M without its Frobenius structure. Its existence is the neutral Tannakian recognition theorem, planned here because no roadmap this one may cite plans it; the Tau Ceti library already has finite comodules with their symmetric and rigid monoidal structure and the reconstruction of an affine group scheme from them (`TauCeti.FGComoduleCat`, `TauCeti.Tannaka.pointsFunctorIsoTensorAutFunctor`), which the proof uses, but not recognition itself.

#### Neutral Tannakian recognition theorem

`RD.6/neutral-tannakian-recognition` (theorem — planet: *Neutral Tannakian recognition theorem*)

**Statement.** Let K be a field, (C, ⊗) a K-linear rigid abelian symmetric monoidal category with End(1) = K, and ω : C → Vec_K an exact faithful K-linear symmetric monoidal functor (a fibre functor; (C, ω) is a neutral Tannakian category). Then (a) the functor on commutative K-algebras S ↦ Aut^⊗(ω ⊗ S) is represented by an affine group scheme G = Spec(B) over K; (b) ω lifts to an equivalence of symmetric monoidal categories C ≃ Rep_K(G) compatible with the fibre functors, where Rep_K(G) is the category of finite-dimensional comodules over B (Tau Ceti's FGComoduleCat K B); (c) if C has a tensor generator X (every object is a subquotient of a finite sum of X^{⊗a} ⊗ (X^∨)^{⊗b}), then G is of finite type and ω(X) is a faithful representation, so G is a closed subgroup of GL(ω(X)).

**Hypotheses.**

- K any field; C rigid abelian with End(1) = K; ω exact, faithful, K-linear and symmetric monoidal.
- This general notion moves down here from MotivesAndAlgebraicCycles:MC.6, which lies outside the roadmaps this roadmap may cite. The pinned Tau Ceti library already has finite comodules over a coalgebra (TauCeti.FGComoduleCat), their symmetric and rigid monoidal structure over a commutative Hopf algebra over a field (TauCeti.FGComoduleCat.instSymmetricCategory, TauCeti.FGComoduleCat.instRigidCategory) and Tannakian reconstruction (TauCeti.Tannaka.pointsFunctorIsoTensorAutFunctor: the points of a commutative Hopf algebra over a field are the tensor automorphisms of scalar extension on its finite comodules, naturally in the algebra). Recognition, that an abstract neutral Tannakian category is such a comodule category, is in neither the library nor TauCetiRoadmap's ReductiveGroups Layer 1.

**Proof outline.**

1. Construct the coalgebra B = colim over finite subcategories ⟨X⟩ of the duals of the finite-dimensional algebras End(ω|⟨X⟩), without using ⊗: ω identifies ⟨X⟩ with modules over End(ω|⟨X⟩), hence with comodules over its dual (Deligne–Milne Lemma 2.13), and C with comodules over B (Proposition 2.14); finite-dimensional comodules are Tau Ceti's FGComoduleCat K B.
2. The tensor structure makes B a commutative bialgebra (Deligne–Milne Proposition 2.16; commutativity from the symmetry); rigidity of C gives the antipode, so G = Spec B is an affine group scheme. Its points represent Aut^⊗(ω ⊗ S): this is Tau Ceti's reconstruction TauCeti.Tannaka.pointsFunctorIsoTensorAutFunctor applied to the commutative Hopf algebra B, transported along C ≃ FGComoduleCat K B.
3. (c) is Deligne–Milne Proposition 2.20 (b): a tensor generator gives a finitely generated Hopf algebra, and the representation on ω(X) is faithful (Proposition 2.21 (b) for the closed-immersion criterion).

**Acceptance.**

- C = FGComoduleCat K H for a commutative Hopf algebra H, with the forgetful functor, recovers G = Spec H (TauCeti.Tannaka.pointsFunctorIsoTensorAutFunctor).
- For a finite group Γ, Rep_K(Γ) with the forgetful functor gives the constant group scheme Γ (compare Mathlib's Tannaka duality for finite groups, TannakaDuality.FiniteGroup).
- The category of finite-dimensional K-vector spaces with a nilpotent endomorphism (char K = 0) gives G_a.

**Prerequisites.** `tauceti:TauCeti.FGComoduleCat`, `tauceti:TauCeti.FGComoduleCat.instSymmetricCategory`, `tauceti:TauCeti.FGComoduleCat.instRigidCategory`, `tauceti:TauCeti.Tannaka.pointsFunctorIsoTensorAutFunctor`, `mathlib:CategoryTheory.MonoidalCategory`, `mathlib:CategoryTheory.RightRigidCategory`, `mathlib:FDRep`.

**Sources.** Deligne et al., *Tannakian categories*, Theorem 2.11, p. 21, with Lemma 2.13 and Propositions 2.14 and 2.16, pp. 22-24: For a rigid abelian tensor category with End(1) = k and an exact faithful k-linear tensor functor to vector spaces, the automorphism functor of the fibre functor is represented by an affine group scheme and the category is equivalent to its representations. The proof identifies each finitely generated subcategory with modules over a finite-dimensional algebra, the whole category with comodules over the colimit coalgebra, and tensor structures with multiplications on it. Deligne et al., *Tannakian categories*, Definition 2.19 and Propositions 2.20-2.21, p. 25: Neutral Tannakian categories; G is algebraic iff the category has a tensor generator; criteria for faithfully flat and closed-immersion homomorphisms.

#### The tensor category generated by an overconvergent isocrystal is neutral Tannakian

`RD.6/coefficient-category-is-neutral-tannakian` (lemma)

**Statement.** Let X be a smooth geometrically connected F_q-scheme (a curve, or more generally smooth), x ∈ X(F_q), and M ∈ Isoc†(X/K) (no Frobenius). Let ⟨M⟩ be the full subcategory of Isoc†(X/K) of objects isomorphic to subquotients of finite direct sums of M^{⊗a} ⊗ (M^∨)^{⊗b}, and ω\_x : ⟨M⟩ → Vec_K the fibre at x. Then (⟨M⟩, ⊗, ω\_x) is a neutral Tannakian category over K with tensor generator M, so by RD.6/neutral-tannakian-recognition its group DGal(M, x) := Aut^⊗(ω\_x) is a closed algebraic subgroup of GL(M_x) and ⟨M⟩ ≃ Rep_K(DGal(M, x)). Under this equivalence, invariants of DGal(M, x) on N_x are H^0_rig(X/K, N) (the largest constant subobject), and for X a smooth affine curve and N quasi-unipotent, coinvariants on N_x are H^2_{c,rig}(X/K, N)(1) (Crew 1998 Corollary 9.6 and the remark after it). This supplies the Tannakian step of parent RD.6/global-monodromy-group.

**Hypotheses.**

- X smooth and geometrically connected with a rational point x; Isoc†(X/K) is abelian with the usual tensor structure and duals (RD.3).
- For the coinvariant description, X a smooth affine curve (Crew's setting) and N quasi-unipotent, hence strict (Crew 1998 Proposition 10.2; true when N underlies an F-isocrystal, RD.2).

**Proof outline.**

1. Isoc†(X/K) is a K-linear rigid abelian tensor category: kernels and cokernels of horizontal maps of locally free modules with connection on a smooth lift are locally free (RD.3/overconvergent-isocrystal), duals and internal Hom exist; ⟨M⟩ is a rigid abelian tensor subcategory.
2. End(1) = H^0_rig(X/K, O_X) = K because X is geometrically connected (RD.4/rigid-cohomology).
3. ω\_x is exact and faithful: pullback to a point with values in a perfect field is exact, and a horizontal map vanishing at one point of a connected X vanishes (Crew 1992, statement after 1.1.2 and 1.9).
4. Apply RD.6/neutral-tannakian-recognition (c) with tensor generator M ⊕ M^∨.

## D. Coefficients over finite fields and conventions

The Dwork and Kummer F-isocrystals are the rank-one coefficients whose trace functions are additive and multiplicative characters. The literature uses opposite signs and directions for their Frobenius structures; this part fixes one convention and records the dictionary, so that trace functions, Fourier normalisations and Kloosterman sums are unambiguous. The finite-field arithmetic of closed points and zeta functions, and descent of rationality, complete the L-function nodes of the first part.

#### Dwork's additive character attached to π

`RD.6/dwork-additive-character` (definition)

**Statement.** Let p be odd, K a field of characteristic 0 complete for a p-adic absolute value and containing π with π^{p−1} = −p, and θ(t) = exp(π(t − t^p)) Dwork's splitting function (an analytic unit on |t| < p^{(p−1)/p^2}, RD.1/dwork-overconvergence-proof, so θ(1) is defined). Then ζ\_π := θ(1) is a primitive p-th root of unity with ζ\_π ≡ 1 + π mod π^2, and the Dwork character is the additive character ψ\_π : F_p → K^×, ψ\_π(a) := ζ\_π^a. For a finite field F_{q'} of characteristic p, ψ\_{π,q'} := ψ\_π ∘ Tr_{F_{q'}/F_p}. Conversely every nontrivial additive character F_p → K^× is ψ\_π for exactly one root π of π^{p−1} = −p (the bijection between these roots and primitive p-th roots of unity), and ψ\_{−π} = ψ\_π^{-1}.

**Hypotheses.**

- p odd (for p = 2 the root −π is π times a unit and the dictionary ψ\_{−π} = ψ\_π^{-1} needs separate care; Crew 1994 and Fresán–Sabbah–Yu take p odd in the rigid setting).
- π ∈ K with π^{p−1} = −p.

**Proof outline.**

1. θ(1) is a p-th root of unity: θ(t)^p = exp(pπt)·exp(−pπt^p) as power series, both factors converge on the closed unit disc because |pπ| < p^{−1/(p−1)}, so θ(1)^p = exp(pπ)·exp(−pπ) = 1; the congruence θ(1) ≡ 1 + π mod π^2 comes from the terms of degree at most one in π of the coefficients of θ (parent RD.6/dwork-isocrystal, acceptance computation for p = 3), and shows θ(1) ≠ 1.
2. ψ\_π(a) := ζ\_π^a is the Mathlib character AddChar.zmodChar attached to the primitive root ζ\_π, composed with the trace Algebra.trace (F_{q'} over ZMod p) for extensions.
3. Uniqueness: ζ ≡ 1 + π mod π^2 determines ζ among the primitive p-th roots of unity, since ζ\_π^a ≡ 1 + aπ mod π^2.
4. For p odd, (−π)^{p−1} = −p, and θ\_{−π}(1) = θ\_π(1)^{-1}, so ψ\_{−π} = ψ\_π^{-1}.

#### The Kummer F-isocrystal K_a on G_m

`RD.6/kummer-isocrystal` (construction — planet: *Kummer F-isocrystal*)

**Statement.** Let q = p^s, K with residue field F_q and σ\_K = id, and a ∈ (q − 1)^{-1}Z, a = b/(q − 1). The Kummer F-isocrystal K_a on G_m = Spec F_q\[t, t^{-1}] is the rank-one (σ, ∇)-module over K⟨t, t^{-1}⟩† with σ(t) = t^q, generator e, connection ∇e = a·e ⊗ dt/t and Frobenius structure (pullback to module) F(σ^\*e) = t^{(q−1)a}·e = t^b·e. Its linear Frobenius at x_0 ∈ F_{q^r}^× (Teichmüller lift x̃\_0) is χ\_a(N_{F_{q^r}/F_q}(x_0)), where χ\_a : F_q^× → μ\_{q−1}(K) is u ↦ ũ^b (a power of the Teichmüller character). The underlying overconvergent isocrystal ∇e = a e ⊗ dt/t, its tensor rules, its isomorphism criterion (K_a ≅ K_b iff a − b ∈ Z) and its p-power Frobenius structure t^{(p−1)a}, which exists when (p − 1)a ∈ Z, are RD.3/kummer-isocrystal; this node adds the q-power Frobenius structure over F_q, which exists for the larger set a ∈ (q − 1)^{-1}Z, and its trace function. Variant conventions are translated by RD.6/dwork-kummer-sign-dictionary.

**Hypotheses.**

- σ\_K = id, K ⊇ W(F_q)\[1/p], so the Teichmüller lifts of F_q^× lie in K.
- a ∈ (q − 1)^{-1}Z; only for such a does the connection admit a Frobenius structure (Xu–Zhu §2.1.5 (ii)).

**Proof outline.**

1. Horizontality: with ∇e = a e dt/t and σ^\*(dt/t) = q dt/t, the condition ∇(t^b e) = t^b·(q a) e dt/t reads b + a = q a, i.e. b = (q − 1)a.
2. Overconvergence of the underlying isocrystal is RD.3/kummer-isocrystal; the Frobenius multiplier t^b and its inverse t^{−b} are units of K⟨t, t^{-1}⟩†.
3. Fibres: at the Teichmüller point x̃\_0 with x̃\_0^{q^r} = x̃\_0, the composite of the r Frobenius pullbacks multiplies by ∏\_{j<r} x̃\_0^{q^j b} = x̃\_0^{b(q^r−1)/(q−1)} = (N x_0)~^b, the Teichmüller lift of the norm raised to b (parent RD.6/frobenius-at-closed-point).
4. K_a ⊗ K_{a'} ≅ K_{a+a'} on generators (RD.3/kummer-isocrystal for the underlying isocrystals; the multipliers multiply); for a ∈ Z the section t^{−a}e is horizontal and Frobenius-fixed, so K_a ≅ O; hence K_a depends only on a mod Z and K_a^{⊗(q−1)} ≅ O.

#### Sign and direction conventions for Dwork and Kummer F-isocrystals

`RD.6/dwork-kummer-sign-dictionary` (lemma)

**Statement.** Fix the convention of this roadmap: a Frobenius structure is an isomorphism F : σ^\*E → E (pullback to module), and the linear q-Frobenius at a closed point is the one of parent RD.6/frobenius-at-closed-point, playing the role of geometric Frobenius (O(−1) has F = q). Let ε ∈ {+1, −1}. (i) On A^1 with σ(x) = x^q, the rank-one module ∇e = επ·e ⊗ dx admits a Frobenius structure F(σ^\*e) = θ\_ε·e with θ\_ε(0) = 1 iff θ\_ε = exp(επ(x^q − x)); the inverse map (module to pullback) is multiplication by exp(επ(x − x^q)). (ii) Its linear Frobenius at x_0 ∈ F_{q^r} is ψ\_{π,q^r}(x_0)^{−ε} (RD.6/dwork-additive-character); the parent's L_π is ε = +1, so its trace function is ψ\_{π}^{-1} ∘ Tr = ψ\_{−π} ∘ Tr. (iii) On G_m, ∇e = ε a e ⊗ dt/t has Frobenius structure F(σ^\*e) = t^{ε(q−1)a} e and trace χ\_a(N x_0)^ε. (iv) Dictionary: Kedlaya's Weil II §4.3 pairs ∇e = πe dx with the multiplier exp(πx − πx^q), which is horizontal only for the opposite direction; Xu–Zhu §2.1.5 pair ∇ = d + π dt with θ\_π = exp(π(t − t^p)) and ∇ = d − a dt/t with t^{(q−1)a}, both consistent only for the module-to-pullback direction, and (ii) prints p for q in the pulled-back connection; their stated trace functions ψ ∘ Tr and χ ∘ Norm are those of the printed multipliers read in the module-to-pullback direction (ε = −1 for the connections as printed). Crew 1994 §1.5 and Fresán–Sabbah–Yu §3.2.2 use ∇ = d + π dz with exp(π(z^p − z)), which is this roadmap's convention, so their Kloosterman traces involve ψ\_π^{-1} (Crew's (1.5.1) prints ψ and omits the sign (−1)^{n−1}; see sourceIssues); Abe–Marmora §3.2.1 use ∇e = −πe ⊗ dt (ε = −1).

**Hypotheses.**

- p odd for the identity ψ\_{−π} = ψ\_π^{-1}; σ(x) = x^q the standard Frobenius lift; K ∋ π.

**Proof outline.**

1. (i) Horizontality of F(σ^\*e) = θ e with ∇e = επ e dx and σ^\*(dx) = q x^{q−1} dx: θ' + επθ = επ q x^{q−1} θ, so θ'/θ = επ(q x^{q−1} − 1) and θ = exp(επ(x^q − x)) given θ(0) = 1; overconvergence of θ follows from Dwork's splitting function (parent RD.6/dwork-isocrystal).
2. (ii) Write q = p^s, so exp(επ(x^q − x)) = ∏\_{i<s} θ(x^{p^i})^{−ε} with θ(t) = exp(π(t − t^p)). At the Teichmüller lift x̃\_0 of x_0 ∈ F_{q^r} the r-fold composite of Frobenius pullbacks multiplies by ∏\_{j<rs} θ(x̃\_0^{p^j})^{−ε}, and Dwork's identity ∏\_{j<rs} θ(x̃\_0^{p^j}) = θ(1)^{Tr_{F_{q^r}/F_p}(x_0)} (Xu–Zhu §2.1.5 (i), citing Dwork) gives ψ\_π(Tr x_0)^{−ε}.
3. (iii) is the computation of RD.6/kummer-isocrystal with a replaced by εa.
4. (iv) Substitute each printed pair into the horizontality equation of (i) or (iii).

**Acceptance.**

- Parent convention: L_π (ε = 1) has Σ\_{x_0 ∈ F_q} Tr(F_{x_0}) = Σ ψ\_π(x_0)^{-1} = 0.
- For p = 3, q = 3: the trace of L_π at x_0 = 1 is ψ\_π(1)^{-1} = ψ\_{−π}(1).
- The Kloosterman F-isocrystal of Crew 1994 and Fresán–Sabbah–Yu has the trace function of the ℓ-adic Kloosterman sheaf for the character ψ\_π^{-1}, given the ℓ-adic convention that geometric Frobenius acts on L_ψ by ψ(Tr).

#### Closed points, point counts and the zeta function over a finite field

`RD.6/closed-points-and-zeta-function` (lemma)

**Statement.** Let X be a separated scheme of finite type over F_q. (i) For each d ≥ 1 the set of closed points of X of degree d (residue field F_{q^d}) is finite, of cardinality a_d(X). (ii) For every r ≥ 1, #X(F_{q^r}) = Σ\_{d | r} d·a_d(X). (iii) The zeta function Z(X, t) := exp(Σ\_{r≥1} #X(F_{q^r}) t^r / r) equals ∏\_{x ∈ |X|} (1 − t^{deg x})^{−1}, hence lies in 1 + tZ\[\[t]]. (iv) Z(X, t) = L(X, O_X, t) for the L-function of parent RD.6/l-function-of-overconvergent-f-isocrystal, and Z is multiplicative for a closed subscheme and its open complement. This supplies the 'local finite-field zeta arithmetic' recorded as a gap by the parent L-function, trace-formula and rationality nodes.

**Hypotheses.**

- X separated of finite type over F_q.

**Proof outline.**

1. (i) Closed points have finite residue fields (SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.0/jacobson-finite-type); a point of degree d is a Frobenius orbit of size d in X(F_{q^d}), which is finite since X has a finite affine cover by subschemes of affine spaces over F_q.
2. (ii) An F_{q^r}-point is a closed point x with an F_q-embedding κ(x) → F_{q^r}; such embeddings exist iff deg x | r and there are exactly deg x of them.
3. (iii) Take the logarithmic derivative: t·(d/dt) log ∏\_x (1 − t^{deg x})^{−1} = Σ\_x Σ\_{m≥1} deg(x) t^{m·deg x} = Σ\_r (Σ\_{d|r} d a_d) t^r; each factor is in 1 + tZ\[\[t]] and only finitely many have a given lowest degree.
4. (iv) For E = O_X every F_x = 1 and det(1 − t^{deg x}F_x | E_x) = 1 − t^{deg x}; closed points of X are those of Z and of U.

**Acceptance.**

- X = A^1: #A^1(F_{q^r}) = q^r, Z = (1 − qt)^{-1}.
- X = Spec F_{q^2} over F_q: a_2 = 1, #X(F_q) = 0, #X(F_{q^2}) = 2, Z = (1 − t^2)^{-1}.
- X = P^1: Z = 1/((1 − t)(1 − qt)).

**Prerequisites.** `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.0/jacobson-finite-type`, `RD.6/l-function-of-overconvergent-f-isocrystal`, `mathlib:PowerSeries.mk`, `mathlib:PowerSeries.exp`.

**Sources.** Kedlaya, *Notes on isocrystals*, Definition 9.5 and Remark 9.7, p. 22: The L-function as a product over closed points of reciprocal characteristic polynomials in T^{deg x}, and its cohomological expression. #### Rationality of a power series descends along field extensions

`RD.6/rational-descent-of-power-series` (lemma)

**Statement.** Let k ⊆ L be fields and f ∈ k\[\[t]]. If f = P/Q in L\[\[t]] with P, Q ∈ L\[t] and Q(0) = 1, then f = P_0/Q_0 with P_0, Q_0 ∈ k\[t], Q_0(0) = 1. In particular, an L-function or zeta function with rational coefficients that the trace formula expresses as a rational function over K is a rational function over Q.

**Hypotheses.**

- k ⊆ L fields.

**Proof outline.**

1. By ClassicalArithmeticCompletion:CA.6/kronecker-hankel-rationality, f is rational over a field iff its Hankel determinants D_n(f) vanish for all large n.
2. The Hankel determinants of f are polynomials in the coefficients of f, hence the same whether computed in k or L; so rationality over L implies rationality over k.

**Acceptance.**

- f = Σ\_{r≥0} (1 + q^r) t^r ∈ Q\[\[t]] is (2 − (1 + q)t)/((1 − t)(1 − qt)), with D_n(f) = 0 for n ≥ 3 in the convention of ClassicalArithmeticCompletion:CA.6/shifted-hankel-determinant (D_n the n × n determinant of the coefficients a_{i+j+1}); here D_1 = 1 + q and D_2 = q(q − 1)^2 ≠ 0.
- f = exp(t) ∈ Q\[\[t]] is not rational over any field containing Q: its Hankel determinants never vanish.

## E. Kloosterman coefficients and middle cohomology

Fresán–Sabbah–Yu compute the rigid cohomology of symmetric powers of the Kloosterman F-isocrystal to realise symmetric power moments of Kloosterman sums p-adically. The rigid side is planned here: the coefficient, its local monodromy and purity, purity of middle cohomology for any pure coefficient, and the identification of middle cohomology of Sym^k Kl_{n+1} with weight-graded cohomology of a torus and of the hypersurface 𝒦. The comparison of this Frobenius module with D_st and D_crys of p-adic Galois representations, and its dimension formula, are RD.7 targets.

#### Middle rigid cohomology of a pure coefficient is pure

`RD.6/middle-cohomology-is-pure` (theorem)

**Statement.** Let X be a smooth separated F_q-scheme of pure dimension d, ι : K^alg → C a field embedding, and E ∈ F-Isoc†(X/K) ι-realizable and ι-pure of weight w. The middle cohomology H^i_mid(X/K, E) := image of the forget-supports map H^i_{c,rig}(X/K, E) → H^i_rig(X/K, E) is ι-pure of weight w + i. If X is a curve and the local invariants H^0_loc(X/K, E) are ι-mixed of weight ≤ w, then H^1_mid(X/K, E) ≅ gr^W_{w+1} H^1_{c,rig}(X/K, E), the quotient of H^1_{c,rig} by its part of weights ≤ w.

**Hypotheses.**

- X smooth of pure dimension d; E ι-realizable and ι-pure of weight w (for the real-weight normalisation see RD.6/weight-normalising-constant-line).
- For the curve statement, the local invariants at the points at infinity are ι-mixed of weight ≤ w (RD.6/local-monodromy-weight-purity gives this for every ι-pure E).

**Proof outline.**

1. H^i_{c,rig}(X/K, E) is ι-mixed of weight ≤ w + i and H^i_rig(X/K, E) is ι-mixed of weight ≥ w + i (parent RD.6/p-adic-weil-ii (a) and (b)).
2. The image of a Frobenius-equivariant map is a quotient of the first and a subobject of the second, hence has all ι-weights both ≤ w + i and ≥ w + i (DeligneWeightsAndPurity:DWP.0/purity-under-subquotients-and-extensions).
3. Curves: the exact sequence H^0_loc(E) → H^1_c(E) → H^1(E) of (2.6.1) (parent RD.6/gysin-sequence-for-curves, RD.5/crew-finiteness-and-duality-on-curves) shows the kernel of H^1_c → H^1 is a quotient of H^0_loc, of weights ≤ w, while H^1_mid is pure of weight w + 1; so the kernel is exactly the part of weight ≤ w and H^1_mid is gr^W_{w+1}.

**Acceptance.**

- X = G_m, E = O: H^1_c = K (weight 0), H^1 = K(−1) (weight 2), the forget-supports map is zero and H^1_mid = 0, consistent with purity of weight 1.
- X = E − {O} for an elliptic curve E: H^1_mid = H^1(E), pure of weight 1 (parent RD.6/weights-of-h1-of-affine-curve).

**Prerequisites.** `RD.6/p-adic-weil-ii`, `RD.6/pointwise-iota-weights`, `RD.6/gysin-sequence-for-curves`, `RD.5/crew-finiteness-and-duality-on-curves`, `RD.4/forget-supports-map`, `DeligneWeightsAndPurity:DWP.0/purity-under-subquotients-and-extensions`, `DeligneWeightsAndPurity:DWP.0/iota-weight`.

**Sources.** Fresán et al., *Hodge theory of Kloosterman connections*, Section 3.2.1, pp. 23-24: In the ℓ-adic case, the image of compactly supported cohomology in ordinary cohomology of Sym^k Kl is identified with gr^W of compactly supported cohomology, using that the local inertia invariants have weights at most kn. Fresán et al., *Hodge theory of Kloosterman connections*, Section 3.2.2, (3.14), p. 25: The rigid analogue, using Kedlaya's weight results for rigid cohomology and the local contributions at 0 and ∞. Kedlaya, *Fourier transforms and p-adic 'Weil II'*, Theorem 6.6.2, pp. 50-51: Weights ≤ w + i on compact supports and ≥ w + i on ordinary cohomology of smooth X.

#### The Kloosterman F-isocrystal Kl_{n+1}

`RD.6/kloosterman-f-isocrystal` (construction — planet: *Kloosterman F-isocrystal*)

**Statement.** Let p be odd, q a power of p, K = W(F_q)\[1/p](π) with π^{p−1} = −p and σ\_K = id, and n ≥ 1 with p ∤ n + 1. Let f : G_m^{n+1} → A^1, f(z, x_1, …, x_n) = x_1 + ⋯ + x_n + z/(x_1⋯x_n), and pr : G_m^{n+1} → G_m the projection to z. The Kloosterman F-isocrystal is Kl_{n+1} := R^n pr_{rig,\*}(f^\*L_π), the relative rigid cohomology in degree n of the Dwork isocrystal L_π (parent RD.6/dwork-isocrystal) pulled back along f, with the Frobenius structure induced by functoriality. It is an overconvergent F-isocrystal of rank n + 1 on G_m, the other relative cohomology groups vanish, and over the dagger algebra K⟨z, z^{-1}⟩† its connection is the Dwork–Sperber hypergeometric connection (Crew 1994 (1.1.5)). Its linear Frobenius at z_0 ∈ F_{q^r}^× has trace (−1)^n Kl_{n+1}(z_0; q^r), where Kl_{n+1}(z_0; q^r) := Σ\_{x_1⋯x_{n+1} = z_0} ψ(Tr(x_1 + ⋯ + x_{n+1})) with ψ = ψ\_π^{-1} (RD.6/dwork-kummer-sign-dictionary).

**Hypotheses.**

- p odd and p ∤ n + 1 (Crew's hypotheses for the explicit connection); the geometric construction is valid for every p, but this packet plans only the case covered by its sources.
- Relative rigid cohomology along pr is an overconvergent F-isocrystal on all of G_m here, which is more than the generic-pushforward theorem (RD.5/generic-pushforward-theorem) gives; this is Crew 1994 §1.5 (citing Sperber §6 and Berthelot), recorded as an imported input.

**Proof outline.**

1. Sperber's Dwork-theoretic computation identifies the relative cohomology with the free module of rank n + 1 carrying the hypergeometric connection (Crew 1994 §1.5, citing Sperber §6); Crew 1994 Proposition 1.2 shows that connection is overconvergent.
2. The Frobenius structure is induced from that of L_π by functoriality of relative rigid cohomology (Crew 1994 §1.5, citing Berthelot).
3. Traces: the fibre at z_0 is H^n_rig of pr^{-1}(z_0) ≅ G_m^n with coefficient f^\*L_π, which agrees with H^n_{c,rig} there, all other degrees vanishing (base change to fibres and this forget-supports isomorphism are part of the imported input); the parent RD.6/lefschetz-trace-formula on the fibre together with the trace function of L_π (RD.6/dwork-kummer-sign-dictionary) then gives the Kloosterman sum with the sign (−1)^n.

#### Local monodromy and purity of the Kloosterman F-isocrystal

`RD.6/kloosterman-local-monodromy-and-purity` (theorem)

**Statement.** In the setting of RD.6/kloosterman-f-isocrystal: (a) Kl_{n+1} is unipotent at 0 with nilpotent residue a single Jordan block of size n + 1; (b) at ∞ it is totally wild: its local monodromy is irreducible of rank n + 1 with all breaks equal to 1/(n + 1), so Irr_∞ = Swan_∞ = 1; (c) H^0_rig(G_m, Kl_{n+1}) = 0 and dim H^1_rig(G_m, Kl_{n+1}) = 1; (d) Kl_{n+1} is ι-pure of weight n for every ι : K^alg → C, i.e. every eigenvalue of F_{z_0} is a Weil q^{deg z_0}-number of weight n.

**Hypotheses.**

- p odd, p ∤ n + 1.

**Proof outline.**

1. (a) Near 0 the hypergeometric connection is equivalent to (1.2.1) of Crew 1994, a unipotent connection with a single nilpotent block (Crew 1994 proof of Proposition 1.2).
2. (b) After pullback along z ↦ z^{n+1} the connection at ∞ splits into Dwork-type rank-one pieces of break 1 (Crew 1994 proof of Proposition 1.2), so the breaks before pullback are 1/(n + 1) (RD.1/differential-swan-conductor, behaviour under tame pullback); Irr_∞ = 1 and Swan_∞ = 1 by RD.6/irregularity-equals-swan.
3. (c) By RD.6/christol-mebkhout-global-euler-characteristic, χ(G_m, Kl_{n+1}) = (n + 1)·0 − 0 − 1 = −1; H^0 = 0 because the monodromy at ∞ has no invariants.
4. (d) The characteristic polynomial of F_{z_0} is determined by the traces of its powers (DeligneWeightsAndPurity:DWP.0/characteristic-power-series-and-traces (iii)), which are Kloosterman sums over all extensions of F_{q^{deg z_0}}; these coincide with the Frobenius traces of Deligne's ℓ-adic Kloosterman sheaf, which is pure of weight n (Deligne, SGA 4½ Sommes trigonométriques 7.4 and 7.8, as used by Crew 1994 §1.5 and Fresán–Sabbah–Yu §3.2.1). The ℓ-adic theorem is not planned by any roadmap this one may cite (its atlas owner, FiniteFieldsAndCharacterSums:FF.2, lies outside them); an intrinsic rigid proof via the Fourier transform (parent RD.6/purity-via-fourier-on-affine-line, applied to the localized Fourier transform description of Fresán–Sabbah–Yu §3.2.2) is the route this roadmap plans, and its verification is recorded as a gap.

**Acceptance.**

- n = 1: the two Frobenius eigenvalues at z_0 ∈ F_q^× have absolute value √q under every ι, the Weil bound |Kl_2(z_0; q)| ≤ 2√q.
- χ(G_m, Kl_{n+1}) = −1.

**Prerequisites.** `RD.6/kloosterman-f-isocrystal`, `RD.6/irregularity-equals-swan`, `RD.6/christol-mebkhout-global-euler-characteristic`, `RD.6/purity-via-fourier-on-affine-line`, `RD.6/pointwise-iota-weights`, `RD.1/differential-swan-conductor`, `RD.2/unipotent-iff-nilpotent-residue`, `DeligneWeightsAndPurity:DWP.0/characteristic-power-series-and-traces`, `DeligneWeightsAndPurity:DWP.0/weil-q-number`.

**Sources.** Crew, *Kloosterman sums and monodromy of a p-adic hypergeometric equation*, Proposition 1.2 and its proof, pp. 5-8: Local form of the connection at 0 (unipotent, single block) and at ∞ (after pullback by the (n+1)-st power map). Crew, *Kloosterman sums and monodromy of a p-adic hypergeometric equation*, Section 1.5, p. 11: Eigenvalues of Frobenius on fibres are algebraic integers pure of weight n − 1 for the rank-n isocrystal, by Deligne's ℓ-adic theorem and equality of traces. Fresán et al., *Hodge theory of Kloosterman connections*, Sections 3.2.1-3.2.2, pp. 23-25: Kl_{n+1} is lisse of rank n + 1 and pure of weight n (Deligne) in the ℓ-adic case; the rigid Kl_{n+1} has rank n + 1 and is pure of weight n.

#### Middle rigid cohomology of symmetric powers of Kloosterman F-isocrystals

`RD.6/symmetric-power-kloosterman-middle-cohomology` (theorem)

**Statement.** In the setting of RD.6/kloosterman-f-isocrystal, let k ≥ 1, f̃\_k the function on G_m^{kn+1} attached to Sym^k (Fresán–Sabbah–Yu (3.9) and §3.2.1), 𝒦 ⊂ G_m^{kn} the hypersurface of Fresán–Sabbah–Yu (3.9), and χ\_n the character of S_k × μ\_{n+1} there. **Hypotheses.**

#### The Frobenius-extended Weyl algebra and the Frobenius of the Fourier transform

`RD.6/fourier-frobenius-extension` (construction)

**Statement.** Let D† be the overconvergent Weyl algebra over K (parent RD.6/overconvergent-weyl-algebra, with ∂ acting as π^{-1}d/dx) and σ(x) = x^q. The Frobenius-extended algebra D†⟨F⟩ is generated over D† by an invertible element F with Fx = x^σF and ∂F = (dx^σ/dx)·F∂. A (σ, ∇)-module over K⟨x⟩† is the same as a left D†⟨F⟩-module that is finite free over K⟨x⟩† (F acting by the Frobenius structure). Let Σ\_i c_i x^i := exp(π(x^σ − x)) ∈ K⟨x⟩† (Dwork). The Fourier automorphism ρ of D† (x ↦ ∂, ∂ ↦ −x; parent RD.6/fourier-automorphism) extends to D†⟨F⟩ by Kedlaya's formula, which writes ρ(F) as an explicit series in ∂ and x with the coefficients c_i, followed by (dx^σ/dx)·F, and ρ(ρ(F)) = qF. For a (σ, ∇)-module M, the Fourier transform M̂ = D†⟨F⟩ ⊗\_ρ M thus carries a Frobenius F̂ (the action of F through ρ), and M̂ is a (σ, ∇)-module whenever M̂ is finite free over K⟨s⟩†.

**Hypotheses.**

- K ∋ π with π^{p−1} = −p; σ(x) = x^q with σ\_K = id.
- The convergence of Σ\_i c_i ∂^i x^i on D†-modules coming from (σ, ∇)-modules uses Corollary 4.2.2 of Kedlaya (parent RD.6/frobenius-structure-gives-weyl-module); Kedlaya states the extension without proof, and its verification is recorded as a gap of this packet.

**Proof outline.**

1. Dwork: exp(π(x^σ − x)) is overconvergent (a product of values of Dwork's splitting function, parent RD.6/dwork-isocrystal).
2. The relations defining D†⟨F⟩ are those satisfied by multiplication operators, derivation and Frobenius structure on a (σ, ∇)-module: F(x v) = x^σ F(v) and ∂(F v) = (dx^σ/dx) F(∂v) (horizontality of the Frobenius structure).
3. ρ must preserve the defining relations, ρ(F)ρ(x) = ρ(x^σ)ρ(F) and ρ(∂)ρ(F) = ρ(dx^σ/dx)ρ(F)ρ(∂), in the completed algebra, and ρ^2 (which is x ↦ −x, ∂ ↦ −∂ on D†) must send F to qF. Kedlaya states both (Remark 4.2.3) without proof; they are identities between series built from Dwork's exp(π(x^σ − x)) and are checked on the Dwork modules L_{ax}, which generate the relevant category under the Fourier transform. This verification is part of the gap recorded for this node.

#### Normalising an ι-pure coefficient to weight zero

`RD.6/weight-normalising-constant-line` (lemma)

**Statement.** Let ι : K^alg → C be a field embedding and E ≠ 0 an overconvergent F-isocrystal on X that is ι-pure of real weight w. Choose a closed point x of degree d and an eigenvalue α of F_x on E_x, and let c ∈ K^alg with c^d = α. Then |ι(c)| = q^{w/2}, c lies in a finite extension K' of K, and E(c^{-1}) = E ⊗ L_{c^{-1}} (RD.6/constant-f-line, over K') is ι-pure of weight 0. For integer w one may instead use the Tate twist E(w/2) when w is even, or c = a square root of q. The ι-realizability statement 'E ⊕ E^∨(−w) is ι-real' needs a constant line L_b with ι(b) = q^w exactly: such b exists in K^alg when w is an integer (b = q^w) or more generally when q^w ∈ ι(K^alg), for instance when ι is an isomorphism K^alg ≅ C (DeligneWeightsAndPurity:DWP.0/embeddings-into-the-complex-numbers); for an arbitrary embedding ι and irrational w it need not exist. This resolves the parent's real-weight normalisation gap for RD.6/purity-via-fourier-on-affine-line and RD.6/p-adic-weil-ii (which only twist to weight 0) and restricts the real-weight realizability claim of RD.6/iota-real-and-realizable to the cases listed.

**Hypotheses.**

- E ι-pure of weight w (pointwise, parent RD.6/pointwise-iota-weights); X nonempty.

**Proof outline.**

1. |ι(α)| = q^{wd/2} by purity, so |ι(c)| = |ι(α)|^{1/d} = q^{w/2}; c is algebraic over K, so K' = K(c) is finite.
2. At a closed point y of degree e, the eigenvalues of F_y on E(c^{-1}) are those of E multiplied by c^{−e} (RD.6/constant-f-line), of absolute value q^{we/2}·q^{−we/2} = 1.
3. For ι-reality of E ⊕ (E^∨ ⊗ L_b): the eigenvalues of E^∨ ⊗ L_b at y are b^e/β for eigenvalues β of E; ι(b^e/β) = conj(ι(β)) for all β of absolute value q^{we/2} iff ι(b)^e = q^{we}, which holds when ι(b) = q^w.

**Acceptance.**

- E = O(−1) on Spec F_q (weight 2): c = q, E(q^{-1}) = O.
- E on Spec F_3 with F = 4 (weight 2·log_3 4): c = 4 and E(4^{-1}) is trivial, although no Tate twist reaches weight 0.

**Prerequisites.** `RD.6/constant-f-line`, `RD.6/pointwise-iota-weights`, `RD.6/iota-real-and-realizable`, `DeligneWeightsAndPurity:DWP.0/iota-weight`, `DeligneWeightsAndPurity:DWP.0/embeddings-into-the-complex-numbers`, `DeligneWeightsAndPurity:DWP.0/twisting-by-rank-one-characters`.

**Sources.** Kedlaya, *Fourier transforms and p-adic 'Weil II'*, Section 6.1, p. 40, and proofs of Theorems 6.5.1 and 6.6.2, pp. 48-50: Pure objects are realizable via the dual twisted by the weight (p. 40); the proofs of Theorems 6.5.1 (p. 48) and 6.6.2 (p. 50) reduce to weight 0 by twisting. #### Weight–monodromy purity at the points at infinity of a curve

`RD.6/local-monodromy-weight-purity` (theorem — planet: *Local weight–monodromy purity*)

**Statement.** Let X be a smooth affine curve over F_q, X̄ its smooth compactification, E ∈ F-Isoc†(X/K) ι-pure of weight β, and y ∈ X̄ − X. After a finite étale cover and a finite extension of K and of F_q (allowed by RD.2/local-monodromy-finite-etale-form), E is unipotent at a rational point η over y; let (V_η, F, N) be its fibre at η: the solution space of the nilpotent residue N with the Frobenius F of the generic fibre, with NF = q_η·FN. Let M be the monodromy filtration of N on V_η, centred at 0 (LefschetzPencilsAndVanishingCycles:LPV.1/monodromy-filtration). Then gr^M_i V_η is ι-pure of weight β + i for every i. Consequently the local invariants ker N = H^0_loc(E) at y are ι-mixed of weights ≤ β, and on W := ker N the filtration W_i := ker N ∩ im N^i has W_i/W_{i+1} ι-pure of weight β − i. This proves the conclusion of Kedlaya's Proposition 6.4.4 used by parent RD.6/local-invariants-strongly-mixed, replacing the shortcut the parent packet found insufficient.

**Hypotheses.**

- X smooth affine curve over F_q; E ι-pure of weight β; the local data (V_η, F, N) are well defined up to canonical isomorphism on gr^M (Crew 1998, 10.6).

**Proof outline.**

1. L-function of the extension across y (Crew 1998 (10.7.1)-(10.7.2), the analogue of Deligne's (1.8.1.1)): the trace formula (parent RD.6/lefschetz-trace-formula) and duality on curves (RD.5/crew-finiteness-and-duality-on-curves) show that the reciprocal ι det(1 − tF | H^0(E ⊗ A(y)))^{-1} has no pole in |t| < q^{−(β+2)/2}, i.e. the determinant has no zero there, so the weights of F on ker N are ≤ β + 2 (this is parent RD.6/weight-drop-lemma in local form).
2. Applying this to tensor powers E^{⊗k} (pure of weight kβ), which contain (ker N)^{⊗k} in their local invariants, gives weights ≤ β + 2/k for every k, hence ≤ β (Crew 1998 10.7, parent RD.6/local-invariants-weakly-mixed).
3. Deligne's argument (Weil II proof of 1.8.4): twist to β = 0, so ker N has weights ≤ 0; for a primitive part P_{−j} ⊂ gr^M_{−j} of lowest weight the bound gives weights ≤ 0; applying the same bound to E ⊗ E (whose monodromy filtration is the convolution of the two, LefschetzPencilsAndVanishingCycles:LPV.1/tensor-dual-and-symmetric-monodromy) and to the dual E^∨ (weights ≥ 0 by symmetry) pins the weights of P_{−j} to exactly −j, using the properties (1.6.14) of the monodromy filtration under tensor products and duals.
4. The statements about ker N and W_i follow from the sl_2-structure of gr^M: ker N meets gr^M_{−i} exactly in the bottoms of the Jordan strings of length i + 1, which are ker N ∩ im N^i modulo ker N ∩ im N^{i+1}.

**Acceptance.**

- E unipotent with N = 0: M is concentrated in degree 0 and V_η is pure of weight β.
- A Tate-curve type degeneration with V_η of rank 2, N ≠ 0 and β = 1: gr^M_{−1} = ker N = im N is pure of weight 0 and gr^M_1 of weight 2.
- Kl_{n+1} at 0 (single block of size n + 1, weight n): ker N is one-dimensional of weight 0 (RD.6/kloosterman-local-monodromy-and-purity).

**Prerequisites.** `RD.6/local-invariants-weakly-mixed`, `RD.6/weight-drop-lemma`, `RD.6/lefschetz-trace-formula`, `RD.5/crew-finiteness-and-duality-on-curves`, `RD.2/local-monodromy-finite-etale-form`, `RD.2/unipotent-iff-nilpotent-residue`, `RD.2/solution-space-phi-n-module`, `LefschetzPencilsAndVanishingCycles:LPV.1/monodromy-filtration`, `LefschetzPencilsAndVanishingCycles:LPV.1/tensor-dual-and-symmetric-monodromy`, `DeligneWeightsAndPurity:DWP.0/purity-under-subquotients-and-extensions`.

**Sources.** Crew, *Finiteness theorems for the cohomology of an overconvergent isocrystal on a curve*, 10.6-10.8, pp. 758-761: The local monodromy formalism (fibre at a unipotent point, monodromy operator N, Frobenius F on the fibre, monodromy filtration well defined on graded pieces), the L-function of the extension across a point, and Theorem 10.8: for E ι-pure of weight β unipotent at η over x, gr^M_i is ι-pure of weight β + i. Crew prints the relation as FN = q_y NF in (10.6.3); horizontality of his Φ : F^\*M → M gives NF = q_y FN, the form used here (see sourceIssues). Deligne, *La conjecture de Weil. #### Constant coefficients on affine curves have H^1_c of nonnegative slopes

`RD.6/nonnegative-slopes-constant-coefficients-on-curves` (lemma)

**Statement.** Let C be a smooth affine curve over F_q and V an F-isocrystal over Spec F_q (a finite-dimensional K-space with linear Frobenius) all of whose slopes are ≥ 0, with pullback V_C to C. Then H^1_{c,rig}(C/K, V_C) has all slopes ≥ 0. This replaces the vanishing assertion of Kedlaya's Lemma 6.7.2 for horizontally constant constituents, which fails (O on G_m has H^1_c = K), as the base case of parent RD.6/nonnegative-slopes-of-h1c-on-curves.

**Hypotheses.**

- C smooth affine over F_q with smooth compactification C̄ and boundary D = C̄ − C; V of slopes ≥ 0.

**Proof outline.**

1. H^1_{c,rig}(C/K, V_C) ≅ H^1_{c,rig}(C/K) ⊗\_K V with Frobenius F ⊗ F_V (constant coefficients; RD.5/kunneth-formula-with-supports with the point), and slopes of a tensor product are sums of slopes (parent RD.6/slope-interval-of-isocrystal), so it suffices to treat V = K.
2. Excision for D ⊂ C̄ gives H^0(C̄) → H^0(D) → H^1_c(C) → H^1(C̄) → 0 (parent RD.6/gysin-sequence-for-curves, RD.4/excision-sequence); the eigenvalues of F on H^0(D) are roots of unity (parent RD.6/rigid-cohomology-of-a-finite-field-point), of slope 0.
3. After a finite extension of F_q make C̄ geometrically connected. Then H^0(C̄) = K with F = 1 and H^2(C̄) ≅ K(−1), so by the trace formula (parent RD.6/lefschetz-trace-formula) Z(C̄, t) = P_1(t)/((1 − t)(1 − qt)) with P_1(t) = det(1 − tF | H^1(C̄)). Since Z(C̄, t) ∈ 1 + tZ\[\[t]] (RD.6/closed-points-and-zeta-function), P_1 = Z·(1 − t)(1 − qt) has integer coefficients; since det(1 − tF) is the reverse of the characteristic polynomial (mathlib:Polynomial.reverse, DeligneWeightsAndPurity:DWP.0/characteristic-power-series-and-traces (i)), its reciprocal roots are the eigenvalues of F on H^1(C̄), which are therefore algebraic integers, of slope ≥ 0.
4. The slopes of H^1_c(C) are those of the subquotients, hence ≥ 0. No circular use of parent RD.6/slope-bounds-for-rigid-cohomology occurs.

**Acceptance.**

- C = G_m: H^1_c = K with F = 1, slope 0.
- C = E − {O} for an elliptic curve E/F_7 with a_7 = 3: H^1_c ≅ H^1(E) with slopes 0 and 1.

#### Alternating traces of Dwork operators pass from the de Rham complex to cohomology

`RD.6/nuclear-trace-on-complexes` (lemma)

**Statement.** Let A be a dagger algebra of Monsky–Washnitzer type over K with a Frobenius lift, M a (σ, ∇)-module over A, and θ^• an endomorphism of the de Rham complex M ⊗ Ω^•\_A by Dwork operators commuting with the differentials (for example the canonical Dwork operator ψ, parent RD.6/canonical-dwork-operator). If the cohomology groups H^i(M) are finite-dimensional, then Σ\_i (−1)^i Tr(θ^i | M ⊗ Ω^i_A) = Σ\_i (−1)^i Tr(H^i(θ) | H^i(M)), the left side being traces of nuclear operators (parent RD.6/dwork-operators-are-nuclear). This is the step from the complex to cohomology in parent RD.6/trace-formula-without-rational-points.

**Hypotheses.**

- A of Monsky–Washnitzer type (smooth special fibre); each M ⊗ Ω^i_A is an LF space ∪\_r M^i(r) of Banach spaces with a common orthogonal basis, as in van der Put Lemma 3.4.
- The trace and Fredholm theory for compact endomorphisms of these LF spaces is requested from LocallyAnalyticDistributions:L4 (the request recorded by the parent packet).

**Proof outline.**

1. The differentials are strict maps of LF spaces with closed images because the cohomology is finite-dimensional (open mapping theorem for LF spaces and strictness criteria, Crew 1998 Part I, 3.4-3.6).
2. Split the complex into strict short exact sequences 0 → Z^i → C^i → B^{i+1} → 0 and 0 → B^i → Z^i → H^i → 0, each stable under θ.
3. The trace of a nuclear operator is additive along strict short exact sequences of such spaces with stable subspace (van der Put (1.5); Serre's theory of completely continuous operators, as supplied by LocallyAnalyticDistributions:L4/fredholm-determinant and L4/cyclic-determinant-identity on Banach stages), and on the finite-dimensional H^i it is the ordinary trace (mathlib:LinearMap.trace). Van der Put's nuclear operators (§1, p. 34) are defined algebraically, by finite-dimensional generalized eigenspaces for each nonzero eigenvalue with eigenvalues tending to 0, and for them (1.5) is linear algebra on those eigenspaces; the LF-space theory is needed only to know that the Dwork operators and the maps they induce on kernels and cokernels are nuclear in that sense.
4. Telescoping the alternating sums gives the identity.

**Acceptance.**

- A = K⟨x⟩†, M = A, θ = ψ = qθ\_0 with θ\_0(Σ c_n x^n) = Σ c_{qn} x^n on A and ψ(x^{qj−1}dx) = x^{j−1}dx on Ω^1: the left side is q − 0 = q, and on cohomology H^0 = K (ψ = q) and H^1 = 0, so the right side is q (= #A^1(F_q)).

**Prerequisites.** `RD.6/dwork-operators-are-nuclear`, `RD.6/canonical-dwork-operator`, `RD.6/dwork-operator`, `LocallyAnalyticDistributions:L4/fredholm-determinant`, `LocallyAnalyticDistributions:L4/cyclic-determinant-identity`, `LocallyAnalyticDistributions:L4`, `mathlib:LinearMap.trace`.

**Sources.** van der Put, *The cohomology of Monsky and Washnitzer*, Section 1, (1.5), p. 35: Traces of nuclear maps are additive along exact sequences, used to pass from the complex of differential forms to cohomology. van der Put, *The cohomology of Monsky and Washnitzer*, Lemma and Definition 3.4, pp. 39-40: Dwork operators on modules over Monsky–Washnitzer algebras are nuclear on the LF spaces built from Banach stages with a common orthogonal basis. Crew, *Finiteness theorems for the cohomology of an overconvergent isocrystal on a curve*, Part I, 3.1-3.13, pp. 725-727: LF spaces, the open mapping theorem and strictness criteria for continuous maps between them.

- **Higher-dimensional rank-one finiteness (Abe, Lemma 6.1)** (for `RD.6/rank-one-finite-geometric-monodromy`, `RD.6/rank-one-purity-via-curves`). - **Relative rigid cohomology of the Kloosterman family** (for `RD.6/kloosterman-f-isocrystal`). ## Acceptance tests of the layer

- Constant coefficient on P^1: Frobenius eigenvalues 1 and q (first part); the zeta function 1/((1 − t)(1 − qt)) is rational over Q (`RD.6/rational-descent-of-power-series`).
- G_m: H^1_c = K of weight 0 and H^1 = K(−1) of weight 2, so the middle cohomology vanishes and is trivially pure of weight 1 (`RD.6/middle-cohomology-is-pure`); H^1_c has slope 0 (`RD.6/nonnegative-slopes-constant-coefficients-on-curves`).
- Dwork coefficient L_{x^d}, p ∤ d: Irr_∞ = Swan_∞ = d, χ(A^1, L_{x^d}) = 1 − d (`RD.6/christol-mebkhout-global-euler-characteristic`, `RD.6/irregularity-equals-swan`); its trace function is ψ\_π^{-1} ∘ Tr (`RD.6/dwork-kummer-sign-dictionary`).
- Kummer coefficient K_a, a ∉ Z: tame, χ(G_m, K_a) = 0, finite monodromy μ\_m (`RD.6/kummer-isocrystal`, `RD.6/coefficient-category-is-neutral-tannakian`).
- Kloosterman coefficient Kl_2: rank 2, Swan_∞ = 1, χ = −1, Frobenius eigenvalues of absolute value √q, the Weil bound |Kl_2(z; q)| ≤ 2√q and the relation Kl_2(z; q^2) = 2q − Kl_2(z; q)^2 (`RD.6/kloosterman-local-monodromy-and-purity`, `RD.6/kloosterman-f-isocrystal`); H^1_mid(G_m, Kl_2) = 0 (`RD.6/symmetric-power-kloosterman-middle-cohomology`).
- A Tate-curve type degeneration: ker N = im N pure of weight 0 inside a weight-1 coefficient (`RD.6/local-monodromy-weight-purity`).
