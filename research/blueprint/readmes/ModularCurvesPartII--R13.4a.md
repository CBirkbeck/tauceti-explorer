# Modular curves, following Katz–Mazur, Part II: R13.4a. Algebraic coarse construction before analytic comparison

## Purpose and scope

This layer constructs the compactified coarse modular curves X_H of every congruence level H over ℤ by algebraic means: from the moduli of level structures away from the level, by normalization of the stack X(1) of generalized elliptic curves with irreducible fibres, and by passage to the Keel–Mori coarse space. It treats full level, fixed-pairing level, Γ₁ and refined Γ₀ level separately, with their bases and stabilisers, proves properness, normality and the base-change statements that hold, identifies the elliptic opens with the affine level problems and coarse schemes of the Tau Ceti roadmap ModularCurves through their moduli interpretation, and proves agreement with that roadmap's Layer 10 curves for the prime-level diamond quotients by a recognition criterion for normalizations. No analytic uniformisation, no connectedness statement and no input from R12.6 is used. The layer supplies the algebraic curves that R12.3 compares with Γ\(ℍ∪ℙ¹(ℚ)), and the curves that R13.4b, R13.5, R14.1 and R14.2 study.

A first pass of this layer, in the R13.3 part, already fixes seven targets; they are cited here by identifier and are summarised in the next section. The targets below refine that pass into the separate constructions the layer description asks for, and add the interfaces that the following layers and other roadmaps request.

## Conventions

- **Levels.** An open level subgroup is an open subgroup H⊂GL₂(Ẑ); for n with Γ(n)=ker(GL₂(Ẑ)→GL₂(ℤ/n))⊂H, H̄ is its image in GL₂(ℤ/n). Everything depends only on H. ±H is the subgroup generated by H and −I.
- **Frames and the right action.** A frame of E[n] is an isomorphism φ:(ℤ/n)²→E[n], written as the ordered basis (P,Q)=(φ(e₁),φ(e₂)); g=[[a,b],[c,d]] acts on the right by φ↦φ∘g, i.e. (P,Q)g=(aP+cQ, bP+dQ), as in ModularCurves Convention 5. Γ₁(N)={a≡1, c≡0} fixes P, Γ₀(N)={c≡0} fixes the line through P, Γ₁^μ(N)={c≡0, d≡1} records an embedding μ_N↪E[N]. The Weil pairing satisfies e_n((P,Q)g)=e_n(P,Q)^{det g}.
- **Stacks and curves.** X(1)=Ell₁ is the stack of generalized elliptic curves with irreducible geometric fibres; 𝒳_H is the level-H stack; X_H its coarse space; the letter Y_H is used both for the elliptic open of 𝒳_H and for its coarse space, and each statement says which is meant; X_H^∞ is the scheme-theoretic fibre of X_H over j=∞. A stack, its coarse space and a model of the coarse space are different objects, and a statement about one is never transferred to another without the theorem that does so.
- **Degrees.** The stack 𝒳_H has degree [GL₂(Ẑ):H] over X(1) and the coarse curve X_H has degree [GL₂(Ẑ):±H] over ℙ¹_j; they differ exactly when −I∉H.
- **Split Cartan notation.** X_s(ℓ) is the ordered (diagonal) split Cartan curve and X_s⁺(ℓ) the normaliser curve. Balakrishnan–Dogra–Müller–Tuitman–Vonk and the first-pass node split-cartan-quotient write X_s(ℓ) for the normaliser curve.
- **Base change.** Coarse formation commutes with flat base change. Arbitrary base change is asserted only in the range of legal-coarse-base-change (ℤ[1/gcd(6,n)]) and where the stack is a scheme; coarse-base-change-failure shows the range cannot be extended in general.

## A. Levels, level structures and the level-H stacks

The level of every curve in this layer is an open subgroup H of GL₂(Ẑ). Away from the level a level-H structure is a section of a finite étale quotient of the frame torsor; over all of ℤ the stack 𝒳_H is obtained by normalizing the stack X(1)=Ell₁ of R13.1 along these structures, following Deligne–Rapoport as presented by Česnavičius. For the standard levels the normalization is recognised as R13.2's moduli stack.

### 1. Open level subgroups and the standard levels

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/level-subgroups` (definition).

An open level subgroup is an open subgroup H of GL₂(Ẑ). Its level is the least n≥1 with Γ(n)=ker(GL₂(Ẑ)→GL₂(ℤ/n)) contained in H; for any n with Γ(n)⊂H write H̄ for the image of H in GL₂(ℤ/n), so that H↦H̄ is a bijection between open subgroups containing Γ(n) and subgroups of GL₂(ℤ/n), and H̄ for a multiple m of n is the full preimage of H̄ for n. Matrices act on ordered bases on the right, (P,Q)·[[a,b],[c,d]]=(aP+cQ, bP+dQ), as in Tau Ceti ModularCurves Convention 5. The standard levels are the subgroups whose images are: Γ(N), the trivial group; Γ₁(N)={a≡1, c≡0 mod N}, the stabiliser of the first basis vector P; Γ₀(N)={c≡0 mod N}, the stabiliser of the line through P; the level Γ₁^μ(N)={c≡0, d≡1 mod N}, whose structures (P,Q)↦(⟨P⟩, Q mod ⟨P⟩) are the embeddings μ_N↪E[N] given by P′↦e_N(P′,Q); the mixed levels Γ₁(N)∩Γ₀(M) for N|M; for a prime ℓ the split Cartan C_s(ℓ) of diagonal matrices and its normaliser C_s⁺(ℓ) (diagonal or antidiagonal); for an odd prime ℓ and a fixed nonsquare ε∈𝔽_ℓ^× the nonsplit Cartan C_ns(ℓ)={[[a,εb],[b,a]] : (a,b)≠(0,0)}, the image of 𝔽_{ℓ²}^× acting on 𝔽_{ℓ²}=𝔽_ℓ⊕𝔽_ℓ√ε, and its normaliser C_ns⁺(ℓ); C_ns(2) is the cyclic subgroup of order 3 of GL₂(𝔽₂). The invariants of H are its index [GL₂(Ẑ):H]=[GL₂(ℤ/n):H̄], its projective index [GL₂(Ẑ):±H] where ±H is generated by H and −I, its determinant image det(H̄)⊂(ℤ/n)^×, and whether −I∈H. Conjugation by g∈GL₂(Ẑ) sends H to g⁻¹Hg and preserves all four invariants.

Hypotheses and conventions:

- n≥1 with Γ(n)⊂H; the nonsplit Cartan needs ℓ odd and ε a nonsquare, or ℓ=2.
- The right-action convention is fixed once; the left action on column vectors of Česnavičius and Zywina gives the same subgroups because g acts on the frame φ:(ℤ/n)²→E[n] by φ↦φ∘g.

Sources:

- [Kęstutis Česnavičius, A modular description of 𝒳₀(n)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.07475), §4.1.2, p.28; §4.4, p.39: An open subgroup H of GL₂(Ẑ) is described by any n with Γ(n)⊂H and its image H̄ in GL₂(ℤ/n); Γ₁(n) is the subgroup a≡1, c≡0 mod n.
- [David Zywina, On the possible images of the mod ℓ representations associated to elliptic curves over ℚ](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.07660), §3.2, p.15; §1, Notation, p.2 (Cartan subgroups): Modular curves X_G are attached to subgroups G of GL₂(ℤ/N) containing −I with surjective determinant, and the degree of the j-map is [GL₂(ℤ/N):G]; the split and nonsplit Cartan subgroups and their normalisers are fixed there.
- [Tau Ceti contributors, Roadmap: modular curves, following Katz–Mazur (Tau Ceti roadmap ModularCurves)](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/tree/main/TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves), Conventions, item 5; §9B: Ordered bases are row vectors with the right action (P,Q)g=(aP+cQ,bP+dQ); the standard quotient problems of 9B are taken by kernel-of-reduction, upper-triangular, diagonal and diamond subgroups.

Proof route:

1. Define each standard level by entry conditions in GL₂(ℤ/n); closure under products and inverses follows from the 2×2 product and adjugate formulas.
2. Define C_s⁺(ℓ) and C_ns⁺(ℓ) as the normalisers in GL₂(𝔽_ℓ) of C_s(ℓ) and C_ns(ℓ); each Cartan has index 2 in its normaliser for ℓ odd.
3. Compute orders from |GL₂(𝔽_ℓ)|=(ℓ²−1)(ℓ²−ℓ): |Γ₀(ℓ)‾|=ℓ(ℓ−1)², |C_s(ℓ)|=(ℓ−1)², |C_ns(ℓ)|=ℓ²−1, and for general N the indices [GL₂:Γ₀(N)]=ψ(N)=N∏_{p|N}(1+1/p) and [GL₂:Γ₁(N)]=φ(N)ψ(N).
4. The projective index is the index when −I∈H̄ and half the index otherwise; −I∉Γ₁(N)‾ and −I∉Γ(N)‾ for N≥3.

Prerequisites: `mathlib:Matrix.GeneralLinearGroup`; `mathlib:Matrix.GeneralLinearGroup.det`; `mathlib:Subgroup.index`; `mathlib:Subgroup.normalizer`; `mathlib:CongruenceSubgroup.Gamma0`; `mathlib:CongruenceSubgroup.Gamma1`; `mathlib:CongruenceSubgroup.Gamma`; `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves#9b-the-standard-quotient-problems`.

Uses:

- this layer, level-h-structures and deligne-rapoport-level-stack: the level of every moduli problem and stack in R13.4a is an open subgroup in this convention
- this layer, coarse-degree-and-cusp-divisor: the coarse degree over the j-line is the projective index
- Zywina 2015, Proposition 3.3: the rational-point criterion is stated for subgroups containing −I with surjective determinant
- EffectiveDiophantineMethods ED.6 and BDMTV 2019 §6: the Cartan normalisers C_s⁺(13), C_ns⁺(13) and the projective S₄ subgroup at 13 are levels in this sense
- Calegari–Geraghty 2018 §3.2: Γ_H(N)={c≡0, d mod N∈H} and the levels U₀(Q), U₁(Q) are open level subgroups

API:

- `levelGamma0` (constructor): The subgroup Γ₀(N)‾ of GL₂(ℤ/N) of matrices with lower-left entry 0.
- `levelGamma1` (constructor): The subgroup Γ₁(N)‾ of matrices with upper-left entry 1 and lower-left entry 0; it is the stabiliser of the first basis vector under the right action.
- `levelGamma1Mu` (constructor): The subgroup Γ₁^μ(N)‾={c=0, d=1}; its level structures are embeddings μ_N↪E[N], the convention of Calegari–Geraghty.
- `splitCartan` (constructor): The diagonal subgroup C_s(ℓ), and splitCartanNormalizer its normaliser.
- `nonsplitCartan` (constructor): For a nonsquare ε, the subgroup {[[a,εb],[b,a]]}, and nonsplitCartanNormalizer its normaliser.
- `projectiveIndex` (data): The index in GL₂(ℤ/n) of the subgroup ±H̄ generated by H̄ and −I; it is the coarse degree of X_H over the j-line.
- `projectiveIndex_eq_index` (characterisation): projectiveIndex H = index H when −I∈H̄, and 2·projectiveIndex H = index H otherwise.
- `levelDet` (data): The image det(H̄)⊂(ℤ/n)^×; its fixed field in ℚ(ζ_n) is the field of definition of the components of X_H.
- `levelPullback` (functoriality): For n|m the preimage of H̄ under GL₂(ℤ/m)→GL₂(ℤ/n) has the same index, projective index and determinant image.
- `levelGamma0_comap` (compatibility): The preimage in SL(2,ℤ) of levelGamma0 N is Mathlib's CongruenceSubgroup.Gamma0 N, of levelGamma1 N is CongruenceSubgroup.Gamma1 N, and of the trivial group is CongruenceSubgroup.Gamma N.

### 2. Level-H structures away from the level

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/level-h-structures` (definition).

Let H be an open level subgroup, n with Γ(n)⊂H, S a ℤ[1/n]-scheme and E/S an elliptic curve. The scheme Isom_S((ℤ/n)²_S, E[n]) of frames is a finite étale right GL₂(ℤ/n)-torsor over S, for the action φ↦φ∘g. A level-H structure on E is a section over S of the finite étale quotient Isom_S((ℤ/n)²_S, E[n])/H̄. Level-H structures on elliptic curves over ℤ[1/n]-schemes form a stack Y_H[1/n], and the forgetful map Y_H[1/n]→Ell_{ℤ[1/n]} is representable, finite étale of degree [GL₂(ℤ/n):H̄]. For n|m reduction of frames modulo n identifies the stacks built from n and from m over ℤ[1/m]. An automorphism α of E fixes the structure φH̄ exactly when φ⁻¹∘α∘φ lies in H̄; in particular [−1] fixes every level-H structure if and only if −I∈H̄. Over ℤ[1/n] this stack is the stack over Ell of the Katz–Mazur quotient problem [Γ(n)]/H̄, which upstream 9A identifies with the fppf sheaf quotient of the naive full level problem.

Hypotheses and conventions:

- n is invertible on the base; H contains Γ(n).
- The quotient is the étale-sheaf quotient; an S-point need not lift to an S-frame.

Sources:

- [Kęstutis Česnavičius, A modular description of 𝒳₀(n)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.07475), §4.1.2, p.28: For Γ(n)⊂H the stack Y_H[1/n] parametrises elliptic curves over ℤ[1/n]-schemes with a section of the finite étale scheme H̄\Isom(E[n],(ℤ/n)²), finite étale over Y(1)[1/n].
- [Kęstutis Česnavičius, A modular description of 𝒳₀(n)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.07475), Proposition 6.3(a), p.63: The Katz–Mazur quotient problem [Γ(n)]/H̄ is identified with this stack over ℤ[1/n] by Katz–Mazur 7.1.3(2).
- [Tau Ceti contributors, Roadmap: modular curves, following Katz–Mazur (Tau Ceti roadmap ModularCurves)](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/tree/main/TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves), §9A, items 1–3 and the final paragraph: After inverting n the Katz–Mazur full-level quotient problem is the fppf sheaf quotient of the naive ordered-basis problem, distinct from the orbit presheaf and from the coarse quotient.

Proof route:

1. E[n] is finite étale of rank n² and étale locally isomorphic to (ℤ/n)² over ℤ[1/n]-schemes (upstream 3N), so the frame scheme is a finite étale GL₂(ℤ/n)-torsor (upstream 5B).
2. A finite étale torsor has a finite étale quotient by any subgroup, formed étale locally as a disjoint union of copies of the base, and its formation commutes with base change (upstream 0C, 0D).
3. For n|m the reduction map GL₂(ℤ/m)→GL₂(ℤ/n) is surjective with kernel inside the preimage of H̄, which gives the change-of-level isomorphism.
4. The automorphism criterion is the torsor identity α∘φ=φ∘(φ⁻¹αφ); for α=[−1] one has φ⁻¹αφ=−I.
5. Stackify over the elliptic moduli stack as in AlgebraicModuli R09.5 and compare with the Katz–Mazur quotient problem by upstream 9A.

Prerequisites: `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/level-subgroups`; `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves#3n-naive-level-structures`; `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves#5b-full-ordered-bases-and-fixed-pairing`; `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves#0c-finite-quotients-and-torsors`; `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves#0d-finite-étale-schemes-and-galois-actions`; `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves#9a-katzmazur-quotient-problems`; `AlgebraicModuliForArithmeticGeometry:R09.5/elliptic-coarse-recovery`; `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.1/polygon-count-stacks-and-decontractions`; `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.1/stack-in-groupoids`.

Uses:

- this layer, deligne-rapoport-level-stack: the stack is normalised over X(1) along this finite étale open
- this layer, coarse-rational-points: K-points of Y_H over j≠0,1728 are read through Galois-stable classes of frames
- this layer, standard-level-stack-identifications: the elliptic opens of R13.2's stacks are these stacks after inverting the level

API:

- `LevelStructure` (constructor): Level-H structures on E/S over a ℤ[1/n]-scheme: sections of Isom((ℤ/n)²,E[n])/H̄.
- `LevelStructure.ofFrame` (constructor): The class φH̄ of a frame φ:(ℤ/n)²≅E[n].
- `LevelStructure.ofFrame_eq_iff` (extensionality): φH̄=ψH̄ if and only if φ⁻¹∘ψ∈H̄.
- `LevelStructure.baseChange` (functoriality): Pullback along S′→S, compatible with composition.
- `LevelStructure.changeLevel` (equivalence): For n|m, reduction of frames gives a natural bijection between the structures built from m and from n.
- `LevelStructure.aut_fix_iff` (characterisation): An automorphism α of E fixes φH̄ iff φ⁻¹αφ∈H̄; [−1] fixes all level-H structures iff −I∈H̄.
- `levelOpenStack_forget_finiteEtale` (structure): Y_H[1/n]→Ell_{ℤ[1/n]} is representable finite étale of degree [GL₂(ℤ/n):H̄].
- `LevelStructure.conj` (functoriality): φH̄↦(φ∘g)(g⁻¹H̄g) identifies level-H and level-g⁻¹Hg structures.
- `LevelStructure.toKatzMazur` (compatibility): Over ℤ[1/n] the stack is that of the Katz–Mazur quotient problem [Γ(n)]/H̄ of upstream 9A.

Unit tests:

- `LevelStructure.full_group` [degenerate]: For H̄=GL₂(ℤ/n) every elliptic curve over a ℤ[1/n]-scheme has exactly one level-H structure.
- `LevelStructure.gamma1_point` [computation]: For H=Γ₁(N) the map φH̄↦φ(e₁) identifies level-H structures with points of exact order N, and for H=Γ₀(N) with cyclic subgroups of order N.
- `LevelStructure.not_frame` [non-example]: For a prime p≥3, an elliptic curve over ℚ with a ℚ-rational subgroup of order p has a ℚ-rational Γ₀(p)-structure but no ℚ-rational frame of E[p], since the Weil pairing of a rational frame would be a rational primitive p-th root of unity: the quotient is a sheaf quotient, not the set of orbits of S-frames.
- `LevelStructure.minusOne_gamma1` [characterisation]: For N≥3, [−1] moves every Γ₁(N)-structure, and fixes every Γ₀(N)-structure.

Acceptance:

- H=GL₂(Ẑ) gives one level structure on every curve; H=Γ(n) gives the frames themselves; H=Γ₀(p) over ℤ[1/p] gives étale-locally the p+1 subgroups of order p.

### 3. The Deligne–Rapoport level-H stack

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/deligne-rapoport-level-stack` (construction).

Let X(1)=Ell₁ be the proper smooth Deligne–Mumford stack over ℤ of generalized elliptic curves with irreducible geometric fibres (R13.1). For an open level subgroup H and n with Γ(n)⊂H, define 𝒳_H as the normalization of X(1) in the representable, quasi-compact and separated composite Y_H[1/n]→Ell_{ℤ[1/n]}⊂X(1). It carries a representable finite morphism 𝒳_H→X(1), the open substack Y_H:=𝒳_H×_{X(1)}Ell, whose restriction to ℤ[1/n] is Y_H[1/n], and the closed substack 𝒳_H^∞ over the boundary X(1)^∞. Up to unique isomorphism over X(1) it does not depend on n. For H′⊂H the map of level structures extends uniquely to a finite morphism 𝒳_{H′}→𝒳_H over X(1), compatibly with composition, and g∈GL₂(Ẑ) gives an isomorphism 𝒳_H≅𝒳_{g⁻¹Hg} over X(1). The universal property: for every representable integral 𝒯→X(1) and every factorisation of Y_H[1/n]→X(1) through 𝒯 there is a unique morphism 𝒳_H→𝒯 over X(1) extending it.

Hypotheses and conventions:

### 4. Geometry of the level-H stacks

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/level-stack-geometry` (theorem).

For every open level subgroup H and n with Γ(n)⊂H: (a) 𝒳_H→X(1) is representable, finite, surjective and locally free of rank [GL₂(Ẑ):H]; (b) 𝒳_H is a normal, Cohen–Macaulay, Deligne–Mumford stack, proper and flat over ℤ, with all fibres of pure dimension one; (c) 𝒳_H is smooth over ℤ[1/n], and over ℤ[1/n] its elliptic open Y_H[1/n] is the quotient stack [Y(n)_{ℤ[1/n]}/H̄] of the full-level elliptic stack by right translation of frames; the quotient stack [X(n)_{ℤ[1/n]}/H̄] of the whole compactified full-level stack is not 𝒳_H[1/n] in general, because the rotations μ_n of the n-gon cusps act on E^sm[n]=μ_n×ℤ/n by unipotent matrices [[1,*],[0,1]] and contract to the identity of X(1), so its map to X(1) is not representable (for H=GL₂(Ẑ) it is Ell_n[1/n], not X(1)[1/n]); (d) for H′⊂H the morphism 𝒳_{H′}→𝒳_H is finite and surjective of degree [H:H′], and it is flat at every point where 𝒳_H is regular; (e) the open locus of 𝒳_H whose geometric points have trivial automorphism group is a quasi-projective ℤ-scheme, and 𝒳_H is a projective scheme when every geometric point has trivial automorphism group. Regularity of 𝒳_H at points of residue characteristic dividing n is not asserted for general H.

Hypotheses and conventions:

- H open; n with Γ(n)⊂H.
- Statement (c) uses the GL₂(ℤ/n)-torsor Y(n)[1/n]→Ell_{ℤ[1/n]} and the full-level formal cusp charts ⊔Spec ℤ[ζ_n][[q^{1/n}]] of 𝒳_{Γ(n)} over the Tate chart of X(1).

Proof route:

1. 2. (b) Normality is inherited from Y_H[1/n] by relative normalization; properness and flatness over ℤ come from X(1) through (a).
3. (c) On the elliptic open, Y(n)[1/n]→Ell_{ℤ[1/n]} is a GL₂(ℤ/n)-torsor (level-h-structures), so Y_H[1/n]=[Y(n)[1/n]/H̄] is finite étale over Ell_{ℤ[1/n]}, hence smooth over ℤ[1/n]. 4. (d) Normalization functoriality gives the finite map; miracle flatness at regular points of the target; the degree is computed on Y_{H′}[1/n]→Y_H[1/n].
5. (e) A Deligne–Mumford stack with trivial geometric automorphism groups and quasi-compact separated diagonal is an algebraic space (Česnavičius Lemma 3.2.2(a)); it is quasi-finite and separated over ℙ¹_ℤ through the coarse j-map, hence a quasi-projective scheme (Lemma 4.1.3 and Remark 4.1.4).

### 5. The standard levels: moduli stacks as normalizations

## B. The coarse curves X_H

The coarse space X_H of 𝒳_H is the integral coarse curve of the first pass (integral-coarse-curves). This group fixes the coarse j-line it maps to, the criterion by which coarse base change is proved, the local structure at a point with automorphisms, the degree and cusp divisor, the cusps away from the level, the cyclotomic structure, and the fixed-pairing and twisted curves.

### 6. The compactified coarse j-line

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/compactified-coarse-j-line` (theorem).

The j-invariant extends to a morphism j:X(1)→ℙ¹_ℤ sending the boundary to ∞ (on generalized elliptic curves it is the ratio of the sections c₄³ and Δ of ω^{⊗12}, which have no common zero), and j is the coarse moduli space of X(1)=Ell₁. More generally, for every m≥1 the composite Ell_m→Ell₁→ℙ¹_ℤ is the coarse space of Ell_m, the open Ell⊂Ell_m has coarse space 𝔸¹_ℤ, and the formation of all these coarse spaces commutes with arbitrary base change S→Spec ℤ. On the elliptic locus this is the upstream coarse j-line over every ring (9E, coarseJLine). X(1) is irreducible with geometrically irreducible fibres over ℤ.

Hypotheses and conventions:

- m≥1; S an arbitrary scheme.

### 7. Coarse base change from the fibres over 𝔽_p

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/fibrewise-coarse-base-change` (theorem).

Let 𝒳 be a separated, flat Deligne–Mumford stack locally of finite type over ℤ with coarse space f:𝒳→X. If for every prime p the base change f_{𝔽_p}:𝒳_{𝔽_p}→X_{𝔽_p} is a coarse space of 𝒳_{𝔽_p}, then for every scheme S the base change f_S:𝒳_S→X_S is a coarse space of 𝒳_S. Equivalently, coarse formation commutes with all base change as soon as it commutes with base change to the prime fields of positive characteristic; it always commutes with flat base change.

Hypotheses and conventions:

- 𝒳 separated, flat and locally of finite type over ℤ, Deligne–Mumford, so with finite inertia.

Sources:

- [Kęstutis Česnavičius, A modular description of 𝒳₀(n)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.07475), Lemma 3.3.1 and proof, pp.23–24: For a separated flat locally finite type Deligne–Mumford stack over ℤ, coarse base change to every 𝔽_p implies coarse base change to every scheme, by the torsion-freeness and dévissage argument on rings of invariants.
- [Kęstutis Česnavičius, Coarse base change fails for some modular curves](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.01249), §1.1, p.1: The criterion is how coarse base change for X_H is established, and why failure must be exhibited over some 𝔽_p.
- [Dan Abramovich and Angelo Vistoli, Compactifying the space of stable maps](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9908167), Lemma 2.2.3 and sketch of proof, pp.4–5 (arXiv v2): The étale-local presentation [U/Γ] of a separated Deligne–Mumford stack over its coarse space, which Česnavičius invokes in the proof of Lemma 3.3.1.

Proof route:

1. Coarse formation commutes with flat base change on X and can be checked fppf locally on X_S (SchemeAndStackFoundations SF.1 keel-mori).
2. Étale locally on X, 𝒳=[Spec B/G] with G finite and X=Spec B^G (étale-local structure of separated Deligne–Mumford stacks over their coarse spaces, Abramovich–Vistoli Lemma 2.2.3, requested from SchemeAndStackFoundations SF.1; Conrad 2005 Theorem 3.1); the coarse space of 𝒳_R is Spec (B⊗R)^G, so one must show B^G⊗R→(B⊗R)^G is an isomorphism for every ring R.
3. ℤ-flatness makes B and B/B^G torsion-free, so the map is injective for every ℤ-module R.
4. Surjectivity: pass to finitely generated R by a filtered colimit, the case R=ℤ is trivial, and dévissage along 0→R′→R→R″→0 with the five lemma reduces to R=ℤ/p, which is the hypothesis.

Prerequisites: `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.1/keel-mori`; `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.1/finite-quotient-coarse`; `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.1`.

Acceptance:

- Applied to X(1) this gives compactified-coarse-j-line over every S; applied to 𝒳_H it reduces legal-coarse-base-change over ℤ[1/n] to the fibres 𝔽_p, p∤n.

### 8. Local structure of coarse level curves

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/coarse-local-structure` (theorem).

Let H be an open level subgroup, π:𝒳_H→X_H the coarse space, x̄ a geometric point of 𝒳_H and Γ_x̄ its finite automorphism group. (a) There are an étale neighbourhood U→X_H of π(x̄) and a finite U-scheme V with a Γ_x̄-action such that 𝒳_H×_{X_H}U≅[V/Γ_x̄] and U=V/Γ_x̄. (b) The strict henselization of X_H at π(x̄) is the ring of Γ_x̄-invariants of the strict henselization of 𝒳_H at x̄, and the same holds after completion: Ô^{sh}_{X_H,π(x̄)}=(Ô^{sh}_{𝒳_H,x̄})^{Γ_x̄}. (c) Γ_x̄ acts through its effective quotient Γ_x̄^{eff}=Γ_x̄/(Γ_x̄∩{±1}) when −I∈H̄, and through Γ_x̄ itself otherwise; this quotient acts faithfully. (d) If x̄ lies over j(x̄)≠∞ in residue characteristic prime to n, then 𝒳_H→X(1) is étale at x̄, Γ_x̄ is the subgroup of Aut(E_x̄) fixing the level structure, and the local degree of X_H→ℙ¹_j at π(x̄), the degree of the extension of fraction fields of the strictly henselian local rings, is [Aut(E_x̄)/{±1} : Γ_x̄^{eff}], where Γ_x̄^{eff} denotes the image of Γ_x̄ in Aut(E_x̄)/{±1}; at a point of the generic fibre this is the ramification index, while at a closed point of residue characteristic p the local rings are two-dimensional and the local degree is the relevant invariant. For H=Γ₀(N) and any geometric point x̄=(E,C), Γ_x̄=Aut(E,C) contains {±1}, and the strictly henselian local ring of X₀(N) is the invariant ring of that of the refined stack under Aut(E,C)/{±1}. (e) On the open of X_H over which every geometric stabiliser has invertible order, in particular over ℤ[1/6] (stabilisers have order dividing 24 because 𝒳_H→X(1) is representable), a line bundle L on 𝒳_H is the pullback of a line bundle on X_H if and only if every Γ_x̄ acts trivially on the fibre L(x̄), and then L≅π*π_*L; ω^{⊗12} on X(1) is the pullback of 𝒪_{ℙ¹}(1) through the section Δ, while ω itself does not descend because −1 acts on it by −1.

Hypotheses and conventions:

### 9. Degree of the j-map and the cusp divisor

### 10. Cusps of X_H away from the level

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/coarse-cusps-away-from-the-level` (theorem).

Let H be an open level subgroup, n with Γ(n)⊂H, and u=[[1,1],[0,1]]. Over every algebraically closed field k with char k∤n, the points of X_H^∞(k) are in bijection with the double cosets ⟨±u⟩\GL₂(ℤ/n)/H̄: the coset of g is the Tate curve over k((q^{1/n})) with the level structure φ₀gH̄, where φ₀=(ζ,q^{1/n}) for a fixed primitive n-th root of unity ζ, the monodromy q^{1/n}↦ζq^{1/n} acting by left multiplication by u and inversion by −I. The width of the cusp of g, equal to its ramification index over ∞ and to its multiplicity in X_H^∞, is the least w≥1 with u^w∈±gH̄g⁻¹, and after base change to a strictly henselian base the completed local ring of X_H at it is a power series ring in a parameter t with t^w equal to q times a unit. ### 11. The cyclotomic structure of X_H

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/coarse-cyclotomic-structure` (construction).

Let H be an open level subgroup, n with Γ(n)⊂H, D_H=det(H̄)⊂(ℤ/n)^×, and O_H the ring of integers of the subfield ℚ(ζ_n)^{D_H} fixed by {σ_d : d∈D_H}, σ_d(ζ)=ζ^d. The Weil pairing gives a morphism det:Y_H[1/n]→Spec O_H[1/n], sending (E,φH̄) to the D_H-orbit of e_n(φ(e₁),φ(e₂)), well defined because e_n((P,Q)g)=e_n(P,Q)^{det g}; it extends uniquely to a morphism X_H→Spec O_H, proper and flat. For H′⊂H the level map X_{H′}→X_H lies over Spec O_{H′}→Spec O_H; for g normalising H̄ the automorphism of X_H induced by g lies over σ_{det g}. For H=Γ(N) this is the morphism X(N)→Spec ℤ[ζ_N] extending the Weil pairing. Whether the fibres of X_H→Spec O_H are geometrically connected is not part of this construction (R12.4 and R12.6).

### 12. The fixed-pairing compactified curve

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/fixed-pairing-coarse-curve` (construction).

For N≥1 and a ring R with a ring map ζ:ℤ[ζ_N]→R, let 𝒳(N,ζ)_R:=𝒳_{Γ(N)}×_{Spec ℤ[ζ_N]}Spec R, using the extended Weil pairing 𝒳_{Γ(N)}→Spec ℤ[ζ_N] (Conrad Theorem 4.1.1(3)), and let X(N,ζ)_R be its coarse space. ## C. Comparison with Tau Ceti ModularCurves, level maps and base change

The elliptic opens are Tau Ceti ModularCurves' Katz–Mazur level problems and coarse schemes, and on the prime-level diamond class the curves are its Layer 10 curves; both identifications rest on a recognition criterion for relative normalization whose three hypotheses are checked one by one. ### 14. Recognising a relative normalization

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/finite-normal-recognition` (theorem).

Let f:Y→J be a quasi-compact quasi-separated morphism of schemes, g:T→J an integral morphism and u:Y→T an open immersion with f=g∘u. Assume T is locally noetherian and normal (every local ring is an integrally closed domain) and that u(Y) is dense in T. Then the morphism normalizationDesc:Norm_J(Y)→T given by the universal property of the relative normalization is an isomorphism. Density and normality are needed: adjoining to T a disjoint copy of J keeps T integral over J and receiving Y but breaks density; a non-normal T (a cuspidal or nodal curve) receives a finite birational map from the normalization which is not an isomorphism. The criterion is not a base-change statement: for the qcqs open immersion D(t)⊂Spec k[t] the normalization of Spec k[t] in D(t) is Spec k[t], but after the base change t=0 the source is empty, so normalization does not commute with this base change. The same statement holds for representable morphisms of Deligne–Mumford stacks, checked on an étale atlas.

Hypotheses and conventions:

- f quasi-compact and quasi-separated; g integral; u an open immersion with dense image.
- T locally noetherian with integrally closed local domains.

Sources:

- [The Stacks Project authors, The Stacks Project](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu), Tags 035I, 035K, 035L, 03GP, 03GV, 0AXP: The universal property and localization of relative normalization (035I, 035K); the normalization in a normal source is a disjoint union of integral normal schemes (035L); normalization in an integral morphism is the source (03GP); compatibility with smooth base change (03GV); generic points of the normalization come from those of the source (0AXP).
- [Tau Ceti contributors, Roadmap: modular curves, following Katz–Mazur (Tau Ceti roadmap ModularCurves)](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/tree/main/TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves), Layer 10, item 1: The literal universal property of normalization does not characterise X_H up to isomorphism; an isomorphism characterisation needs T finite and normal, an open immersion with schematically dense image, and every irreducible component of T meeting Y_H; normalization does not commute with the base change t=0 for D(t)⊂Spec k[t].

Proof route:

1. Work over an affine open Spec A⊂J; then T is Spec C with C integral over A, Y∩Spec C=V is a dense open, and Norm is Spec A′ with A′ the integral closure of A in Γ(V,O) (Stacks 035I, 035K).
2. Since T is reduced and V dense, restriction C→Γ(V,O) is injective, and C lands in A′ because C is integral over A.
3. C is a finite product of normal domains C_i and Γ(V,O) embeds in the product of their fraction fields because V meets every component; an element of Γ(V,O) integral over A is integral over C, hence lies in C. So A′=C (Stacks 035L, 03GP).
4. The map normalizationDesc is the gluing of these identifications, hence an isomorphism; for stacks apply this to an étale atlas, using that normalization commutes with étale base change (Stacks 03GV).

Prerequisites: `mathlib:AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.normalization`; `mathlib:AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.normalizationDesc`; `mathlib:AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Hom.normalization.hom_ext`; `mathlib:AlgebraicGeometry.IsIntegralHom`; `mathlib:AlgebraicGeometry.IsOpenImmersion`; `mathlib:IsIntegrallyClosed`.

Acceptance:

- Applied with T=X(1), Y=Ell over ℤ[1/n] it gives levelStack GL₂(Ẑ)=X(1).
- Applied to Tau Ceti ModularCurves Layer 10's X_H it is the agreement prime-diamond-agreement.

### 16. Agreement with the prime-level diamond quotients

Let N≥5 be prime, Δ=(ℤ/N)^×/{±1}, H≤Δ, and G_H⊂GL₂(Ẑ) the open subgroup whose image modulo N is {[[a,b],[0,d]] : a mod ±1 ∈ H}. Let X_H^{MC} be Tau Ceti ModularCurves Layer 10's compactified coarse curve over ℤ[1/N], the normalization of ℙ¹_{ℤ[1/N]} in Y₁(N)/H. Then the restriction of X_{G_H} to ℤ[1/N] is isomorphic to X_H^{MC} by a unique isomorphism over ℙ¹_{ℤ[1/N]} restricting on 𝔸¹ to the identification of Y_{G_H} with Y₁(N)/H. The isomorphism matches the cusp divisors, the diamond action of Δ/H, the map X₁(N)→X₀(N) (the cases H=1 and H=Δ) and the cyclotomic structure (both over ℤ). The proof checks the recognition hypotheses on X_{G_H}[1/N]: it is normal, finite over ℙ¹_{ℤ[1/N]}, contains Y_{G_H}[1/N] as a dense open, and each of its irreducible components meets it. No new compactification, cusp width or Shimura covering is built for this class; those stay Layer 10's.

Hypotheses and conventions:

- N≥5 prime; H≤(ℤ/N)^×/{±1}; base ℤ[1/N].
- X_H^{MC} is the Layer 10 carrier, built on Mathlib's relative normalization.

Sources:

- [Tau Ceti contributors, Roadmap: modular curves, following Katz–Mazur (Tau Ceti roadmap ModularCurves)](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/tree/main/TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves), Layer 10, items 1–2: For N≥5 prime and H≤Δ, X_H is the normalization of ℙ¹_j over ℤ[1/N] in Y_H=Y₁(N)/H, with cusps the scheme-theoretic fibre over ∞; the recognition criterion requires finiteness, normality, schematic density and every component meeting Y_H.

Proof route:

### 17. Level maps between coarse curves

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/coarse-level-maps` (construction).

For open level subgroups H′⊂H the finite morphism 𝒳_{H′}→𝒳_H induces f_{H′,H}:X_{H′}→X_H over ℙ¹_ℤ, finite and surjective of degree [±H:±H′], with f_{H,H}=id and f_{H′,H}∘f_{H″,H′}=f_{H″,H}. For g∈GL₂(Ẑ), conjugation gives c_g:X_H≅X_{g⁻¹Hg}. The normaliser N(H̄) of H̄ in GL₂(ℤ/n) acts on X_H on the right through N(H̄)/±H̄, by (E,φH̄)↦(E,φgH̄) on Y_H[1/n] extended by normalization; on X₁(N) the element [[d,0],[0,1]] acts as the diamond operator P↦dP and −I acts trivially. These maps are compatible with the cyclotomic structures, send the cusp of a double coset ⟨±u⟩g H̄′ to that of ⟨±u⟩g H̄, and multiply widths by the local degree. Over ℤ[1/n] each f_{H′,H} is finite flat between smooth curves; over ℤ it is flat at every point of X_{H′} over which X_H is regular. A coarse map is not a substitute for a stack correspondence: Hecke correspondences are R14.1's.

Hypotheses and conventions:

- H′⊂H open; n with Γ(n)⊂H′.
- Flatness assertions at bad primes require regularity of X_H at the image point.

Sources:

- [Kęstutis Česnavičius, A modular description of 𝒳₀(n)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.07475), §4.1.2, p.28: H′⊂H gives the finite X(1)-morphism 𝒳_{H′}→𝒳_H from the map of level structures; regularity of 𝒳_H would make these maps flat by EGA IV₂ 6.1.5.
- [Tau Ceti contributors, Roadmap: modular curves, following Katz–Mazur (Tau Ceti roadmap ModularCurves)](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/tree/main/TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves), §9B, last paragraph; Layer 10, item 3: The diamond action on [Γ₁(N)] is P↦aP; the covering Y₁(N)→Y₀(N) is Galois with group (ℤ/N)^×/±1 and extends to X₁(N)→X₀(N) along the normalizations.
- [Brian Conrad, Arithmetic moduli of generalized elliptic curves](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/papers/kmpaper.pdf), Lemma 4.2.3, p.36: Contraction maps between level stacks such as M_{Γ₁(N)}→M_{Γ₀(N)} are finite flat surjective of constant rank.

Proof route:

1. The stack map is levelStack.map; the coarse universal property gives f_{H′,H} and the functor laws (SchemeAndStackFoundations SF.1 coarse-moduli-space).
2. The degree is the ratio of the j-degrees from coarse-degree-and-cusp-divisor.
3. The normaliser acts on Y_H[1/n] by right translation of frames; the action extends to X_H by the universal property of normalization over ℙ¹, and −I acts trivially on the coarse space because [−1] is an isomorphism (E,φH̄)≅(E,φ(−I)H̄).
4. Compatibility with the cyclotomic structure follows from e_n((P,Q)g)=e_n(P,Q)^{det g}; the cusp statement from coarse-cusps-away-from-the-level.
5. Flatness: finite morphisms from a Cohen–Macaulay scheme of pure dimension two to a regular one are flat (Stacks 00R4).

### 18. Coarse curves as quotients

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/coarse-level-quotients` (theorem).

Let H′⊂H be open level subgroups with H′ normal in H. Then H/H′ acts on X_{H′} over X_H through the normaliser action, and X_H is the categorical and geometric quotient X_{H′}/(H/H′); in particular X_H=X(n)/H̄ for Γ(n)⊂H. For a ring A the comparison (X_{H′}⊗A)/(H/H′)→X_{H′}/(H/H′)⊗A is an isomorphism if A is flat over ℤ or if the order of the image of H/H′ in Aut(X_{H′}) is invertible in A; in general it is a universal homeomorphism. Neither side need be the coarse space of the base-changed stack 𝒳_H⊗A when A is not flat (coarse-base-change-failure).

Hypotheses and conventions:

- H′⊴H open; A an arbitrary ring for the comparison map.

Sources:

- [Kęstutis Česnavičius, A modular description of 𝒳₀(n)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.07475), Lemma 6.2, p.63: For Γ(n)⊂H, X_H is the categorical quotient X(n)/H̄.
- [Kęstutis Česnavičius, Coarse base change fails for some modular curves](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.01249), §1.2, p.1: The ring of invariants of a finite group commutes with arbitrary base change when the group order is invertible.
- [Tau Ceti contributors, Roadmap: modular curves, following Katz–Mazur (Tau Ceti roadmap ModularCurves)](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/tree/main/TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves), §9A, Remark 7.1.4 statement: For a ring map R→R′ the comparison of quotients is an isomorphism for flat R′, for |H| invertible in R′, or for a free action, and is in general surjective and radicial.

Proof route:

1. The quotient X_{H′}/(H/H′) exists: X_{H′} is projective, so every finite orbit lies in an affine open (SchemeAndStackFoundations SF.1 finite-group-quotient).
2. Over ℤ both X_{H′}/(H/H′) and X_H are normal and finite over ℙ¹_ℤ and agree over the elliptic open over ℤ[1/n], where Y_H[1/n]=[Y_{H′}[1/n]/(H/H′)] (level-h-structures) and coarse spaces of finite quotients are invariant quotients; so they coincide by finite-normal-recognition (Česnavičius Lemma 6.2, citing Deligne–Rapoport IV.3.10(iii)). The quotient stack [𝒳_{H′}/(H/H′)] is not used: it need not be 𝒳_H at the cusps (level-stack-geometry (c)).
3. Formation of rings of invariants commutes with flat base change, and with every base change when the group order is invertible; in general the comparison is integral, surjective and radicial.

Prerequisites: `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/coarse-level-maps`; `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/level-stack-geometry`; `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/finite-normal-recognition`; `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/integral-coarse-curves`; `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.1/finite-group-quotient`; `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.1/finite-quotient-coarse`; `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves#0c-finite-quotients-and-torsors`; `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/level-h-structures`; `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves#9a-katzmazur-quotient-problems`.

Acceptance:

- X₀(N)=X₁(N)/((ℤ/N)^×/±1) for N≥3.
- X_{C_s⁺(ℓ)}=X_{C_s(ℓ)}/(ℤ/2).

### 19. Failure of coarse base change at Γ₁(4) in characteristic 2

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/coarse-base-change-failure` (theorem).

Let H be an open level subgroup for which there is an open substack U⊂(Y_H)_{ℤ_{(2)}} such that the geometric points of U_ℚ have trivial automorphism groups and some nonempty open U′⊂U_{𝔽₂} has inertia the constant group {±1}. Then the base change to 𝔽₂ of the coarse map π:𝒳_H→X_H is not the coarse map of (𝒳_H)_{𝔽₂}, and the failure persists on every ℤ_{(2)}-fibrewise dense open substack. This applies to H=Γ₁(4): on the open of Y₁(4)_{𝔽₂} where the subgroup generated by the point of order 4 is of multiplicative type and j≠0, the inversion [−1] fixes the Drinfeld ℤ/4-structure, while over ℚ the problem is rigid. Hence Proposition 6.4(b)'s requirement that gcd(6,n) be invertible cannot be dropped in general.

Hypotheses and conventions:

- The two conditions (i) generic rigidity over ℚ and (ii) constant inertia {±1} on a dense open of the 𝔽₂-fibre.

Sources:

- [Kęstutis Česnavičius, Coarse base change fails for some modular curves](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.01249), Theorem 2.1 and Remark 2.2, p.3; Theorem 3.2, p.5: Under the rigidity and constant-{±1}-inertia hypotheses the 𝔽₂ base change of the coarse map of 𝒳_H is not a coarse map, even on any fibrewise dense open; Γ₁(4) satisfies them.
- [Kęstutis Česnavičius, A modular description of 𝒳₀(n)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.07475), Remark 6.6, p.64: For some H the invertibility of gcd(6,n) in Proposition 6.4(b) cannot be removed, e.g. Γ₁(4) over 𝔽₂.

Proof route:

1. Reduce to the regular part of U and pass to an auxiliary odd level p∤n where the stack becomes a scheme; the coarse space of U_{𝔽₂} is the quotient of a scheme by an action in which {±1} acts trivially, while the base change of the coarse space of U is computed from the rigid generic fibre, and the two differ (Česnavičius 2016, Theorem 2.1).
2. For Γ₁(4): over ℤ[1/2] the problem is rigid (Katz–Mazur 2.7.4); over the locus in characteristic 2 where the subgroup G of order 4 generated by α is of multiplicative type, the image of α(1) in G/G₂ generates G/G₂, which is the Frobenius kernel of E/G₂, and the scheme of generators of G/G₂ is the zero section, so α(1) lies in G₂ and 2·α(1)=0; hence [−1] preserves α (Theorem 3.2).

Prerequisites: `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/deligne-rapoport-level-stack`; `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/fibrewise-coarse-base-change`; `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/legal-coarse-base-change`; `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/coarse-local-structure`; `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.1/keel-mori`.

Acceptance:

- The same failure holds for Γ₁(4)∩Γ₀(4n) and for balanced Γ₁(4) (Česnavičius 2016, Theorems 4.2 and 5.2).

### 20. Cartan modular curves

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/cartan-level-curves` (construction).

Let ℓ be a prime and H̄⊂GL₂(𝔽_ℓ) a subgroup, with H⊂GL₂(Ẑ) its preimage. The coarse curve X_H is a normal projective curve over ℤ, smooth over ℤ[1/ℓ], of degree [GL₂(𝔽_ℓ):±H̄] over ℙ¹_ℤ, with cyclotomic base ℤ when det H̄=𝔽_ℓ^×. For the Cartan levels write X_s(ℓ)=X_{C_s(ℓ)}, X_s⁺(ℓ)=X_{C_s⁺(ℓ)}, X_ns(ℓ)=X_{C_ns(ℓ)}, X_ns⁺(ℓ)=X_{C_ns⁺(ℓ)}, of degrees ℓ(ℓ+1), ℓ(ℓ+1)/2, ℓ(ℓ−1) and ℓ(ℓ−1)/2 for ℓ odd. Over ℤ[1/ℓ], a level-C_s⁺(ℓ) structure on E is an unordered pair of distinct subgroups of order ℓ (equivalently a split Cartan subalgebra 𝔽_ℓ×𝔽_ℓ of End(E[ℓ])), a level-C_s(ℓ) structure an ordered such pair, a level-C_ns⁺(ℓ) structure a subalgebra of End(E[ℓ]) étale locally conjugate to 𝔽_{ℓ²}, and a level-C_ns(ℓ) structure such a subalgebra with a chosen isomorphism to 𝔽_{ℓ²}. The ordered split Cartan curve is identified with X₀(ℓ²) over ℤ[1/ℓ] by (E,C₁,C₂)↦(E/C₁, [ℓ]⁻¹(C₂)/C₁), and X_s⁺(ℓ) with X₀(ℓ²)/⟨w_{ℓ²}⟩, over ℚ by split-cartan-quotient and over ℤ[1/ℓ] because the ordered identification carries the exchange of C₁ and C₂ to w_{ℓ²}. Every other subgroup of GL₂(𝔽_ℓ), for example the preimage of an exceptional S₄⊂PGL₂(𝔽₁₃), gives its curve by the same construction. Hypotheses and conventions:

- ℓ prime; ℓ odd for the nonsplit Cartan description with a nonsquare ε, ℓ=2 using the order-3 subgroup.

### 21. Rational points of X_H and Galois images

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/coarse-rational-points` (theorem).

Let H be an open level subgroup with −I∈H̄, n with Γ(n)⊂H, and K a field of characteristic not dividing n. If E/K is an elliptic curve whose mod-n image ρ̄_{E,n}(G_K)⊂GL₂(ℤ/n), computed in some frame, is conjugate into H̄, then j(E) is the image of a K-point of Y_H. Conversely, if x∈Y_H(K) has j(x)∉{0,1728} (j(x)≠0 when char K∈{2,3}), then every elliptic curve E/K with j(E)=j(x) has ρ̄_{E,n}(G_K) conjugate into H̄. Without −I∈H̄ the converse holds for a suitable quadratic twist of E only; at j=0 or 1728 a K-point need not come from a curve with image in H̄. For H=C_s⁺(ℓ) or C_ns⁺(ℓ) this gives the correspondence between non-cuspidal rational points of X_s⁺(ℓ), X_ns⁺(ℓ) with j∉{0,1728} and elliptic curves over ℚ with image in the Cartan normaliser, with no twist. In particular the fibres of X_s⁺(13) over j=0 and j=1728 contain ℚ-points, coming from y²=x³+1 and y²=x³−x, whose mod-13 images lie in a split Cartan normaliser because 13 splits in ℚ(√−3) and in ℚ(i).

Hypotheses and conventions:

- −I∈H̄ for the converse without twist; char K∤n; j(x)∉{0,1728}.

Sources:

- [David Zywina, On the possible images of the mod ℓ representations associated to elliptic curves over ℚ](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.07660), Proposition 3.3, p.15; proof in §3.5, pp.17–19: For G containing −I with surjective determinant and E/ℚ with j_E∉{0,1728}, ρ_{E,N}(G_ℚ) is conjugate into G if and only if j_E∈π_G(X_G(ℚ)).
- [Jennifer S. Balakrishnan, Netan Dogra, J. Steffen Müller, Jan Tuitman and Jan Vonk, Explicit Chabauty–Kim for the split Cartan modular curve of level 13](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v189-n3-p06-s.pdf), §1, pp.887–888, Theorem 1.2: The rational points of the normaliser-of-split-Cartan curve decide which non-CM elliptic curves over ℚ have mod-ℓ image in a split Cartan normaliser; for ℓ=13 there are none.

Proof route:

1. The coarse map is bijective on K̄-points (SchemeAndStackFoundations SF.1 coarse-moduli-space) and Gal(K̄/K)-equivariant, so x∈Y_H(K) is a Galois-stable isomorphism class of pairs (E_{K̄},φH̄).
2. Given E/K with j(E)=j(x)∉{0,1728}, Aut(E_{K̄})={±1} (Tau Ceti WeierstrassCurve.eq_one_or_eq_negVariableChange_of_smul_eq); stability means σ(φ)H̄=±φH̄=φH̄ for every σ, using −I∈H̄, i.e. φ⁻¹∘σ∘φ=ρ̄_{E,n}(σ)∈H̄ in the frame φ.
3. Conversely, if ρ̄(G_K)⊂gH̄g⁻¹ in a frame φ, the class (φg)H̄ is Galois-stable, hence a K-point of the finite étale scheme Isom/H̄ (Galois descent for finite étale schemes, upstream 0D), i.e. a K-point of the stack Y_H over j(E), which maps to a K-point of Y_H.
4. Without −I∈H̄, stability up to ±1 defines a quadratic character by which E must be twisted.
5. For j=0,1728 the larger automorphism group allows Galois-stable classes not realised by a K-rational structure on any curve with that j-invariant; CM points are therefore checked separately, here by the splitting of 13 in the CM fields.

Prerequisites: `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/level-h-structures`; `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/affine-open-identification`; `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/coarse-local-structure`; `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/cartan-level-curves`; `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.1/coarse-moduli-space`; `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves#0d-finite-étale-schemes-and-galois-actions`; `tauceti:WeierstrassCurve.eq_one_or_eq_negVariableChange_of_smul_eq`.

Acceptance:

### 22. Atkin–Lehner quotient curves X₀⁺(N)

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/atkin-lehner-quotient-curves` (construction).

For N≥2 let w_N be the Fricke involution of X₀(N)_ℚ (R14.1 exact-divisor-atkin-lehner with d=N), extended to an involution of the normal ℤ-curve X₀(N) as in the proof. Define X₀⁺(N):=X₀(N)/⟨w_N⟩, the quotient of the projective normal ℤ-curve X₀(N) by the group of order two. It is a normal projective ℤ-curve, flat with fibres of pure dimension one, smooth over ℤ[1/N], with a finite surjective quotient map π_N:X₀(N)→X₀⁺(N) of degree 2 whose formation commutes with flat base change and with base change to ℤ[1/2]-algebras. Over ℤ[1/N] its elliptic part is the coarse space of unordered pairs {φ,φ^∨} of mutually dual cyclic N-isogenies. The j-map of X₀(N) does not descend: the functions j+j∘w_N and j·(j∘w_N) do, and the map X₀⁺(N)→ℙ¹ they define with the modular polynomial is not the j-line. For N=ℓ² it is X_s⁺(ℓ) (split-cartan-quotient).

Hypotheses and conventions:

- N≥2; w_N as in R14.1 on X₀(N)_ℚ, extended to X₀(N) over ℤ in the first proof step.
- Base change beyond flat and ℤ[1/2]-algebras is not asserted.

Sources:

- [Jennifer S. Balakrishnan, Netan Dogra, J. Steffen Müller, Jan Tuitman and Jan Vonk, Explicit Chabauty–Kim for the split Cartan modular curve of level 13](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v189-n3-p06-s.pdf), §6, p.926; proof of Theorem 6.6, p.929: X₀⁺(ℓ²), the quotient of X₀(ℓ²) by w_{ℓ²}, is ℚ-isomorphic to the normaliser-of-split-Cartan curve, and the quotient of a semistable model by w_{ℓ²} is used for it.
- [Benedict H. Gross and Don B. Zagier, Heegner points and derivatives of L-series](https://people.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/zagier/files/doi/10.1007/BF01388809/fulltext.pdf), Chapter I, §1, p.226: Over a field of characteristic zero the points of X₀(N) are cyclic N-isogenies of (generalized) elliptic curves, and the function field is ℚ(j,j′) with Φ_N(j,j′)=0.
- [Tau Ceti contributors, Roadmap: modular curves, following Katz–Mazur (Tau Ceti roadmap ModularCurves)](https://github.com/TauCetiProject/TauCetiRoadmap/tree/main/TauCetiRoadmap/ModularCurves), §9D, smoothness of finite quotients: A finite quotient of a smooth affine relative curve over a regular base is a smooth relative curve, without invertibility of the group order.

Proof route:

1. Extension of w_N over ℤ: Φ_N is monic of degree ψ(N) in each variable and symmetric (modular-polynomial), so the closure in ℙ¹_ℤ×ℙ¹_ℤ of the image of (j,j′) meets j′=∞ only over j=∞ and is finite over ℙ¹_ℤ by either projection. Hence the normalization of ℙ¹_ℤ×ℙ¹_ℤ in Y₀(N)_ℚ is finite and normal over ℙ¹_ℤ via j and via j′=j∘w_N, so it is X₀(N) for both projections (finite-normal-recognition), and the involution of ℙ¹×ℙ¹ exchanging the factors, which w_N covers on Y₀(N)_ℚ, extends w_N to X₀(N) by functoriality of relative normalization (Stacks 035J).
2. X₀(N) is projective, so ⟨w_N⟩-orbits lie in affine opens; the geometric quotient exists and π_N is finite surjective of degree 2 (SchemeAndStackFoundations SF.1 finite-group-quotient).
3. Normality and flatness pass to the invariant rings; smoothness over ℤ[1/N] of the quotient of a smooth relative curve by a finite group holds without invertibility of the order (upstream 9D, smoothness of finite quotients, applied on invariant affine opens).
4. Invariants commute with flat base change and with base change on which 2 is invertible.
5. Moduli over ℤ[1/N]: w_N sends (E,C) to (E/C,E[N]/C), i.e. φ to its dual up to the identification of E/C, so orbits are unordered dual pairs.
6. Symmetric functions of j and j∘w_N are invariant; j itself is not, since w_N swaps j and j′ and j≠j′ on the generic fibre.

### 23. Proper rigidifying covers and coherent finiteness

Target `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/proper-rigidifying-covers` (theorem).

Let m=m₁m₂ with coprime m₁,m₂≥3. Then 𝒳_{Γ(m)} is a projective ℤ-scheme and its contraction to X(1) is representable, finite and locally free of rank |GL₂(ℤ/m)|. For every open level subgroup H the fibre product P_H:=𝒳_H×_{X(1)}𝒳_{Γ(m)} is a projective ℤ-scheme with a finite locally free surjection p:P_H→𝒳_H of rank |GL₂(ℤ/m)|, equivariant for GL₂(ℤ/m) acting on the second factor. Consequently: (a) for every quasi-coherent sheaf F on 𝒳_H, H⁰(𝒳_H,F) is the equaliser of the two pullback maps from H⁰(P_H,p*F) to the sections over P_H×_{𝒳_H}P_H, and, when F is locally free, it is H⁰(P_H,p*F)^{GL₂(ℤ/m)}, because p is finite locally free, a GL₂(ℤ/m)-torsor over the elliptic locus over ℤ[1/m], and 𝒳_H is normal; p is not a torsor over all of 𝒳_H[1/m], being ramified along the cusps; (b) for coherent F, H⁰(𝒳_H,F) is a finitely generated ℤ-module whose formation commutes with flat base change ℤ→A, and over ℤ[1/m] every H^i(𝒳_H,F) is finitely generated over ℤ[1/m]; (c) for every ring A, a section of F over 𝒳_H⊗A is the same as a section of p*F over P_H⊗A whose two pullbacks agree (effective descent along the finite locally free p). No descent of F to the coarse space X_H is asserted: ω^{⊗k} for odd k and stabilisers acting nontrivially do not descend.

Hypotheses and conventions:

- m₁,m₂≥3 coprime; F quasi-coherent, coherent for finiteness.
- The invariant description of sections is for locally free F; over ℤ the equaliser is used for quasi-coherent F. Higher coherent cohomology over ℤ is not asserted here.

Sources:

- [Kęstutis Česnavičius, A modular description of 𝒳₀(n)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.07475), Proposition 4.3.4, p.38; Proposition 4.3.6, p.39: The contraction X(nm)→X(n) is representable finite locally free of rank #GL₂(ℤ/nm)/#GL₂(ℤ/n); X(n) is a projective scheme unless n=p^s or 2p^s.
- [Brian Conrad, Arithmetic moduli of generalized elliptic curves](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/papers/kmpaper.pdf), Theorem 4.2.1(2) and Remark 4.2.2, pp.35–36: For enough level the moduli stacks are schemes and coincide with the Katz–Mazur projective schemes; for d|N with d≥3 invertible the full-level stack is a scheme with ample boundary.

Proof route:

1. 𝒳_{Γ(m)} is a projective scheme because m is not of the form p^s or 2p^s (standard-level-stack-identifications), and its contraction to X(1) is representable finite locally free of rank |GL₂(ℤ/m)| (Česnavičius Proposition 4.3.4 with n=1).
2. P_H is finite over the projective scheme 𝒳_{Γ(m)} because 𝒳_H→X(1) is representable and finite, so P_H is a projective scheme; p is the base change of 𝒳_{Γ(m)}→X(1).
3. (a),(c): fpqc descent of quasi-coherent sheaves and their sections along the finite locally free surjection p (SchemeAndStackFoundations SF.1 qcoh-fpqc-descent, stack-quasi-coherent).
4. (b): H⁰(𝒳_H,F) is a submodule of the finitely generated H⁰(P_H,p*F) on a projective ℤ-scheme, and an equaliser of flat-base-change-compatible modules; for every i, H^i(𝒳_H,F) is the abutment of the descent spectral sequence of the finite locally free cover p, with E₁ terms the coherent cohomology of the (a+1)-fold fibre products of P_H over 𝒳_H, which are projective schemes finite over P_H; only finitely many terms contribute to each degree, so H^i(𝒳_H,F) is finitely generated (requested from SchemeAndStackFoundations SF.1).
5. Invariants: P_H is flat over 𝒳_H (base change of 𝒳_{Γ(m)}→X(1)) and GL₂(ℤ/m) acts on it over 𝒳_H through 𝒳_{Γ(m)}; over the elliptic locus over ℤ[1/m] it is the GL₂(ℤ/m)-torsor of frames (level-h-structures), so (p_*𝒪_{P_H})^{GL₂(ℤ/m)} is a finite torsion-free 𝒪_{𝒳_H}-algebra equal to 𝒪_{𝒳_H} on a dense open, hence equal to 𝒪_{𝒳_H} by normality, and (p_*p*F)^{GL₂(ℤ/m)}=F for F locally free. Along the cusps p is ramified (index m in the q-expansion charts of R13.3 formal-level-cusp-charts), so X(1)[1/m] is not [X(m)[1/m]/GL₂(ℤ/m)] (that quotient is Ell_m[1/m]).

Prerequisites: `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/standard-level-stack-identifications`; `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/level-stack-geometry`; `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/deligne-rapoport-level-stack`; `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.1/qcoh-fpqc-descent`; `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.1/stack-quasi-coherent`; `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4a/level-h-structures`; `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.3/formal-level-cusp-charts`; `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.1`.

Acceptance:

- For H=Γ₀(N), weight-k modular forms of level Γ₀(N) over ℤ form a finitely generated ℤ-module, computed as the GL₂(ℤ/m)-invariants of H⁰(P_H,p*ω^{⊗k}) on a projective ℤ-scheme.
- Over ℤ the sheaf ω on X(1) has H⁰=0, since −1 acts on it by −1 and H⁰ is torsion-free; over 𝔽₂ the Hasse invariant is a nonzero section of ω, so H⁰ does not commute with the non-flat base change ℤ→𝔽₂. ω is a line bundle on the stack that does not come from ℙ¹_ℤ.

## Source issues

- `ModularCurvesPartII/E401` (gap, Benedict H. Gross and Don B. Zagier, Chapter III, §1, the paragraph after (1.3), p.255; the stack defined on p.254; Invent. Math. 84 (1986), published version (MPIM scan)). The stack used is that of cyclic N-isogenies of generalized elliptic curves whose kernel meets every component of each geometric fibre, cyclic in the Cartier-divisor sense; the source then asserts that at every geometric point the strict henselization of the coarse scheme X is the quotient of the strict henselization of this stack by Aut_k(x)/{±1}, with Aut_k(x) the group of automorphisms of the pair, and derives Proposition (1.4) from it. Correction: For N divisible by p² this is the naive Γ₀(N) stack, which is not Deligne–Mumford at the cusps of residue characteristic p, so it has no étale strict henselization there and the group Aut_k(x) of k-points misses its infinitesimal automorphisms. The statement holds for Česnavičius's refined stack 𝒳₀(N), which is Deligne–Mumford and regular and has the same coarse space; with that stack the argument for Proposition (1.4) goes through (Česnavičius Theorem 6.7 recovers the regularity away from the points with j=0 or 1728 in characteristics dividing N, and Česnavičius §6 reports that Edixhoven 1990 §1.2 computes the completions along the cusps). Reason: On the standard p-gon over 𝔽_p the infinitesimal μ_p of automorphisms fixing the identity component stabilises suitable ample cyclic subgroups of order N whose p-part is non-étale and disconnected, so the automorphism group scheme of that point is not étale (Česnavičius arXiv:1511.07475v2 §1.2, p.2; Conrad 2007 §1.1, p.1). The coarse spaces of the naive and refined stacks coincide (Česnavičius Proposition 6.9), so the coarse scheme X and the conclusion of (1.4) are unaffected.
- `ModularCurvesPartII/E113`, recorded in the R12.1 part, applies to targets 4, 5 and 23: Česnavičius Proposition 4.3.6 omits N=1 from the levels at which X(N) is not a scheme.
