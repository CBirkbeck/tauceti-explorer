# Roadmap: Scholze’s mod p functor and the mod p cohomology of the Lubin–Tate and Drinfeld towers

**Summary.** Scholze’s functor attaches to a smooth admissible mod p representation π of GLₙ(F) the étale cohomology Sⁱ(π) of a sheaf on projective space built from the Lubin–Tate tower at infinite level, an admissible representation of the units of the division algebra with a Galois action. This roadmap plans the functor and Scholze’s finiteness theorem, the Cartan–Leray spectral sequence of Colmez, Dospinescu and Nizioł comparing it with the cohomology of the tower, Ludwig’s vanishing of S² on principal series, Fust’s continuous cohomology of SL₂, and the resulting finite-length theorem for the mod p cohomology of the Drinfeld tower over ℚ\_p with its consequences for multiplicities of Galois and GL₂-representations.

This roadmap plans Scholze’s functor π ↦ Sⁱ(π) from On the p-adic cohomology of the Lubin–Tate tower (arXiv:1506.04022, Ann. Sci. ENS 51 (2018)) and §§3–4 of Colmez, Dospinescu and Nizioł, Factorisation de la cohomologie étale p-adique de la tour de Drinfeld (Forum Math. Pi 11 (2023), e16), together with the results of other papers that CDN use in full and that no other roadmap plans: Ludwig’s vanishing theorem (Forum Math. Sigma 5 (2017)), Fust’s computation of continuous cohomology (Münster J. Math. 15 (2022)), Paškūnas’s comparison of S¹ on Hecke-torsion parts (J. Inst. Math. Jussieu 21 (2022)), the globalisation of Gee–Kisin and Dospinescu–Paškūnas–Schraen, Strauch’s connected components and Moore’s computation for SL₂. Its end theorems are Scholze’s finiteness theorem (SZ.1), the Cartan–Leray spectral sequence and comparison map for S¹ (SZ.2), Ludwig’s vanishing (SZ.3), the key local proposition (SZ.4), the finite length of the mod p cohomology of the arithmetic Drinfeld levels over ℚ\_p (SZ.5) and the finiteness and vanishing of multiplicities, including the geometric multiplicities Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) and the smooth vectors on the Lubin–Tate side (SZ.6). Locators to CDN refer to the published version, whose printed page equals the PDF page; locators to Scholze refer to arXiv v1, whose numbering CDN follow.

## Conventions

- F is a finite extension of ℚ\_p with ring of integers O\_F, uniformiser ϖ and residue field of cardinality q; n ≥ 1; D is the central division algebra over F of invariant 1/n; G = GLₙ(F) (GL₂(F) from SZ.2 on), Ǧ = D^×, Ǧ\_n = 1 + 𝔪\_D^n, G\_n = 1 + ϖ^n M₂(O\_F), and G′ = G/diag(ϖ, ϖ)^ℤ; for F = ℚ\_p we take ϖ = p. F̆ is the completion of the maximal unramified extension, C a complete algebraically closed extension of F̆, by default ℂ\_p.
- M\_{LT,K} is the Lubin–Tate tower over F̆ with all quasi-isogeny heights, M\_∞ = M\_{LT,∞,ℂ\_p} ≅ M\_{Dr,∞,ℂ\_p}; GLₙ(F) acts on the right on the tower, and functions are made left-equivariant through g ↦ g⁻¹. M^ϖ\_{n,K} is the level-n Drinfeld space modulo ϖ^ℤ over a finite extension K of F (ET.6a/rank-two-arithmetic-descent).
- (A, 𝔪) is a complete noetherian local ring with finite residue field of characteristic p; admissibility of A[H]-modules is Emerton’s (SZ.0/compact-lie-admissible-cohomology). L/ℚ\_p is a finite coefficient field with ring of integers O\_L, uniformiser ϖ\_L and residue field k\_L, enlarged when needed.
- The local Artin map sends geometric Frobenius elements to uniformisers; characters of ℚ\_p^× are viewed as characters of Gal\_{ℚ\_p} through it. ε is the reduction of x ↦ x|x| (the mod p cyclotomic character, written ω in Fust, whose character α of the torus is diag(a, d) ↦ ω(a)ω(d)⁻¹); a pair (χ₁, χ₂) of smooth characters ℚ\_p^× → 𝔽̄\_p^× is generic if χ₁χ₂⁻¹ ≠ 1, ε^{±1}, and I(χ₁, χ₂) = Ind\_B^G(χ₂ ⊗ χ₁ε⁻¹).
- π^∨ is the Pontryagin dual of an O\_L-torsion module; Hom^cont between prodiscrete modules carries the topology lim\_j colim\_i Hom(U\_i, V\_j). Continuous group cohomology Hⁱ(G, M) is computed by continuous homogeneous cochains (Mathlib’s continuousCohomology) with discrete or prodiscrete M.
- Almost mathematics is relative to the maximal ideal of O\_C (PerfectoidSpaces P0). Suggested declaration names live in the namespace TauCetiRoadmap.ScholzeFunctor.

## How to read the build

## Layer 0: The Lubin–Tate tower at infinite level as a torsor

The geometric input of Scholze’s functor. **Equivariant étale sites** (X/G)\_ét of a continuous action of a locally profinite group on a locally noetherian analytic adic space or a perfectoid space: the limit formula H^i((X/H₀)\_ét, ℱ) = colim\_H H^i((X/H)\_ét, ℱ) over open H ⊃ H₀, slices, conservativity, the invariant sheaf O⁺\_{X/G}/ϖ, the equivalence (X/J)\_ét ≃ (X\_∞/G × J)\_ét for a profinite torsor tower with perfectoid limit, and the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence H^i\_cont(K, H^j(X, 𝒢)) ⇒ H^{i+j}((X/K)\_ét, 𝒢) (Scholze §2, Lemma 3.12). **The Gross–Hopkins torsor**: the period maps M\_{LT,K} → ℙ^{n−1}\_{F̆} are étale surjective with fibres GLₙ(F)/K, D^×- and Weil-equivariant, and M\_{LT,∞,C} → ℙ^{n−1}\_C is a pro-étale GLₙ(F)-torsor with M\_∞ ×\_{ℙ^{n−1}} M\_∞ ≅ M\_∞ × GLₙ(F). **Strauch’s components**: π₀(M\_{LT,∞,C}) ≅ F^× with (g, d, w) acting by det(g)·Nrd(d)⁻¹·Art\_F(w)⁻¹, and the Drinfeld-side consequence H⁰\_ét(M^p\_{Dr,∞}, k\_L) ≅ C⁰(ℚ\_p^×/p^{2ℤ}, k\_L). **Admissible representations of compact p-adic Lie groups**: admissible A[H]-modules form a Serre subcategory, are Pontryagin dual to finitely generated A[[H]]-modules, and have finite continuous cohomology.

### 0.1 Equivariant étale sites of continuous actions and of towers

**Theorem** (`SZ.0/equivariant-site-toolkit`; planet: Equivariant étale site of a tower). Let G be a locally profinite group acting continuously on X, a locally noetherian analytic adic space or a perfectoid space (X is covered by affinoids Spa(A, A⁺) whose stabiliser H ⊂ G is open and acts continuously on A), and let (X/G)\_ét be the equivariant étale site: objects are adic spaces Y with a continuous G-action and a G-equivariant étale map Y → X, covers are jointly surjective families. Then: (a) (limit formula) if X is qcqs and G is profinite, for every closed subgroup H₀ ⊂ G the topos of (X/H₀)\_ét is the projective limit of the topoi of (X/H)\_ét over the open subgroups H ⊃ H₀, so H^i((X/H₀)\_ét, ℱ) = colim\_{H ⊃ H₀ open} H^i((X/H)\_ét, ℱ) for every sheaf ℱ on (X/G)\_ét; for H₀ = 1 this computes H^i(X\_ét, ℱ). (b) (slices) for H ⊂ G open, U ↦ U ×^H G identifies (X/H)\_ét with the slice of (X/G)\_ét over X ×^H G. (c) (conservativity and O⁺/ϖ) a pointed sheaf on (X/G)\_ét is trivial iff its pullback to X\_ét is trivial; if X lies over Spa(K, O\_K) with pseudo-uniformiser ϖ, U ↦ ((O⁺\_X/ϖ)(U))^G is a sheaf O⁺\_{X/G}/ϖ on (X/G)\_ét whose pullback to X\_ét is O⁺\_X/ϖ. (d) (towers) if G is profinite, (X\_H)\_H → X is a G-torsor tower (each X\_H → X a finite étale G/H-torsor for H ⊴ G open, compatibly), X\_∞ is a perfectoid space with X\_∞ ∼ lim\_H X\_H, and a locally profinite group J acts continuously on X and on every X\_H commuting with G, then pullback (X/J)\_ét → (X\_∞/G × J)\_ét is an equivalence of sites, and for J trivial O⁺\_X/p corresponds to O⁺\_{X\_∞/G}/p under (X\_∞/G)\_ét ≃ X\_ét. (e) (Hochschild–Serre) if X is qcqs and K is a profinite group acting continuously, for every sheaf 𝒢 of abelian groups on (X/K)\_ét there is a spectral sequence E₂^{ij} = H^i\_cont(K, H^j(X\_ét, 𝒢)) ⇒ H^{i+j}((X/K)\_ét, 𝒢), with the K-modules H^j(X\_ét, 𝒢) smooth (discrete).

*Hypotheses.* G locally profinite and the action continuous in the sense of Scholze’s Definition 2.1; X locally noetherian analytic (locally Spa of a strongly noetherian Tate ring) or perfectoid. In (a) and (e) X is qcqs and the group profinite; in (d) X is a locally noetherian analytic adic space over Spa(ℚ\_p, ℤ\_p) (not perfectoid), G is profinite, J locally profinite, and X\_∞ ∼ lim X\_H in the sense of Scholze–Weinstein Definition 2.4.1; the O⁺/p correspondence in (d) is asserted for J trivial, as in the source. Scholze spells out the locally noetherian case and notes that perfectoid X works the same way; he states (e) for ℙ^{n−1}\_C, and the proof applies verbatim to any qcqs X, smoothness of the K-modules coming from (a).

*Proof sketch.* (a) Show that qcqs objects of (X/H₀)\_ét form the 2-colimit of the qcqs objects of (X/H)\_ét over open H ⊃ H₀: rational subsets have open stabilisers (Scholze Lemma 2.2), and actions on finite étale covers spread out from H₀ to an open H by the approximation theorem for finite étale algebras over completed filtered colimits of complete Tate rings (Elkik–Gabber–Ramero; Scholze Theorem 2.4 and Lemma 2.3, Corollary 2.5); then apply the limit theorem for fibred coherent topoi (SGA 4 VI 8.2.3) to get the topos and the cohomology colimit. (b) The inverse functor sends a G-equivariant U → X ×^H G to its fibre over X = X ×^H H; covers correspond. (c) Conservativity: a section trivial on X\_ét is trivial on (X/H)\_ét for some open H by (a), and U ×^H G → U is a cover. The O⁺/ϖ sheaf: take G-invariants in the sheaf property; by continuity of the action every section over a qcqs U is invariant under an open subgroup, which gives the pullback by (a). (d) Reduce to J compact and X, X\_H, X\_∞ qcqs; quasi-compact opens descend because |X\_∞| = lim |X\_H|, and finite étale covers descend by the approximation theorem again; finite étale descent along X\_H → X gives the equivalence. For O⁺/p compute the pullback along (X\_∞)\_ét → X\_ét with Scholze’s comparison for tilde-limits (perfectoid P5/P7) and check it on X\_∞ by (c). IgusaVarieties IG.4 supplies only the site (X/G)\_ét with its pullback and slice functors; statements (a)–(e) are proved here. (e) For K′ ⊴ K open, X ×^{K′} K → X is a cover in (X/K)\_ét whose Čech nerve is X × (K/K′)^•, giving a Cartan–Leray spectral sequence H^{i}(K/K′, H^j((X/K′)\_ét, 𝒢)) ⇒ H^{i+j}((X/K)\_ét, 𝒢); pass to the colimit over K′ using (a), which turns the colimit of finite-group cohomology into continuous cohomology of K with discrete coefficients (ProfiniteCohomology).

**Checks.**

- For G trivial (X/G)\_ét is X\_ét and (e) degenerates to H^j(X\_ét, 𝒢).
- For K finite acting trivially on a qcqs X, (e) is the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence of the finite group K acting trivially on RΓ(X\_ét, 𝒢), and H^i((X/K)\_ét, 𝒢) = H^i(K, RΓ(X\_ét, 𝒢)) by (a)–(b).
- For the Lubin–Tate tower over P^{n−1} with J = D^× (SZ.0/gross-hopkins-torsor), (d) gives (P^{n−1}/D^×)\_ét ≃ (M\_{LT,∞}/GL\_n(O\_F) × D^×)\_ét after restriction to height-zero components, the site on which Scholze’s sheaf lives.

SCH18, Definition 2.6, Lemma 2.7, Propositions 2.8–2.9, Lemmas 2.10–2.11, pp. 7–9: The equivariant étale site, its qcqs objects, the limit formula over open subgroups, slices, the invariant O⁺/ϖ sheaf and conservativity. SCH18, Lemma 3.12, p. 17: The Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence from continuous cohomology of a compact open subgroup to cohomology of the equivariant site, as a colimit of Cartan–Leray spectral sequences (stated there for projective space).

### 0.2 The Gross–Hopkins period map as a pro-étale GLₙ(F)-torsor

**Theorem** (`SZ.0/gross-hopkins-torsor`; planet: Gross–Hopkins period torsor). Let F/ℚ\_p be finite, n ≥ 1, D the central division algebra over F of invariant 1/n, G = GLₙ(F), and (M\_{LT,K})\_K the Lubin–Tate tower over F̆ (all quasi-isogeny heights), indexed by the compact open subgroups K ⊂ G, with D^× acting on each level, G acting on the tower (g : M\_{LT,K} ≅ M\_{LT,g⁻¹Kg}) and Weil descent data; for a compact open K not of the form 1 + ϖ^m Mₙ(O\_F), M\_{LT,K} is the quotient of M\_{LT,K\_m} (K\_m ⊂ K normal) by the free action of the finite group K/K\_m. (a) The Gross–Hopkins period maps π\_GH : M\_{LT,K} → ℙ^{n−1}\_{F̆} are compatible in K, étale and surjective, with fibres GLₙ(F)/K over every geometric point; they are D^×-equivariant when ℙ^{n−1}\_{F̆} is identified with the Brauer–Severi variety of D (split over F̆), and compatible with the Weil descent data. (b) For K′ ⊂ K compact open with K′ normal in K, M\_{LT,K′} → M\_{LT,K} is a finite étale K/K′-torsor; the infinite level M\_{LT,∞} ∼ lim\_K M\_{LT,K} (preperfectoid over F̆, perfectoid after base change to a complete algebraically closed C) therefore makes M\_{LT,∞} → M\_{LT,K} a profinite K-torsor tower in the sense of SZ.0/equivariant-site-toolkit (d), for every K. (c) After base change to C = ℂ\_p (or any complete algebraically closed C/F̆), write M\_∞ = M\_{LT,∞,C}; there is a G-equivariant isomorphism of diamonds over ℙ^{n−1}\_C, M\_∞ × G → M\_∞ ×\_{ℙ^{n−1}\_C} M\_∞, (x, g) ↦ (x, x·g), and more generally the (k+1)-fold fibre product of M\_∞ over ℙ^{n−1}\_C is M\_∞ × G^k; thus π\_GH : M\_∞ → ℙ^{n−1}\_C is a pro-étale G-torsor whose Čech nerve is M\_∞ × G^•.

*Hypotheses.* n ≥ 1, F/ℚ\_p finite; G is viewed as a locally profinite set, so M\_∞ × G is the disjoint union over the compact open cosets. The torsor statement (c) is a statement about diamonds (or about the equivariant site of SZ.0/equivariant-site-toolkit); the sources assert it with this proof sketch, Scholze noting that a precise formulation needs care and working instead with equivariant sites.

*Proof sketch.* (a) The period map is the Grothendieck–Messing map of ET.6a/period-morphisms, étale on the generic fibre. Surjectivity is Gross–Hopkins’ theorem; in the infinite-level language it says that for the basic one-dimensional EL datum the Grothendieck–Messing period map of M\_{LT,∞} is onto the whole flag variety, equivalently (through the duality ET.6a/el-duality) that the Hodge–Tate period map of the Drinfeld tower is onto ℙ^{n−1}. The fibres: by the infinite-level moduli description (ET.6a/infinite-level-moduli) the points of M\_{LT,∞} over x are the frames of the rational Tate module of one quasi-isogeny class, a GLₙ(F)-torsor, so the fibre at level K is GLₙ(F)/K. D^× acts on the Grothendieck–Messing target through its action on the rational Dieudonné module of the framing object, which is the Brauer–Severi identification; Weil descent is ET.6a/actions-and-weil-descent. Levels K that are not principal congruence subgroups are finite étale quotients by free actions (PerfectoidSpaces P8/free-action-quotient-is-torsor). (b) Change of level: the level structures at K′ over a point at level K form a K/K′-torsor (ET.6a/lubin-tate-tower API levelTorsor); infinite level and perfectoidness are ET.6a/infinite-level-moduli and ET.6a/perfectoid-limit. (c) On the infinite-level moduli description a point of M\_∞ over x is a framed deformation with a trivialisation α of the rational Tate module V ≅ Fⁿ; two points over the same x have the same (G, ρ) up to unique quasi-isogeny and their frames differ by a unique g ∈ GLₙ(F), giving (x, g) ↦ (x, x·g); the inverse is continuous because it is defined on each compact open coset S ⊂ G by finite-level data, and both sides are diamonds (DiamondsAndVStacks D4).

**Checks.**

- For n = 1, ℙ⁰ is a point, the geometric points of M\_{LT,K} form the discrete set F^×/K (Lubin–Tate theory), and (c) is the statement that F^× is an F^×-torsor.
- The fibre of π\_GH over a point at level K = GLₙ(O\_F) has the cardinality of GLₙ(F)/GLₙ(O\_F), the set of O\_F-lattices in Fⁿ.

### 0.3 Connected components of the Lubin–Tate and Drinfeld towers (Strauch)

**Theorem** (`SZ.0/lubin-tate-components`; planet: Connected components of the Lubin–Tate tower). With the notation of SZ.0/gross-hopkins-torsor and C a complete algebraically closed extension of F̆: (a) for m ≥ 0 and K\_m = 1 + ϖ^m Mₙ(O\_F) (K₀ = GLₙ(O\_F)), the geometric connected components of the height-zero part of M\_{LT,K\_m} are in natural bijection with (O\_F/ϖ^m)^×, compatibly with GLₙ(O\_F) × O\_D^× × Gal(C/F̆) acting through (g, b, τ) ↦ det(g)·Nrd(b)⁻¹·χ\_LT(τ)⁻¹ mod 1 + ϖ^m O\_F, χ\_LT the Lubin–Tate character; including all heights, π₀(M\_{LT,K\_m,C}) is a principal homogeneous space under F^×/(1 + ϖ^m O\_F). (b) At infinite level π₀(M\_{LT,∞,C}) ≅ F^× as a topological G × D^× × W\_F-set, where (g, d, w) acts by multiplication by det(g)·Nrd(d)⁻¹·Art\_F(w)⁻¹, Art\_F the local Artin map normalised so that geometric Frobenius elements go to uniformisers. (c) Through the duality isomorphism M\_{LT,∞} ≅ M\_{Dr,∞}, which is GLₙ(F) × D^×-equivariant (no Weil-group compatibility is used here), π₀(M\_{Dr,∞,C}) ≅ F^× as a GLₙ(F) × D^×-set, the two groups acting through the determinant and the reduced norm as in (b); after dividing by diag(ϖ, ϖ)^ℤ (n = 2), which acts on π₀ through det(diag(ϖ, ϖ)) = ϖ², the components form F^×/ϖ^{2ℤ}; for F = ℚ\_p, ϖ = p, H⁰\_ét(M^p\_{Dr,∞}, k\_L) ≅ C⁰(ℚ\_p^×/p^{2ℤ}, k\_L), an induced module C⁰(ℚ\_p^×, k\_L) = Ind\_1^{p^ℤ}C⁰(ℤ\_p^× ⊔ pℤ\_p^×, k\_L) for the action of p^ℤ.

*Hypotheses.* Strauch’s computation is for the height-zero component of the Lubin–Tate space with Drinfeld level structures; heights are added by the action of elements of G of non-zero determinant valuation. Sign conventions: Strauch and LubinTate LT.2 normalise local reciprocity to send arithmetic Frobenius to uniformisers; the formula in (b) is stated with Scholze’s normalisation (geometric Frobenius to uniformisers), the two Artin maps being inverse to each other on Frobenius elements (ClassFieldTheory layer 7). The diagonal F^× acts trivially, consistently with det(z)·Nrd(z)⁻¹ = zⁿ·z⁻ⁿ = 1.

*Proof sketch.* The determinant morphism to the height-one Lubin–Tate tower: the n-th exterior power of the universal formal module is a height-one formal O\_F-module, so a Drinfeld level-ϖ^m structure induces a primitive ϖ^m-torsion point of the Lubin–Tate formal module, giving M\_{K\_m} → Spf(Ŏ\_{F,m}) with Ŏ\_{F,m} the ring of the Lubin–Tate extension of level m (Strauch Theorem 3.3, Theorem 4.3). The fibres of this map over C are connected (Strauch Theorem 4.3 via normality of the deformation rings, Proposition 4.2), so π₀ is Gal(F̆\_m/F̆) ≅ (O\_F/ϖ^m)^× by the Lubin–Tate character (LubinTate LT.2/lt-character, LT.2/reciprocity-comparison). Equivariance: GLₙ(O\_F) acts on the n-th exterior power through det; o\_D^× through Nrd⁻¹ (computed on CM points by local class field theory); the Galois group through the inverse Lubin–Tate character (Strauch Theorem 4.4). Pass to all heights and to infinite level: π₀ of the limit is the limit of the π₀ of the levels (the tower is a tilde-limit), giving F^× with the stated action (Scholze, proof of Proposition 4.7); transport to the Drinfeld tower along ET.6a/el-duality, which exchanges the two groups.

**Checks.**

- For n = 1 the statement is Lubin–Tate theory: π₀(M\_{LT,K\_m,C}) = F^×/(1 + ϖ^m O\_F) with W\_F acting through the Artin map.
- At level K₀ = GLₙ(O\_F) the height-zero part is connected (an open (n − 1)-ball), and π₀(M\_{LT,K₀,C}) ≅ F^×/O\_F^× ≅ ℤ by the height.
- The element diag(ϖ, …, ϖ) ∈ G acts on π₀(M\_{LT,∞,C}) = F^× by multiplication by ϖⁿ, so it moves the height by n.

### 0.4 Admissible representations of compact p-adic Lie groups: Serre subcategory and finite cohomology

**Theorem** (`SZ.0/compact-lie-admissible-cohomology`). Let (A, 𝔪) be a complete noetherian local ring with finite residue field of characteristic p and H a p-adic Lie group; call a smooth A[H]-module V admissible if V = ∪\_{K, i} V^K[𝔪^i] (K ⊂ H compact open, i ≥ 1) and every V^K[𝔪^i] is a finitely generated A/𝔪^i-module. Then: (a) admissible A[H]-modules form an abelian Serre subcategory of the smooth A[H]-modules; for H compact and V killed by a power of 𝔪, V is admissible iff its Pontryagin dual V^∨ is a finitely generated module over the noetherian ring A[[H]]; (b) if H = K is compact and V is admissible and killed by 𝔪^i, the continuous cohomology groups H^j\_cont(K, V) (V discrete) are finitely generated A/𝔪^i-modules for all j ≥ 0, in particular finite; (c) the restriction of an admissible A[H]-module to an open subgroup of H is admissible.

*Hypotheses.* (A, 𝔪) complete noetherian local with finite residue field of characteristic p; H a p-adic Lie group (locally ℚ\_p-analytic), K compact open. Restriction to a closed subgroup that is not open (such as SL₂(F) ⊂ GL₂(F)) need not preserve admissibility without further hypotheses; the case used later (a central character) is treated in SZ.2/fust-cohomology. The sources state (b) for 𝔽\_p-coefficients (Scholze) and O/ϖⁿ-coefficients (Fust), and cite Emerton for (a); the general A/𝔪^i case is the same argument, recorded here.

*Proof sketch.* (a) Noetherianity: by the Cohen structure theorem A is a quotient of W(k)[[x₁, …, x\_r]] (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra R03.1/cohen-series-presentation), so A[[K]] is a quotient of W(k) ⊗\_{ℤ\_p} ℤ\_p[[K × ℤ\_p^r]], noetherian by Lazard’s theorem for the compact p-adic Lie group K × ℤ\_p^r (NE.0/iwasawa-noetherian) and finiteness of W(k) over ℤ\_p. Duality: Pontryagin duality exchanges smooth 𝔪-power-torsion A[K]-modules with compact A[[K]]-modules; for V killed by 𝔪^i, V is admissible iff V[𝔪] is (Emerton Lemma 2.2.11), and V^∨ is finitely generated iff V^∨/𝔪V^∨ = V[𝔪]^∨ is (topological Nakayama), which reduces to the residue field, CC.5/smooth-dual-admissibility. Finitely generated modules over a noetherian ring form a Serre subcategory, and admissibility is tested on one compact open subgroup. For A = O\_L this notion is ModpRepresentations MP.0/torsion-admissibility, and for A = k it agrees with SmoothRepresentations’ admissibility (finite-dimensional invariants). (b) Continuous cohomology of the compact group K with discrete coefficients is the derived functor of K-invariants on smooth modules (SR.0 derived invariants); resolve V^∨ by finite free A/𝔪^i[[K]]-modules (noetherianity), dualise to an injective resolution of V by duals of finite free modules, whose K-invariants are finite A/𝔪^i-modules; so the cohomology is finitely generated (Scholze Lemma 3.13, Fust Lemma 6.2).

**Checks.**

- For K = ℤ\_p and V = 𝔽\_p trivial, H⁰ = H¹ = 𝔽\_p and H^j = 0 for j ≥ 2.
- For V = C⁰(K, 𝔽\_p) (the smooth regular representation, admissible), H^j\_cont(K, V) = 0 for j > 0 and H⁰ = 𝔽\_p (Shapiro).
- The non-admissible V = C⁰(K, 𝔽\_p)^{⊕ℕ} has infinite H⁰ = 𝔽\_p^{⊕ℕ}, so admissibility cannot be dropped in (b).

*Sources.* SCH18, Definition 4.1 and Theorem 4.3, p. 18: Admissible A[G]-modules over a complete noetherian local ring with finite residue field, and Emerton’s result that they form an abelian Serre subcategory of the smooth modules. SCH18, Lemma 3.13 and proof, p. 17: For a compact p-adic Lie group and an admissible smooth 𝔽\_p-representation, continuous cohomology is finite-dimensional, via the derived-functor description, the duality with finitely generated Iwasawa modules and Lazard’s noetherianity. FUST22, Lemma 6.2 and proof: For a compact p-adic analytic group and an admissible representation over O/ϖ^n, the continuous cohomology groups are finitely generated O/ϖ^n-modules, by a resolution of the dual by finite free O/ϖ^n[[K]]-modules.

## Layer 1: Scholze’s functor and its finiteness

**Scholze’s sheaf** ℱ\_π on (ℙ^{n−1}\_{F̆}/D^×)\_ét for a smooth A[GLₙ(F)]-module π: sections are continuous equivariant maps from the pullback of the Gross–Hopkins torsor to π; exact in π, with geometric fibres π. **The functors** Sⁱ(π) = Hⁱ\_ét(ℙ^{n−1}\_C, ℱ\_π) with their D^× × W\_F-actions, as a δ-functor commuting with filtered colimits, and the dual functors Šⁱ. **Almost finiteness** of Hⁱ(V, O⁺/p) → Hⁱ(U, O⁺/p) for U strictly contained in a separated smooth quasicompact V (Scholze Corollary 3.6), and the **Frobenius almost-module lemma** (Scholze 2013 Lemma 2.12). **Scholze’s finiteness theorem** (CDN Theorem 3.1 (1)–(2)): for admissible π over a complete noetherian local ring with finite residue field, Sⁱ(π) is an admissible D^×-module independent of C, Sⁱ(π) ⊗ O\_C ≅^a Hⁱ(ℙ^{n−1}\_C, ℱ\_π ⊗ O⁺), and Sⁱ(π) = 0 for i > 2(n − 1). **Galois action and degree zero** (CDN Theorem 3.1 (3)–(4), DPS Lemmas 7.3–7.4): the Weil action extends to Gal\_F, S⁰(π) = S⁰(π^{SLₙ(F)}) with the stated action through F^×, central characters match, twisting by δ∘det twists Sⁱ by δ⁻¹ ⊠ δ, and for n = 2, S¹(δ∘det) = 0 and S²(δ∘det) is a character.

### 1.1 Scholze’s sheaf ℱ\_π on projective space

**Construction** (`SZ.1/scholze-sheaf`; planet: Scholze’s sheaf ℱ\_π). Let F/ℚ\_p be finite, n ≥ 1, G = GLₙ(F), D the division algebra of invariant 1/n, M\_{LT,∞} → ℙ^{n−1}\_{F̆} the Gross–Hopkins torsor (SZ.0/gross-hopkins-torsor), and (A, 𝔪) a complete noetherian local ring with finite residue field of characteristic p. For a smooth A[G]-module π (every vector has open stabiliser and is killed by a power of 𝔪; π carries the discrete topology), define a presheaf ℱ\_π on the equivariant site (ℙ^{n−1}\_{F̆}/D^×)\_ét by ℱ\_π(U) = the A-module of continuous maps f : |U ×\_{ℙ^{n−1}} M\_{LT,∞}| → π with f(x·g) = g⁻¹·f(x) for g ∈ G and f(d·x) = f(x) for d ∈ D^× (equivalently, after pullback to (ℙ^{n−1})\_ét, ℱ\_π(U) = C⁰\_G(|U\_∞|, π) = (π\_{GH,∗}π̲)^G(U) with U\_∞ = U ×\_{ℙ^{n−1}} M\_{LT,∞}). Then ℱ\_π is a sheaf of A-modules, equivariant for the Weil descent datum; π ↦ ℱ\_π is an exact A-linear functor commuting with filtered colimits; and for every geometric point x̄ of ℙ^{n−1}\_{F̆} with a chosen lift x̃ to M\_{LT,∞}, evaluation at x̃ is an isomorphism (ℱ\_π)\_{x̄} ≅ π, functorial in π (changing x̃ to x̃·g composes with π(g)).

*Hypotheses.* π smooth; the stalk formula and exactness use smoothness (for a non-smooth discrete π the stalk is the submodule of smooth vectors). The right action of G on M\_{LT,∞} is turned into a left action on π-valued functions by g ↦ g⁻¹; D^× acts trivially on π.

*Proof sketch.* Sheaf property: étale covers are open and jointly surjective on topological spaces, so continuous G-equivariant maps glue (Scholze Proposition 3.1); Weil equivariance is inherited from the period map. Pullback to (ℙ^{n−1})\_ét is computed by the limit formula of SZ.0/equivariant-site-toolkit (a): a G-equivariant map on a qcqs U\_∞ is automatically invariant under an open subgroup of D^×. Stalks: for a cofinal system of connected affinoid étale neighbourhoods U\_i of x̄, G acts transitively on π₀(|U\_i,∞|), so evaluation at x̃ is injective; choosing U\_i to factor through M\_{LT,K\_m} (K\_m = 1 + ϖ^m Mₙ(O\_F)) gives a G-equivariant surjection |U\_i,∞| → G/K\_m, through which any K\_m-fixed vector extends, so evaluation is surjective onto π^{K\_m} for every m, hence onto π when π is smooth (Scholze Proposition 3.1). Exactness and colimits: tested on stalks after pullback, using conservativity (SZ.0/equivariant-site-toolkit (c)).

API:

- `scholzeSheaf` (constructor): π ↦ ℱ\_π, a functor from smooth A[GLₙ(F)]-modules to Weil-equivariant sheaves of A-modules on (ℙ^{n−1}\_{F̆}/D^×)\_ét.

- `scholzeSheaf_exact` (other): π ↦ ℱ\_π is exact.
- `scholzeSheaf_colim` (other): π ↦ ℱ\_π commutes with filtered colimits.
- `scholzeSheaf_stalk` (characterisation): For a geometric point x̄ with lift x̃ ∈ M\_{LT,∞}, evaluation at x̃ is an isomorphism (ℱ\_π)\_{x̄} ≅ π natural in π; replacing x̃ by x̃·g composes it with π(g).

### 1.2 Scholze’s functors Sⁱ

**Definition** (`SZ.1/scholze-functor`; planet: Scholze’s mod p functor). With the notation of SZ.1/scholze-sheaf and C a complete algebraically closed extension of F̆ (by default C = ℂ\_p), define for every smooth A[GLₙ(F)]-module π and i ≥ 0 Sⁱ(π) := Hⁱ\_ét(ℙ^{n−1}\_C, ℱ\_π), the étale cohomology of the pullback of ℱ\_π to ℙ^{n−1}\_C. It is an A[D^×]-module (D^× acting through the equivariant structure; Sⁱ(π) = colim\_K Hⁱ((ℙ^{n−1}\_C/K)\_ét, ℱ\_π) over compact open K ⊂ D^×, hence a smooth D^×-module) with a commuting action of the Weil group W\_F (inertia acting through C = ℂ\_p, Frobenius through the Weil descent datum). The Sⁱ form a cohomological δ-functor in π: a short exact sequence of smooth A[G]-modules gives a long exact sequence of A[D^× × W\_F]-modules. They commute with filtered colimits. On compact modules the dual functors Šⁱ(M) := Sⁱ(M^∨)^∨ (M^∨ = Hom\_cont(M, L/O)) form a covariant homological δ-functor commuting with projective limits.

*Hypotheses.* A and π as in SZ.1/scholze-sheaf; when π is admissible, SZ.1/scholze-finiteness shows that Sⁱ(π) does not depend on C. Sⁱ(π) is not the π-isotypic part of the cohomology of the tower: the two are compared in SZ.2/comparison-map.

*Proof sketch.* Define Sⁱ by étale cohomology of the sheaf pulled back to ℙ^{n−1}\_C; the D^×-action comes from the equivariant site and is smooth by the limit formula SZ.0/equivariant-site-toolkit (a). The δ-functor structure follows from exactness of π ↦ ℱ\_π (SZ.1/scholze-sheaf) and the long exact cohomology sequence; compatibility with filtered colimits holds because the étale site of the qcqs space ℙ^{n−1}\_C is coherent, and cohomology on a coherent site commutes with filtered colimits of sheaves (DiamondsAndVStacks D0/filtered-colimits-and-cohomology-on-coherent-sites). The dual Šⁱ: Pontryagin duality (Mathlib’s PontryaginDual for the underlying locally compact abelian groups) is an anti-equivalence between discrete torsion modules and compact modules; projective limits of compact modules go to filtered colimits (DPS §7).

### 1.3 Almost finiteness of restriction to strictly contained opens

**Lemma** (`SZ.1/strict-image-almost-finite`). Let C be a complete algebraically closed extension of ℚ\_p, V a separated smooth quasicompact adic space over Spa(C, O\_C) and U ⊂ V a quasicompact open subset strictly contained in V (for every maximal point x = Spa(K, O\_K) of U and every open bounded valuation subring K⁺ ⊂ K, the map Spa(K, O\_K) → U extends to Spa(K, K⁺) → V). Then for every i ≥ 0 the image of Hⁱ(V\_ét, O⁺/p) → Hⁱ(U\_ét, O⁺/p) is an almost finitely generated O\_C-module.

*Hypotheses.* V separated, smooth and quasicompact; no properness and no global good coordinates are assumed.

*Proof sketch.* Affinoid case with good coordinates (V → 𝕋ⁿ a composite of finite étale maps and rational embeddings, U ⊂ V strict rational): this is the local lemma of the primitive comparison theorem (P8:local-rational/primitive-local-cohomology). **Checks.**

### 1.4 Almost modules with Frobenius structure are almost free (Scholze 2013 Lemma 2.12)

**Lemma** (`SZ.1/frobenius-almost-module-lemma`). Let K be a complete algebraically closed non-archimedean field of characteristic p with valuation ring O and pseudo-uniformiser π, and for every k ≥ 1 let M\_k be an O/π^k-module such that: (i) M₁ is almost finitely generated; (ii) there are maps p\_k : M\_{k+1} → M\_k and q\_k : M\_k → M\_{k+1} with p\_k ∘ q\_k = π, and M₁ → M\_{k+1} → M\_k (the first map q\_k ∘ ⋯ ∘ q₁) is exact in the middle; (iii) there are isomorphisms φ\_k : M\_k ⊗\_{O/π^k, φ} O/π^{pk} ≅ M\_{pk} compatible with the p\_k and q\_k. Then there are r ≥ 0 and isomorphisms of almost modules M\_k^a ≅ (O^a/π^k)^r for all k carrying p\_k to the projection, q\_k to multiplication by π and φ\_k to the coordinatewise Frobenius.

*Hypotheses.* K algebraically closed of characteristic p (in the application, the tilt C^♭ of C, with π = p^♭); almost mathematics relative to the maximal ideal of O.

*Proof sketch.* Attach to an almost finitely generated torsion O-module its length invariant γ (Scholze 2013 Proposition 2.10, an almost version of the length of a finitely generated torsion module over a valuation ring). From (ii), γ(M\_k) ≤ kγ(M₁); from (iii), γ(M\_{pk}) = pγ(M\_k); hence γ(M\_k) = kγ(M₁), the sequences 0 → M₁^a → M\_{k+1}^a → M\_k^a → 0 are exact, and M^a = (lim\_k M\_k)^a is flat with M^a/π almost finitely generated. A flat, almost finitely generated module over the almost-complete O with a Frobenius structure is almost free of finite rank (Scholze 2013 Lemma 2.13 and the classification of φ-modules over O), compatibly with p\_k, q\_k and φ\_k.

**Checks.**

- For M\_k = (O/π^k)^r with the obvious maps the lemma is an identity.
- For M\_k = 0 for all k one gets r = 0.
- The family M\_k = O/π (constant) violates γ(M\_{pk}) = pγ(M\_k), so condition (iii) cannot hold for it: the Frobenius structure is what forces freeness.

*Sources.* SCH13, Lemma 2.12 with proof, pp. 11–12: For O/π^k-modules M\_k over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p with M₁ almost finitely generated, transition maps as in (ii) and Frobenius isomorphisms as in (iii), M\_k^a ≅ (O^a/π^k)^r compatibly with all the structure. ### 1.5 Scholze’s finiteness theorem for Sⁱ

### 2.1 Continuous cohomology of p-adic groups with smooth coefficients (Fust)

**Theorem** (`SZ.2/fust-cohomology`; planet: Fust’s comparison and vanishing). Let G be a locally profinite group, k a commutative ring and π a smooth k[G]-module with the discrete topology, regarded as a continuous representation; Hⁱ(G, −) denotes continuous cohomology computed by continuous homogeneous cochains (Mathlib’s continuousCohomology). (a) There are natural isomorphisms Hⁱ(G, π) ≅ Extⁱ\_G(1, π), the Ext groups taken in the abelian category of smooth k[G]-modules. (b) (Shapiro) If H ⊂ G is a closed subgroup such that G → H\\G has a continuous section and V is a topological H-module, then Hⁱ(G, Ind\_H^G V) ≅ Hⁱ(H, V) for the continuous induction Ind\_H^G V = {f ∈ C(G, V) : f(hg) = h·f(g)}. (c) If G is a p-adic reductive group (the F-points of a connected reductive group over a finite extension F/ℚ\_p; for instance SL₂(F) or GL₂(F)), O the integers of a finite extension of ℚ\_p with uniformiser ϖ, and π an admissible smooth O/ϖⁿ[G]-module, then every Hⁱ(G, π) is a finitely generated O/ϖⁿ-module. (d) If k is a finite field of characteristic p and π is an absolutely irreducible smooth k-representation of GL₂(ℚ\_p) that is not isomorphic to the twist by a character of 1, of the Steinberg representation Sp, or of Ind\_B^G α with α(diag(a, d)·u) = ω(a)ω(d)⁻¹ (ω the mod p cyclotomic character, so that Ind\_B^G α = I(1, ε) in the notation of SZ.4), then Hⁱ(SL₂(ℚ\_p), π) = 0 for all i ≥ 0. (e) If π is an admissible smooth k-representation of GL₂(F) with a central character, its restriction to SL₂(F) is admissible.

*Hypotheses.* In (b) the section is continuous (for G = GL₂(F), H = SL₂(F) one can take x ↦ diag(x, 1)); V is any topological H-module, in particular a prodiscrete one. In (d) the excluded representations are exactly the members of the twists of the block containing the trivial representation; for p ≥ 5 this is the block {χ, St ⊗ χ, I(χ, χε)} of SZ.2/comparison-map.

*Proof sketch.* (a) Apply the functor of smooth vectors to the resolution of π by continuous cochain modules C(G^{n+1}, π); it gives a resolution of π by acyclic objects of the smooth category, because C(G, −)^{sm} is right adjoint to the forgetful functor and preserves injectives, and the G-invariants of the cochains are unchanged (Fust Lemmas 2.1–2.3, 4.1–4.3, Corollary 4.4; for compact G this is Emerton’s Proposition 2.2.6). ### 2.2 Low-degree cohomology of SL₂(ℚ\_p) with trivial mod p coefficients (Moore)

**Theorem** (`SZ.2/moore-sl2-vanishing`). Let k be a non-archimedean local field and μ(k) its group of roots of unity. For every separable locally compact abelian group A with trivial SL₂(k)-action, H²(SL₂(k), A) ≅ Hom(μ(k), A), where H² classifies topological central extensions (Moore); for A discrete this group is the continuous cohomology H²\_cont(SL₂(k), A). Consequently, for p odd: H¹\_cont(SL₂(ℚ\_p), 𝔽\_p) = 0 and H²\_cont(SL₂(ℚ\_p), 𝔽\_p) = 0.

*Hypotheses.* A discrete in the comparison with continuous cochain cohomology; SL₂(k) with its topology as a locally profinite group. p odd in the consequence: μ(ℚ\_p) is cyclic of order p − 1, prime to p.

*Proof sketch.* Moore’s theorem, planned here: SL₂(k) is its own commutator subgroup; its central extensions are described by Steinberg cocycles, whose symbol relations are Matsumoto’s presentation of K₂ (K2SymbolsBrauer T.2/matsumoto supplies only that presentation); the continuous (topological) ones factor through the norm residue symbol by Moore’s uniqueness theorem, so the universal topological central extension has kernel μ(k) and H²(SL₂(k), A) ≅ Hom(μ(k), A) (Moore Theorem 1.1 for the transgression, Theorems 2.3–2.4 for the topological universal extension, Theorems 10.3–10.4). Comparison with continuous cochains (argued here; Moore works with Borel cochains): for discrete A and a totally disconnected locally compact group, every topological central extension by A splits continuously over a compact open subgroup and hence admits a continuous section, so the measurable classes are the continuous ones. H¹\_cont(SL₂(ℚ\_p), 𝔽\_p) = Hom\_cont(SL₂(ℚ\_p), 𝔽\_p) = 0 because SL₂(ℚ\_p) is perfect; H²\_cont(SL₂(ℚ\_p), 𝔽\_p) ≅ Hom(μ\_{p−1}, 𝔽\_p) = 0 for p odd.

**Checks.**

- For p = 2, μ(ℚ\_2) = {±1} and H²\_cont(SL₂(ℚ\_2), 𝔽\_2) ≅ ℤ/2 ≠ 0, so the oddness of p is needed.
- H⁰\_cont(SL₂(ℚ\_p), 𝔽\_p) = 𝔽\_p.
- The metaplectic double cover of SL₂(ℚ\_p) is the pushout of the universal topological extension along μ(ℚ\_p) → μ(ℚ\_p)/μ(ℚ\_p)² ≅ {±1}.

### 3.2 Ludwig’s vanishing theorem for S²

**Theorem** (`SZ.3/ludwig-vanishing`; planet: Ludwig’s vanishing theorem). Let F = ℚ\_p, n = 2, G = GL₂(ℚ\_p). (a) For smooth characters χ₁, χ₂ : ℚ\_p^× → 𝔽\_q^×, S²(Ind\_B^G(χ₁ ⊗ χ₂)) = 0 (the characters need not be distinct). (b) For every smooth character μ, S²(St ⊗ μ∘det) = 0, St the mod p Steinberg representation. (c) For every smooth character μ, S²(μ∘det) is one-dimensional over 𝔽\_q, Gal\_{ℚ\_p} × D^× acting by a character (from SZ.1/scholze-galois-and-degree-zero (e)). (d) Consequently, if X is a smooth 𝔽\_q[G]-module of finite length whose Jordan–Hölder constituents are characters, Steinberg twists or principal series, S²(X) is finite-dimensional and is a successive extension of one-dimensional characters of Gal\_{ℚ\_p} × D^×; in particular S²(X)^H is a successive extension of Galois characters for every compact open H ⊂ D^×, the form CDN use.

*Hypotheses.* F = ℚ\_p and n = 2; coefficients in a finite field of characteristic p.

*Proof sketch.* (a) As S²(Ind χ) is an 𝔽\_p-vector space it suffices that S²(Ind χ) ⊗ O\_{ℂ\_p}/p is almost zero. By SZ.1/scholze-finiteness (2) it is almost isomorphic to H²(ℙ¹, ℱ\_{Ind χ} ⊗ O⁺/p) = H²(ℙ¹, π̄\_{GH,∗}(ℱ\_χ ⊗ O⁺/p)) = H²(M\_∞/B, ℱ\_χ ⊗ O⁺/p) (SZ.3/borel-quotient (c)). Compute the last group through π̄\_GH : (M\_∞/B)\_ét → |ℙ¹\_{ℂ\_p}|: the stalks of the higher direct images are cohomology of the affinoid perfectoid fibres, almost zero in positive degrees (PerfectoidSpaces P3/etale-almost-acyclicity); and sheaf cohomology on |ℙ¹\_{ℂ\_p}| vanishes above degree 1 (cohomological dimension of the underlying space of a rigid curve). Hence H² is almost zero (Ludwig Theorem 4.6). (b) From 0 → μ∘det → Ind\_B^G(μ ⊗ μ) → St ⊗ μ → 0 and the long exact sequence, S²(Ind) → S²(St ⊗ μ) → S³(μ∘det) = 0 (SZ.1/scholze-finiteness (3)) and S²(Ind) = 0 (Ludwig Corollary 4.7). (c) SZ.1/scholze-galois-and-degree-zero (e). (d) Induction on the length: for 0 → X′ → X → X″ → 0 the sequence S²(X′) → S²(X) → S²(X″) → S³(X′) = 0 is exact, so S²(X) is an extension of S²(X″) by a quotient of S²(X′); conclude with (a)–(c).

**Checks.**

- For a generic pair (χ₁, χ₂) and the non-split extension Π\_{χ₁,χ₂} of I(χ₂, χ₁) by I(χ₁, χ₂), S²(Π\_{χ₁,χ₂}) = 0.

### 5.1 Functors of finite image on irreducible representations

**Definition** (`SZ.5/finite-image-functor`). Let G′ = GL₂(ℚ\_p)/p^ℤ and k\_L a finite field of characteristic p. A function V assigning to every isomorphism class of smooth irreducible k\_L[G′]-modules π a k\_L-vector space V(π) is of finite image if V(π) is finite-dimensional for every π and V(π) = 0 for all but finitely many π. For functors defined on all smooth k\_L[G′]-modules the condition is imposed on the irreducible ones.

*Hypotheses.* Only irreducible π are tested; no condition is imposed on reducible arguments.

*Proof sketch.* A predicate on functions from isomorphism classes of irreducibles to finite-dimensional k\_L-vector spaces: the support {π : V(π) ≠ 0} is finite and every value is finite-dimensional. Stability under subobjects and extensions is checked pointwise: dimensions add along exact sequences and supports are contained in unions.

API:

- `IsFiniteImage` (constructor): The predicate IsFiniteImage V on functions V from isomorphism classes of smooth irreducible k\_L[G′]-modules to k\_L-vector spaces.
- `IsFiniteImage.of_injective` (other): If V(π) ↪ W(π) for every π and W is of finite image, so is V.
- `IsFiniteImage.of_exact` (other): If V′(π) → V(π) → V″(π) is exact for every π and V′, V″ are of finite image, so is V.
- `isFiniteImage_iff` (characterisation): V is of finite image iff its values are finite-dimensional and its support {π : V(π) ≠ 0} is finite.
- `IsFiniteImage.cosocle_iff` (characterisation): For V a smooth k\_L[G′]-module of finite type, π ↦ Hom\_{G′}(V, π) is of finite image iff the cosocle of V has finite length.
- `IsFiniteImage.cohomology` (relation): If V is of finite image and a compact p-adic Lie group H (resp. the Weil group W\_K) acts continuously on each V(π), then π ↦ Hⁱ(H, V(π)) (resp. Hⁱ(W\_K, V(π)), i ≤ 1) is of finite image.

*Uses.* CDN23, Lemme 4.13, p. 43: reducing the finite-length theorem to the finite image of π ↦ Hom^cont\_{k\_L[G]}(π^∨, H¹\_ét(M\_∞, k\_L))^{W\_K × Ǧ\_n}; CDN23, Lemme 4.17, p. 45: reducing further to the finite image of π ↦ S¹(π)^{𝒢\_K × Ǧ\_n}, using that V₁, V₂ and their cohomology are of finite image; CDN23, proof of Théorème 4.1, pp. 42–45: the cosocle criterion of finite length is a finite-image statement for π ↦ Hom\_{G′}(V, π).

**Checks.**

- `IsFiniteImage_zero` (degenerate): The zero function is of finite image.
- `IsFiniteImage_hom_finiteLength` (computation): For V = Π\_{χ₁,χ₂} (length two, with central character trivial at p so that it is a G′-module), π ↦ Hom\_{G′}(V, π) is non-zero exactly at π = I(χ₂, χ₁), where it is one-dimensional over End(π).
- `IsFiniteImage_single` (computation): A family supported at a single index, with a one-dimensional value there, is of finite image.
- `IsFiniteImage_not_iwahori` (non-example): π ↦ π^{I₁}, I₁ the pro-p Iwahori subgroup, is finite-dimensional on every π (admissibility) but non-zero for infinitely many π, so it is not of finite image.
- The zero functor is of finite image.
- π ↦ Hom\_{G′}(V, π) for V of finite length is of finite image.
- π ↦ π^{I₁} (pro-p Iwahori invariants) is not of finite image: it is non-zero on every π.

*Sources.* CDN23, §4.2, p. 43: A functor π ↦ V(π) to k\_L-modules is said to be of finite image if V(π) is finite-dimensional for every smooth irreducible k\_L[G′]-module π and zero for almost every π.

### 5.2 Ascent from the arithmetic Drinfeld levels to infinite level (CDN Lemmas 4.13 and 4.17)

**Theorem** (`SZ.5/drinfeld-tower-ascent`). Let F = ℚ\_p with p > 2, ϖ = p, G = GL₂(ℚ\_p), G′ = G/p^ℤ, Ǧ = D^× and Ǧ\_n = 1 + 𝔪\_D^n, n ≥ 0, K/ℚ\_p finite, M^p\_{n,K} the arithmetic level-n Drinfeld space (ET.6a/rank-two-arithmetic-descent), M^p\_{Dr,∞} = M\_{Dr,∞,ℂ\_p}/p^ℤ and M\_∞ = M\_{LT,∞,ℂ\_p} ≅ M\_{Dr,∞,ℂ\_p} (ET.6a/el-duality). ### 5.3 Vanishing of multiplicities for almost every irreducible (CDN Theorem 0.9)

**Theorem** (`SZ.5/almost-all-vanishing`). Let F = ℚ\_p with p > 2, ϖ = p, n ≥ 0 and K/ℚ\_p finite. For every smooth irreducible k\_L[G′]-module π, Hom^cont\_{k\_L[G′]}(π^∨, H¹\_ét(M^p\_{n,K}, k\_L)) is finite-dimensional over k\_L, and it vanishes for all but finitely many isomorphism classes of such π; that is, the functor is of finite image.

*Hypotheses.* F = ℚ\_p and p > 2; the statement is specific to ℚ\_p (CDN Theorem 2.13 gives non-admissibility phenomena for F ≠ ℚ\_p).

*Proof sketch.* The central character of a smooth irreducible k\_L[G′]-module takes only finitely many values (ModpRepresentations MP.1/central-ext, Lemma 1.22), so fix it, equal to δ; each such π is admissible (PadicLocalLanglandsForGL2Qp R30.2/modp-irreducibles). By SZ.5/drinfeld-tower-ascent (b)–(c) it suffices that π ↦ S¹(π)^{Gal\_K × Ǧ\_n} is of finite image. Finite-dimensionality: S¹(π) is an admissible Ǧ-module (SZ.1/scholze-finiteness). Finiteness of the support: put π̄ = 𝔽̄\_p ⊗\_{k\_L} π; then S¹(π̄) ≅ 𝔽̄\_p ⊗\_{k\_L} S¹(π) (SZ.1/scholze-functor, base change, as ℙ¹\_{ℂ\_p} is quasicompact). Let T be the set of irreducible 𝔽̄\_p[G′]-modules τ with S¹(τ)^{Gal\_K} ≠ 0. By the classification only finitely many τ with given central character are not generic principal series (Remark 1.9, R30.2/modp-irreducibles), and among generic principal series only finitely many have S¹(τ)^{Gal\_K} ≠ 0 by SZ.4/key-local-proposition; so T is finite. If π has S¹(π)^{Gal\_K × Ǧ\_n} ≠ 0 then π̄ has a constituent in T; π̄ is semisimple with pairwise distinct Galois-conjugate constituents (MP.0/scalar-extension, Lemma 1.7), and Hom commutes with field extension (Lemma 1.6), so two non-isomorphic such π cannot share a constituent; hence only finitely many π occur.

**Checks.**

- For K = ℚ\_p only finitely many π with S¹(π)^{Gal\_{ℚ\_p} × Ǧ\_n} ≠ 0 occur, by the same proof.
- The finitely many exceptional π include the irreducible constituents of the cosocle of H¹\_ét(M^p\_{n,K}, k\_L)^∨ (SZ.5/finite-length).
- Twisting by characters of ℚ\_p^×/p^{2ℤ} that are trivial on N\_{K/ℚ\_p}(K^×) preserves the finite set of exceptions up to the twist.

*Sources.* CDN23, Théorème 0.9, p. 5, with Remarque 0.10: For [K : ℚ\_p] < ∞, Hom^cont\_{k\_L[G′]}(π^∨, H¹\_ét(M^p\_{n,K}, k\_L)) = 0 for all but finitely many smooth irreducible π. ### 5.4 Finite length of the mod p cohomology of the Drinfeld tower (CDN Theorem 4.1)

**Theorem** (`SZ.5/finite-length`; planet: Finite length of mod p Drinfeld cohomology). Let F = ℚ\_p with p > 2, ϖ = p, n ≥ 0 and K/ℚ\_p finite. (a) H¹\_ét(M^p\_{n,K}, k\_L) is the Pontryagin dual of a smooth k\_L[G′]-representation of finite length; in particular this representation is admissible and finitely presented, and H¹\_ét(M^p\_{n,K}, μ\_p)^∨ is a smooth admissible G-representation of finite length as a ℤ\_p[G]-module. (b) For every k ≥ 1, H¹\_ét(M^p\_{n,K}, O\_L/ϖ\_L^k)^∨ is a smooth admissible G′-representation of finite length. ### 6.1 Finite-dimensionality of mod p multiplicities for every F (CDN Theorem 4.22)

### 6.2 The geometric multiplicity Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) of a residual Galois representation

**Definition** (`SZ.6/geometric-multiplicity`; planet: Geometric multiplicity Π^geo). Let F = ℚ\_p, ϖ = p, n ≥ 0 and ρ̄ : Gal\_{ℚ\_p} → GL\_d(k\_L) a continuous representation on a finite-dimensional k\_L-vector space. Define Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) := Hom\_{k\_L[Gal\_{ℚ\_p}]}(ρ̄, H¹\_ét(M^p\_{n,ℂ\_p}, k\_L))^∨, the k\_L-linear dual of the space of Galois-equivariant maps from ρ̄ to the first étale cohomology of the level-n Drinfeld space over ℂ\_p, with the G′-action induced by the action on the cohomology. More generally, for F/ℚ\_p finite and ρ̄ : Gal\_F → GL\_d(k\_L), Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) := Hom\_{k\_L[Gal\_F]}(ρ̄, H¹\_ét(M^ϖ\_{n,ℂ\_p}, k\_L))^∨.

*Hypotheses.* The dual is taken with the topology for which Hom(ρ̄, H¹) is prodiscrete; Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) is a smooth G′-module (CDN Corollary 2.12, Theorem 4.23 (a)).

*Proof sketch.* The Galois-equivariant maps form a closed G′-stable subspace of Hom\_{k\_L}(ρ̄, H¹\_ét(M^p\_{n,ℂ\_p}, k\_L)) ≅ ρ̄^∨ ⊗ H¹; take its continuous dual. If ρ̄ factors through a finite quotient H = Gal(F′/ℚ\_p), Hom\_{Gal}(ρ̄, H¹) = Hom\_H(ρ̄, H¹(M^p\_{n,ℂ\_p}, k\_L)^{Gal\_{F′}}); if H¹(M^p\_{n,ℂ\_p}, k\_L)^{Gal\_{F′}} ≅ π^∨ then Hom\_H(ρ̄, π^∨) ≅ (ρ̄^∨ ⊗ π^∨)^H ≅ ((ρ̄ ⊗ π)\_H)^∨, so Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) ≅ (ρ̄ ⊗\_{k\_L} π)\_H, the H-coinvariants (CDN proof of Corollary 2.12).

API:

- `geometricMultiplicity` (constructor): Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) = Hom\_{k\_L[Gal\_F]}(ρ̄, H¹\_ét(M^ϖ\_{n,ℂ\_p}, k\_L))^∨ with its G′-action.
- `geometricMultiplicity_map` (functoriality): A Galois-equivariant map ρ̄₁ → ρ̄₂ induces Π^geo\_n(ρ̄₁) → Π^geo\_n(ρ̄₂), compatibly with composition and identities.
- `geometricMultiplicity_directSum` (relation): Π^geo\_n(ρ̄₁ ⊕ ρ̄₂) ≅ Π^geo\_n(ρ̄₁) ⊕ Π^geo\_n(ρ̄₂).
- `geometricMultiplicity_rightExact` (other): A surjection ρ̄ ↠ ρ̄″ induces a surjection Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) ↠ Π^geo\_n(ρ̄″); exact sequences 0 → ρ̄′ → ρ̄ → ρ̄″ → 0 give right-exact sequences Π^geo\_n(ρ̄′) → Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) → Π^geo\_n(ρ̄″) → 0.

- `geometricMultiplicity_smooth` (other): Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) is a smooth G′-module; for F = ℚ\_p it has finite length (SZ.6/geometric-multiplicity-theorem).

### 6.3 Finite length and vanishing of geometric multiplicities (CDN Theorem 4.23)

**Theorem** (`SZ.6/geometric-multiplicity-theorem`; planet: Geometric multiplicity theorem). Let F = ℚ\_p with p > 2, n ≥ 0 and ρ̄ : Gal\_{ℚ\_p} → GL\_d(k\_L) continuous. (a) Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) is a smooth G-module of finite length. (b) If d > 2 and ρ̄ is absolutely irreducible, Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) = 0. (c) If d = 2 and ρ̄ is absolutely irreducible, Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) is a successive extension of copies of Π(ρ̄), the supersingular representation attached to ρ̄ by the mod p local Langlands correspondence (the normalisation of the block bijection B ↔ ρ̄\_B). Along the way: (d) (Lemma 4.24) in the setting of SZ.4/global-block-occurrence with r̄ = χ₁ ⊕ χ₂ reducible, for every G-submodule Z of M = M\_{ψ,λ,𝔪} and compact open H ⊂ D^×, S¹(Z)^H and H¹(H, S¹(Z)) are finite-dimensional Gal\_{ℚ\_p}-representations all of whose irreducible subquotients are one-dimensional.

*Hypotheses.* p > 2 and F = ℚ\_p; in (c) the source proves the supersingular case of (b) in detail and indicates (c) in Remarque 4.25; the argument recorded here combines the proof of (b) with the block decomposition.

*Proof sketch.* (a) Choose F′/ℚ\_p finite Galois with ρ̄ factoring through H = Gal(F′/ℚ\_p); the Hochschild–Serre sequences of SZ.5/drinfeld-tower-ascent (a) compare H¹\_ét(M^p\_{n,ℂ\_p}, k\_L)^{Gal\_{F′}} with H¹\_ét(M^p\_{n,F′}, k\_L) up to finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel, the latter dual of finite length (SZ.5/finite-length); then Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) is built from (ρ̄ ⊗ π)^H as in SZ.6/geometric-multiplicity (CDN proof of Corollary 2.12). (b) Let π be an irreducible quotient of Π^geo\_n(ρ̄); then ρ̄ embeds into Hom^cont\_G(π^∨, H¹\_ét(M^p\_{n,ℂ\_p}, k\_L)); the ascent of SZ.5/drinfeld-tower-ascent, the comparison sequence of SZ.2/comparison-map and the fact that ρ̄ (irreducible of dimension > 1) does not occur in H⁰\_ét(M\_∞, k\_L) (characters only) give Hom\_{Gal}(ρ̄, S¹(π)) ≠ 0. π is not a character since S¹ kills characters (SZ.1/scholze-galois-and-degree-zero (e)); enlarge L so that π is absolutely irreducible, let B be its block and r̄ = ρ̄\_B, globalise and form M (SZ.4/residual-globalisation, SZ.4/global-block-occurrence). If π is supersingular, B = {π} and 0 → π → M → π′ → 0 with π′ a successive extension of copies of π; S⁰(π′) = 0, so S¹(π) ↪ S¹(M) ≅ Ĥ¹\_{ψ,λ}(U^𝔭, k\_L)\_𝔪[𝔪\_A] ⊂ r̄ ⊗ N′ (SZ.4/hecke-torsion-comparison, whose isomorphism case applies as r̄ is irreducible, and R31.4 isotypy for Ĥ¹); hence S¹(π) is r̄-isotypic (Scholze Proposition 5.4), so Hom\_{Gal}(ρ̄, S¹(π)) ≠ 0 forces ρ̄ ≅ r̄, impossible for d > 2. If π is not supersingular, r̄ = χ₁ ⊕ χ₂. Lemma 4.24 (d): the kernel of S¹(Z) → S¹(M) is a quotient of S⁰((M/Z)^{SL₂(ℚ\_p)}), a successive extension of characters (SZ.1/scholze-galois-and-degree-zero (b)), and the image lies in r̄ ⊗ U, hence has a filtration with graded pieces χ₁ ⊗ U₁ and χ₂ ⊗ U₂; take H-invariants and H¹(H, −). Choose H pro-p, write 0 → X → Y → π → 0 with X, Y ⊂ M; S⁰(π) = 0 gives 0 → (S¹(Y)/S¹(X))^H → S¹(π)^H → S²(X)^H; the first term is controlled by (d) and the last is a successive extension of characters by SZ.3/ludwig-vanishing (d); so Hom\_{Gal}(ρ̄, S¹(π)^H) = 0 for absolutely irreducible ρ̄ of dimension ≥ 2, a contradiction. (c) For d = 2 the same argument excludes every non-supersingular irreducible quotient and forces every supersingular one to be the member Π(ρ̄) of the block of ρ̄; a finite-length object decomposes along blocks (R30.5/blocks), and each block component has an irreducible quotient in its block, so Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) lies in the block {Π(ρ̄)}.

**Checks.**

- For every absolutely irreducible three-dimensional ρ̄ (for instance induced from a suitable character of the unramified cubic extension), Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) = 0.
- For d = 2 and ρ̄ absolutely irreducible, every irreducible constituent of Π^geo\_n(ρ̄) is the supersingular Π(ρ̄).

### 6.4 Smooth vectors on the Lubin–Tate side (CDN Theorem 4.26)

**Theorem** (`SZ.6/lubin-tate-smooth-vectors`; planet: Lubin–Tate smooth vectors). Let F = ℚ\_p with p > 2, n ≥ 0, LT\_{n,ℂ\_p} = M\_{LT,G\_n} ⊗ ℂ\_p the level-n Lubin–Tate space (G\_n = 1 + p^n M₂(ℤ\_p)), LT^p\_{n,ℂ\_p} its quotient by p^ℤ ⊂ Z(Ǧ), which descends to ℚ\_p, and LT^p\_{n,K} its base change to a finite K/ℚ\_p; M^p\_∞ = M\_∞/p^ℤ. Then: (a) the Ǧ-smooth vectors of H¹\_ét(LT^p\_{n,K}, k\_L) form a finite-dimensional k\_L-vector space; (b) (Lemma 4.27) for every j, the G-smooth vectors of H¹\_ét(M^p\_∞, k\_L)^{Gal\_K × Ǧ\_j} form a finite-dimensional space; (c) (Lemma 4.28) H¹\_ét(M^p\_∞, k\_L) contains no non-zero finite-dimensional G-submodule; (d) (Remark 4.29) statement (a) is equivalent to SZ.5/finite-length (a) through the criterion of finite length by smooth vectors of the dual.

*Hypotheses.* F = ℚ\_p and p > 2 (through SZ.5/finite-length and SZ.2/moore-sl2-vanishing); the source expects (a) for F ≠ ℚ\_p as well, which is not asserted.

*Proof sketch.* (a) As in step 1 of SZ.5/drinfeld-tower-ascent reduce to the smooth vectors of H¹\_ét(LT^p\_{n,ℂ\_p}, k\_L)^{Gal\_K}; the G\_n-torsor M^p\_∞ → LT^p\_{n,ℂ\_p} gives 0 → A → H¹\_ét(LT^p\_{n,ℂ\_p}, k\_L) → H¹\_ét(M^p\_∞, k\_L)^{G\_n} with A finite-dimensional, so it suffices that colim\_j H¹\_ét(M^p\_∞, k\_L)^{G\_n × Gal\_K × Ǧ\_j} is finite-dimensional; by (b) and (c) it is zero. (b) The ascent sequences give a map H¹\_ét(M^p\_{j,K}, k\_L) → H¹\_ét(M^p\_∞, k\_L)^{Gal\_K × Ǧ\_j} with finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel; the source is the dual of an admissible finite-length G-representation (SZ.5/finite-length), whose dual has finite-dimensional smooth vectors (ModpRepresentations MP.1/smooth-dual-length); finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel preserve finite-dimensionality of smooth vectors. (c) A non-zero finite-dimensional smooth G-module is trivial on SL₂(ℚ\_p) and contains a character after a finite extension of L, so it suffices that Hom^cont\_G(δ, H¹\_ét(M\_∞, k\_L)) = 0 for every smooth character δ (pass from M^p\_∞ to M\_∞ by step 3 of the ascent). The comparison sequence (SZ.2/comparison-map) gives S¹(δ⁻¹) → Hom^cont\_G(δ, H¹\_ét(M\_∞, k\_L)) → H²(SL₂(ℚ\_p), δ⁻¹); S¹(δ⁻¹) = 0 by twisting S¹(1) = 0 (SZ.1/scholze-galois-and-degree-zero (d)–(e)), and H²(SL₂(ℚ\_p), δ⁻¹) = H²(SL₂(ℚ\_p), k\_L) = 0 by SZ.2/moore-sl2-vanishing. (d) Theorem 1.24 (MP.1/smooth-dual-length) with the argument above runs in both directions. Descent of LT^p\_n to ℚ\_p: the quotient by p^ℤ ⊂ Z(Ǧ) of the Lubin–Tate tower, like the Drinfeld one, carries an effective Weil descent datum (ET.6a/actions-and-weil-descent; CDN p. 48 asserts the descent).

**Checks.**

- H¹\_ét(M^p\_∞, k\_L) contains no non-zero G-invariant vector.
- The finite-dimensional space in (a) contains the contribution of the connected components of LT^p\_{n,K}.

- **E1** (CDN23, proof of Corollaire 3.13, p. 37, and proof of Théorème 4.22, p. 46 (published version; the same text in arXiv:2204.11214v2); misprint, affects nothing). Correction: In the published paper cited, the finiteness for a p-adic reductive group is Lemma 6.6; Lemma 6.3 there is the auxiliary statement for a group with an open normal subgroup N of finitely generated cohomology and finite or finitely generated abelian quotient.
