# Modular symbols and analytic p-adic L-functions of modular forms — blueprint

This blueprint covers stages L0–L4. After two checkpoints, **L0 is source-decomposed**. Stages L1–L4 are not yet read.

L0 plans Pollack–Stevens' **Hom-side (cohomological) modular symbols** for arbitrary coefficient modules. These are what
L2's distribution-valued symbols need. The homological side is imported from Tau Ceti's ModularForms roadmap,
Layer 8, which the reviewed library audit AUDIT-26 lists as a duplicate of this layer. Imported from Layer 8:
- the degree-zero divisors Div⁰(P¹(ℚ));
- Manin's theorem and the fundamental-domain presentation;
- the coinvariant module 𝕄;
- the analysis of the period map.

The sources are:
- **Pollack–Stevens:** Ann. Sci. ÉNS (4) 44 (2011), §§2–3 and §5.1. It is open access on Numdam.
- **Wiese:** the course notes *Computational Arithmetic of Modular Forms*, arXiv:1809.04645v1, §§1, 4–7.
- **Pollack:** the Arizona Winter School 2011 notes, §2.

## Purpose

Modular symbols carry the periods and critical L-values of modular forms, and later their p-adic L-functions. L0 supplies:
- symbols Symb_Γ(V) = Hom_{ℤ[Γ]}(Div⁰(P¹(ℚ)), V) with values in any right Γ-module V (polynomials now, distributions in L2);
- their Hecke and diamond operators, the involution ι and characters;
- their comparison with group cohomology;
- the period symbol ψ_f of a cusp form;
- the Eichler–Shimura isomorphism;
- one-dimensional newform eigenspaces over the coefficient field.

L1 builds periods and critical values on the last of these.

## Conventions

- **Actions.**
  - Pollack–Stevens' right action v|γ is recorded as the left representation v ↦ v|γ^{-1}.
  - A symbol satisfies φ(γD) = φ(D)|γ^{-1}.
  - Hecke operators act by (φ|δ)(D) = φ(δD)|δ.
- **Coefficients.**
  - V_k(R) is the degree-k homogeneous polynomials with (P|γ)(X, Y) = P((X, Y)γ*), γ* = adj γ. No determinant twist.
  - Layer 8's Sym^k = W_k(R) is the same module with the left action (γ·Q)(X, Y) = Q((X, Y)γ).
- **Diamond operators** use the lower-right entry: ⟨d⟩ = (·)|σ_d for σ_d ∈ Γ₀(N) with lower-right entry ≡ d mod N. This matches
  Tau Ceti's `diamondOp`.
- **Characters.** Symbols with character ε are Γ₀(N)-symbols with coefficients V ⊗ R_ε, where r·γ = ε(d_γ)^{-1}r.
- **Fixed matrices.** σ = (0 −1; 1 0), τ = (0 −1; 1 −1), T = (1 1; 0 1), Δ = T − 1 and ι = (−1 0; 0 1).
- **Paths.** A divisor {s} − {r} is the path from r to s, so ψ_f({s} − {r}) = 2πi ∫_r^s f(z)(zX + Y)^k dz.

## Milestones

### Milestone 1: symbols and their Manin description

**Definition: modular symbols** (`symbols`; node `modular-symbols`; planet "Modular symbols"; PS §2.1).
Symb_Γ(V) = Hom_{ℤ[Γ]}(Div⁰, V), the Γ-equivariant additive maps.

*API.* `symbols_ext`: a symbol is determined on the unimodular divisors (Manin, imported).

*Unit tests.*
- Symb_{SL₂(ℤ)}(ℤ) = 0.
- Symb_{Γ₀(11)}(ℤ) has rank 3.
- The zero symbol.

**Lemma: the coset basis** (`free_monoidAlgebra`; node `coset-basis`). ℤ[G] is free over ℤ[Γ] on right coset
representatives, so Hom_{ℤ[Γ]}(ℤ[G], V) ≅ ∏_j V.

**Lemma: symbols as functions on cosets** (node `symbols-coset-description`; PS §2.2). Evaluation at d(g_j)
identifies Symb_Γ(V) with the families (v_j) satisfying the two-term relations from 1 + σ, the three-term relations
from 1 + τ + τ², and the relations from −1. The relations are twisted by the γ with g_jσ = γ g_{j′}.

*Proof.* Combine Manin's theorem (imported) with the coset basis.

**Theorem: values on the fundamental-domain generators** (node `symbols-generator-values`; planet "Presentation";
PS Corollaries 2.7 and 2.10, corrected). The statement:
- **The setting.** Γ ⊊ PSL₂(ℤ) has finite index and contains T. The generators D_i, D∞, D′_i, D″_i and the matrices
  γ_i, γ′_i, γ″_i are read off a Farey fundamental domain.
- **The conditions.** Take v_i, v∞, v′_i, v″_i with
  v∞|Δ = Σ v_i|(1 − γ_i) − Σ v′_i − Σ v″_i, v′_i|(1 + γ′_i) = 0 and v″_i|(1 + γ″_i + γ″_i²) = 0.
- **The conclusion.** Exactly one symbol takes these values, and every symbol arises this way.

*Proof.* The relation in Div⁰ is Σ(γ_i^{-1} − 1)D_i + ΣD′_i + ΣD″_i = (1 − T^{-1})D∞. It follows by applying ∂ to
Pollack–Stevens' printed boundary relation on p. 11. The printed Theorem 2.6 has the opposite sign on the D∞ side
(sourceIssues E1). Apply φ to the relation.

*Acceptance.*
- Γ₀(2), where D′₁ = (1 − T^{-1})D∞ = {0} − {−1}.
- Pollack's worked Γ₀(11) relation.
- For V = ℚ trivial, dim Symb_Γ(ℚ) = t + 1.

**Lemma: flat base change** (node `symbols-base-change`). Symb_Γ(V) ⊗_R S = Symb_Γ(V ⊗_R S) for flat S. Over 𝔽_p the
cokernel is Ext¹(Div⁰, V)[p].

### Milestone 2: boundary symbols and group cohomology

**Definition: boundary symbols** (`bsymbols`, `res`, `eisSymbols`; node `boundary-symbols`; Wiese Definition 1.5,
Lemma 4.13).
- BSymb_Γ(V) = Hom_{ℤ[Γ]}(ℤ[P¹(ℚ)], V) ≅ ⊕_{cusps c} V^{Γ_c}.
- res restricts to Div⁰, and Eis_Γ(V) is its image.

*Unit tests.*
- BSymb_{SL₂(ℤ)}(ℤ) ≅ ℤ.
- BSymb_{Γ₀(p)}(ℤ) ≅ ℤ².
- Eis_{SL₂(ℤ)}(ℤ) = 0.
- res is not injective.

**Construction: the map to H¹** (`toH1`; node `symbols-to-cohomology`). δ(φ) is the class of γ ↦ φ({γx} − {x}). It is
independent of the base cusp x, and it is the connecting map of the degree sequence.

*Unit tests.*
- Base points ∞ and 0 give the same class.
- δ∘res = 0.
- δ = 0 at level one.

**Theorem: the boundary exact sequence** (node `symbols-cohomology-sequence`; planet "Boundary exact sequence"). The
sequence is exact integrally:

  0 → V^Γ → BSymb_Γ(V) → Symb_Γ(V) → H¹(Γ, V) → ⊕_c H¹(Γ_c, V)

*Proof.* Take Hom_Γ(−, V) of 0 → Div⁰ → ℤ[P¹(ℚ)] → ℤ → 0. Then use Ext¹(ℤ, V) = H¹(Γ, V), and
ℤ[P¹(ℚ)] = ⊕ Ind_{Γ_c}^Γ ℤ with Shapiro.

*Acceptance.* For Γ₀(11) and V = ℚ the dimensions are 0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 3 → 2.

**Definition: parabolic cohomology** (`H1par`; node `parabolic-cohomology`; planet; Wiese Definition 6.12,
Proposition 4.14). The kernel of restriction to all cusp stabilisers.

*Unit tests.*
- dim H¹_par(Γ₀(11), ℚ) = 2.
- H¹_par(SL₂(ℤ), ℚ) = 0.
- H¹_par ≠ H¹ for Γ₀(11).

**Theorem: Symb/Eis ≅ H¹_par** (`ker_toH1`, `range_toH1`; node `symbols-mod-eisenstein`). It holds integrally and is
Hecke-, diamond- and ι-compatible.

**Lemma: descent** (`symbols_le_of_le`; node `symbols-level-descent`). Take Γ′ ⊴ Γ. There are two statements:
- **Integrally**, Symb_Γ(V) = Symb_{Γ′}(V)^{Γ/Γ′}, with no division.
- **When [Γ : Γ′] is invertible**, H¹ and H¹_par also descend by restriction. This is how elliptic stabilisers are
  handled: pass to a torsion-free Γ′ such as Γ ∩ Γ₁(M) with M ≥ 4.

*Acceptance.* The index cannot be dropped for cohomology: χ : SL₂(ℤ) → 𝔽₂ restricts to zero on ker χ.

**Theorem: the dimension of parabolic cohomology** (node `parabolic-cohomology-dimension`; Wiese Proposition 4.16).
Take characteristic 0 and trivial stabilisers in H. Then:
- dim H¹(Γ, V_k) = (k + 1)μ/6 + δ_{k,0};
- dim H¹_par(Γ, V_k) = (k + 1)μ/6 − ν∞ + 2δ_{k,0}.

*Acceptance.*
- Γ₁(11), k = 0: the dimension is 2.
- Γ₁(5): the dimensions are 0 for k = 0 and 2 for k = 2.

### Milestone 3: coefficients, Hecke, diamond and characters

**Definition: V_k** (`Vk`, `slashVk`, `vkRep`; node `polynomial-coefficients`; PS §3.4).

*Unit tests.*
- rank V_k = k + 1.
- A scalar c·1 acts by c^k.
- V_0 is trivial.

**Lemma: the invariant pairing** (`vkPairing`; node `polynomial-duality`). If k! is invertible, then:
- the pairing ⟨(uX + vY)^k, (sX + tY)^k⟩ = (ut − vs)^k is perfect;
- ⟨P|γ, Q⟩ = ⟨P, γ·Q⟩;
- so V_k ≅ Hom(W_k, R) as right modules.

On monomials the pairing is ⟨X^iY^{k−i}, X^{k−i}Y^i⟩ = (−1)^{k−i}/C(k, i). So over ℤ it is not integral for k ≥ 2.

*Acceptance.*
- k = 1 gives the determinant pairing.
- ⟨XY, XY⟩ = −1/2.

**Construction: Σ₀(N)** (`Sigma0`; node `hecke-monoid`).

**Construction: Hecke operators** (`heckeT`, `heckeU`; node `hecke-operators`; planet; PS §2.1). At level Γ₀(N):
- T_ℓ = (ℓ 0; 0 1) + Σ_a (1 a; 0 ℓ);
- U_q = Σ_a (1 a; 0 q).

*Unit tests.* On Symb_{Γ₀(11)}(ℤ):
- T₂ has characteristic polynomial (x − 3)(x + 2)²;
- T₃ has characteristic polynomial (x − 4)(x + 1)².

**Construction: diamond operators** (`diamond`; node `diamond-operators`; Wiese §1.2, Definition 7.23).
- ⟨d⟩φ = φ|σ_d.
- It is a (ℤ/N)^×-action that commutes with T_ℓ, U_q and ι.
- The diamond-fixed Γ₁(N)-symbols are the Γ₀(N)-symbols.

*Unit tests.*
- ⟨1⟩ = id.
- ⟨−1⟩ = (−1)^k on V_k-symbols.
- ⟨d⟩ is trivial on Γ₀(N)-symbols.
- Upper-left entry convention: it gives ⟨d^{-1}⟩ instead.

**Construction: Hecke operators at Γ₁(N)** (node `hecke-operators-gamma1`; Wiese Proposition 7.9(b)).
T_ℓ = Σ_a (1 a; 0 ℓ) + σ_ℓ(ℓ 0; 0 1). These commute with each other and with ⟨d⟩, and restrict to the Γ₀(N) operators.

**Definition: symbols with character** (`symbolsChar`; node `nebentypus-symbols`; Wiese §1.2).
- Symb_{Γ₁(N)}(V)[ε] = Symb_{Γ₀(N)}(V ⊗ R_ε).
- It vanishes when ε(−1) ≠ (−1)^k.

**Theorem: the character decomposition** (node `nebentypus-decomposition`). An internal direct sum over the characters
when φ(N) is invertible and the character values lie in R. It mirrors `isInternal_cuspFormCharSpace` on the form side.

**Construction: the involution at infinity** (`iota`; node `involution-at-infinity`). ι acts by x ↦ −x and normalises
Γ₀(N) and Γ₁(N).

**Lemma: plus/minus** (node `plus-minus-decomposition`). If 2 is invertible, Symb = Symb^+ ⊕ Symb^−.

### Milestone 4: periods and Eichler–Shimura

**Comparison: duality with Layer 8** (node `symbols-coinvariants-duality`).
- Symb_Γ(Hom_ℤ(W, M)) ≅ Hom_ℤ((Div⁰ ⊗ W)_Γ, M), by tensor–hom and the coinvariants adjunction.
- Hecke, diamond and ι operators become transposes of Layer 8's operators on 𝕄.

**Construction: the period symbol** (`periodSymbol`; node `period-symbol`; planet "Period symbol"; Pollack AWS §2.7).
ψ_f({s} − {r}) = 2πi ∫_r^s f(z)(zX + Y)^k dz. It is:
- a Γ-symbol;
- equivariant for T_ℓ, U_q and ⟨d⟩;
- Layer 8's period map transported: ⟨ψ_f(D), Q⟩ = 2πi·periodMap′(f)(D ⊗ w·Q) with w = (0 1; −1 0).

*Proof of Γ-invariance.* With z = γu,

  f(γu)(cu + d)^{−2}(γu X + Y)^k = f(u)(u X′ + Y′)^k, where (X′, Y′) = (X, Y)γ.

This is P|γ^{-1} for det γ = 1.

*Unit tests.*
- ψ_f({0} − {∞}) = L(f, 1) > 0 for the level-11 newform.
- The Y^k coefficient of ψ_f({0} − {∞}) is L(f, 1).
- ψ_0 = 0.
- The unadjugated action is not invariant.

**Theorem: the Eichler–Shimura isomorphism** (`periodSymbol_toH1_injective`, `finrank_H1par_eq_two_mul`; node
`eichler-shimura-isomorphism`; planet; Wiese Theorem 6.15, Corollary 7.33).
- S_{k+2}(Γ) ⊕ \overline{S_{k+2}(Γ)} ≅ H¹_par(Γ, V_k(ℂ)), Hecke- and diamond-compatibly.
- The ± versions give S_{k+2}(Γ) ≅ H¹_par^±.

*Proof.*
- **Injectivity:** the cup product pairing equals a nonzero multiple of the Petersson norm (Wiese Propositions 6.13
  and 6.14), which is positive definite (Tau Ceti).
- **Surjectivity:** the dimensions agree, by Wiese Proposition 4.16 and the dimension formula (requested from 10C).
- **General Γ:** by descent.

**Theorem: newform eigenspaces** (node `eigenspace-dimension`). Let f be a newform with character ε and coefficient
field K_f. Then Symb_{Γ₁(N)}(V_k(K_f))^±[ε][λ_f] is one-dimensional over K_f. It meets the Eisenstein symbols
trivially, and after ⊗ℂ it is spanned by (1 ± ι)ψ_f. It rests on:
- Eichler–Shimura;
- multiplicity one (Tau Ceti);
- the Layer 5 separation of Eisenstein eigensystems (requested);
- flat base change from K_f to ℂ.

This is the input for L1's period lines.

## Mistakes found in the sources

**E1 (error, reaches a stated result): the sign in Pollack–Stevens Theorem 2.6 and Corollary 2.7 (p. 12).** The same
applies to Theorem 2.9 and Corollary 2.10.
- **The printed relation:** Σ(γ_i^{-1} − 1)D_i + ΣD′_i = ((1 −1; 0 1) − 1)D∞.
- **What follows from the paper's own boundary relation on p. 11:** (1 − (1 −1; 0 1))D∞.
- **Counterexample (Γ₀(2)):** the printed relation forces 2D′₁ = 0 in the torsion-free Div⁰.
- **Corrected criterion:** v∞|Δ = Σ v_i|(1 − γ_i) − Σ v′_i.
- **Independent confirmation:** Pollack's AWS notes write the Γ₀(11) relation with the corrected sign, and the Sage
  implementation sets v∞ = −μ.
- **Effect:** the proof of Theorem 5.1 works with ν∞ replaced by −ν∞.

**E2 (misprint, reaches nothing), p. 11.** "(γ′_i + 1)e_i ∈ J" should read (γ′_i + 1)e′_i.

**E3 (misprint, reaches nothing): Corollary 2.10.** "(1 + γ′_i + (γ″_i)²)" should read (1 + γ″_i + (γ″_i)²), and the
undefined m′_i, m″_i should read v′_i, v″_i.

No published erratum was found, either on Numdam or in the Crossref record.
