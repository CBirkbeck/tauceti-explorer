## Conventions and acceptance discipline

Charts use additive notation for monoids and multiplication for their images in rings. A prelog homomorphism may send a chart element to zero. A log structure is an étale sheaf satisfying the inverse-image-of-units condition. Associated log structures use the sheafified pushout along that inverse image. Pullback includes logification, so elements that become invertible are identified with the new units. Characteristic monoids are quotients by units, rather than images in the ring. Groupification is the existing Grothendieck group.

Integral, fine and saturated have separate meanings. Cancellation characterizes integrality; fine adds finite generation; saturation is root closure inside the groupification. The monoid generated by 2 and 3 is fine but unsaturated, whereas the nonnegative rationals are saturated but not fine. Exactness is the groupification pullback condition on characteristic stalks. An integral map of arbitrary monoids need not be exact; integrality of a local log morphism has the appropriate exactness consequence. Flatness of monoid algebras in the integral chart criterion retains injectivity. Ordinary, integral and fs log fiber products use their respective universal properties.

Fine log smoothness uses local finite presentation and étale-local square-zero lifting against exact closed log immersions, including surjectivity on pulled-back log stalks. Kato’s chart criterion retains the finite kernel and invertible torsion conditions; log smoothness does not supply integrality, saturation or Cartier type. A root chart with degree divisible by the residue characteristic is Kummer but fails the log étale criterion. Log regularity here requires fs log structures and locally noetherian schemes. The non-fine valuation examples use a distinct finite-model theory and do not extend the noetherian log-regularity theorem by definition.

Finite-level log PD sites are formed over p-nilpotent compatible PD bases. In the integral quasi-coherent theory the defining PD ideal is nil; a single ordinary nilpotence bound is not imposed without a source hypothesis. Fine exactification precedes ordinary PD enveloping. Beilinson’s finite-level integral quasi-coherent envelopes cover the non-fine branch. Their construction does not settle the uncompleted, non-exact envelope question over a base where p is not nilpotent, discussed in Česnavičius–Koshikawa’s footnote 11. PD smoothness uses its stated affine lifting property; its étale locality is not asserted.

Connections are actual additive Leibniz maps into coefficient log differentials, and curvature is the degree-two differential. Logarithmic quasi-nilpotence uses products of falling factorials of the coordinate connection operators, with a sufficiently large total multiindex degree, or their p-adic convergence in formal evaluations. Replacing these operators by plain powers changes the condition. The PD differential identity is dγₙ(x)=γₙ₋₁(x)dx; the structure-complex filtration is FᵐΩᵃ=J^[m−a]Ωᵃ, with nonpositive PD powers equal to the whole ring.

Complexes are cohomological. Formal crystalline cohomology uses the derived inverse limit. The point model must have degree-zero Witt cohomology and zero positive cohomology; a separate inverse system with transition multiplication by p detects the failure of replacing a derived limit by an underived one. Rationalization is explicit inversion of p and retains a distinction from integral lattices with torsion.

The Hyodo–Kato Witt log base has generator 1 mapping to zero, rather than p. Frobenius is Witt-semilinear and multiplies a logarithmic q-form by p^q on a coordinate model. Fix θ=dlog(t) and the injection into the absolute complex by right wedge with θ. Its boundary is N, and Nφ=pφN. With this convention changing π to πu gives ρ_(πu)=ρ_π exp(log_K(u)N). The unit logarithm kills Teichmüller and torsion units; the construction assigns no value to a logarithm of π. Nilpotence makes this exponential a finite sum on each finite-dimensional rational cohomology space.

Proper comparisons, Qian’s proper log-family comparison, convergent tube comparisons and Stein/tower comparisons have distinct hypotheses and targets. Disegni–Liu’s supported complex is defined by an exact kernel on the quasi-étale analytic tube site; the map from special-fiber support need not be an isomorphism. Sato’s residue variant has the q+1 shift and ordered-stratum signs, and its Witt object is a module. Stein cohomology requires strict continuous limits, its Fréchet topology and completed scalar extension; the tower uses the category of ind-Fréchet objects with continuous actions. Geometric symmetries act linearly, while arithmetic Galois transport is semilinear for its action on K̆₀; its linearization has the coefficient-twisted object as domain.

Dieudonné theory is contravariant. The constant p-divisible group has F=σ and V=pσ⁻¹; the multiplicative group has F=pσ and V=σ⁻¹. A rational twist by r scales Frobenius by p^(−r), so the multiplicative group corresponds to K₀(−1) and Cartier duality to D(G)∨(−1). The Hodge sequence is 0→ω_G→D(G)→Lie(G∨)→0. Over a nilpotent PD thickening a chosen lift of G determines the lifted Hodge filtration; there is no canonical filtration depending only on the special-fiber group. An individual relative cohomology group receives a finite locally free Gauss–Manin interface only with the stated cohomology base-change and rank/torsion hypotheses; perfectness of the derived image alone is insufficient.

## CrystallineCohomology:CR.5:log-algebra — Log algebra and logarithmic geometry

Coverage: **planned**.

The algebraic prefix starts with chart monoids and logification, then passes to log structures on the actual small étale site. Strict, exact, integral and Kummer properties use their respective pullback or stalk conditions. The full chart criterion is étale-local on both source and target and compares the canonical map to the toric fiber product. The regular SNC carrier is imported from R09.7a; this stage adds its log structure and the logarithmic theorems, with the missing local proof inputs recorded.

Integral quasi-coherent logs form a separate branch. Their smoothness is witnessed by fine log-smooth models after source and base étale localization and integral base change. The valuation log on O_C has rational sharp characteristic, so finite generation cannot be inserted into this branch. Completed semistable charts use the supplied arithmetic frame and actual finite reductions; the nonnoetherian boundary comparison retains its finite-model hypotheses.

Planets: **Prelog rings**; **Log structures**; **Log smooth morphisms**; **Kato chart criterion**; **Divisorial log structures**; **Quasi-coherent log schemes**.

### Prelog rings

Node `CrystallineCohomology:CR.5:log-algebra/prelog-ring`; definition; declaration `TauCeti.LogCrystalline.PrelogRing`. Proposed module: `TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/LogCrystalline`.

A prelog ring is a commutative ring A, a commutative monoid P and a homomorphism α:P→(A,×), including elements mapping to zero. A morphism consists of a ring map and a monoid map commuting with α. Additive notation for charts corresponds to multiplication in A.

Construction or proof outline:

1. Use the multiplicative monoid of the ring, not its unit group.
2. Define morphisms by the commuting square and componentwise composition.

Direct dependencies: commutative ring and monoid primitives in the pinned libraries.

Source locators: [Kazuya Kato, Logarithmic structures of Fontaine–Illusie](https://math.uchicago.edu/~drinfeld/p-adic_periods/Kato_log-structures.pdf), §1.1, pp.192–193; multiplicative convention at the start of §1.

Suggested signature scope: The affine carrier and commuting ring/monoid maps are retained. Additive chart notation is represented through Multiplicative; the zero-image chart remains permitted.

Use driving the interface: `CrystallineCohomology:CR.5:log-algebra/associated-log` — Its nonunit and zero images determine the pushout along the inverse image of units; the morphism components give the adjunction and pullback maps.

Suggested signature scope: Retain LogStructure for affine algebra and introduce LogScheme with an actual Scheme, a monoid sheaf on its small étale site, a multiplicative structure map and the unit-fibre condition.
