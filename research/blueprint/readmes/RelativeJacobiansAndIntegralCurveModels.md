# The Jacobian challenge, Part II: relative Jacobians and integral curve models

This roadmap connects relative Jacobians with the integral models used in arithmetic. A smooth proper pointed curve family over a Dedekind base has an abelian scheme of degree-zero line classes. A universal bundle makes that description useful on arbitrary test schemes. A generic divisor class then determines an actual Jacobian point, and the full Néron model specializes that point at every closed point of the base. Singular and reducible fibres remain in the base. Under semistability their Picard identity components describe the connected part of reduction; they do not replace the full Néron model.

The two torsion results have different proofs. Unit-order torsion is controlled by étaleness over a strict henselization. Residue-primary torsion is controlled by Raynaud's uniqueness of finite flat models under the strict absolute-ramification bound. The latter is applied to the finite cyclic subgroup generated by a single rational torsion section. It does not require the whole rational torsion group to be finite.

This is a target-level plan, with every implementation status unchecked. All six layers are **planned**. Three supplier-scope gaps and eleven explicit requests remain; none of the layers is claimed closed. The mathematical targets have full proof paths to existing declarations, exact supplier nodes or those requests. The finite-normalization and Néron inputs are stated with their actual scope instead of silently treating integral as finite, or excellent as arbitrary.

## Scope and conventions

Unless a target states a broader base, S is a connected locally noetherian Dedekind scheme with function field K. Work componentwise on a disconnected base. For global finite-type Néron gluing take S noetherian. A smooth curve family π:X→S is smooth proper, of relative dimension one, with geometrically connected fibres and a specified section e. Genus is locally constant. No positive-genus convention excludes P¹: RJ.1 includes genus zero explicitly.

For the rigidification layer the more general input is a proper flat finitely presented pointed morphism for which O_T→(π_T)_*O_(X_T) is an isomorphism for every S-scheme T. Smooth proper geometrically connected curve families satisfy this imported cohomological-flatness contract. Pic^rig(T) means isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves on X_T with a specified trivialization on e_T, using the existing rigidified Picard functor. Normalization of a line means N_e(L)=L⊗π_T*(e_T*L)⁻¹ with its evaluation trivialization. This formula removes a base line, not an arbitrary divisor class.

Pic⁰ on a smooth curve is fibre-degree zero. On a reducible semistable curve it means degree zero on **each geometric irreducible component**. Write P for the larger total-degree-zero Picard functor, E for the schematic closure of the generic identity, N for the full finite-type Néron model and N⁰ for its open identity component. These objects have different roles. The specialization target is N_s(κ(s)); projection to a component group is a separate homomorphism.

For a DVR, normalize v_R by v_R(π_R)=1. In mixed characteristic (0,ℓ), e_R=v_R(ℓ) is the absolute ramification index. Recalculate it after extending the DVR. The strict inequality e_R<ℓ−1 implies ℓ≥3. It imposes no perfectness or completeness requirement. A divisor at a closed point has degree [κ(x):k], including purely inseparable residue extensions. The separable closure K^sep is not assumed algebraically closed when K is imperfect.

## RJ.0. Rigidified Picard representation and normalized universal bundles

Layer dependencies: `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/JacobianChallenge#layer-d-the-relative-picard-functor-and-the-jacobian-scheme`, `JacobianChallengePartII:JC4`.

### Rigidified classes and the relative Picard sheaf

Target `RJ.0/rigidified-comparison` (comparison). For every S-scheme T, normalization N_e(L)=L⊗π_T*e_T*L⁻¹ with its evaluation trivialization at e_T induces a natural group isomorphism Pic(X_T)/π_T*Pic(T) ≅ Pic^rig_(X/S,e)(T). The comparison of this functor with the fppf relative Picard sheaf is an isomorphism. It is compatible with tensor product, pullback and the distinguished trivial rigidified class. The rigidified functor itself is imported from current Tau Ceti, not defined here.

Hypotheses: π:X→S is proper, flat and finitely presented, e:S→X is a section, and O_T→(π_T)_*O_(X_T) is an isomorphism for every T→S.

Proof route. Use the imported rigidified line bundles and their pullback functor. Evaluation gives e_T*N_e(L)≅O_T, canonically. Tensoring L by π_T*M gives the same normalized rigidified class. Conversely a normalized class has zero quotient class exactly when it is the trivial rigidified line. Universal global functions make an automorphism fixing the section the identity. The section kills the Picard descent obstruction on every T; apply BLR 8.1/4 or Kleiman 2.5, 2.9–2.10 to obtain the sheaf comparison, not merely its field-valued points.

Acceptance. For L=π_T*M the image is the identity, even when M is nontrivial on T. The statement holds for T with nilpotents; a comparison only on geometric points fails acceptance.

Direct inputs: `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/JacobianChallenge#layer-d-the-relative-picard-functor-and-the-jacobian-scheme`, `JacobianChallengePartII:JC4/actual-to-relative-obstruction`, `tauceti:TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.InvertibleSheaf`.

Sources. [blr] §8.1 Proposition 4 and section-rigidification discussion, pp.204–205. The section removes the descent obstruction and provides the normalized line-bundle description. The displayed normalization is the induced functorial construction. [kleiman] Theorem 2.5, Definition 2.8, Lemmas 2.9–2.10, pp.18–20. These statements compare the sheaf and quotient functors and identify the rigidified classes with no remaining automorphisms.

### A normalized representation of a Picard subfunctor

Target `RJ.0/normalized-representation` (definition). Let F be a subfunctor of the imported rigidified Picard functor, stable under pullback. A NormalizedPicardRepresentation of F consists of an S-scheme P, an invertible U on X×_S P with a specified trivialization τ along e×id_P, and a natural bijection η_T:Hom_S(T,P)≅F(T) whose composite with F(T)↪Pic^rig(T) sends a to [(id_X×a)*U,a*τ]. The bijection is for all S-schemes T. It includes uniqueness of the classifying morphism; membership in F alone is insufficient.

Hypotheses: π:X→S is proper, flat and finitely presented, e:S→X is a section, and O_T→(π_T)_*O_(X_T) is an isomorphism for every T→S. F is a specified subfunctor; representability is data to prove, not an automatic property of an arbitrary subfunctor.

Proof route. Use existing InvertibleSheaf and the current rigidified Picard functor as carriers. Package the universal rigidified bundle together with the all-test-scheme representing bijection. Transport the naturality and inclusion equation as named fields, then derive the classification and uniqueness API. Do not store an arbitrary point-set group as the representing scheme.

Uses. RJ.1 smooth Jacobian representation: Provides the actual universal curve×Jacobian bundle, rather than only its geometric point classes. RJ.2 divisor Abel morphism: Its classify API turns normalized divisor families into a morphism to the representing scheme.

Proposed API:

- `NormalizedPicardRepresentation.classify` (universal-property): For ξ∈F(T), classify(ξ):T→P is the unique morphism with η(classify(ξ))=ξ.
- `NormalizedPicardRepresentation.classify_pullback` (functoriality): For b:T′→T, classify(b*ξ)=b followed by classify(ξ).
- `NormalizedPicardRepresentation.universal_eq` (characterisation): A morphism a classifies ξ exactly when (id×a)*U is rigidified-isomorphic to the line representing ξ.
- `NormalizedPicardRepresentation.uniqueIso` (equivalence): Two representations are uniquely isomorphic over S, preserving their universal rigidified bundles.
- `NormalizedPicardRepresentation.baseChange` (compatibility): For S′→S, P_S′ and U_S′ represent the restricted functor over S′.

Checks:

- `normalizedRepresentation_trivial` (degenerate): For the functor consisting of the identity class alone, P=S and U=O_(X) with its canonical section trivialization give a representation.
- `normalizedRepresentation_projectiveLine` (computation): For X=P¹_S with its infinity section and F=Pic^rig, P=the disjoint union of copies of S indexed by Z; on component d the universal bundle is the infinity-normalized O(d).
- `normalizedRepresentation_infinitesimal` (characterisation): For an elliptic curve E/k, F=Pic^rig,0 and P=E∨, the tangent fibre over the identity on T=Spec k[ε]/(ε²) is one-dimensional; replacing F(T) by F(T_red) does not represent it.
- `normalizedRepresentation_baseTwist` (non-example): For a nontrivial M on T, π_T*M is the zero relative class but is not an actual trivial line; the classifying condition must use normalization rather than require π_T*M≅O.

Acceptance. Recover P uniquely up to the unique isomorphism carrying the universal rigidified class.

Direct inputs: `RelativeJacobiansAndIntegralCurveModels:RJ.0/rigidified-comparison`, `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/JacobianChallenge#layer-d-the-relative-picard-functor-and-the-jacobian-scheme`.

Sources. [kleiman] §4 Exercises 4.3–4.4, p.27; §2 Lemmas 2.9–2.10, p.19. Universal bundles classify normalized classes when the functor is represented. The structure here records the Yoneda condition explicitly for a subfunctor. [blr] §8.1 Proposition 4, pp.204–205. Rigidification makes the universal object unique up to its unique compatible isomorphism.

### Universal-bundle criterion for representability

Target `RJ.0/universal-criterion` (theorem). Under RJ.0 hypotheses, giving a normalized representation of F is equivalent to giving a representing S-scheme P for F and its universal rigidified line-bundle class. The universal class admits an actual rigidified representative; the resulting (U,τ) is unique up to unique rigidified isomorphism. A family of classes without the natural bijection is not a representability proof.

Hypotheses: π:X→S is proper, flat and finitely presented, e:S→X is a section, and O_T→(π_T)_*O_(X_T) is an isomorphism for every T→S.

Proof route. Evaluate a representing natural isomorphism at id_P. This lies in F(P), hence in the existing set of rigidified isomorphism classes; choose a representative. For every a:T→P, naturality identifies its pullback with the corresponding class. Conversely the all-T universal property defines the representing natural bijection. The no-automorphism theorem upgrades uniqueness of class to unique compatible isomorphism of representatives. Apply Yoneda for uniqueness of P.

Acceptance. The criterion distinguishes a universal Poincaré bundle from a family that merely has the desired fibre classes.

Direct inputs: `RelativeJacobiansAndIntegralCurveModels:RJ.0/normalized-representation`, `RelativeJacobiansAndIntegralCurveModels:RJ.0/rigidified-comparison`.

Sources. [kleiman] §4 Exercise 4.3, p.27; Lemma 2.10, p.19. The universal-sheaf criterion and rigidity give this direct Yoneda consequence, here restricted to a specified subfunctor.

### Restriction of a universal bundle to a represented subfunctor

Target `RJ.0/represented-subfunctor` (construction). Suppose Q with U_Q represents the ambient rigidified Picard functor. Let i:P→Q be a monomorphism of S-schemes and suppose that for every T, F(T) is exactly the image of Hom_S(T,P) under i in Pic^rig(T). Pulling U_Q back along id_X×i gives a normalized representation of F on P. The conclusion requires the stated image equality on all test schemes, not just geometric fibres.

Hypotheses: π:X→S is proper, flat and finitely presented, e:S→X is a section, and O_T→(π_T)_*O_(X_T) is an isomorphism for every T→S. Q and i:P→Q are as in the statement; no representability assertion is made for unspecified subfunctors.

Proof route. Monomorphy makes Hom(T,P)→Hom(T,Q) injective. The assumed image condition identifies its image with F(T). Pull back the ambient universal bundle and its section trivialization. The representing bijections and pullback identities prove the defining equation of a normalized representation.

Uses. RJ.1 degree-zero representation: Restricts the full Picard universal bundle to the already owned degree-zero Jacobian. Representable Picard subfunctors in arithmetic moduli: Supplies a checked universal bundle only after the separate representability and image hypotheses have been proved.

Proposed API:

- `NormalizedPicardRepresentation.restrict` (constructor): Construct the representation on P by pulling the universal bundle along id×i.
- `NormalizedPicardRepresentation.restrict_classify` (simp): Classification in F followed by i equals ambient classification in Q.
- `NormalizedPicardRepresentation.restrict_baseChange` (compatibility): Restriction commutes with every S′→S, retaining the all-test-scheme image condition.
- `NormalizedPicardRepresentation.restrict_comp` (functoriality): For P′→P→Q, the two restriction constructions agree by the canonical rigidified isomorphism.

Checks:

- `representedSubfunctor_identity` (degenerate): For i=id_Q the representation is isomorphic to the original ambient representation.
- `representedSubfunctor_degreeZero` (compatibility): For P¹_S, restricting Z_S to component 0 gives P=S with trivial universal bundle.
- `representedSubfunctor_nonreduced` (non-example): A morphism Spec k→Spec k[ε]/(ε²) is bijective on geometric points but does not have the same functor of points; replacing the image condition by geometric-point equality must fail.
- `representedSubfunctor_composition` (characterisation): Restricting first to Pic⁰ and then to its zero section gives the same rigidified bundle as restricting directly to that zero section.

Acceptance. Open-and-closed degree-zero inclusion recovers the curve×J bundle.

Direct inputs: `RelativeJacobiansAndIntegralCurveModels:RJ.0/normalized-representation`, `RelativeJacobiansAndIntegralCurveModels:RJ.0/universal-criterion`.

Sources. [kleiman] §4 Exercises 4.3–4.4, p.27. Pullback of a universal bundle realizes the Yoneda restriction to a represented subfunctor. This is a formal consequence, not a theorem that every subfunctor is represented.

### Normalization at the section and at zero

Target `RJ.0/zero-normalization` (theorem). If F is a subgroup functor and (P,U,τ) is its normalized representation, the zero element defines z:S→P. There is a unique trivialization of U along X×z compatible with τ at e×z. Thus U is normalized along both e×P and X×z, their trivializations agree at the intersection, and the normalization is preserved by arbitrary base change. The group structure on P is transported from F by Yoneda.

Hypotheses: π:X→S is proper, flat and finitely presented, e:S→X is a section, and O_T→(π_T)_*O_(X_T) is an isomorphism for every T→S.

Proof route. Classify zero and classify the group operations using the natural bijection; Yoneda proves the group identities. The restriction to X×z represents the trivial rigidified line. Its rigidified isomorphism to O is unique, so its overlap with τ is the prescribed unit identification. Pullback preserves the two rigidifications, and uniqueness proves their compatibility without testing only geometric fibres.

Acceptance. For a base line M, tensoring U by pr_P*M generally spoils τ; renormalization cancels M. The zero of P must classify the trivial rigidified line, not a translated degree-zero class.

Direct inputs: `RelativeJacobiansAndIntegralCurveModels:RJ.0/normalized-representation`, `RelativeJacobiansAndIntegralCurveModels:RJ.0/universal-criterion`.

Sources. [blr] §8.1 Proposition 4 and rigidification, pp.204–205. Section normalization removes automorphisms; applying it to the identity class supplies the second axis normalization. [kleiman] Lemma 2.10 p.19 and Exercise 4.3 p.27. Uniqueness of rigidified representatives makes the two-axis compatibility canonical.

### Pointed proper curves enter the projective suppliers

Target `RJ.1/local-projectivity` (theorem). Under the smooth arithmetic hypotheses, the image Γ_e is an effective relative Cartier divisor of fibre degree one. O_X(Γ_e) is π-ample. In particular X is projective over each affine open of S, and the projective relative-Picard results apply there and glue canonically. This statement does not require a global embedding in P^n_S on a nonaffine base.

Hypotheses: S is a connected locally noetherian Dedekind scheme with function field K; π:X→S is smooth proper of relative dimension one with geometrically connected fibres and section e. Genus is locally constant; apply componentwise if S is disconnected.

Proof route. Import the section-Cartier theorem and the positive-degree criterion for proper curve fibres from StableReduction Layer 2. Over an affine noetherian base, a sufficiently high power gives a projective presentation. Apply the relative Picard supplier locally; its natural all-test-scheme description identifies and glues the overlaps.

Acceptance. For P¹_S the section line has degree one on each fibre. A section on a reducible semistable special fibre has degree zero on components it misses; this proof of ampleness cannot be reused there.

### Generic points and degree-zero divisor classes

Target `RJ.1/generic-class-equivalence` (construction). Let C=X_K and let Ω=K^sep be a chosen separable closure. There is a canonical group isomorphism genericClassEquiv:J_K(Ω)≅Cl⁰(C_Ω), where Cl is the group of divisors at all closed points modulo principal divisors, degree weights a closed point by [κ(x):Ω], and Cl⁰ is the kernel of degree. The inverse sends [D] to the relative class of the section-normalized O(D). This concerns actual divisor classes, not arbitrary effective divisors or only Ω-rational points.

Hypotheses: S is a connected locally noetherian Dedekind scheme with function field K; π:X→S is smooth proper of relative dimension one with geometrically connected fibres and section e. Genus is locally constant; apply componentwise if S is disconnected. Ω is a separable closure with its specified K-algebra structure.

Proof route. Base change the represented rigidified degree-zero functor to Ω and use its Ω-points. The section removes the quotient by Pic(Ω), which is trivial. Import the smooth regular proper curve divisor/line-bundle dictionary from JacobianChallenge Layer A and the scheme/function-field comparison from AlgebraicCurves Layer 12. Restriction of the degree comparison identifies Pic⁰ with the kernel of the residue-weighted divisor degree. Multiplication of line bundles agrees with addition of divisors, giving the group isomorphism.

Uses. EisensteinIdealAndRationalTorsion, proposed arithmetic torsion consumer: Transfers rational torsion classes to the actual Jacobian point group before reduction. RJ.5 every-prime export: Identifies the generic arithmetic input to the specialization homomorphism without replacing it by a free divisor group.

Proposed API:

- `genericClassEquiv` (equivalence): The displayed degree-preserving group isomorphism on Ω-points.
- `genericClassEquiv_ofDivisor` (simp): The classifying point of N_e(O(D)) maps to [D] for deg D=0.
- `genericClassEquiv_add` (structure): Addition in J corresponds to addition of divisor classes.
- `genericClassEquiv_scalarExtension` (compatibility): For a field extension Ω→Ω′, scalar extension of points corresponds to flat pullback of divisor classes, with its residue multiplicities.
- `genericClassEquiv_places` (compatibility): Using the imported closed-point/place equivalence, scheme divisor coefficients and residue-weighted degrees agree with the existing function-field class group.

Checks:

- `genericClassEquiv_principal` (degenerate): For f≠0, genericClassEquiv sends the point represented by O(div f) to zero.
- `genericClassEquiv_elliptic` (computation): For an elliptic C with origin e and P∈C(Ω), the usual point P corresponds to [P−e], with positive coefficient at P.
- `genericClassEquiv_genusZero` (compatibility): For C=P¹_K the source and the degree-zero class group are trivial.

### Divisor families compute specialization when the hypotheses hold

### Finite flat closure of a finite group of integral points

Target `RJ.5/finite-subgroup-closure` (construction). Let R be a DVR with fraction field K, G/R a separated finite-type group scheme, and H⊂G(R) a finite subgroup whose generic points are distinct. The schematic closure H̄ of the finite reduced constant generic subgroup H_K in G is a finite flat closed subgroup scheme over R, with generic fibre H_K. The map H_R→H̄ from the finite constant R-group scheme is generically an isomorphism but need not be an isomorphism on the special fibre. For specialization injectivity it suffices to apply this construction to the finite cyclic subgroup generated by one torsion section; no finiteness of all of A(K)_tors is assumed.

Hypotheses: R is a DVR, G is separated and of finite type, H is a finite subgroup of its R-points. The generic embedding is part of the data.

Proof route. Every section Spec R→G is closed by separatedness. The finite disjoint union of these sections is finite over G and over R; its schematic image equals the generic schematic closure because its source is R-flat and the generic fibre is dense. This finite surjective cover makes the image proper over R by descent of properness. Its fibres have finitely many points, so it is quasi-finite; proper quasi-finite implies finite. In affine coordinates the closure ring injects into K^H, so it is R-torsion-free and therefore flat. Raynaud §2.1 gives compatibility of generic schematic closure with flat products, so multiplication, inverse and identity restrict to the closure. The constant-group map and its generic identity are retained explicitly.

Uses. RJ.5 residue-primary torsion injectivity: Provides the actual finite flat prolongation to which Raynaud uniqueness applies. Raynaud §2.1 and Theorem 3.3.3: Connects integral sections of a possibly nonproper smooth group to the finite-flat generic-fibre theorem.

Proposed API:

- `integralFiniteSubgroupClosure` (constructor): The finite flat closed subgroup of G generated schematically by the specified finite generic subgroup.
- `integralFiniteSubgroupClosure.genericIso` (equivalence): Its generic fibre is the reduced constant subgroup H_K with the specified embedding in G_K.
- `integralFiniteSubgroupClosure.constantMap` (data): The morphism H_R→H̄ extending the identity on H_K.
- `integralFiniteSubgroupClosure.flatBaseChange` (functoriality): Flat DVR base change of the closure agrees with closure of the base-changed generic subgroup.
- `integralFiniteSubgroupClosure.unique` (characterisation): A flat closed subgroup of G with that generic fibre equals the schematic closure.

Checks:

- `finiteSubgroupClosure_trivial` (degenerate): For H={0}, the closure is the identity section Spec R.
- `finiteSubgroupClosure_distinctReductions` (computation): If the finitely many sections have pairwise distinct reductions, the closure is their disjoint union and constantMap is an isomorphism.
- `finiteSubgroupClosure_rootsOfUnity` (non-example): Over R=Z_l[ζ_l], let G=G_m and H be all l-th roots of unity. The closure is μ_l; its special fibre is nonreduced, while the constant group has l distinct special points. Generic equality does not make constantMap an isomorphism.
- `finiteSubgroupClosure_native` (compatibility): When G is itself finite flat, this closure agrees with R07.1/schematic-closure-of-generic-subgroups. For general separated finite-type G, its affine ideal is still contraction of the generic subgroup ideal and its coordinate ring has zero R-torsion; the extra finite-union argument is required.

Acceptance. The construction is of a finite flat scheme; a union of reductions as a set cannot replace it.

Sources. [raynaud] §2.1, pp.259–260. The affine contraction-ideal description makes generic closures flat and preserves subgroup operations. Finiteness here is an additional finite-union-of-integral-sections argument; it is not inferred from an arbitrary closure inside a nonproper group.

### Injectivity on prime-to-residue torsion

Target `RJ.5/prime-to-injectivity` (theorem). Let R be any DVR with residue field k and N/R the full Néron model of A/K. For every n≥1 invertible in k, specialization A(K)[n]→N_k(k)[n] is injective. If char k=ℓ>0, its restriction to the subgroup of torsion of order prime to ℓ is injective. If char k=0 it is injective on all torsion. No good-reduction, semistability, completeness or perfect-residue-field hypothesis is imposed.

Hypotheses: The Néron model exists; n is a unit of R. Torsion denotes points killed by n, not necessarily points of exact order n.

Proof route. Replace R by its strict henselization, which preserves the Néron model. The original point maps injectively to generic points after this faithfully flat field extension. By BLR 7.3/2, multiplication by n on the smooth commutative group N is étale. BLR 7.3/3 identifies its n-torsion sections over the strict henselization with n-torsion points over the separable residue closure; hence an n-torsion section reducing to zero is zero. Descend equality to R. To compare two prime-to-ℓ torsion points use a common annihilator prime to ℓ on their difference; characteristic-zero residue allows every n.

Acceptance. At split multiplicative reduction the theorem still applies to torsion lying in nonidentity components. For n=1 the source is trivial. When n is divisible by ℓ the étale proof does not apply; the separate ramification theorem is required.

Direct inputs: `RelativeJacobiansAndIntegralCurveModels:RJ.4/reduction-hom`, `NeronModelsAndSemistableAbelianVarieties:R11.1/etale-basechange`, `NeronModelsAndSemistableAbelianVarieties:R11.2`.

Sources. [blr] §7.3 Lemma 2 and Proposition 3, p.179. For smooth commutative finite-type group schemes, unit-order torsion sections over the strict henselization are determined by their residue points. Applying this to the full Néron model proves injectivity.

### Residue-primary torsion at small absolute ramification

Target `RJ.5/small-ramification-injectivity` (theorem). Let R be a DVR of mixed characteristic (0,ℓ), with normalized valuation v_R, e=v_R(ℓ), and e<ℓ−1. For the full Néron model N of A/K, specialization is injective on A(K)[ℓ^∞]. More generally the reduction G(R)[ℓ^∞]→G_k(k) is injective for any separated finite-type commutative R-group scheme G. Thus completeness, finite extension of Q_ℓ, perfect residue field and good reduction are not needed. The strict bound forces ℓ≥3.

Hypotheses: R has mixed characteristic, v_R(π)=1 and e=v_R(ℓ)<ℓ−1; points are defined over this K, so e is not inherited unchanged after ramified extension.

Proof route. For a point of order dividing ℓ^m in the reduction kernel, take the finite cyclic group H generated by its integral section and construct its finite flat schematic closure H̄. Both H_R and H̄ are finite flat commutative ℓ-power group schemes with the same generic fibre. Import Raynaud uniqueness/full faithfulness from R07.1/raynaud-uniqueness to make constantMap an isomorphism. Distinct elements of a finite constant group have distinct reductions; the chosen kernel point is therefore zero. Apply this to differences to obtain injectivity on the union of ℓ-power torsion subgroups.

Acceptance. For R=Z_ℓ and ℓ≥3, the ℓ-primary specialization is injective at good and bad reduction alike. At e=ℓ−1 over Z_ℓ[ζ_ℓ], μ_ℓ and the constant group have identical generic fibres but different special fibres, so the proof and its bound cannot be weakened. For a split Tate curve over Q_ℓ(ζ_ℓ), the nonzero ℓ-torsion point represented by ζ_ℓ reduces to the identity in its G_m identity component, exhibiting failure at the boundary.

Sources. [raynaud] §3.3 Theorem 3.3.3 and Corollary 3.3.6, p.268; §2.1 pp.259–260. The strict ramification bound gives uniqueness of the finite flat prolongation. Applying it to the finite closure of a rational cyclic torsion group is the specialization consequence proved here.

### Arithmetic specialization at every prime

Target `RJ.5/every-prime-export` (theorem). Let A be the pointed generic Jacobian over the function field K of a noetherian Dedekind scheme S, with full Néron model N. For every closed s, sp_s is the natural homomorphism A(K)→N_s(κ(s)). It is injective on torsion of order prime to char κ(s), with the characteristic-zero interpretation of RJ.5/prime-to-injectivity. Through genericClassEquiv this specializes K-rational degree-zero divisor classes, equivalently the Galois-invariant geometric class group. The theorem applies to bad primes without discarding them. On regular semistable curve models, the Picard quotient and identity-component computations agree with sp_s under their exact multidegree hypotheses.

Hypotheses: Use the separate existence contract for N. At a given s, only the stated primary subgroup is claimed injective unless the strict ramification bound holds.

Proof route. Combine the point-class equivalence and Galois descent with the full Néron specialization construction. Apply the prime-to theorem independently at every closed point. Under the mixed-characteristic bound apply the residue-primary theorem as well; split a finite-order point into its ℓ-primary and prime-to-ℓ parts to prove the full-torsion kernel is zero. Use the functoriality and divisor-reduction comparisons to identify the maps in arithmetic consumers. Neither the finite residue-field point count nor a nonzero component group changes the target of the reduction map.

Acceptance. Over a number field unramified at a prime above ℓ≥3, all rational torsion injects into the full Néron special-fibre group, even at a bad fibre. At a prime above 2 the strict bound never holds; prime-to-2 torsion still injects and no general 2-primary injection is claimed. At a semistable reducible fibre the component contribution is retained, so the export can serve integral modular-Jacobian correspondences.

Direct inputs: `RelativeJacobiansAndIntegralCurveModels:RJ.1/galois-equivariance`, `RelativeJacobiansAndIntegralCurveModels:RJ.4/reduction-functoriality`, `RelativeJacobiansAndIntegralCurveModels:RJ.4/divisor-reduction`, `RelativeJacobiansAndIntegralCurveModels:RJ.5/prime-to-injectivity`, `RelativeJacobiansAndIntegralCurveModels:RJ.5/small-ramification-injectivity`.

Sources. [blr] §7.3 Proposition 3 p.179; §9.5 Theorem 4 pp.267–268. These supply reduction and the semistable Picard comparison. [raynaud] §3.3 Theorem 3.3.3, p.268. Its small-ramification consequence supplies the missing residue-primary injection. The all-torsion combination is the elementary primary-decomposition argument stated here.

Worked ramification-bound check. In R=Z_ℓ[ζ_ℓ] the normalized valuation has v_R(ℓ)=ℓ−1. The ℓ distinct integral roots define a map from the constant cyclic group of order ℓ to μ_ℓ. It is an isomorphism generically, but modulo the uniformizer all roots become 1 and μ_ℓ is nonreduced. For a split Tate curve with parameter q of positive valuation, ζ_ℓ represents a nonzero ℓ-torsion point in its identity component and reduces to 1. Over Z_ℓ with ℓ≥3, by contrast, e_R=1<ℓ−1 and both primary arguments apply even at bad reduction. At ℓ=2 the strict inequality has no solutions.
