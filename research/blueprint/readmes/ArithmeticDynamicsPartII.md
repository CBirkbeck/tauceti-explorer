# Markoff dynamics over finite fields

This continuation studies the polynomial dynamics of Markoff triples over finite fields. It imports the classical integer descent and surface conventions, proves the weight divisibility mechanism, develops the giant-orbit argument and its arithmetic estimates, and reaches prime and primitive squarefree approximation. Effective computation and nonabelian-level congruences have precise proof contracts; source corrections and unverified certificate inputs remain visible.

The endpoint of the elementary route is eventual connectivity of the ordered Markoff graph and positive integral lifting at every sufficiently large prime. A second route retains Chen’s oriented and generalized congruences, whose nonabelian-level input has not yet been assigned an atlas supplier. The squarefree endpoint is simultaneous lifting on the primitive locus. Full affine lifting at squarefree moduli is false: the point (40,40,40) modulo 65 gives a concrete obstruction.

Independently reviewed on 19 October 2026: accepted at target level after the corrections recorded below. All five named proof/certificate gaps remain open.

This is a completed **target-level design pass**, with all eight layers `planned`. It is not a closed or source-decomposed proof package. In particular the quoted bound p>3.489·10^392 remains a target with a local-estimate and certificate gap. The corresponding Lean statement is a goal, not evidence that the gap is closed.

## Conventions and existing ownership

Write M for x²+y²+z²=3xyz and X for x²+y²+z²=xyz; a star removes only the origin unless a primitive composite-modulus locus is explicitly named. Vertices and triples are ordered. For p≠3 the CA.4 twist multiplies all coordinates by 3; it also gives an integral bijection because each integral X-coordinate is divisible by 3. It does not identify the two mod-3 surfaces: M*(F₃) has eight points, X*(F₃) is empty.

V is generated by the three Vieta involutions. Γ adds coordinate permutations. Δ also adds double sign changes. Neither the Γ action nor a move-graph component is silently quotiented by signs. Γ⁺ is the oriented Nielsen **image**, not a faithful copy of Aut⁺(F₂). Graph reachability means V-orbits. Equality with Γ-orbits is proved only at the symmetric root when needed. The loopless SimpleGraph forgets a fixed-move loop; the labelled involutions and their fixed points remain in the averaging proof.

CA.4 already owns the integer/affine carriers and moves. Import these nodes from `ClassicalArithmeticCompletion:CA.4/`: `markoff-triples`, `markoff-vieta-involution`, `markoff-root-generation`, `markoff-triple-coordinates-coprime`, `markoff-reduction-mod-p`, `markoff-strong-approximation`, `markoff-level-surface`, `markoff-coefficient-three-twist`, `markoff-positive-zero-orbit`, `markoff-hurwitz-variety`, `markoff-hurwitz-moves`, and `markoff-type-point-count-mod-p`. The Hurwitz move owner includes even signs; MD.0 selects the smaller generator set explicitly. The point count is imported and adapted, not replanned.

For an affine variety V/Z, “strong approximation mod n” is the ordinary surjectivity predicate for V(Z)→V(Z/n); it is not CA.4’s positive-nonzero root predicate. No new scheme or point-functor carrier is constructed here. Its API is: every residue point has an integral preimage; integral isomorphisms preserve the predicate; prime Markoff connectivity implies the full affine predicate after adding the origin. Three checks distinguish it: A¹ lifts at every positive modulus; x²+1=0 fails modulo 5 despite having two residue roots; M fails modulo 65 at the mixed-zero point. See C24 Definition 5.5.6, p. 415.

## MD.0 — Actions and move graphs

Import the CA.4 surfaces and polynomial involutions. Distinguish V generated by moves, Γ adding permutations, and Δ adding even signs. Construct the ordered loopless graph while retaining labelled fixed moves; identify its components with V-orbits and certify the small-prime graphs.

### Vieta and Markoff groups

`ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.0/action-groups` — definition. **Planet.**

On coefficient-one X*(K) use the conjugate formulas when 3 is invertible, or the directly imported coefficient-one moves in every characteristic. Γ does not include double sign changes. Write Δ for the larger signed group only when explicitly stated.

Proof or construction route:

1. Restrict the imported involutions to the nonzero locus; each fixes the origin and is bijective.
2. Use Subgroup.closure in the permutation group, and conjugate R_i by coordinate permutations to obtain normality of V in Γ.

Proof or construction route:

2. Graph vertices remain ordered; no quotient by permutations or signs is taken.

Inputs: `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.0/action-groups`, `mathlib:SimpleGraph`, `mathlib:ZMod`.

## MD.1 — Penner weights and divisibility

Extend the coordinate weights across the unique-zero-coordinate locus. Prove vertex sum and edge complementarity, then average over finite invariant sets, with fixed points permitted. Deduce p-divisibility for coefficient-three components for p≠3 and exhibit the characteristic-three exception.

### Extended Penner weights

`ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.1/penner-weights` — definition. **Planet.**

Let K be a field with 2≠0 and 3≠0. For x∈M*(K), if all coordinates are nonzero set w_i(x)=x_i/(3x_jx_k), where {i,j,k}={0,1,2}. If the unique zero coordinate is i₀, set w_{i₀}=0 and w_i=1/2 for i≠i₀. A nonzero solution has at most one zero coordinate, so these cases exhaust the carrier. Raw formulas outside M* carry no assertion.

Proof or construction route:

1. If two coordinates vanish the equation forces the third square, hence the third coordinate, to vanish.
2. Define by the indicated finite case distinction; prove independence of the order of j,k using commutativity.

Inputs: `ClassicalArithmeticCompletion:CA.4/markoff-triples`, `mathlib:ZMod`.

Source: M25, §2, equation (2.1), pp. 624–625. These weights make summation work on vertices with a zero coordinate.

API obligations:

- `weight_nonzero_product` (simp): If x_jx_k≠0 then w_i=x_i/(3x_jx_k), including x_i=0.
- `weight_zero_coordinate` (simp): If x_i=0 then w_i=0 and the other two weights are 1/2.
- `weight_perm` (compatibility): w_{σ(i)}(σx)=w_i(x).
- `weight_sum` (relation): Σ_i w_i(x)=1 on M*.

Distinguishing checks:

- [ ] **computation** — At the coefficient-three root over Q, all three weights are 1/3.
- [ ] **computation** — At (0,1,2) over F₅ the weights are (0,3,3).
- [ ] **non-example** — At the excluded origin the all-nonzero formula and singular branch do not define weights summing to one; the carrier hypothesis is essential.

Used by: `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.1/weight-identities`: the changed coordinate has complementary weights; `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.1/component-divisibility`: sum weights first by vertex and then by coordinate

Acceptance:

- [ ] Every singular-denominator branch has a value; no use of field division by zero as an extension.

### Penner weight identities

`ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.1/weight-identities` — theorem.

For x∈M*(K) in the preceding characteristic range, Σ_i w_i(x)=1 and w_i(x)+w_i(R_i x)=1 for every i. In particular R_i x=x implies w_i(x)=1/2.

Proof or construction route:

1. When the product is nonzero divide the Markoff equation by 3x₀x₁x₂ to obtain the total.
2. For edge complementarity, if x_jx_k≠0 substitute 3x_jx_k−x_i; the same expression applies when the changed coordinate becomes zero.
3. If a different coordinate is zero, both i-weights are 1/2. Use 2≠0 for fixed points.

Inputs: `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.1/penner-weights`, `ClassicalArithmeticCompletion:CA.4/markoff-vieta-involution`.

Source: M25, §2, p. 625. The edge calculation includes both denominator cases and fixed moves.

Acceptance:

- [ ] At a fixed point prove 2w_i=1; never pair points into two-element orbits.

### Finite involution balance

`ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.1/involution-balance` — theorem.

If C is a finite type, K a field with 2≠0, r:C→C an involution and f:C→K satisfies f(x)+f(r x)=1 for every x, then Σ_x f(x)=(#C:K)/2. The conclusion permits fixed points and an empty C.

Proof or construction route:

1. Use involutivity to make r a permutation and reindex the finite sum.
2. Add the original and reindexed sums termwise; divide by 2.

Inputs: `mathlib:Equiv.Perm`.

Source: M25, §2, equations (2.3)–(2.5), p. 625. The averaging identity is the full finite-set argument underlying the proof.

Acceptance:

- [ ] A singleton with r=id and f=1/2 satisfies the formula; a two-cycle is not required.

### Chen’s divisibility theorem

`ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.1/component-divisibility` — theorem. **Planet.**

For prime p>3, every finite subset S⊆M*(F_p) stable under all R_i satisfies p∣#S. In particular each V-orbit, each Γ-orbit and each graph component has size divisible by p. Together with the p=2 computation this holds for coefficient-three graphs precisely in the asserted range p≠3. It does not hold for their eight-point component at 3.

Proof or construction route:

1. Restrict each R_i to S and apply involution balance separately to each coordinate weight.
2. Sum weight_sum over S: (#S:F_p)=(3/2)(#S:F_p). Rearrange using invertibility of 2 to deduce #S=0 in F_p.
3. Convert the cast equality into divisibility; use the separate p=2 enumeration.

Inputs: `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.1/weight-identities`, `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.1/involution-balance`, `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.0/components`, `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.0/finite-regressions`, `mathlib:ZMod.natCast_eq_zero_iff`.

Source: M25, Theorem 1.4, p. 624; proof §2, pp. 624–625. This is Martin’s elementary proof with the necessary characteristic-three correction.

Acceptance:

- [ ] Use the invariant subset statement, which is stronger than a component-only target; retain the counterexample p=3.

## MD.2 — Conic sections and rotations

Parametrize split, nonsplit and parabolic slices on coefficient-one X. Import CA.4’s total point count. Compute the explicit divisor-sum rotation orbit count, separating eigenvalue orders from unipotent periods and special zero-trace fibres.

### Conic sections and rotations

`ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.2/conic-rotations` — definition. **Planet.**

For odd prime p and a∈F_p set C_i(a)={x∈X*(F_p):x_i=a}. Rotation ρ₀(a,b,c)=(a,c,ac−b) fixes C₀(a), with cyclic analogues. On (b,c) it has matrix [[0,1],[-1,a]] and characteristic polynomial T²−aT+1. For a≠±2 define its order by the order of either root λ in F_{p²}ˣ; inversion leaves this order unchanged. At a=±2 use the actual unipotent matrix order, not the eigenvalue order.

Proof or construction route:

1. Restrict the product of a transposition and a Vieta map to the conic.
2. Compute the matrix and characteristic polynomial; split the repeated-root cases before defining order.

Inputs: `ClassicalArithmeticCompletion:CA.4/markoff-level-surface`, `ClassicalArithmeticCompletion:CA.4/markoff-hurwitz-moves`, `mathlib:orderOf`.

Source: BGS16, §2, equations (6)–(15), pp. 4–6. Slice rotations turn the action into multiplication in cyclic tori.

API obligations:

- `rotation_formula` (simp): ρ₀(a,b,c)=(a,c,ac−b).
- `rotation_slice` (relation): ρ_i preserves C_i(a).
- `rotation_torus` (compatibility): If a²≠4, λ+λ⁻¹=a and the matrix diagonalizes over F_{p²}; ord λ divides p−1 or p+1.
- `rotation_inverse_root` (relation): The order is unchanged when λ is replaced by λ⁻¹.

Distinguishing checks:

- [ ] **computation** — At the coefficient-one root, ρ₀(3,3,3)=(3,3,6).
- [ ] **degenerate** — At a=2 the eigenvalue is 1 but the rotation matrix has order p for odd p.
- [ ] **non-example** — At a=0 the eigenvalues have order 4, not 2; a point on a degenerate line must be handled separately.

Used by: `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.2/slice-classification`: parametrize and count rotation cycles; `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.3/middle-game`: increase a coordinate’s multiplicative order

Acceptance:

- [ ] Distinguish coordinate order from the period of a special point on a degenerate conic.

### Split and nonsplit conic sections

`ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.2/slice-classification` — theorem.

For odd p and a≠0,±2, put κ=a²/(a²−4). If a²−4 is a nonzero square, choose λ∈F_pˣ with λ+λ⁻¹=a: t↦(a,t+κ/t,λt+κ/(λt)) parametrizes C₀(a) bijectively for t∈F_pˣ. In the nonsplit case use t∈F_{p²}ˣ with t^{p+1}=κ, and the same formula; this parametrizes C₀(a), with rotation t↦λt. Thus the slice sizes are p−1 and p+1 and all periods equal ord λ. At a=0 the nonzero conic has 2(p−1) points if −1 is a square and is empty otherwise. At ±2 it has 2p points if −1 is a square and is empty otherwise; the ±2 rotation periods are p and 2p respectively.

Proof or construction route:

1. Solve the two eigenline coordinates, using a²−4≠0 and κ≠0.
2. For nonsplit λ^p=λ⁻¹; the coordinates lie in F_p exactly when t^p=κ/t.
3. Treat a=0 and ±2 by factoring the quadratic equation rather than specializing the generic formula.

Inputs: `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.2/conic-rotations`.

Source: BGS16, §2, pp. 4–6. The explicit parametrizations include all exceptional slice types.

Source: C24, §5.3, pp. 406–408. Chen uses these slice types in the point and rotation counts.

Acceptance:

- [ ] Check that κ−1=4/(a²−4) is nonzero; omitting this invalidates the subsequent irreducibility step.

### Rotation orbit count

`ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.2/rotation-orbit-count` — definition. **Planet.**

Proof or construction route:

2. Count elements of exact order d by φ(d) and divide each slice size by d.
3. Replace the excluded eigenvalues ±1 and the degenerate slices by their direct cycle counts.

## MD.3 — The giant orbit and sparse exceptions

Connect maximal-order slices by sharp curve estimates; increase medium orders by the rational-function subgroup estimate; exclude small orbits by cyclotomic norms. Prove a p^ε giant complement and the x^ε exceptional-prime bound, with separate exact supplier contracts for each arithmetic estimate.

### The cage

`ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.3/cage` — definition. **Planet.**

For sufficiently large odd p, a point of X*(F_p) lies in the cage if some coordinate is a nondegenerate split trace of eigenvalue order p−1 or a nondegenerate nonsplit trace of order p+1; include the nonempty parabolic slices at ±2 as specified by the BGS endgame. The cage is a union of complete rotation cycles, not by definition a Γ-orbit. Its nonemptiness and membership in a single Γ-orbit are theorem obligations.

Proof or construction route:

1. Select maximal-order elements of the two cyclic tori and form their trace slices.
2. Add the parabolic slices only when nonempty; keep membership a finite predicate.

Inputs: `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.2/conic-rotations`, `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.2/slice-classification`.

Source: BGS16, §3.2, p. 11; endgame §3.1, pp. 7–11. The endgame supplies a common orbit for the largest rotation cycles.

API obligations:

- `cage_member` (characterisation): Membership is the disjunction of the maximal-order slice and stated parabolic cases.
- `cage_rotation_closed` (relation): A rotation fixing a selected maximal coordinate preserves its cage slice.
- `cage_sign_stable` (compatibility): The eventual giant orbit containing the cage is stable under the Klein four sign action, by uniqueness and commutation/normalization.

Distinguishing checks:

- [ ] **non-example** — Having merely a coordinate of order dividing p−1 is insufficient: the required order is p−1.
- [ ] **degenerate** — For p≡3 mod4 no parabolic point is added, since those slices are empty.
- [ ] **compatibility** — At a=0 an eigenvalue has order 4; it is not treated as a generic maximal-order nondegenerate trace.

Used by: `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.3/primitive-intersections`: large orders enter a common orbit; `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.5/effective-intervals`: disconnectedness is tested against the cage orbit

Acceptance:

### Incidence of conic sections

`ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.3/slice-incidence` — theorem.

For every sufficiently large prime p, the graph of nonempty conic sections in different coordinate directions, with an edge when the slices meet, has diameter at most two after identifying coincident cases in the BGS construction. More explicitly, for a,b nonexceptional there is c such that C₁(a) meets C₃(c) and C₂(b) meets C₃(c); pairwise intersections are governed by 1+χ(a²b²−4(a²+b²)).

Proof or construction route:

1. Solve the remaining quadratic; treat a zero discriminant with χ(0)=0.
2. Count candidate c with both discriminants squares by expanding products of quadratic characters.
3. Remove exceptional c and common-root cases separately, then apply the cited character bound.

Inputs: `ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.2/slice-classification`, `FiniteFieldsAndCharacterSums:FF.2/weil-bound-multiplicative`.

Source: BGS16, Proposition 6 and proof, pp. 6–7. Quadratic-character estimates connect the conic incidence graph.

Acceptance:

- [ ] A positive count must retain the finite exceptional set and a stated lower bound for p.

### Primitive-coordinate intersections

`ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.3/primitive-intersections` — theorem.

Fix δ>0. ### The middle game

`ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.3/middle-game` — theorem.

For every ε>0 there exist δ>0 and P such that for primes p>P, any Γ-orbit containing a coordinate of nondegenerate order L≥p^ε reaches the cage. A non-cage cycle in the middle range admits a point whose coordinate order is strictly larger than L; iteration terminates in the endgame.

### The opening game

`ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.3/opening-game` — theorem. **Planet.**

There are c>0 and P such that for every prime p>P every Γ-orbit on X*(F_p) has at least c(log p)^(1/3) points. There is no finite Γ-orbit on X*(Qbar).

Proof or construction route:

1. A finite characteristic-zero orbit forces all coordinate eigenvalues to be roots of unity; after a complex embedding their traces lie in [-2,2]. For u²+v²+w²=uvw the bound |uvw|≤(2/3)(u²+v²+w²) forces the origin.
2. For a purported small finite-field orbit, bound the three coordinate eigenvalue orders by its size, excluding the already large parabolic cycles. Put N equal to their lcm; p∤N. In Q(ζ_N), choose roots reducing to the given eigenvalues at a prime above p, and form the integral Markoff relation α.
3. The characteristic-zero argument makes α nonzero. RingOfIntegers.dvd_norm shows α divides its integral norm, so reduction at that prime forces p to divide the nonzero integer norm. Algebra.norm_eq_prod_embeddings bounds its absolute value by 20^{[Q(ζ_N):Q]}=20^{φ(N)}. Since N is at most the product of the three orders, obtain the logarithmic lower bound.
4. Treat orders 1,2 and trace zero separately; trace zero has eigenvalue order 4.

`ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.5/subgroup-intersection` — theorem. **Planet.**

### Divisor antichain bounds

`ArithmeticDynamicsPartII:MD.5/rank-bound` — theorem.

For n≥1 and x≥1, let [r,s] contain all Ω(d) for d∈M_x(n), and choose an integer k∈[r,s] closest to Ω(n)/2. Then #M_x(n)≤C_k(n). In particular #M_x(n)≤C_floor(Ω(n)/2)(n)≤τ(n)·binom(Ω(n),floor(Ω(n)/2))/2^{Ω(n)}. No analogous normalized binomial bound is asserted for every off-central rank.

3. For the central normalized bound, split a repeated prime factor into a new prime and compare τ-normalized central rank counts; iterate to a squarefree factorization.

### Primitivity of the Markoff block action

### Independence of prime actions
