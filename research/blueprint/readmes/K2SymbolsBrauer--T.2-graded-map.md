# Explicit K₂: the Milnor to Quillen graded comparison

This part covers **T.2:graded-map**. For a field \(F\), its target is the natural
unital graded ring homomorphism
\[
  \Phi_F:K^M_*(F)\longrightarrow K_*(F),\qquad
  \{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\longmapsto [a_1]\cdots[a_n].
\]
Here \([a]\) is the degree-one class of the unit matrix \((a)\), transported
from classical \(K_1\) to the chosen Quillen model. The order of the entries is
part of the formula. Degree two agrees with the classical Matsumoto
isomorphism followed by the classical-to-Quillen comparison, normalized by one
sign that is fixed on the torus. Degree three is exported to
**K3BlochGroups V.2**.

The construction and its degree-three component already belong to
`K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2/graded-map` and
`K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2/graded-map-degree-three` in
[K2SymbolsBrauer--T.1.json](../packets/K2SymbolsBrauer--T.1.json).
They retain those identities. This continuation specifies their interfaces and
adds two targets: the product of two unit classes,
`K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2/unit-loop-product-symbol`, and the equality of the
degree-two homomorphisms, `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2/graded-map-degree-two-identification`.
It does not supply a second Milnor ring, a second higher K-theory product, or a
second degree-three construction.

## Objects and conventions

All fields are commutative. Field homomorphisms preserve one. All tensor
products used to define Milnor K-theory are over \(\mathbb Z\). Write
\(U_F=\operatorname{Additive}(F^\times)\); the multiplicative identity in
\(F^\times\) becomes zero in this additive group. Its integer module structure
is the canonical one.

The imported `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2/milnor-k-theory` is the graded ring
\[
 T_{\mathbb Z}(U_F)/\langle\ell(a)\ell(1-a):a\ne0,1\rangle.
\]
The brackets denote the two-sided ideal generated by the displayed elements.
Each generator has degree two. The grading, the degree decomposition and the
field-map action are planned in **T.2:symbols** by
`K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2:symbols/field-proof/homogeneous-components`: the
degree-\(n\) part is the \(n\)-fold tensor power modulo the relation module
built from one Steinberg tensor and arbitrary tensors on either side, and in
degree two it is the tensor quotient \(Q_2(F)\) of
`T.2:symbols/field-proof/tensor-realization`. An ungraded ring quotient does
not by itself give these components. The group \(K_n^M(F)\) is the
degree-\(n\) component; \(K_0^M(F)=\mathbb Z\), \(K_1^M(F)=U_F\), and products
of symbols concatenate the ordered tuples. The empty symbol is the ring unit.

The target is the nonnegative graded ring formed from the Quillen groups,
with addition in each homogeneous component and multiplication
\(K_p(F)\times K_q(F)\to K_{p+q}(F)\). **GeneralAlgebraicKTheory K.7** owns the
pairing produced by the biexact tensor-product functor, its associativity,
unitality, naturality and symmetry coherence. Its unit is the class \([F]\)
in degree zero. For homogeneous classes, the symmetry law is
\(xy=(-1)^{pq}yx\). The total ring is used as an integer algebra; it must not
be assumed to be an ordinary commutative ring. In particular, swapping two
degree-one factors changes a sign. A square of a degree-one class need only
be killed by two; this construction does not impose its vanishing.

Write \(\theta_{1,F}:K_1^{\mathrm{cl}}(F)\simeq K_1(F)\) for the natural
comparison supplied by
`KTheoryLowDegrees:U.6/pi1-plus-construction`, including its identification
of the loop of a matrix with that matrix's quotient class.
`KTheoryLowDegrees:U.3/units-to-K1` supplies the unit matrix class, and
`KTheoryLowDegrees:U.3/SK1-field` identifies its inverse with determinant.
Thus
\[
 \iota_F(a)=\theta_{1,F}(K_1.\mathrm{ofUnits}(a))
\]
is a homomorphism from the multiplicative unit group to additive \(K_1(F)\).
In particular \(\iota_F(1)=0\) and
\(\iota_F(ab)=\iota_F(a)+\iota_F(b)\).

Write \(\theta_{2,F}:K_2^{\mathrm{cl}}(F)\simeq K_2(F)\) for the natural
comparison of the target `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/k2-pi2` in its Hurewicz
normalization \(\beta\), fixed by `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/chosen-k2-comparisons`
in the T.1 follow-up packet: the Hopf kernel equivalence of classical \(K_2\)
with \(H_2(E(F);\mathbb Z)\), the plus-construction homology isomorphism, the
inverse of the Hurewicz isomorphism of the simply connected universal cover
\(BE(F)^+\) and the cover map, transported to the Quillen model.
Classical \(K_2\) is the kernel of the stable Steinberg extension; it can be
written multiplicatively. Quillen \(K_2\) is written additively. The product
comparison is
\[
 \iota_F(a)\iota_F(b)=\theta_{2,F}\bigl(\{a,b\}_{\mathrm{cl}}^{\,\varepsilon}\bigr),
 \qquad
 \theta_{2,F}^{\natural}:=\theta_{2,F}\circ(x\mapsto x^{\varepsilon}),
 \tag{1}
\]
where \(\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}\) is the single sign of the next section. The
classical symbol in (1) is the parent's fixed symbol, the commutator of
Steinberg lifts of \(\operatorname{diag}(a,a^{-1},1)\) and
\(\operatorname{diag}(b,1,b^{-1})\). The sign is computed once from the
conventions; \(\theta_2\) is never replaced by its inverse to force (1).

## The product of two unit classes

The existence of bilinear products alone does not give (1), and it does not
give the Steinberg relation for degree-one values. The new theorem
`K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2/unit-loop-product-symbol` supplies both, for every
commutative ring \(R\) and units \(a,b\) of \(R\).

**The sign.** Present \(\mathbb Z^2\) with generators \(s,t\) and the single
relator \(sts^{-1}t^{-1}\). Hopf's formula (`T.1/hopf-formula`) identifies
\(H_2(\mathbb Z^2;\mathbb Z)\) with the infinite cyclic group generated by
the class of that relator. The torus \(B\mathbb Z^2=S^1\times S^1\) is oriented so that the
quotient \(S^1\times S^1\to S^1\wedge S^1\cong S^2\) used to multiply classes
of \(\pi_1\) has degree one. Then \(\varepsilon\) is defined by: the
fundamental class of the torus corresponds to the class of
\((sts^{-1}t^{-1})^{\varepsilon}\). It depends on the orientation, the
commutator convention \(xyx^{-1}y^{-1}\) and the Hopf isomorphism, and on
nothing else.

**The theorem.** With \(\theta_1\) and \(\theta_2\) as above and the
degree-\((1,1)\) product of \(R\) (K.7's external product followed by the
multiplication \(R\otimes R\to R\)),
\[
 \theta_1[a]\cdot\theta_1[b]=\theta_2\bigl(\{a,b\}^{\varepsilon}\bigr)
 =\theta_2^{\natural}(\{a,b\}),
\]
naturally in \(R\). In particular \(\theta_1[a]\cdot\theta_1[1-a]=0\)
whenever \(a\) and \(1-a\) are units, for either value of \(\varepsilon\).

**Proof plan.** Naturality reduces the computation to the external product
in \(A\otimes A'\) of the units \(a\otimes1\) and \(1\otimes b\). By the
model comparison requested from K.7 (below), the product of two loop classes
is Loday's plus-construction product, his §2.1.10, printed p.335 / PDF p.28.
On two unit loops it is represented, after composing with the quotient of the
torus onto \(S^1\wedge S^1\), by the plus construction of the torus map of
the commuting matrices
\[
 D_a=\operatorname{diag}(a,a^{-1},1),\qquad
 D'_b=\operatorname{diag}(b,1,b^{-1})
\]
in \(E_3\); this is the first display of the proof of Loday's
**Proposition 2.2.3, printed p.337 / PDF p.30**, which uses that subtraction
for the block-sum H-space structure is block sum with the inverse matrix. The
map lands in \(BE^+\), the universal cover of \(BGL^+\)
(`StableHomotopyKTheory:H.3/plus-universal-cover`). Its Hurewicz image is the
image of the torus fundamental class: this is Loday's **Lemma 2.2.1, printed
p.336 / PDF p.29**, for \(n=2\) and \(\Gamma=\mathbb Z^2\), using absolute
Hurewicz (Tau Ceti AlgebraicTopology stage 8) and the cross-product
fundamental class of the torus (stage 5). Through
`H.3/plus-integral-homology` this is the image of the fundamental class in
\(H_2(E)\). Naturality of Hopf's formula
(`T.1:classical/hopf-formula-natural`) sends the relator class of
\(\mathbb Z^2\) to the class of the free commutator of the two matrices, and
the identification of the universal central extension kernel with \(H_2\)
(`T.1:classical/uce-kernel-h2-natural`, `T.1/k2-h2-elementary`) sends that
class to the commutator of Steinberg lifts, because in a central extension of
a perfect group a commutator depends only on the images of its entries. By
**Weibel III.5.10–5.10.1, printed p.225 / PDF p.233**, the matrices \(D_a\)
and \(D'_b\) are the images of \(h_{12}(a)\) and \(h_{13}(b)\), so this
commutator is the classical symbol \(\{a,b\}\), and the definition of
\(\varepsilon\) gives the formula.

**Loday's sign.** Loday identifies \(\pi_2(BE^+)\) with the Steinberg kernel
through the connecting map of the extension instead, and finds the
**inverse** of the commutator (Proposition 2.2.3, printed pp.337–338 / PDF
pp.30–31); the Remark after the proof, printed p.338 / PDF p.31, points to the
Schur-multiplier route used above. The connecting-map identification of
`H.3/plus-pi2-universal-central-extension` and T.1's Hurewicz identification
must therefore be compared on the torus class before one is substituted for
the other. The T.1 follow-ups contain both: `T.1/chosen-k2-comparisons` fixes
the Hurewicz normalization \(\beta\) used here, and
`T.1:plus/normalised-comparison` fixes a normalization \(\chi\) through the
connecting map of the homotopy fibre. Both are natural equivalences between
the same groups and can differ by a sign; \(\varepsilon\) is defined relative
to \(\beta\). This is why citing the product's bilinearity cannot settle the
orientation.

**What K.7 is asked for.** Only the model comparison: for rings \(A,A'\),
under the identification of the base-point component of the K-theory space
with \(BGL^+\) (`GeneralAlgebraicKTheory:K.2:plus/plus-equals-Q`) and of
\(K_1\) with \(\pi_1BGL^+\) (U.6), the degree-\((1,1)\) part of K.7's
biexact external product equals Loday's plus product (his Theorem 2.1.11;
Weibel IV.1.10), naturally in both rings, with the identification
\(S^1\wedge S^1\cong S^2\) of K.7's sign conventions made explicit. Neither
K.7's product node nor its unit-multiplication node states this comparison,
and the locators read here state the plus product only. The value on two unit
classes and its sign stay here, with the classical \(K_2\) they concern.

**Sources for the product.** Weibel's author copy, **IV.1.10–1.10.1, printed
p.266 / PDF p.274**, states the natural associative product, its graded
symmetry, and agreement in degree \((1,1)\) with the classical matrix-symbol
product of **III.5.12–5.12.2, printed pp.227–228 / PDF pp.235–236**. Loday's
**§2.1, Proposition 2.1.8 and Theorems 2.1.11–2.1.12, printed pp.334–335 / PDF
pp.27–28**, gives the stabilized pairing and its product laws.

**Two source corrections.** First, the combined K-book author copy assigns
rank \(m+n\) to \(R^m\otimes R^n\) in the introduction to III.5.12 and repeats
that rank in the proof of III.5.12.1 on printed p.227. The correct rank is
\(mn\), with endomorphism ring \(M_{mn}(R)\); already \(m=n=1\) distinguishes
them, and the source itself notes that \(E=R\) in that case. Second, Weibel's
IV.1.10–1.10.1 asserts agreement with the classical symbol while
Corollary IV.1.7.1, printed p.265 / PDF p.273, gives the identification of
\(K_2\) with \(\pi_2BGL^+\) with no sign convention; Loday, the result cited,
proves agreement only up to sign. The statement is right for a suitable
normalization, which (1) makes explicit. Both findings are recorded against
the inspected author copy, without claims about the published edition; the
archived errata list for the published edition has neither.

## The graded map

Import the target `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2/graded-map`. Send \(U_F\) into the
degree-one component of the total target ring by \(\iota_F\). This is an
integer-linear map. **Mathlib's `TensorAlgebra.lift` already extends it
uniquely to an integer-algebra homomorphism from the tensor algebra**; there
is no new tensor-algebra universal property to develop.

For \(a\ne0,1\), the product theorem and the classical Steinberg identity give
\(\iota_F(a)\iota_F(1-a)=0\). Hence the extension kills each generator of the
two-sided Steinberg ideal and descends through the source quotient. Mathlib's
`RingQuot.liftAlgHom` supplies the ungraded relation-quotient factorization,
with `liftAlgHom_mkAlgHom_apply` and `liftAlgHom_unique` fixing its
evaluation and uniqueness. The degree presentation of
`T.2:symbols/field-proof/homogeneous-components` shows that the descended map
preserves degree: the degree-\(n\) part is spanned by products of \(n\)
degree-one generators, which go to products of \(n\) degree-one classes.
This determines the restriction \(\Phi_{F,n}:K_n^M(F)\to K_n(F)\).

For a unital field map \(f:F\to E\), both composites around the naturality
square send \(\{a\}\) to \(\iota_E(f(a))\). The imported Milnor universal
property proves their equality. The same argument gives uniqueness of
\(\Phi_F\) among unital graded maps with this degree-one generator formula.
Unitality fixes degree zero: \(n\mapsto n[F]\).

The nearest pinned degree-zero carrier is
`TauCeti.SplitK0 (FGModuleCat F)`.
`TauCeti.SplitK0.finrankEquiv` identifies this split Grothendieck group with
\(\mathbb Z\), under its stated division-ring and universe-smallness
hypotheses; `finrankEquiv_of` sends the class of a line to one. After the
canonical K.1/K.2 degree-zero model comparison, \(\Phi_{F,0}\) is the identity
of \(\mathbb Z\). The pinned split-group computation is reused; it is not
itself a theorem identifying this group with the higher K-theory space.

The imported construction has the following API. Each item serves symbol
calculation, the degree-two comparison or the V.2 consumer without unfolding
the quotient.

| Declaration | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| `milnorToQuillen` | The unique unital graded map determined by the unit classes. |
| `milnorToQuillen_zero` | \(\Phi_0(n)=n[F]\), agreeing with dimension in the existing finite-dimensional K₀ model. |
| `milnorToQuillen_one` | \(\Phi_1\) is the transported unit inclusion; the field determinant identifies it with the identity on \(U_F\). |
| `milnorToQuillen_two` | The actual degree-two map equals \(\theta_2^{\natural}\circ m_F\), as specified below. |

| `milnorToQuillen_three_symbol` | \(\Phi_3\{a,b,c\}=(\iota(a)\iota(b))\iota(c)\); associativity changes parenthesization. |

## Degree two and the degree-three export

The comparison target is
`K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2/graded-map-degree-two-identification`.
Let \(m_F:K_2^M(F)\simeq K_2^{\mathrm{cl}}(F)\) be the symbol-preserving
Matsumoto equivalence, obtained from `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2/matsumoto` through
the degree-two presentation \(K_2^M(F)\cong Q_2(F)\) of T.2:symbols. For every
field \(F\),
\[
 \Phi_{F,2}=\theta^{\natural}_{2,F}\circ m_F.                 \tag{2}
\]
Both sides agree on every two-symbol, by the symbol formula and the product
theorem; those symbols generate the degree-two additive group. Therefore the
homomorphisms agree. The right side is a composition of natural equivalences
(\(\theta_2^\natural\) is natural because \(\varepsilon\) is a constant), so
the degree-two component is a natural equivalence. Checking the Steinberg
relations proves descent, not injectivity: the completeness half of
Matsumoto's presentation remains an input, planned for infinite fields in
T.2:symbols. This comparison identifies the map already constructed; it is
not an input to that construction, so there is no circular descent argument.
For a finite field both sides vanish (Weibel III.6.1.1, printed p.231, and
III.7.2(a), printed p.245), so the sign is tested only over infinite fields.

The imported target `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2/graded-map-degree-three` is simply
\(\Phi_{F,3}\) and its ordered triple-symbol formula. The producer needs
neither its image nor its cokernel to construct it.
**K3BlochGroups V.2** imports this component and owns the decomposable
subgroup as its actual image, the indecomposable quotient as its cokernel,
the quotient maps and exactness, and the injectivity theorem. The current
`K3BlochGroups:V.2/milnor-to-quillen-degree-three` node already imports this
stage. The dependency goes from **T.2:graded-map to V.2**. No reverse edge
or independent degree-three product construction is introduced. This is
the ownership resolution of **RT-AREA-ktheory-2/20**.

## Checks and worked examples

The first eight checks belong to the imported graded construction, whose API
and tests are expanded in the continuation packet; the ninth was added in
review. Arithmetic and residue checks use computations owned by their stated
consumers; they do not add incoming dependencies to construct the map.

1. **Empty symbol.** The empty tensor is one, so its image is \([F]\).
   The pinned dimension equivalence sends the class of this line to one.
   This rejects the zero map and a map omitting the degree-zero unit.
2. **An identity entry.** In \(U_F\), the unit \(1\) is zero. Consequently
   \(\iota_F(1)=0\), and each positive-degree symbol with an entry one maps
   to zero. Treating the source group identity as the total ring's one fails
   this check.
3. **A rational unit.** For \(F=\mathbb Q\), the degree-one image of \(\{2\}\)
   has classical determinant two. A trivial map or an inverted generator
   map fails. The determinant comparison comes from U.3, not from a new
   definition of \(K_1\).
4. **Steinberg descent.** For \(a\ne0,1\), the product
   \(\iota_F(a)\iota_F(1-a)\) vanishes. Multiplying this relation by any
   symbols on either side still gives zero. Killing only an additive
   span of relations, without their two-sided products, fails the descent
   contract.
5. **An orientation-sensitive two-symbol.** In \(\mathbb Q(t)\) at \(t=0\),
   apply \((\theta_2^\natural)^{-1}\) to \(\Phi_2\{2,t\}\) and use **T.3's**
   tame-symbol convention \(\partial\{u,t\}=\bar u\). The result is two.
   Reversing the product, or using the opposite normalization, gives \(1/2\).
   Weibel III.6.3, printed p.234 / PDF p.242, uses the inverse residue
   convention; its formula must be inverted before this check. A check on
   \(\{-1,-1\}\) alone cannot detect the error because inversion fixes its
   two-torsion.
6. **Naturality.** Under \(\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q(t)\), the image of the
   degree-one class of two agrees with the class of the mapped unit. The
   same square commutes on \(\{2,3\}\). Identity and composite field maps
   obey the source and target functor laws; checking only an identity map
   would not test the field-map adapter.
7. **Triple-symbol degeneracies.** The ordered product formula gives
   \(\Phi_3\{a,b,1\}=0\) and \(\Phi_3\{a,1-a,b\}=0\) whenever all entries
   exist. These follow from the identity-entry and Steinberg checks,
   without using the V.2 injectivity theorem.
8. **No general degree-three surjectivity.** The external computation for
   \(\mathbb Q\) gives \(K_3^M(\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Z/2\) and
   \(K_3(\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Z/48\). The canonical map is injective by the
   theorem owned in V.2, so it cannot be onto. The source locators are
   Weibel VI.4.3.2, printed p.482 / PDF p.490, and VI.5.2.1, printed p.488 /
   PDF p.496. These are external acceptance results, not a proof of the
   graded map or new arithmetic targets of this part.
9. **No general injectivity.** The symbol \(\{-1,-1,-1,-1\}\) is nonzero in
   \(K_4^M(\mathbb Q)\), because its image over \(\mathbb R\) is detected by the
   sign symbol (Weibel III.7.2(c), printed p.245 / PDF p.253), but
   \(\Phi_4\) sends it to zero in \(K_4(\mathbb Q)\) (Weibel Exercise
   IV.1.12(d), printed p.275 / PDF p.283, through the vanishing of \(\eta^4\)
   in the stable stems). An interface that asserts injectivity of \(\Phi\) in
   every degree fails this check; degree three is the special case V.2 owns.

The [suggested Lean file](../suggested/K2SymbolsBrauer--T.2-graded-map.lean)
contains only statements expressible over existing baseline carriers: unique
ungraded tensor-Steinberg descent, tensor-generator extensionality, and
examples for the empty tensor, the identity unit, an ordered triple and a
Steinberg relation. It imports individual Mathlib modules. It does not
introduce axiomatic Milnor or Quillen carriers. The product theorem, the
degree-two identification and the complete mathematical API above remain the
specification for those missing carriers.

## Dependencies and coverage

The input chain is **T.2:symbols** for the Milnor quotient, its degree
presentation and the classical symbol presentation; **T.1** for classical
\(K_2\), Hopf's formula, the universal central extension and the
classical-to-Quillen \(K_2\) comparison (with its Hurewicz normalization in the
T.1 follow-up); **KTheoryLowDegrees U.3/U.6** for the
unit inclusion, determinant and its plus-model comparison;
**StableHomotopyKTheory H.1/H.3** for classifying spaces, the universal cover
\(BE^+\), the plus-construction homology isomorphism and functoriality;
**Tau Ceti AlgebraicTopology** stages 5 and 8 for the torus fundamental class
and Hurewicz; and **GeneralAlgebraicKTheory K.7** for products, with the
requested degree-\((1,1)\) model comparison. The higher K-theory space and its
model comparisons remain with **K.1/K.2:plus**.

The scoped stage is **planned**. Its two existing targets are reused, the
product theorem is planned here, and the degree-two comparison is explicit.
Two boundaries prevent a claim of closure: the K.7 model comparison, which is
this packet's request, and the residual Matsumoto gaps of T.2:symbols (the
primary finite-field presentation proof and the stable finite-rank witness).
The cover/Hurewicz boundary that T.1's \(K_2=\pi_2\) node records is now
planned in the T.1 follow-ups, on the accepted H.3 plan and AlgebraicTopology;
those follow-ups await review, and their two normalizations \(\beta\) and
\(\chi\) must be related there or in the assembly. The orientation is no
longer an open boundary: it is the sign \(\varepsilon\), relative to
\(\beta\). The atlas retains the existing planet
**Milnor to Quillen graded map** and adds **Steinberg symbols as K-theory
products** for the product theorem.
