# Relative K₁ and its maps

This document specifies the missing comparison work of `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.5`, building on [the parent plan](KTheoryLowDegrees--U.1.md). The generic relative K-space belongs to `GeneralAlgebraicKTheory:K.5`; stable Steinberg groups belong to `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1:classical`; classifying spaces, fibres and plus constructions belong to `StableHomotopyKTheory:H.1–H.3`. The parent milestone `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.6/relative-K1-homotopy-comparison` realizes U.5 as well as U.6. The declarations below supply its proof decomposition. Assembly keeps that milestone and the existing carriers.

The central task is to identify the *actual* matrix-to-relative-loop homomorphism, prove that its kernel is the relative elementary subgroup, and check both connecting maps. An isomorphism between abstract groups would not determine these arrows. The targets for congruence groups, relative Whitehead, transfers, norms, projection formulas and the valuation boundary use the parent declarations listed below.

## Objects and conventions

Let A be an associative unital ring, I a two-sided ideal, B=A/I, and q:A→B the quotient. No commutativity assumption is imposed except where an imported result requires it. All groups GL, E and St are stable. Set

- N=GL(A,I)=ker(GL(q)).
- E(A,I) equal to the normal closure **inside E(A)** of the elementary matrices eᵢⱼ(x), x∈I. Its stable normality inside GL(A) is the parent relative Whitehead theorem.
- K₁(A,I)=N/E(A,I), an abelian group with its parent map to K₁(A). The denominator is not replaced by E(A)∩N. The latter yields the image of the K₂ connecting map, not the entire relative group.
- φ_R:St(R)→GL(R), with image E(R), and classical K₂(R)=ker φ_R.
- j_R:BGL(R)→BGL(R)⁺ and V_R=fib(j_R).
- F₀=fib(BGL(q)), F⁺=fib(BGL(q)⁺), and W=fib(V_A→V_B).

Fibres use paths **f(x)→base**, the convention of `H.2/connecting-map`. Connecting maps come from inclusion of Ωtarget. Based coherent plus squares, including their square homotopies, are part of the imported functorial data. The unique UCE isomorphism κ_R:π₁V_R≃St(R) is over E(R). Pin β_R:K₂(R)≃π₂BGL(R)⁺ as the inverse of κ_R∘∂j_R on its central kernel. This pins the K₂ orientation without assuming that a separately chosen Hurewicz isomorphism has that orientation.

For degree zero use the parent augmented double D=A⊕I, with product (a,x)(b,y)=(ab,ay+xb+xy), pr(a,x)=a and add(a,x)=a+x. The common section is Δ(a)=(a,0). Under D≃A×_B A the coordinates are (pr,add), and J=ker pr maps isomorphically to I by add. The parent K₀(I) is ker(K₀(pr)). A projective triple (P,Q,α:fP≃fQ) glues second-coordinate P to first-coordinate Q; subtracting diagonal Q gives a class in that kernel, whose add-image is [P]−[Q]. These coordinates are fixed throughout.

## Proof route and ownership

The acyclic fibre of j_R has π₁ equal to the universal central extension of E(R). Uniqueness identifies it with St(R), including the map to GL(R), and uniqueness of UCE lifts proves naturality of the whole group. For q, the Steinberg presentation gives a surjection St(A)→St(B); its kernel is normally generated by roots with coefficients in I. Their image under φ_A is exactly E(A,I).

The basepoint component of F₀ is B N. This uses that classifying spaces are K(G,1), and does **not** assume GL(q) is surjective. Indeed GL(ℤ)→GL(𝔽₅) cannot reach a matrix of determinant 2. The map B N→F₀→F⁺ defines c_I:N→π₁F⁺ before any quotient comparison is asserted.

Interchange of iterated fibres gives W→F₀→F⁺ with the two projection formulas. Both V spaces are connected, and the map of their fundamental groups is the surjective St(q); hence W is connected. Its exact sequence makes c_I surjective. The other sequence W→V_A→V_B identifies the image of π₁W in St(A) with ker St(q); the projection formula identifies its image in N with E(A,I). Consequently ker c_I=E(A,I). Existing Mathlib quotient descent now yields C_I:K₁(A,I)≃π₁F⁺ with C_I([n])=c_I(n).

A natural based weak equivalence from the plus component to K(A)₀ transports C_I to Ĉ_I:K₁(A,I)≃π₁K(A,I). The π₂ terms matter for this fibre comparison. An objectwise product description, or a comparison that is natural only on π₀ and π₁, does not establish it. No natural splitting of the K-space product as H-spaces is assumed.

For z∈K₂(B), choose w∈St(A) with St(q)(w)=z and define δ_I(z)=[φ_A(w)⁻¹]. Differences of lifts map to E(A,I), making this independent of the choice. The quotient is abelian, so the inverse-word rule is multiplicative. The signed path-prism identity is c_I(φ_A(w))=∂F⁺(−β_B(z)): contraction of the plus loop uses the reversed prefix of its reduced null-homotopy to keep the fibre path pointed toward base. Thus C_Iδ_I=∂F⁺β_B. The generic interchange supplier must prove this sign identity explicitly. Changing β to its negative would change the word convention; it cannot be done silently.

This sign has a discriminating test with no two-torsion ambiguity: the square S¹→* over *→S² whose based square homotopy is adjoint to degree +1 in ΩS² induces opposite signs on the two outer fibre maps. The algebra prototype also uses a typed Multiplicative ℤ diagram, where the inverse-word boundary sends n to −n. That diagram is not asserted to arise from ring Steinberg groups.

K.5 already owns the projective-triple-to-component map and the ideal degree-zero patching comparison. Import these actual maps. The parent free gluing relation in row coordinates is pr(x)=add(y)a, so its triple automorphism is a, with second-coordinate P and first-coordinate Q. This identifies the parent K₁(B)→K₀(I) boundary with the generic connecting path, without exchanging pr and add.

The double-ring algebra calculation is also an explicit prerequisite: congruence matrices correspond to pairs (1,g), their inverse pairs are (1,g⁻¹), and elementary conjugators lift through Δ. The resulting classical relative K₁ isomorphism, together with naturality of Ĉ, gives the degree-one fibre isomorphism for (D,J)→(A,I). It is a consequence of the direct plus-square proof. It is not used as an excision assumption to prove that comparison. Degree-zero excision remains K.5's, and no higher-degree excision is inferred.

The resulting diagram compares the parent sequence

K₂(A) → K₂(B) → K₁(A,I) → K₁(A) → K₁(B) → K₀(I) → K₀(A) → K₀(B)

with the generic fibre sequence. The ring-relative arrow uses δ_I, the degree-two comparisons use β, and every matrix projection and both connecting squares are pinned. `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.6/relative-steinberg-group` supplies its existing relative sequence after its boundary convention is calibrated; its unproved comparison of relative K₂ with π₂ of the fibre is not required here.

### Steinberg group of the acyclic plus fibre

`plus-fibre-steinberg` · comparison · `TauCeti.PlusFibre.steinbergEquiv`

Write G_R=GL(R), P_R=E(R), j_R:BG_R→BG_R⁺ and V_R=fib(j_R) with paths j_R(x)→base. There is a unique isomorphism κ_R:π1(V_R)≃St(R) over P_R, hence over G_R. Its restriction to the kernel identifies ∂j_R:π2(BG_R⁺)→π1(V_R) with the inclusion of classical K2(R)=ker φ_R in St(R). Define β_R:K2(R)≃π2(BG_R⁺) as the inverse of that restricted map; this fixes its sign.

Proof or construction:

1. H.3 identifies π1(V_R)→P_R as a universal central extension. T.1 supplies St(R)→P_R as another; uniqueness and rigidity give the unique isomorphism over P_R.
2. The fibre long exact sequence and π2(BG_R)=0 make ∂j_R injective with image ker(π1(V_R)→G_R). Transport that kernel through κ_R to ker φ_R.
3. Invert this particular map to obtain β_R. Do not silently replace it by an uncalibrated Hurewicz isomorphism.

Acceptance:

- All maps preserve the displayed basepoint and the specified loop orientation.

### Naturality of the whole Steinberg identification

`plus-fibre-steinberg-natural` · lemma · `TauCeti.PlusFibre.steinbergEquiv_natural`

For a unital ring map f:R→S and a based functorial square of plus maps, κ_S∘π1(V_f)=St(f)∘κ_R. Consequently β_S∘K2(f)=π2(BGL(f)⁺)∘β_R.

Proof or construction:

1. Both candidate maps between the two central extensions lift E(f). Pull back the target extension along E(f); uniqueness of the lift from the source UCE identifies them.
2. Check that St(f) lifts E(f) on root generators. This proves naturality of the full group, not merely its central kernel.
3. Naturality of the fibre boundary then gives the displayed β square. Based coherent squares are part of the supplier interface; arbitrary independently chosen free homotopies are not used.

Acceptance:

- All maps preserve the displayed basepoint and the specified loop orientation.

### Image of the Steinberg reduction kernel

`steinberg-kernel-elementary-image` · lemma · `TauCeti.SteinbergReduction.elementaryImage`

For q:A→A/I, φ_A(ker St(q))=E(A,I) as subgroups of GL(A). This is the normal closure inside E(A), not inside GL(A), before stable normality is invoked.

Proof or construction:

1. Use request R-St-quotient: St(q) is onto and its kernel is the normal closure in St(A) of all x_ij(x), x∈I.
2. The Steinberg map onto E(A) sends precisely those generators to e_ij(x). Apply the pinned normal-closure image theorem to this surjection with codomain E(A), then embed into GL(A).
3. This identifies both images by their conjugate generators. The fact that GL(A)→GL(A/I) can fail to be onto is irrelevant.

Acceptance:

- I=0 gives the trivial image.
- For I=A and B the zero ring, the image is E(A).
- Conjugators are lifted through St(A)→E(A); no finite-rank normality assumption is inserted.

Dependencies: `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1:classical`, `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1:classical/to-elementary`, `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/stabilisation`, `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.5/relative-elementary-subgroup`, `mathlib:Subgroup.map_normalClosure`, `mathlib:Ideal.Quotient.mk_surjective`.

Sources: [Weibel, K-book](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~weibel/Kbook/Kbook.pdf), Definition III.5.1 and Exercise III.5.1, printed pp.217,228 / PDF225,236; III.2.2, printed p.193 / PDF201. ### The component of the unmodified reduction fibre

`classifying-reduction-fibre-component` · comparison · `TauCeti.ReductionFibre.baseComponentEquiv`

Let F0=fib(BGL(q)). The map BGL(A,I)→F0 given by the inclusion of N=ker GL(q) and its constant reduction null-homotopy is a based weak equivalence onto the basepoint path component of F0. It induces π1(F0)≃N, with projection to GL(A) the subgroup inclusion. This assertion needs no surjectivity of GL(q).

Proof or construction:

1. Classifying spaces are K(G,1). Their fibre sequence gives πn(F0)=0 for n≥2 and identifies π1(F0) with ker GL(q).
2. The constant null-homotopy of the composite BN→BG_A→BG_B gives the actual comparison. Its π1 map is the kernel inclusion by naturality of the group/classifying-space identification.

Acceptance:

### Congruence matrices as relative plus loops

`congruence-loop-map` · construction · `TauCeti.CongruenceLoop.toPlus`

Define c_I:N=GL(A,I)→π1(F⁺), F⁺=fib(BGL(q)⁺), by the map BN→F0→F⁺ induced by the based square of plus maps and its specified homotopy. A matrix loop with constant reduction is sent to its plus loop together with the resulting reduction path. The square homotopy H has direction j_B∘BGL(q)→BGL(q)⁺∘j_A. The induced F0→F⁺ map uses its reversal; it is part of the map even when one strictifies the square.

Proof or construction:

1. Use the constant null-homotopy to construct BN→F0.
2. Apply the based-square fibre map for the chosen plus square. In a homotopy-commuting model, concatenate its square homotopy with the image of the old fibre path, with the orientation required by paths f(x)→base.
3. Apply π1 and the classifying-space comparison to obtain the group homomorphism. Functoriality is through coherent based squares, so no free-homotopy basepoint-change action is used.

Uses:

- U.5/congruence-loop-surjective and congruence-loop-kernel: Determine the actual map from congruence matrices, then descend it to relative K1.
- ArithmeticKTheory:N.1 and KTheoryLowDegrees:U.6: Track matrix classes and the maps in the relative exact sequence.

API:

- `CongruenceLoop.toPlus` (constructor): The homomorphism c_I with its specified based-square construction.
- `CongruenceLoop.projection` (compatibility): Its composite with π1(F⁺)→π1(BGL(A)⁺)=K1(A) sends n to [n].
- `CongruenceLoop.map` (functoriality): Maps of ring-ideal pairs commute with c_I through the induced map of fibres.
- `CongruenceLoop.one` (simp): c_I(1)=1.
- `CongruenceLoop.mul` (structure): c_I(nm)=c_I(n)c_I(m).

Unit tests:

- `congruenceLoop_zeroIdeal` (degenerate): I=0 gives the trivial domain and trivial target fundamental group.
- `congruenceLoop_wholeIdeal` (compatibility): I=A identifies c_I with the ordinary matrix-to-plus π1 map GL(A)→K1(A).
- `congruenceLoop_integer_minusOne` (computation): For A=Z,I=A, c_I(diag(-1)) is the nonidentity order-two class in K1(Z).
- `congruenceLoop_nonSurjectiveReduction` (non-example): The construction still applies to (Z,5Z); it must not request a preimage of the unit 2 of F5 in GL(Z).

Acceptance:

- All maps preserve the displayed basepoint and the specified loop orientation.
