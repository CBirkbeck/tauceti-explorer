## Conventions and the retained targets

Rings are associative, unital and may be noncommutative; maps preserve the unit. Finite matrix indices are `Fin n`, written 0 through n−1. Matrix multiplication uses its usual row-column order. Write Eᵢⱼ(a) for the matrix with entry a in position (i,j) and zero elsewhere. The elementary unit eᵢⱼ(a) has underlying matrix 1+Eᵢⱼ(a) for i≠j. For the inner 2×2 matrix ring used as a parameter ring, E₁₂ and E₂₁ use traditional one-based notation: they are Matrix.single 0 1 1 and Matrix.single 1 0 1. These units and all associative-ring matrix identities belong to `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.1`.

Group commutators are [g,h]=ghg⁻¹h⁻¹, exactly Mathlib’s `commutatorElement`. Stable GL uses identity-block padding. A distinguished last column in rank n remains that same column under padding; it is not a newly appended last column. Reindexing transports the distinguished index explicitly. No injectivity is assumed for the Steinberg transitions.

Integral homology means `groupHomology (Rep.trivial ℤ G ℤ) n`. The pinned group-homology API has G in `Type`; larger universes require compatible universe lifts. Classical K₂ is initially a multiplicative kernel subgroup; its abelian group is converted with `Additive` when comparing it with homology, or homology is converted with `Multiplicative`. Degree-two homotopy uses `HomotopyGroup (Fin 2) X x₀`, with an actual chosen vertex and based induced maps.

### Finite presentation and its API, imported

For n≥3, Stₙ(R) is the group generated by xᵢⱼ(a), for i≠j, with the following relations:

- xᵢⱼ(a)xᵢⱼ(b)=xᵢⱼ(a+b).
- [xᵢⱼ(a),xₖₗ(b)]=1 if j≠k and i≠l.
- [xᵢⱼ(a),xⱼₗ(b)]=xᵢₗ(ab) if i,j,l are pairwise distinct.
- [xᵢⱼ(a),xₖᵢ(b)]=xₖⱼ(−ba) if k,i,j are pairwise distinct.

Opposite roots have no prescribed commutator relation. The predecessor owns this presentation via Mathlib’s `PresentedGroup`: `Steinberg.x`, `x_add`, `x_zero`, inverse with parameter −a, the index-qualified commutator relations, `Steinberg.lift`, `hom_ext` and the ring-map operation `map`. A homomorphism out is uniquely determined by a family satisfying these relations. Rank increase preserves indices and parameters. Ring maps act on parameters, commute with stabilization, and satisfy identity and composition laws. This is the API needed by every new construction, not a second presentation.

**Checks.** In rank three, x₀₁(0)=1 and x₀₁(a)⁻¹=x₀₁(−a). Over R=M₂(ℤ), taking a=E₁₂ and b=E₂₁ makes the forward product ab=E₁₁ and the reverse product ba=E₂₂ different. The elementary image distinguishes these choices. Mapping generators to eᵢⱼ(a) agrees, over commutative R, with Tau Ceti’s existing `transvectionUnit` under the pinned convention. The opposite-root pair must not acquire the disjoint relation. These checks retain the predecessor’s discriminating tests. Source: Weibel III.5.1, printed p. 217, PDF p. 225.

### Stable objects, K₂ and extension APIs, imported

`T.1/stabilisation` owns St(R), the directed colimit of Stₙ for n≥3. The canonical homomorphisms send a finite generator to its stable version, commute with transitions, and admit the compatible-family universal property. Common finite representatives exist; equality is witnessed at some higher rank. The stable map φ sends xᵢⱼ(a) to eᵢⱼ(a), and its image is E(R). The new surjection theorem below supplies the proof boundary using the actual pinned colimit API.

`T.1/k2-definition` owns K₂(R)=ker φ. Its API consists of the kernel inclusion, the criterion that φ(z)=1, the induced map for a ring homomorphism, identity/composition, and the commutative group structure obtained after centrality. `T.1:classical/to-elementary` owns the finite homomorphism and its generator equation. **Checks:** the zero ring gives trivial St, E and K₂; stabilized finite generators have the same elementary image; the kernel inclusion is injective; identity and composite ring maps induce the respective identity and composite kernel maps. A word whose elementary image is nonidentity cannot be accepted as a K₂ element. No new K₁ construction is needed here.

`T.1/central-extension` owns surjective homomorphisms with central kernel, and `T.1/universal-central-extension` owns their initial object over a fixed target. `T.1:classical/central-extension-hom` owns maps commuting with the projections; it does not demand a fixed external kernel group. Their API exposes surjectivity, centrality, maps over the target, pullback along a target homomorphism, unique universal lifts, and uniqueness of universal objects. **Checks:** the identity is central; A×G→G is central when A is abelian; a projection with a noncentral kernel is excluded. Universal lifts compose by uniqueness, the identity target map lifts to identity, and a nonperfect target admits no universal central extension. Classification by H², unlike the UCE morphism category, fixes both the abelian kernel identification and the quotient identification. Sources: Weibel III.5.2–5.4, printed pp. 217–220, PDF pp. 225–228. The general Hopf and recognition package remains owned by the accepted predecessor.

## T.1 refinements, in dependency order

### 1. The stable Steinberg map from the existing group colimit

Owner: `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/stable-elementary-surjection`. Kind: theorem.

The compatible maps St_n(R) → E_n(R) induce a surjective homomorphism φ_R : St(R) → E(R), with φ_R(x_ij(a)) = e_ij(a). Every finite family in St(R) has representatives in one common finite rank. Equality of representatives means equality in some larger rank; no injectivity of St_n(R) → St(R) is assumed.

**Hypotheses.** R is an associative unital ring; commutativity is not assumed.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Use the predecessor presentation and its homomorphism universal property for the transition maps, and import all three associative-ring elementary relations from U.1. Their compatibility on generators gives compatible finite maps to GL_n(R).
2. Index St by n = m+3, m in the natural numbers, so every group in the system has its specified presentation. The tail is cofinal in the GL tower. Use the existing DirectLimit Group instance, mul_def and inv_def, not a newly constructed colimit group.
3. Apply DirectLimit.map to the compatible finite matrix maps, or lift into stable GL and package multiplicativity with lift_mul and lift_one. Every image lies in E by finite-stage representation. Corestrict to E.
4. An element of E has a finite-stage elementary representative by U.1/stable-elementary-subgroup. Raise rank to at least three, express it as a finite word of elementary generators and inverses, and lift that word in St_n. This proves surjectivity.
5. For common representatives use exists_eq_mk₂ repeatedly, then raise the common rank to any prescribed lower bound. Use DirectLimit.setoid for equalities. The stronger fixed-rank criterion and mk_injective belong to the GL system only.

**Acceptance.** φ maps a stabilized generator to the identically indexed elementary matrix. The zero ring gives trivial St and E. The proof uses no claim that Steinberg transitions are monomorphisms.

**Direct inputs.** `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/steinberg-group-finite-rank`, `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/stabilisation`, `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1:classical/to-elementary`, `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.1/elementary-matrix`, `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.1/elementary-commute`, `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.1/elementary-commutator-chain`, `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.1/elementary-commutator-reverse`, `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.1/stable-general-linear-group`, `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.1/stable-elementary-subgroup`, `mathlib:DirectLimit`, `mathlib:DirectLimit.setoid`, `mathlib:DirectLimit.exists_eq_mk`, `mathlib:DirectLimit.exists_eq_mk₂`, `mathlib:DirectLimit.eq_of_le`, `mathlib:DirectLimit.map`, `mathlib:DirectLimit.lift`, `mathlib:DirectLimit.lift_def`, `mathlib:DirectLimit.mul_def`, `mathlib:DirectLimit.lift_mul`, `mathlib:DirectLimit.lift_one`, `mathlib:DirectLimit.inv_def`.

**Source.** III.5.1–5.2, printed pp. 217–218 (PDF 225–226). The source defines the stable Steinberg map. The application of the pinned directed-group-colimit API and the word-lifting surjectivity proof are the follow-up refinement.

### 2. A faithful column root subgroup

Owner: `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/column-root-embedding`. Kind: construction.

For n≥3 with indices 0,…,n−1 and last index c=n−1, define ρ^col_n : (R^(n−1),+) → St_n(R) by v ↦ ∏_{i<c} x_ic(v_i), in increasing index order. It is an injective homomorphism. Its composite with φ_n has matrix 1+∑_{i<c} E_ic(v_i). The same parameter map into stable St is injective, even when other finite-rank transition injectivity is unknown.

**Hypotheses.** R is an associative unital ring; commutativity is not assumed. n≥3; c is the last index.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Distinct column factors commute by the presentation, including all parameters in the same root. Hence coordinatewise addition becomes group multiplication; zero and negatives give identity and inverse.
2. Matrix multiplication contains no cross terms E_ic(a)E_jc(b), since j<c. Read entry (i,c) of φ_nρ(v) to recover v_i. This proves finite injectivity.
3. Equality of stable column words implies equality of their finite matrices in stable GL. U.1/stable-equality-criterion, using injective GL stabilization, gives equality in GL_n and hence equality of every v_i. It does not give general injectivity of St_n stabilization.
4. For i,j<c distinct, conjugation by x_ij(a) changes v_i to v_i+a v_j and leaves the other coordinates fixed, by the forward root relation and commuting column factors. The inverse change has parameter −a. Thus every lower-rank word normalizes the column subgroup.

**API.** Proposed names are interface suggestions; the mathematics above is normative.

- `Steinberg.columnRoot` (constructor): ρ^col_n is the homomorphism from the multiplicative type tag of the additive group R^(n−1).
- `Steinberg.columnRoot_zero` (simp): ρ^col_n(0)=1.
- `Steinberg.columnRoot_add` (relation): ρ^col_n(v+w)=ρ^col_n(v)ρ^col_n(w).
- `Steinberg.columnRoot_matrix` (compatibility): φ_nρ^col_n(v)=1+∑ E_ic(v_i), as a matrix unit.
- `Steinberg.columnRoot_injective` (characterisation): Both the finite column map and its stable composite are injective.
- `Steinberg.columnRoot_conjugate` (relation): x_ij(a)ρ^col_n(v)x_ij(−a)=ρ^col_n(v with coordinate i replaced by v_i+a v_j), for distinct i,j<c.
- `Steinberg.columnRoot_map` (functoriality): A unital ring map acts coordinatewise and commutes with the column map; zero padding under stabilization keeps the designated column index c, and reindexing transports it to its new position. It does not identify the old last column with a newly appended column.

**Uses.** `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/k2-is-centre`: A kernel commutator in the normalized column subgroup must be the identity.

**Unit tests.**

- `column_zero` (degenerate): In rank three, columnRoot(0,0)=1.
- `column_recovery` (computation): Over Z in rank three, columnRoot(2,3) maps to the matrix with diagonal entries 1 and last column (2,3,1); therefore it is nonidentity.
- `column_tau_compatibility` (compatibility): Over a commutative ring, the matrix unit equals the product of the existing TauCeti.transvectionUnit at (0,2) and (1,2).
- `column_noncommutative_order` (non-example): Over M₂(Z), conjugating columnRoot(0,E21) by x_01(E12) yields columnRoot(E11,E21); the incorrect right multiplication gives E22.
- `column_stable_faithful` (characterisation): A column word with one nonzero coordinate remains nonidentity in stable St.

**Acceptance.** The parameter a v_j stays in that order over a matrix ring. Injectivity is detected by the elementary image, not by injectivity of the whole Steinberg tower.

**Direct inputs.** `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/steinberg-group-finite-rank`, `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/stable-elementary-surjection`, `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.1/elementary-matrix`, `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.1/stable-equality-criterion`, `mathlib:DirectLimit.mk_injective`.

**Source.** Exercise III.5.2, printed p. 228 (PDF 236), and III.5.2.1, printed pp. 217–218 (PDF 225–226). The exercise asks for column injectivity and normalization. Entry recovery, the noncommutative conjugation formula and stable injectivity are expanded here.

### 3. A faithful row root subgroup

Owner: `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/row-root-embedding`. Kind: construction.

For n≥3, c=n−1, define ρ^row_n(v)=∏_{i<c} x_ci(v_i). This is an injective homomorphism R^(n−1)→St_n(R), with elementary image 1+∑ E_ci(v_i); its stable composite is injective. For distinct i,j<c, conjugation by x_ij(a) replaces v_j by v_j−v_i a and leaves every other coordinate fixed.

**Hypotheses.** R is an associative unital ring; commutativity is not assumed. n≥3; c is the last index.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Use disjoint-root commutation to make the row product additive. Recover parameters from entries (c,i) of the elementary image; stable faithfulness follows from the injective GL tower exactly as for columns.
2. In conjugation, only x_ci(v_i) contributes: [x_ij(a),x_ci(v_i)]=x_cj(−v_i a). All row factors commute, so combine that factor with the existing j-coordinate. Reverse multiplication and the minus sign are essential.
3. Conjugation by x_ij(−a) reverses this transformation. Induction on a lower-rank word therefore proves row normalization.

**API.** Proposed names are interface suggestions; the mathematics above is normative.

- `Steinberg.rowRoot` (constructor): ρ^row_n is the homomorphism on the multiplicative type tag of the additive parameter group.
- `Steinberg.rowRoot_add` (relation): ρ^row_n(v+w)=ρ^row_n(v)ρ^row_n(w).
- `Steinberg.rowRoot_matrix` (compatibility): φ_nρ^row_n(v)=1+∑ E_ci(v_i).
- `Steinberg.rowRoot_injective` (characterisation): The finite map and its stable composite are injective.
- `Steinberg.rowRoot_conjugate` (relation): Conjugation by x_ij(a), i,j<c distinct, replaces v_j by v_j−v_i a.
- `Steinberg.rowRoot_map` (functoriality): Ring maps act on each coordinate; stabilization keeps the designated row index and reindexing transports it; a newly appended last row is a different subgroup.

**Uses.** `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/k2-is-centre`: The second family of fresh roots lets every other root be written as a commutator of two elements commuting with the kernel word.

**Unit tests.**

- `row_zero` (degenerate): In rank three, rowRoot(0,0)=1.
- `row_recovery` (computation): Over Z, rowRoot(2,3) has final row (2,3,1), while its last column above the diagonal is zero.
- `row_tau_compatibility` (compatibility): Over a commutative ring, the image agrees with the product of TauCeti.transvectionUnit at (2,0) and (2,1).
- `row_noncommutative_order` (non-example): Over M₂(Z), conjugating rowRoot(E21,0) by x_01(E12) gives rowRoot(E21,−E22); −E11 is incorrect.

**Acceptance.** The changed row coordinate is v_j−v_i a, not v_j−a v_i.

**Direct inputs.** `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/steinberg-group-finite-rank`, `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/column-root-embedding`, `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.1/elementary-matrix`, `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.1/elementary-commutator-reverse`, `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.1/stable-equality-criterion`.

**Source.** III.5.2.1, printed pp. 217–218 (PDF 225–226); Exercise III.5.2, printed p. 228 (PDF 236). The centre proof uses the row argument alongside its column argument. The row formula is derived from the reverse Steinberg relation rather than from an unqualified transpose over a noncommutative ring.

### 4. Stable centrality from faithful fresh roots

Owner: `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/stable-centre-completion`. Kind: theorem.

For every associative unital ring R, ker(φ_R)=Z(St(R)). In particular the kernel defining classical K₂(R) is abelian. This is a stable theorem and asserts no arbitrary finite-rank centrality.

**Hypotheses.** R is an associative unital ring; commutativity is not assumed.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Take y in ker φ_R and any Steinberg generator to be tested. Represent y by a finite word and choose a fresh index c outside both that word and the tested generator; raise rank using finite representatives. The finite image of y is the identity because its stable GL image is the identity and GL stabilization is injective.
2. Lower-rank generators normalize the column and row subgroups by their explicit API formulas; so does the whole word y. For p in either subgroup, [y,p] belongs to that subgroup and has trivial elementary image. Column or row injectivity forces [y,p]=1.
3. For k,l<c distinct, x_kl(a)=[x_kc(a),x_cl(1)]. Both factors commute with y, so y commutes with every generator. Presentation generation and the stable colimit show y central.
4. Conversely a central element of St maps to a central element of E because φ is surjective. Import U.1/stable-elementary-centre to force its image to be the identity. Thus centre equals kernel.

**Acceptance.** The proof separates centre⊆kernel from kernel⊆centre. Over the zero ring both sides are trivial. No statement about ker(St_n→E_n) follows merely by restricting this stable equality.

**Direct inputs.** `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/k2-definition`, `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/steinberg-group-finite-rank`, `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/stable-elementary-surjection`, `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/column-root-embedding`, `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/row-root-embedding`, `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.1/stable-elementary-centre`, `KTheoryLowDegrees:U.1/stable-equality-criterion`.

**Source.** III.5.2.1, printed pp. 217–218 (PDF 225–226), with Exercise III.5.2, printed p. 228 (PDF 236). The source proves centre equals kernel using fresh columns and rows. The omitted faithful-root-subgroup and normalization inputs now have explicit constructions.

### 5. Normalized lifts of Steinberg generators

Owner: `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/normalised-root-lift`. Kind: construction.

Let n≥5 and q:Y→St_n(R) be a surjective homomorphism with central kernel. For i≠j and a∈R define y_ij(a)=[u,v], where q(u)=x_ik(1), q(v)=x_kj(a), and k differs from i,j. The resulting element is independent of u,v and of k, and q(y_ij(a))=x_ij(a). More generally, for i,k,j pairwise distinct, any lifts U,V of x_ik(a),x_kj(b) satisfy [U,V]=y_ij(ab).

**Hypotheses.** R is an associative unital ring; commutativity is not assumed. n≥5. q is surjective and ker q≤Z(Y); no centrality of St_n→E_n is assumed.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Changing either lift multiplies it by a central kernel element, so its commutator is unchanged. The forward root relation gives the asserted image.
2. First prove the exact commuting-lifts fact. Given lifts y,z of roots (i,j),(k,l) with j≠k and i≠l, choose t outside {i,j,k,l}. Pick U,V lifting x_it(1),x_tj(a). Their commutator has the same image as y. The elements [U,z],[V,z] lie in the central kernel since both image root pairs commute. Conjugation by z therefore changes U,V only by central factors, so fixes [U,V]; y differs from [U,V] by a central factor and commutes with z. This is where n≥5 is used.
3. For four distinct indices i,j,k,l choose lifts u,v,w of x_ij(a),x_jk(b),x_kl(c). Put A=[u,v], B=[v,w], C=[A,w], D=[u,B]. Exact commuting lifts give [u,w]=1, A commuting with u,v,B and B commuting with v,w. The images of C,D are x_il(abc), so C,D commute with u,v,w,A,B by the same fact. Reorder uvw as ABwvu and as DC⁻¹ABwvu; cancel to obtain C=D. Thus [[u,v],w]=[u,[v,w]] in this configuration. This proves precisely the special case needed, rather than assuming a general unexpanded Hall–Witt step.
4. With u,v,w lifting x_ik(1),x_kl(1),x_lj(a), the triple identity equates the candidates using k and l. This proves intermediate-index independence.
5. For arbitrary parameters a,b choose an additional l and lifts of x_il(1),x_lk(a),x_kj(b). The same triple identity identifies [lift x_ik(a),lift x_kj(b)] with [lift x_il(1),lift x_lj(ab)], which is y_ij(ab). Products remain in the order ab.

**API.** Proposed names are interface suggestions; the mathematics above is normative.

- `Steinberg.normalisedRootLift` (constructor): y_ij(a) is the commutator of any indicated two preimages under q.
- `Steinberg.normalisedRootLift_image` (compatibility): q(y_ij(a))=x_ij(a).
- `Steinberg.normalisedRootLift_choice` (characterisation): Changing either preimage or the intermediate index leaves y_ij(a) unchanged.
- `Steinberg.normalisedRootLift_zero` (simp): y_ij(0)=1, since any lift of x_kj(0)=1 lies in the central kernel.
- `Steinberg.normalisedRootLift_product` (relation): For arbitrary lifts of x_ik(a),x_kj(b), their commutator is y_ij(ab).
- `Steinberg.normalisedRootLift_extensionHom` (functoriality): A homomorphism Y→Y′ over St_n carries normalized lifts to normalized lifts; q′ must again be a surjective central extension.

**Uses.** `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/finite-rank-splitting`: These canonical preimages satisfy the presentation and define the unique section. `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/stable-splitting-completion`: Unique finite sections are compatible on the cofinal tail of ranks.

**Unit tests.**

- `normalised_identity_extension` (compatibility): For q=id on St_n, y_ij(a)=x_ij(a).
- `normalised_zero` (degenerate): For every central extension, y_ij(0)=1 even when the arbitrary set section sends 1 to a nonidentity kernel element.
- `normalised_central_twist` (characterisation): Multiplying either chosen lift by any kernel element leaves the commutator unchanged.
- `normalised_intermediate_index` (characterisation): In rank five the choices k=2 and k=3 for i=0,j=1 give the same y_01(a).

### 6. The lifted relations give the unique finite section

Owner: `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/finite-splitting-relations`. Kind: theorem.

## Worked acceptance calculations

For the two fresh-root maps in rank three, write c=2. The column image of (2,3) over ℤ is the unit matrix with rows (1,0,2), (0,1,3), (0,0,1). The row image has rows (1,0,0), (0,1,0), (2,3,1). The relevant entries recover the parameters, so a nonzero coordinate cannot disappear in either finite or stable St. Stabilization keeps c=2 while adding identity coordinates; changing which index is distinguished requires reindexing.

For the order tests over M₂(ℤ), set a=E₁₂ and b=E₂₁. Then ab=E₁₁ and ba=E₂₂. Conjugating the column vector (0,b) by x₀₁(a) changes it to (ab,b). Conjugating the row vector (b,0) by x₀₁(a) changes it to (b,−ba). Both computations use precisely the sided multiplications that the source relations prescribe. The normalized lift of the chain x₀₂(a),x₂₁(b) has image x₀₁(E₁₁), so it cannot be the lift with E₂₂ as parameter.

In the identity extension of St₅, every normalized lift is the original root generator. In the direct-product central extension A×St₅→St₅ for abelian A, arbitrary preimages have arbitrary A-coordinates, but their commutator has A-coordinate 1.
