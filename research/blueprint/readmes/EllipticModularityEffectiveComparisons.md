# Modularity of elliptic curves over Q, Part II: effective residual comparisons

## Purpose and boundary

EllipticCurveModularity supplies modularity over Q and comparisons of compatible systems. This Part II addresses a different question: how large must a residual prime be before a congruence forces a stronger conclusion? Its first quantitative argument uses one multiplicative prime removed from a conductor. Its second uses an explicit threshold to replace an elliptic curve by a full-two-torsion curve of exactly the residual conductor. Uniform irreducibility consequences and the rational-cyclic-isogeny case of Serre uniformity form separate endpoints.

The common input is an actual normalized weight-two newform with trivial character. Its coefficients, coefficient field, Galois conjugates and residual representation are not arbitrary compatible data. The coefficient field must be the one generated by the coefficients of that form, its ring of integers must use the same inclusion into that field, and the prime above the residual prime must be the one in the congruence. Every norm and every coefficient embedding in the estimates refers to these objects.

No modularity theorem, generic newform, generic isogeny, generic Weil estimate or generic norm construction is rebuilt here. The new mathematical work is the sharp arithmetic estimate, its effective comparison applications, and the source-specific classification arguments. A theorem about a fixed curve outside finitely many residual primes is not a uniform numerical cutoff. Likewise, Lemos's conclusion for curves with a nontrivial rational cyclic isogeny is not the unrestricted uniformity conjecture.

## Conventions

Write g(N) for the complex dimension of the **trivial-character newspace**, at positive integer level N. At the pinned library, cuspFormsNew(N,2) is a subspace of cusp forms on Gamma1(N). Thus g(N) is the finrank of its intersection with cuspFormCharSpace(2,1), transported to Gamma0(N) by the existing trivial-character equivalence. Using the dimension of the entire Gamma1 newspace changes the estimate and the thresholds. Finite dimensionality and the comparison with the classical dimension are theorem inputs, not inferred from the total finrank function.

Write I(N)=[SL2(Z):Gamma0(N)]. Its Euler product is N times the product of 1+1/p over the distinct prime divisors p of N. This positive index is called mu in the Kraus application; here I avoids confusion with the Möbius function. The prime-power factors in the dimension expression use the separate Möbius correction. Factorization, primeFactors, totient, subgroup index, real square root, field norm and finite dimension use the existing library meanings.

An elliptic curve is the native Weierstrass curve over Q with its ellipticity hypothesis, not a new bundled curve type. End over Qbar equals Z is the non-CM condition in the Lemos endpoint. The conductor M and a nonzero minimal discriminant Δ are those of the same curve. The notation ord_q(Δ) means the nonnegative valuation exponent, not the prime power q raised to that exponent.

Keep three level quantities distinct. M is the original conductor. M0 is obtained by deleting q once when q divides M exactly once and the residual prime ell divides ord_q(Δ). N is the prime-to-ell Artin conductor of the residual representation. The identity M0=N requires a coefficient-prime and Serre-weight argument; it is not part of the definition of either level. The removed prime p in the norm argument is different from ell. The p in the Lemos endpoint is instead the residual prime; each theorem names its role.

All inequalities with fractions or square roots are inequalities of rational or real numbers. In particular N−6, N−9 and N−18 in the arithmetic estimates are not truncated subtraction in the natural numbers. Integer exponents and real exponents are distinguished when passing from 2[K:Q] to (M0+1)/6. The base sqrt(p)+1 is greater than one where exponent monotonicity is used.

### Bounds for the five correction products
`EllipticModularityEffectiveComparisons:EC.0/product-estimates` · lemma.

For N>0 and the five products (S,I,A,B,M) defining martinValue, NS≤φ(N), |A|≤2^ω, |B|≤2^ω, 0≤I≤sqrt(N), and |M|≤1, where ω is the number of distinct prime divisors of N.

**Hypotheses.** N>0; φ is the native Euler totient and ω=N.primeFactors.card.

**Proof or construction.**

1. For each prime power check 0≤s≤1−1/p from the three s cases. Multiply these inequalities and use the totient product formula.

2. Each v2 and v3 entry has absolute value at most 2, and each m entry has absolute value at most 1; finite product inequalities give the stated bounds.

3. For v∞ an odd exponent gives zero. At e=2 use p−2≤p; at even e≥4 use (p−1)²≤p². Multiplication gives I²≤N, hence the square-root bound.

**Acceptance.** N=1 must retain I=1, not I=0.

### Martin’s sharp weight-two newspace bound
`EllipticModularityEffectiveComparisons:EC.0/martin-bound` · theorem.

For N>0 the actual trivial-nebentypus weight-two newspace has dimension g(N)≤(N+1)/12. Equality holds if and only if N=35 or N is prime and N≡11 mod12.

**Hypotheses.** Positive level; weight two; trivial nebentypus.

**Proof or construction.**

### Why the finite cases cover the infinite range

The proof is a partition, not an extrapolation from a numerical table. First remove level one, prime levels and composite levels with at most two distinct prime divisors. For a remaining level, a prime power of exponent at least six gives its own bound. Two distinct primes greater than five give another bound. If there are at least three distinct prime divisors but at most one exceeds five, at least one of two or three divides the level. Therefore a prime above 41 falls in the near-six branch. Otherwise all prime factors lie among two, three, five and at most one of the ten listed primes, with every exponent at most five.

This leaves a finite set of 10,125 distinct levels. There are 11,250 parameter tuples because the tuples with the fourth exponent zero repeat for all ten choices of the fourth prime. The finite certificate must check the parameter condition and the bound, or check the distinct set together with the factorization argument. Treating the tuple count as the number of different levels obscures this completeness step.

The small-composite check retains 772 levels; the small near-six check retains 505. Exact rational arithmetic suffices for these comparisons. The reference computation also checks the closed expression against the independent recursive old/new inversion at 2,000 levels. That comparison tests the arithmetic formulas; it does not establish their equality with a space of modular forms. The native dimension-comparison theorem remains a separate proof obligation.

## EC.1. Effective bounds from a removed conductor prime

For an irreducible elliptic residual representation at ell≥3 and a removed multiplicative prime p≠ell, prove ell≤(sqrt(p)+1)^((M0+1)/6). Keep the explicit deletion level, integral norm, nonvanishing, all embeddings, coefficient orbit size and local trace congruence as separate inputs. Do not identify the deletion level with the prime-to-ell residual conductor without a coefficient-prime theorem.

### Nonvanishing of the removed-prime comparison integer
`EllipticModularityEffectiveComparisons:EC.1/trace-gap` · lemma.

Let K be a number field, c∈K, p≥2, s∈{1,−1}, and σ:K→C an embedding with |σ(c)|≤2 sqrt(p). Then α=p+1−s c is nonzero.

**Hypotheses.** p is an integer at least 2; an actual embedding σ is supplied; s=1 or s=−1.

**Proof or construction.**

1. If α=0, applying σ gives p+1=|σ(c)|≤2 sqrt(p).

2. But p+1−2 sqrt(p)=(sqrt(p)−1)²>0, a contradiction.

**Acceptance.** At the forbidden p=1, c=2 and s=1 give α=0. Divisibility of a zero norm would provide no bound.

**Prerequisites.** Only the displayed elementary construction or algebraic identities; no external geometric theorem.

**Source.** bennett-siksek-2020, Lemma 2.2 proof, p.359; explicit nonvanishing step.

### Archimedean bound on the comparison norm
`EllipticModularityEffectiveComparisons:EC.1/trace-norm` · lemma.

For K/Q of degree d, c∈K, p≥2 and s∈{1,−1}, if |σ(c)|≤2 sqrt(p) for every complex embedding σ, then |Norm_K/Q(p+1−s c)|≤(sqrt(p)+1)^(2d).

**Hypotheses.** K is a number field; every complex embedding is included; p≥2.

**Proof or construction.**

1. Apply the native product-of-embeddings formula to α=p+1−s c. Each factor has absolute value at most p+1+2 sqrt(p).

2. The native card_algHom_le_finrank bounds the number of factors by d; equality of the number of embeddings with d is not needed. Since p+1+2 sqrt(p)≥1, enlarge the product bound to its d-th power. Use p+1+2 sqrt(p)=(sqrt(p)+1)².

**Acceptance.** A bound at only the preferred embedding is not sufficient for the product.

**Prerequisites.** `mathlib:Algebra.norm_eq_prod_embeddings`, `mathlib:card_algHom_le_finrank`

**Source.** bennett-siksek-2020, Lemma 2.2 proof, p.359.

### The removed-prime residual exponent bound
`EllipticModularityEffectiveComparisons:EC.1/removed-prime-bound` · theorem.

Let E/Q have conductor M and nonzero minimal discriminant Δ. Let ell≥3 be prime with E[ell] irreducible. Define M0 by deleting each prime q exactly once when ord_q(M)=1 and ell divides the integer valuation ord_q(Δ). If p≠ell is prime, ord_p(M)=1 and ell divides ord_p(Δ), then ell≤(sqrt(p)+1)^((M0+1)/6).

**Hypotheses.** All conductors and valuations refer to the actual curve; ord is the valuation exponent, not the prime power. Residual representations and coefficient fields use the supplier conventions.

**Proof or construction.**

1. Import the hypothesis-qualified level-lowering newform at M0 and its integral coefficient c_p, prime λ above ell and removed-prime congruence p+1≡s c_p modulo λ. Here p does not divide M0 by the explicit deletion rule.

2. Purity applied to all coefficient conjugates gives |σ(c_p)|≤2 sqrt(p). The trace-gap lemma makes α nonzero, so its integer norm is nonzero.

### The norm is a positive integer in absolute value

The congruence by itself gives a divisor of a norm, not a bound: the zero norm is divisible by every prime. The trace gap excludes zero before the divisibility estimate is used. The ring of integers is the actual integral closure of Z in the coefficient field, and the chosen coefficient is regarded as an element of that ring using coefficient integrality.

The prime ideal lambda lies above ell, meaning its contraction to Z is (ell). The existing ideal-norm membership theorem puts the integer absNorm(lambda) in lambda. Contracting gives ell dividing this integer. The existing ideal-norm divisibility theorem, applied to alpha in lambda, gives absNorm(lambda) dividing its integer norm. The integer-to-field norm comparison then identifies this with the field norm whose complex embeddings are bounded. No normality of the coefficient-field extension is needed and no choice of a single real embedding suffices for the product estimate.

The product-of-embeddings theorem and the native upper bound on the number of embeddings are enough: each factor is bounded by p+1+2sqrt(p), a number at least one. Thus an upper bound by the field degree gives the desired exponent without needing a second theorem identifying the exact embedding count. The two conjugates in a complex pair must not be collapsed to one factor.

## EC.3. Full-two-torsion realization at exact residual conductor

For E/Q with full rational two-torsion, ell≥5 prime, irreducible E[ell] of Serre weight two and prime-to-ell residual conductor N, prove that ell>H(N) produces F/Q of conductor exactly N with full rational two-torsion and the same residual representation. Follow Kraus, Théorème 4: finite coefficient recognition, rationality, elliptic realization and the two-torsion isogeny selection are distinct proof obligations. Prove the M0=N adapter in every application before substituting the deletion level.

### The required realization argument

Fix a prime ell at least five and an elliptic curve with all its two-torsion rational. Its residual representation is required to be irreducible, of Serre weight two and of exact prime-to-ell Artin conductor N. Then ell strictly larger than H(N) is to produce an elliptic curve F over Q with conductor exactly N, rational full two-torsion and an isomorphic residual representation. The output is not merely an isogenous curve of unspecified conductor or a rational newform with no two-torsion conclusion.

The source proof must separate finite coefficient recognition from realization. A Sturm or Hecke coefficient bound identifies an eigensystem under integral congruence hypotheses; it does not automatically prove that every coefficient is rational. The rationality conclusion must be established with the F(N) bound and the correct coefficient degree. The rational newform is then realized by an elliptic curve through the existing modularity/realization direction, preserving exact level and conductor. ## EC.5. Surjectivity with a rational cyclic isogeny

Do not export unrestricted Serre uniformity or replace finite certified calculations by a live database lookup.

### Restricted uniformity and certified exceptions

Let E/Q be non-CM in the geometric sense, and suppose it has a nontrivial cyclic Q-isogeny. For every prime p greater than 37, Lemos's endpoint is surjectivity onto GL2(F_p), not merely irreducibility. The determinant is the cyclotomic character and its surjectivity must be kept in the subgroup argument. Non-CM excludes the persistent Cartan-normalizer obstruction; the rational-isogeny hypothesis supplies the extra restriction used to treat the remaining non-split Cartan case.

The proof route has several genuinely different ingredients. The residual subgroup classification reduces non-surjectivity in the stated range to the normalizer of a non-split Cartan. The local Tate-curve argument constrains potentially multiplicative reduction and gives potential good reduction at the relevant primes. For the small rational-isogeny degrees, the formal-immersion result restricts the denominators of j, and the coefficient-prime argument removes the remaining denominator. Rational parameterizations of the genus-zero modular curves then reduce integral j to finite explicit lists. The remaining isogeny degrees require their own listed j-values.
