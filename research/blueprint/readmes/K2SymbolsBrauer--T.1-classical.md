# Classical K₂: stable Steinberg groups and universal central extensions

This is the reader for `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1:classical`. For every associative unital ring R it constructs the finite Steinberg presentations, their stable group St(R), and the surjection φ:St(R)→E(R). Classical K₂(R) is the kernel of φ. The central results identify this kernel with the centre of St(R), prove that φ is the universal central extension of E(R), and identify K₂(R) naturally with H₂(E(R);ℤ). The integral homology in these statements always has trivial coefficients.

This supplement builds on the [T.1 packet](../packets/K2SymbolsBrauer--T.1.json) and its accepted scoped review. It imports the 43 nodes belonging to the classical stage. It supplies two new targets, `central-trivial-action-criterion` and `column-embedding`, and adds thirteen keyed refinements to existing targets. These include the corrected marked-extension statement, immediate K₂ tests, exact K₁ suppliers, removal of an obsolete Hopf-gap sentence, and source locators. The [supplemental packet](../packets/K2SymbolsBrauer--T.1-classical.json) specifies every replaced field; other imported fields retain their existing owners. The parent file is unchanged. Its symbol, arithmetic and plus-construction gaps are outside this stage.

The plan for this stage is closed and accepted by the [independent review](../reviews/REV-K2SymbolsBrauer--T.1-classical.md). No implementation is claimed. The [suggested file](../suggested/K2SymbolsBrauer--T.1-classical.lean) states only the compact subset expressible on existing pinned carriers. Steinberg presentations and their colimit, column constructions, and the inherited Hopf and bar-kernel constructions are mathematical targets in this reader; they are not developed in that file.

## The finite presentation and its elementary image

The inherited definition is `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/steinberg-group-finite-rank`. For n≥3, define Stₙ(R) as the presented group on xᵢⱼ(r), with i≠j and r∈R, subject to:

- xᵢⱼ(r)xᵢⱼ(s)=xᵢⱼ(r+s).
- [xᵢⱼ(r),xₖₗ(s)]=1 when j≠k and i≠l.
- [xᵢⱼ(r),xⱼₗ(s)]=xᵢₗ(rs) when i,j,l are pairwise distinct.
- [xᵢⱼ(r),xₖᵢ(s)]=xₖⱼ(−sr) when i,j,k are pairwise distinct.

These relations impose no formula for the opposite-root pair (i,j),(j,i). That exclusion applies in every rank. The reverse relation uses sr, not rs. Additivity yields xᵢⱼ(0)=1 and xᵢⱼ(r)⁻¹=xᵢⱼ(−r). The presentation's universal property identifies homomorphisms Stₙ(R)→H with root families in H satisfying these relations.

This is Weibel Definition III.5.1 and relations (5.1.2)–(5.1.3), printed p.217, PDF p.225, in the 2013 combined author draft. The proof that the elementary matrices satisfy the relations is imported from the four U.1 nodes above; the pinned transvection declarations over commutative rings are compatibility checks rather than the general-ring proof. The resulting inherited construction `T.1:classical/to-elementary` is the unique homomorphism φₙ sending xᵢⱼ(r) to eᵢⱼ(r). Its image contains all elementary generators, and subgroup-generation induction proves that its image is exactly Eₙ(R). The elementary-image paragraph immediately follows the definition on that page; it has no separate theorem number.

The presentation API retains the inherited names. `Steinberg.x` supplies the roots; `Steinberg.x_add`, `Steinberg.commutator` and `Steinberg.x_zero` expose the relations. `Steinberg.lift` constructs homomorphisms from a root family, and `Steinberg.hom_ext` proves equality by generators. `Steinberg.map` sends xᵢⱼ(r) to xᵢⱼ(f(r)) for a unital ring homomorphism f; identity and composition follow by generator extensionality. These are used by stabilization, splitting, and the symbol words owned by T.2. The finite-map API is `toElementary`, `toElementary_x`, `toElementary_surjective` and `toElementary_map`: construction, generator evaluation, surjectivity, and the commuting ring-map square.

Checks for the presentation include x₁₂(0)=1 in rank three, x₁₂(2)x₁₂(−2)=1 over ℤ, and [x₁₂(r),x₂₃(s)]=x₁₃(rs). Over R=M₂(ℤ), take r=E₁₂ and s=E₂₁: the reverse-root relation [x₁₂(r),x₃₁(s)]=x₃₂(−E₂₂) distinguishes sr from rs=E₁₁. The nonchaining test [x₁₂(r),x₁₃(s)]=1 distinguishes the relation's index conditions from the excluded opposite-root case. The elementary-map checks are φ₃(x₁₂(2))=I+2E₁₂, preservation of x₁₂(r)x₂₃(s), and the exclusion of diag(2,1,1) over ℚ from the image: its determinant is 2 whereas elementary matrices have determinant 1.

Finite perfectness is the inherited `T.1:classical/steinberg-perfect`. Given a third index k, xᵢⱼ(r)=[xᵢₖ(r),xₖⱼ(1)]. Thus every generator lies in the commutator subgroup, so Stₙ(R) is perfect for every n≥3. This uses the presentation, not a homological theorem.

## Stabilization, finite representatives and classical K₂

For n≤m, include the indices and send each root to the root with the same parameter. Presentation extensionality proves the transition laws. To use an uncomplicated natural-number system, take ranks 3,4,…, indexed by m↦m+3. Define St(R) as its `DirectLimit`. This retains the inherited owner `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/stabilisation` and the planet “Stable Steinberg group” (Weibel, stabilization paragraphs following Definition III.5.1, printed p.217 / PDF p.225).

The pinned directed setoid equates (n,x) and (m,y) exactly when their images agree at a common later rank. Every element has a finite-stage representative by `DirectLimit.exists_eq_mk`. For finitely many elements, choose a common rank to perform group operations. The group instance and `DirectLimit.lift_mul` supply multiplicativity of a compatible lift; `DirectLimit.lift_def` supplies its evaluation. The transition maps of St need not be injective. A proof of stable equality may require a later rank than either displayed representative.

The finite φₙ commute with the imported GL block embeddings and elementary subgroup maps. Their colimit lift gives φ:St(R)→E(R). An element of E(R) is a finite elementary word by the supplier's `stable-elementary-subgroup` and `finite-representatives` interfaces. Put all its indices in one rank and replace each eᵢⱼ(r) by xᵢⱼ(r). This supplies a preimage, proving stable surjectivity. A homomorphism on St(R) is determined by its finite roots: first use presentation extensionality at each rank, then finite-stage representatives in the colimit.

The stabilization API is `Steinberg.stabilise`, the carrier `StableSteinberg`, `StableSteinberg.phi`, `StableSteinberg.phi_surjective`, and `StableSteinberg.map`. `StableSteinberg.lift` packages a compatible family of finite homomorphisms; `StableSteinberg.hom_ext` compares maps on all finite roots. This API serves the K₂ kernel, the stable splitting proof, and V.1's use of St. Checks are that x₁₂(2) at rank three keeps its parameter in the stable group, a forward commutator relation is preserved at every common later rank, and an elementary word at rank five is the image of its rank-five Steinberg word. The ranks n≥5 form a cofinal subsystem: move any finite representative to at least rank five. That fact will glue splitting sections without an injectivity assumption on St transitions.

The inherited definition `T.1/k2-definition` is K₂(R)=ker φ as a subgroup of St(R). It has the group structure inherited from that subgroup. Commutativity is a conclusion of the centre theorem, not additional defining data. A ring homomorphism gives a commuting square of stable Steinberg and elementary maps and therefore a map on kernels.

Its API is `K2`, `K2.subtype`, `K2.mem_iff`, `K2.map` and `K2.ext`: the carrier, inclusion, kernel characterization, induced ring map, and equality by included elements. It is used by the symbol stages and by the H₂ comparison. For the zero ring, every parameter is zero, every presentation generator is identity, and K₂ is trivial. For an identity ring map, `K2.map` is identity, and composition agrees with composition of the maps on St. A concrete root x₁₂(1) over ℤ is outside the kernel because its elementary matrix is nonidentity. These are immediate kernel-level tests. The parent also names K₂(ℤ)≅C₂ and vanishing for finite fields as tests supplied by its arithmetic and Matsumoto stages; this supplement does not re-plan those computations.

The inherited `T.1:classical/k2-k1-exact` extends the kernel sequence to

\[
1\longrightarrow K_2(R)\longrightarrow\mathrm{St}(R)
\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}(R)\longrightarrow K_1(R)\longrightarrow1.
\]

The middle map is φ followed by E(R)↪GL(R), its image is E(R), and the last map is the imported quotient map. Its direct suppliers are `T.1/k2-definition`, `T.1/stabilisation`, `U.1/stable-elementary-subgroup`, `U.1/stable-elementary-normal` and `U.2/K1`. Exactness uses precisely these images and kernels; it does not assert that St surjects onto all of GL. Both the kernel definition and this sequence are Definition III.5.2, printed p.217 / PDF p.225.

## Column and row embeddings and the stable centre theorem

The new construction `T.1:classical/column-embedding` fills the exercise-level input to the inherited centre theorem. Fix n≥3 and t. For a vector v with coordinates indexed by i≠t define

\[
\rho_t(v)=\prod_{i\ne t}x_{it}(v_i),\qquad
\eta_t(v)=\prod_{i\ne t}x_{ti}(v_i).
\]

All roots in each product commute by the nonchaining relation, including the same-root case, so the order does not matter. Their additive law makes these maps homomorphisms from the additive group Rⁿ⁻¹. Let Pₜ and Qₜ be their image subgroups. The elementary image of ρₜ(v) is I+∑ᵢEᵢₜ(vᵢ): every cross product is zero because t differs from each other index. Reading entry (i,t) recovers vᵢ. The row formula reads entry (t,i). Both maps are injective in finite rank.

They remain injective after passing to St(R). Indeed their composites with φ and then GL(R) have these same matrix entries. The finite GL-to-stable GL map is injective by U.1. This proves injectivity of these specific column and row groups, without asserting that all of Stₙ(R) embeds in St(R).

If a,b,t are pairwise distinct, conjugation by xₐᵦ(c) gives the following exact coordinate changes:

\[
 x_{ab}(c)\rho_t(v)x_{ab}(c)^{-1}
 =\rho_t(v\text{ with }v_a\mapsto v_a+c v_b),
\]

\[
 x_{ab}(c)\eta_t(v)x_{ab}(c)^{-1}
 =\eta_t(v\text{ with }v_b\mapsto v_b-v_a c).
\]

For the column, only xᵦₜ(vᵦ) participates in a forward chain. For the row, only xₜₐ(vₐ) participates in a reverse chain. The additional root commutes with all factors of the relevant product. A word using no t therefore normalizes Pₜ and Qₜ. The row argument is obtained from the relations directly; transposing a matrix over a noncommutative ring is not a ring automorphism that could replace it.

The API has nine items: `Steinberg.column`, `Steinberg.row`, `Steinberg.column_add`, `Steinberg.column_basis`, `Steinberg.phi_column`, `Steinberg.column_injective`, `Steinberg.column_conj`, `Steinberg.row_conj` and `Steinberg.column_map`. The additive, basis, image and injectivity APIs also include their row counterparts. A basis vector evaluates to one root; ring maps act coordinatewise; stabilization adds zero coordinates. Their use is the kernel commutator argument below. The source is Exercise III.5.2, printed p.228 / PDF p.236, together with the proof of III.5.2.1, printed pp.217–218 / PDF pp.225–226. The coordinate proof and reverse-product row formula are supplied here rather than left as an external exercise.

The six checks in the packet are:

1. Zero vectors map to identity for both maps, and the zero ring gives trivial images.
2. In outer rank three over ℤ, ρ₃(2,3) maps to I+2E₁₃+3E₂₃, so it is nonidentity.
3. In outer rank three over M₂(ℤ), c=E₁₂ and v₂=E₂₁ make column conjugation by x₁₂(c) add E₁₁ to v₁; reversing coefficient order would add E₂₂.
4. For the corresponding row with v₁=E₂₁, conjugation adds −E₂₂ to v₂; left multiplication would give −E₁₁.
5. Over ℤ, the stable image of ρ₃(1,0) still has nonzero (1,3) entry in GL(R), so the particular column map remains injective.
6. Conjugating e₁₃(1) by e₃₁(1) over ℤ changes diagonal entries and produces a nonzero (3,1) entry. It does not preserve the column subgroup. This checks the exclusion of the opposite-root indices from the normalizer formula.

Now prove the inherited `T.1/k2-is-centre`. Let y lie in ker φ and test commutation with a stable root xₖₗ(r). Represent y by a finite word, enlarge to include k,l, and choose a further fresh index t. The word normalizes Pₜ, so [y,xₖₜ(r)] belongs to Pₜ. Its stable elementary image is identity because φ(y)=1. The injective stable column map forces it to be identity. The row argument gives [y,xₜₗ(1)]=1. Since xₖₗ(r)=[xₖₜ(r),xₜₗ(1)], y commutes with this root. Every stable element is a root word; hence ker φ is central.

Conversely, if z is central in St(R), its image commutes with E(R) because φ is surjective. The imported `stable-elementary-centre` states the stronger result that a stable GL element commuting with all elementary elements is identity. Thus φ(z)=1. Therefore K₂(R)=Z(St(R)), and this subgroup is commutative. The fresh index must be outside both the kernel word and the tested root. No finite-rank centrality statement is deduced from this argument.

## Central extensions, markings and the trivial-action bridge

The inherited `T.1/central-extension` uses the actual `GroupExtension A E G`: an injective map i:A→E, a surjection p:E→G, and range(i)=ker(p). A central extension has range(i)≤Z(E); in that case A is commutative. A set section σ of p is a right inverse as a function. A splitting is a homomorphic right inverse. For fixed A and G, an equivalence must commute with both i and p. This retains the chosen identification of the kernel with A.

Its API is `IsCentralExtension`, `CentralExtension.split`, `CentralExtension.Equiv`, `CentralExtension.product` and `CentralExtension.section_equiv`. The product supplies the marked extension A↪A×G→G. A splitting s gives the marked equivalence (a,g)↦i(a)s(g); centrality supplies multiplicativity and the exactness conditions supply its inverse. These interfaces serve the classification and universal-property arguments. Three tests distinguish centrality, splitting and markings: C₂×C₂→C₂ is central and split; reduction C₄→C₂ is central but nonsplit, since every preimage of the nonidentity element has order four; and C₉→C₃ with kernel maps 1↦3 and 1↦6 gives inequivalent marked extensions. In the last test, a group automorphism of C₉ over C₃ has multiplier 1 modulo 3, whereas preserving the two different kernel markings would require multiplier 2 modulo 3.

A morphism of central extensions p:X→G and q:Y→G is a homomorphism h:X→Y with qh=p. This inherited `T.1:classical/central-extension-hom` does not impose an identity marking on two kernel groups: universal properties range over varying kernels. `HomOver.mk` packages the commuting square, `HomOver.ext` compares underlying maps, and `HomOver.id_comp` gives identity, composition and their laws. Identity of A×G is over the product projection; g↦(1,g) is over id:G→G; and the constant map G→A×G fails the condition for nontrivial G. These are its three discriminating tests. The source is Definition III.5.3.1, printed p.219 / PDF p.227; X, Y and G are the ambient groups.

For the new `central-trivial-action-criterion`, fix a commutative group A and a specified G-action on it satisfying g•a=a. The pinned `TauCeti.GroupExtension.InducesAction S` means that conjugation by e∈E on the kernel is the action of p(e). The extension is central if and only if it induces this specified trivial action. Forward, use `conjAct_eq_one_of_le_center`. Reverse, the action condition gives S.conjAct(e)(a)=a; `GroupExtension.inl_conjAct_comm` identifies its included value with e i(a)e⁻¹. Therefore e commutes with each i(a), and `Subgroup.mem_center_iff` gives centrality. Merely installing a trivial ambient action says nothing about the actual conjugation action of an arbitrary extension.

Löh Definition 1.5.6 and Remark 1.5.7, printed pp.32–33 / PDF pp.40–41, motivate this action. The existing `inducesAction_iff_smul_eq_self` already assumes centrality, so it cannot supply the missing reverse implication. The product and C₄ examples both satisfy the new criterion; the sign quotient S₃→C₂ with kernel C₃ fails it, because conjugation by a transposition acts by inversion on C₃. This also checks that trivial action does not mean split extension.

The inherited `T.1:classical/central-extension-classification` now specializes the actual fixed-action theorem. Given a central marked extension, choose a set section and replace it by `normalizeSection`, which sends identity to identity. With the action condition supplied by the criterion, `factorSet` gives a normalized factor set α:G×G→A. In multiplicative notation it satisfies

\[
 \sigma(g)\sigma(h)=i(\alpha(g,h))\sigma(gh).
\]

`factorSetToGroupExtensionEquiv` identifies its actual twisted-product extension with S by (a,g)↦i(a)σ(g), preserving both markings. The coefficient representation is precisely `Rep.ofMulDistribMulAction G A`, on the additive type tag of A; under the trivial-action hypothesis this is the trivial integral representation on that coefficient group.

The class [α] is independent of the normalized section by `cohomologyClass_factorSet_eq`. For two marked extensions inducing that action, `nonempty_equiv_iff_cohomologyClass_factorSet_eq` gives equivalence exactly when their classes agree. For every class in actual H², `FactorSet.exists_cohomologyClass_eq` gives a factor set; its `groupExtension` is central by `inl_range_le_center`. Canonical-section recovery and `FactorSet.cohomologyClassEquiv` provide the inverse. The existing quotient of factor sets is a set-sized model for extension classes, avoiding a quotient over all possible total-group carrier types. The pinned comparison here has G,A in `Type`; the prototype makes that universe limitation explicit.

Finally transport a splitting across `factorSetToGroupExtensionEquiv` and apply `FactorSet.nonempty_splitting_iff_cohomologyClass_eq_zero`. Thus the zero class corresponds precisely to the marked product extension. For G=A=C₂, C₂×C₂ gives zero and C₄ gives a nonzero class. Changing a normalized section can change α without changing [α]. The S₃ example belongs to a different action and is excluded from this trivial-action classification. The source is Löh Theorem 1.5.10 and its proof, printed pp.33–37 / PDF pp.41–45, with Remark 1.5.11 on printed p.37; Weibel III.5.3, printed pp.218–219 / PDF pp.226–227, describes the central specialization. H²(G;ℤ) is not the coefficient group for an arbitrary fixed kernel A.

## The inherited Hopf and Recognition package

The following package already has owner nodes in the parent packet. It is imported here, not recreated. Its integral quotient-bar construction was independently reviewed on 10 October; those pinned chain comparisons remain inherited evidence. This review also reads the K-book recognition statements and Löh's extension-classification, free-resolution and Hopf passages. The Löh locators are Theorem 1.5.1, printed pp.30–31 / PDF pp.38–39; Proposition 1.6.21 and proof, printed pp.54–56 / PDF pp.62–64, and Corollary 1.6.23, printed pp.56–57 / PDF pp.64–65; and the Hochschild–Serre statements 3.2.12–3.2.14, printed pp.123–125 / PDF pp.131–133, with Theorem 3.2.18 and proof, printed pp.129–132 / PDF pp.137–140. The direct bar-kernel proof below is the earlier worker derivation, not a construction attributed to those notes. These existing nodes supply the four-term input; assembly removes the obsolete `G-Hopf` sentence from the inherited Hopf proof.

For a group E and normal subgroup N, the inherited `relation-central-extension` is E/[E,N]→E/N. Its kernel is N/[E,N] and is central. Here [E,N] is the subgroup generated by mixed commutators; it is not replaced by [N,N]. `relatorProjection`, `relatorProjection_kernel` and `relatorProjection_map` expose the projection, identified kernel and functoriality under maps preserving N. The construction supplies the free-presentation proof and central lift calculations. Tests: N=1 gives id:E→E; E=ℤ and N=mℤ gives ℤ→ℤ/m with the nonzero kernel mℤ; and the free presentation F(a,b)→ℤ killing b has a nonzero relation kernel, detected by b-exponent sum.

For a free presentation π:F→G with N=ker π, the inherited `commutator-central-extension` restricts that projection to [F,F]/[F,N]→[G,G], with kernel (N∩[F,F])/[F,N]. `commutatorProjection`, `commutatorProjection_kernel` and `commutatorProjection_perfect` give the map, kernel, and its identification as an extension of G when G is perfect. Tests: N=1 gives the identity on [F,F]; F=ℤ and N=mℤ gives trivial commutator source and target, although the larger relation extension has nonzero kernel; and the presentation of ℤ² by F(a,b) has nonzero kernel [F,F]/[[F,F],F], detected by [a,b] in the integral Heisenberg quotient. These are Weibel Examples III.5.3.4–5, printed p.219 / PDF p.227.

The inherited `quotient-bar-kernel` uses a surjective group map q:E→Q. On the actual inhomogeneous complexes with trivial ℤ coefficients, let L(q) be the kernel of `groupHomology.chainsMap(q,id)`. The resulting sequence of complexes 0→L(q)→C(E)→C(Q)→0 is short exact: surjectivity lifts each tuple basis element. Its degree-zero map is id:ℤ→ℤ, hence L₀=0. In each degree the kernel is generated by differences of basis tuples with the same q-image, by collecting coefficients within each tuple fibre.

Its six API names are `GroupQuotient.barKernel`, `barKernelShortComplex`, `barKernelShortExact`, `barKernel_zero`, `barKernel_pairGenerators` and `barKernel_map`. They expose the actual kernel, short exact complex, degree-zero simplification, basis differences, and induced map for a commuting quotient square. Checks: id:E→E has zero kernel complex; any surjective q has L₀=0; [a]−[b] lies in the tuple kernel exactly when q(a)=q(b); and 1→C₂ misses the nonidentity degree-one basis element, so the short exactness assertion cannot drop surjectivity.

The inherited `quotient-bar-kernel-h1` identifies H₁(L(q)) with N/[E,N], N=ker q. It sends [a]−[b] to the class of ab⁻¹ and sends the inverse generator n to [n]−[1]. The degree-two boundary [b]−[ab]+[a] proves multiplicativity and invariance under conjugation, so the inverse kills [E,N]. Conversely the boundary of [n|b]−[1|b] recovers every pair difference. The inclusion into H₁(E;ℤ)=E_ab is the actual map induced by N↪E. This fixes the comparison map, not merely the abstract isomorphism type.

The inherited construction `hochschild-serre-integral-five-term` is

\[
 H_2(E;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_2(Q;\mathbb Z)
 \xrightarrow{\delta}N/[E,N]\longrightarrow E_{ab}
 \longrightarrow Q_{ab}\longrightarrow0.
\]

It uses the actual short-exact-complex connecting map followed by the specified H₁(L) comparison. The transgression of a cycle is the class of the positive boundary of a lifted chain. The generic homology exact sequence and L₀=0 give exactness at all terms after the first. It is natural under commuting maps of quotient groups by the pinned connecting-map naturality. No full spectral sequence is constructed here.

Its four APIs are `GroupQuotient.transgression`, `transgression_lift`, `fiveTerm_exact` and `fiveTerm_maps`: the specified map, its lift formula, exactness, and agreement with the actual homology/inclusion maps. The identity quotient gives an identity H₂ map and zero middle quotient. For C₄→C₂ the middle C₂ injects into C₄ by its element 2. For S₃→C₂ the mixed quotient N/[E,N] is zero even though N_ab=C₃. The sign check uses the Heisenberg group over ℤ/3, with multiplication (x,y,z)(x′,y′,z′)=(x+x′,y+y′,z+z′+xy′). Take a=(1,0,0), b=(0,1,0) and forget z. The quotient cycle [q(a)|q(b)]−[q(b)|q(a)] lifts to a boundary [ba]−[ab], whose comparison value is ba(ab)⁻¹=(0,0,2). The negative sign would give 1 and fails this test.

Specialize to a free F→G. Free-group H₂ vanishes, so `hopf-four-term-sequence` is 0→H₂(G;ℤ)→N/[F,N]→F_ab→G_ab→0. The kernel of its middle map is (N∩[F,F])/[F,N]. This proves the inherited `T.1/hopf-formula` with actual trivial integral coefficients. The naturality node compares induced maps of presentation quotients with the pinned homology map. Freeness is used for H₂(F)=0, not as a reason that arbitrary quotient transgression is injective.

A universal central extension p:X→G is central and surjective and has a unique homomorphism over G to every central extension q:Y→G, with no fixed kernel group. The inherited definition `T.1/universal-central-extension` provides `IsUniversalCentralExtension`, `uce_hom`, `uce_hom_unique`, `uce_unique` and `UCE.equiv_over`. Two universal objects have a unique isomorphism over G, obtained by composing their unique maps. The identity of the trivial group is universal. The identity of C₂ is not universal: the two maps g↦(0,g) and g↦(g,g) into C₂×C₂ are different maps over C₂. For a universal source X and abelian A the unique map into A×G is x↦(1,p(x)), since universal sources are perfect. These are the three universal-property checks.

The inherited `uce-perfect` proves perfectness of both X and G, and `perfect-extension-rigidity` proves that two maps from a perfect group into a central extension with the same projected map coincide. Their pointwise difference lies in the central kernel, defines a map into an abelian group, and therefore vanishes on the perfect source. Neither statement is reproved as a new target.

The inherited pullback construction for q:Y→G and f:H→G is P={(h,y):f(h)=q(y)}. Its projection P→H is surjective and central if q is. The API `CentralExtension.pullback`, `pullbackFst`, `pullbackSnd`, `pullbackLift`, `pullbackKerEquiv` and `pullbackId` exposes the carrier, projections, universal lift, kernel identification and identity-base comparison. It serves recognition and UCE functoriality. Checks: along id_G it is Y via y↦(q(y),y); pulling C₄→C₂ back to the trivial group gives C₂→1; pulling A×G→G back along f gives A×H→H; and pulling the sign quotient S₃→C₂ along the identity retains its noncentral kernel, demonstrating the centrality hypothesis.

Composing two central surjections Y→X→G preserves centrality when the intermediate X is perfect (`central-extension-comp`). For an element z in the composite kernel, the commutator y↦[y,z] takes values in the central first kernel. It kills [Y,Y] and that first kernel; perfectness of X implies these generate Y. Thus z is central. This is the input to `uce-extensions-split`: compose an extension of a universal source with its universal projection, use universality, and use uniqueness to obtain a section. The intermediate perfectness hypothesis is essential to the composition lemma.

Define a group to be superperfect when H₁(G;ℤ) and H₂(G;ℤ) both vanish. The inherited `superperfect` uses the actual trivial integral representation. Its APIs are `Group.IsSuperperfect`, `Group.isSuperperfect_iff`, `Group.IsSuperperfect.isPerfect`, `of_mulEquiv` and `of_subsingleton`: the predicate, its perfectness-plus-H₂ characterization, perfectness, isomorphism invariance and the trivial case. This API supplies recognition and the output to V.1. Three immediate tests are the trivial group, which is superperfect; C₂, whose H₁ is C₂ and is not superperfect; and the free cyclic group ℤ, whose H₂ is zero but H₁ is nonzero. The last test prevents a definition using H₂ alone. Agreement with `Group.IsPerfect` requires both perfectness and H₂=0.

The inherited Recognition Theorem `T.1/recognition-theorem` says that for a central surjection p:X→G with G perfect, the following conditions are equivalent: p is universal; X is perfect and every central extension of X splits; X is superperfect. Every perfect G has a universal central extension, and its kernel is canonically H₂(G;ℤ). These are Weibel III.5.3.2–3 and Recognition Theorem III.5.4, printed pp.219–220 / PDF pp.227–228.

The prerequisite route is explicit. The Hopf extension [F,F]/[F,N] is perfect when G is: F=[F,F]N, and commuting its factors modulo [F,N] shows that its commutator quotient is perfect. Lifting free generators to any central extension of G induces a map from the Hopf extension; perfect-source rigidity makes it unique (`perfect-uce-exists`). A universal source is perfect and all its central extensions split. If all central extensions by ℚ/ℤ split, fixed-action classification kills H²(G;ℚ/ℤ). Characters of H₂ extend to the chain group because ℚ/ℤ is injective, and character separation then kills H₂ (`split-extensions-kill-h2`). Conversely H₁=H₂=0 makes the Hopf extension of X an isomorphism, so every central extension of X splits. Finally a splitting of the pullback of any q:Y→G along p gives a map X→Y; perfect-source rigidity gives uniqueness (`split-central-extension-universal`). This last implication is the form needed for the stable Steinberg theorem.

The inherited `uce-kernel-h2` compares any universal extension with the canonical Hopf extension and restricts its unique isomorphism to kernels. The inherited construction `uce-lift` sends a homomorphism f:G→G′ between perfect bases to its unique lift between chosen universal extensions, using the pullback along f. Its six APIs are `IsUniversalCentralExtension.lift`, `proj_comp_lift`, `lift_unique`, `lift_id`, `lift_comp` and `kerMap`. They express the lift, its square, uniqueness, functor laws and restriction to kernels. Its checks are that the identity lifts to identity, the trivial map lifts to the trivial map by perfectness, and a ring map sends xᵢⱼ(r) to xᵢⱼ(f(r)). Without a universal source, the C₂ product example has two lifts. The inherited `hopf-formula-natural` and `uce-kernel-h2-natural` identify the kernel map with the actual H₂(f;ℤ), not merely some abstract isomorphism between the same groups.

## Exact lifted relations and splitting in rank at least five

The inherited `T.1/finite-rank-splitting` is Proposition III.5.5.1, printed p.220 / PDF p.228: for n≥5, every central extension of Stₙ(R) splits. The following calculation supplies the index independence, four-root identity and additive relation left abbreviated in that proof. It uses no homological comparison or finite centrality assertion.

Let q:Y→Stₙ(R) be central and surjective. Any two lifts of one element differ by an element of ker q, hence centrally. Their commutators with another fixed lift are therefore equal.

First, lifts of nonchaining roots commute exactly. Suppose u lifts xᵢⱼ(a), z lifts xₖₗ(b), with j≠k and i≠l. Choose t outside {i,j,k,l}, possible because n≥5, and choose v over xᵢₜ(1), w over xₜⱼ(a). The images of [v,z] and [w,z] are identity by the nonchaining relations, so both commutators are central. Conjugation by z changes v and w only by central factors, hence fixes [v,w]. Its image is xᵢⱼ(a), so u differs from [v,w] centrally and also commutes with z. This includes two lifts of the same root.

Next take four distinct indices i,k,l,j and lifts u,v,w of xᵢₖ(a),xₖₗ(b),xₗⱼ(c). Set C=[u,v], D=[v,w], e=[C,w] and f=[u,D]. The preceding step gives [u,w]=1 and [C,D]=1. Both e and f lie over xᵢⱼ(abc). Again by nonchaining commutation, each commutes with u,v,w,C,D. Reorder uvw first by moving u past v and then v past w; the result is CDwvu. Reorder first v past w and then u past D and v; the result is fe⁻¹CDwvu. Cancellation yields e=f, hence

\[
 [[u,v],w]=[u,[v,w]]
\]

in this particular four-index configuration. This establishes the required identity directly, with all commutation hypotheses checked, rather than invoking an unrestricted identity in a metabelian group.

Define cᵢⱼ(a)=[lift(xᵢₖ(1)),lift(xₖⱼ(a))], where k≠i,j. It is independent of the chosen lifts by centrality. To compare k with another auxiliary index l, apply the four-index identity to lifts of xᵢₗ(1),xₗₖ(1),xₖⱼ(a). The left side is the k-definition and the right side the l-definition: the intermediate commutators are lifts of xᵢₖ(1) and xₗⱼ(a), and lift independence removes their choices. If k=l there is nothing to compare.

For additivity choose u over xᵢₖ(1), v over xₖⱼ(a), and w over xₖⱼ(b). The product vw lifts xₖⱼ(a+b). Lifts v,w commute, and v commutes with [u,w] by the first step because the latter lies over root (i,j). The group identity [u,vw]=[u,v]v[u,w]v⁻¹ therefore gives cᵢⱼ(a+b)=cᵢⱼ(a)cᵢⱼ(b). Its zero case gives cᵢⱼ(0)=1, and its inverse case gives cᵢⱼ(a)⁻¹=cᵢⱼ(−a).

For the forward chaining relation with distinct i,j,l, choose k outside those indices. Use the four-index identity on lifts of xᵢₖ(1),xₖⱼ(a),xⱼₗ(b). The result is [cᵢⱼ(a),cⱼₗ(b)]=cᵢₗ(ab). For the reverse chain, swap the two roots in this result and use [u,v]⁻¹=[v,u] and the additive inverse formula; the parameter is −ba. All nonchaining relations follow from the first step. Thus the c-family satisfies every Steinberg presentation relation exactly in Y.

The presentation lift s:Stₙ(R)→Y sending each xᵢⱼ(a) to cᵢⱼ(a) has qs=id on every generator, and hence everywhere. This is the required homomorphic splitting. The proof's acceptance checks are exact nonchaining commutation, independence of both kinds of lift choice, additivity, both chaining relations with noncommutative order, and the projection equation for s.

The finite universality implication has an explicit hypothesis: if φₙ:Stₙ(R)→Eₙ(R) has central kernel, then finite perfectness and this splitting theorem make it universal. This proof does not establish that centrality hypothesis for every ring. The inherited `finite-rank-caveat` gives a sufficient condition: injectivity of the finite-kernel map into stable K₂ implies finite centrality, since a kernel commutator maps trivially into the central stable kernel and injectivity forces it to vanish. That sufficient condition is not asserted unconditionally.

## Stable universality and the outgoing comparisons

The inherited `T.1/steinberg-is-uce` is Theorem III.5.5, printed p.220 / PDF p.228. Stable St(R) is perfect by the same generator-commutator formula used in finite rank. Its map to E(R) is central and surjective by the arguments above.

Let q:Y→St(R) be any central extension. Pull it back along each stage map jₙ:Stₙ(R)→St(R), n≥5. A pullback along an arbitrary homomorphism is central and surjective; jₙ need not be injective. Finite splitting gives a section, equivalently a homomorphism sₙ:Stₙ(R)→Y over jₙ. The next section composed with stabilization and sₙ are two maps into the same pullback over Stₙ(R). Since Stₙ(R) is perfect, `perfect-extension-rigidity` makes them equal. The sections are consequently compatible, including every composite transition.

Lift them through the cofinal n≥5 group colimit to s:St(R)→Y. The colimit evaluation formula gives qs=id on all finite roots, and extensionality gives the full identity.
