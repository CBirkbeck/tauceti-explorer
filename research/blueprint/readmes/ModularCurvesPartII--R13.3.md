## Purpose

This document plans part R13.3 of `ModularCurvesPartII`, which runs from the boundary of the integral modular curves (R13.3–R13.6) to the algebraic Hecke correspondences and the Hecke action on Jacobians (R14.1–R14.2).

## Conventions

- N ≥ 5.
- The Weil pairing follows Katz–Mazur and Mumford, with ⟨1/N, z/N⟩_N = e^{2πi/N} on ℂ/[1, z].

- Under S₂ ≅ Cot₀(J₁(N)_ℂ), the (·)^*-action is the Petersson adjoint of the classical action, and the (·)_*-action is the classical one.

## R14.1 Algebraic Hecke correspondences

### Objects

#### Construction. The degeneracy maps π₁, π₂ : X₁(N, p) → X₁(N) and the Hecke correspondence T_p

*Module* `TauCeti/ModularCurves/HeckeCorrespondence.lean`. *Node* `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.1/degeneracy-maps-and-hecke-correspondence`.

Fix N ≥ 5 and a prime p. Over ℤ[1/Np], Y₁(N, p) is the base of the universal triple (E, P, C): E an elliptic curve, P a point of exact order N, C ⊂ E a finite locally free subgroup scheme of order p, not contained in the subgroup generated by P on geometric fibres when p | N. X₁(N, p) is its compactification, proper smooth over ℤ[1/Np] and finite flat over ℙ¹. The degeneracy maps are π₁^{(p)}(E, P, C) = (E, P) and π₂^{(p)}(E, P, C) = (E/C, P mod C) from Y₁(N, p) to Y₁(N)[1/p]. They extend uniquely to finite flat maps X₁(N, p) → X₁(N)[1/p], of degree p + 1 if p ∤ N and p if p | N. The Hecke correspondence T_p is the pair (π₁^{(p)}, π₂^{(p)}), i.e. X₁(N, p) → X₁(N) ×_{ℤ[1/Np]} X₁(N).

*Hypotheses.*

- N ≥ 5, as in the source, where X₁(N) is a proper smooth ℤ[1/N]-scheme with a universal object over Y₁(N) (S₂(Γ₁(N)) = 0 for N < 5). The curves X₁(N), X₁(N, p) with their geometrically connected fibres are ModularCurvesPartII R13.2; this node builds the maps.
- When p | N the disjointness of C from ⟨P⟩ is needed for π₂ to make sense: otherwise P mod C has order N/p.
- π₂ does not preserve the fibration over 𝔸¹ given by the j-line coordinates of the two moduli problems. Its extension to the compactifications uses minimal regular proper models of curves over a Dedekind base, not the moduli interpretation at the cusps.
- The quotient E/C and the isogeny E → E/C are imported from Tau Ceti ModularCurves §2B (isogenies and quotients) and AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli A3, as RS-06 directs.
- Degree counts: for p ∤ N the fibre of π₁ over (E, P) is the set of the p + 1 subgroups of order p of E[p] ≅ (ℤ/p)² over an algebraically closed field of characteristic ≠ p. For p | N, the one subgroup meeting ⟨P⟩ is excluded, leaving p.

*API.*

- `X1Np` (*data*) — X₁(N, p), from R13.2, with its universal triple (E, P, C) over Y₁(N, p).
- `degeneracy1` (*constructor*) — π₁^{(p)} : X₁(N, p) → X₁(N)[1/p], (E, P, C) ↦ (E, P).
- `degeneracy2` (*constructor*) — π₂^{(p)} : X₁(N, p) → X₁(N)[1/p], (E, P, C) ↦ (E/C, P mod C).
- `degeneracy_finite_flat` (*characterisation*) — π₁^{(p)}, π₂^{(p)} are finite flat.
- `degree_degeneracy` (*characterisation*) — deg π_i^{(p)} = p + 1 if p ∤ N and p if p | N.
- `heckeCorr` (*constructor*) — T_p = (π₁^{(p)}, π₂^{(p)}) : X₁(N, p) → X₁(N) ×_{ℤ[1/Np]} X₁(N).

*Used by.*

- `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.2/hecke-operators-on-the-jacobian` — T_p^* = Alb(π₁)∘Pic⁰(π₂) and (T_p)_* = Alb(π₂)∘Pic⁰(π₁)
- `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.3/weight-two-shimura-isomorphism` — T_p^* = (π₁)_*∘(π₂)^* on H¹
- `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.6/special-fibre-eichler-shimura` — the reduction of X₁(N, p) and of π₂ at p

*Unit tests.* A wrong definition fails one of these.

- `degree_degeneracy_coprime` (value) — For p ∤ N, deg π₁ = p + 1, the number of lines in 𝔽_p².
- `degree_degeneracy_dvd` (value) — For p | N, deg π₁ = p, the lines of E[p] other than the one meeting ⟨P⟩.
- `degeneracy2_disjoint` (non-example) — Without the disjointness condition, π₂ is undefined for p | N: for C ⊂ ⟨P⟩, P mod C has order N/p, not N.
- `degeneracy1_analytic` (compatibility) — π₁^{(p)} is induced by z ↦ z on 𝔥 for the standard uniformisations.

*Construction.*

1. Representability of Y₁(N, p) and its compactification: R13.2 (request). This source cites Deligne–Rapoport and Katz–Mazur for it.
2. π₁ is forgetful. π₂ uses the quotient E/C by a finite locally free subgroup, an elliptic curve with an isogeny E → E/C of degree p (AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli A3). P mod C has exact order N by the disjointness condition (automatic when p ∤ N).
3. Extension to X₁(N, p) → X₁(N)[1/p]: the maps are defined on the dense open Y₁(N, p), and the source extends them using the theory of minimal regular proper models of curves over a Dedekind base (R13.4). Finiteness and flatness hold because a non-constant map of proper smooth curves with geometrically connected fibres is finite flat.
4. Degrees: count geometric points in a general fibre, as in the hypotheses.

*Acceptance.*

- N = 11, p = 2: deg π₁ = 3 (three subgroups of order 2 in E[2]).
- N = 10, p = 5: deg π₁ = 5; of the six subgroups of order 5 in E[5], the one containing 2P is excluded.
- Analytically π₁ is induced by z ↦ z on 𝔥, for the uniformisations (ℂ/[1, z], 1/N, ⟨1/p⟩) of Y₁(N, p) (p ∤ N) and (ℂ/[1, z], 1/N) of Y₁(N).

*Uses.* `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.2`, `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.4`, `AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli:A3`.

*Planet:* Hecke correspondence.

#### Construction. Diamond operators I_n on X₁(N) and X₁(N, p)

*Module* `TauCeti/ModularCurves/Diamond.lean`. *Node* `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.1/diamond-operators`.

For n ∈ (ℤ/Nℤ)^×, I_n : Y₁(N) → Y₁(N) over ℤ[1/N] is (E, P) ↦ (E, nP), and on Y₁(N, p) it is (E, P, C) ↦ (E, nP, C). They extend to automorphisms of X₁(N) over ℤ[1/N] and of X₁(N, p) over ℤ[1/Np]. They satisfy I_n I_m = I_{nm} and I_{−1} = id, so they give an action of (ℤ/Nℤ)^×/{±1}. They commute with the degeneracy maps: π_i^{(p)} ∘ I_n = I_n ∘ π_i^{(p)}.

*Hypotheses.*

- n must be a unit mod N: otherwise nP does not have exact order N.
- I_{−1} = id because [−1] : E ≅ E carries (E, P) to (E, −P); so only ±n matter.
- Analytically I_n is induced by any γ_n ∈ SL₂(ℤ) with γ_n ≡ (n^{−1} *; 0 n) mod N.

*API.*

- `diamond` (*constructor*) — I_n : X₁(N) ≅ X₁(N) for n ∈ (ℤ/Nℤ)^×.
- `diamond_mul` (*simp*) — I_n ∘ I_m = I_{nm}.
- `diamond_neg_one` (*simp*) — I_{−1} = id.
- `diamond_comm_degeneracy` (*compatibility*) — π_i^{(p)} ∘ I_n = I_n ∘ π_i^{(p)}.
- `diamondAction` (*instance*) — An action of (ℤ/Nℤ)^×/{±1} on X₁(N).

*Used by.*

- `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.2/hecke-operators-on-the-jacobian` — ⟨n⟩^* = Pic⁰(I_n) and ⟨n⟩_* = Alb(I_n)
- `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.1/atkin-lehner-involution` — w_{ζ^j} ∘ w_ζ = I_j

*Unit tests.* A wrong definition fails one of these.

- `diamond_one` (degenerate) — I_1 = id.
- `diamond_group_order_11` (value) — N = 11: the image of (ℤ/11)^× in Aut X₁(11) has order φ(11)/2 = 5.
- `not_diamond_nonunit` (non-example) — For n not prime to N, (E, nP) is not a point of X₁(N): nP does not have exact order N.
- `diamond_neg_one_eq_id` (compatibility) — I_{−1} = id, via [−1] : (E, P) ≅ (E, −P).

*Construction.*

1. Moduli: (E, P) ↦ (E, nP) preserves the moduli problem, and I_n I_m = I_{nm} is immediate.
2. I_{−1}: the automorphism [−1] of E is an isomorphism (E, P) ≅ (E, −P).
3. Extension: as for the degeneracy maps (node degeneracy-maps-and-hecke-correspondence). An automorphism of Y₁(N) extends to X₁(N).
4. Commutation with π₂: (E/C, nP mod C) = (E/C, n(P mod C)).

*Acceptance.*

- N = 11: (ℤ/11)^×/{±1} has order 5, and the diamond operators generate a cyclic group of order 5 acting on X₁(11).
- Analytically γ_n acts on 𝔥 and induces I_n; on q-expansions it is the classical ⟨n⟩ (via R14.3's Shimura isomorphism).

*Uses.* `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.1/degeneracy-maps-and-hecke-correspondence`, `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.2`.

*Sources.*

- Lectures on Serre's conjectures (with an appendix by Brian Conrad, The Shimura construction in weight 2, and by Kevin Buzzard), Chapter 5, §5.2, p. 71: “and In(E, P) = (E, nP).” The diamond operator on the moduli problem.
- Lectures on Serre's conjectures (with an appendix by Brian Conrad, The Shimura construction in weight 2, and by Kevin Buzzard), Chapter 5, §5.1, p. 64: “can then described on the underlying set by (E, P) 7→(E, nP), so it is “intrinsic”, depending only on n ∈(Z/NZ)∗.” The analytic diamond operator agrees with the moduli one.

#### Construction. The Atkin–Lehner operator w_ζ on X₁(N) over ℤ[1/N, ζ_N]

*Module* `TauCeti/ModularCurves/AtkinLehner.lean`. *Node* `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.1/atkin-lehner-involution`.

For a primitive N-th root of unity ζ, w_ζ : Y₁(N) → Y₁(N) over ℤ[1/N, ζ_N] is (E, P) ↦ (E/⟨P⟩, P′). Here ⟨P⟩ is the étale subgroup of order N generated by P, and P′ ∈ (E[N]/⟨P⟩)(S) is determined by ⟨P, P′⟩_N = ζ for the Weil pairing (any lift of P′ to E[N]). It extends to X₁(N). It satisfies w_{ζ^j} ∘ w_ζ = I_j for j ∈ (ℤ/Nℤ)^×; in particular w_ζ² = id. Since w_ζ = w_{ζ^{−1}}, w_ζ descends to ℤ[1/N, ζ_N]^+. Over ℂ, with ζ = e^{2πi/N}, it is w_N : z ↦ −1/(Nz) for the sign convention ⟨1/N, z/N⟩_N = e^{2πi/N} on ℂ/[1, z].

*Hypotheses.*

- The Weil pairing sign convention is that of Katz–Mazur and Mumford, opposite to Silverman's. With the other convention w_{e^{2πi/N}} becomes w_N composed with a diamond operator.
- w_ζ is not defined over ℤ[1/N]: g ∈ G_ℚ carries w_ζ to w_{g(ζ)}.
- w_ζ = w_{ζ^{−1}} because (E, P) ≅ (E, −P) via [−1].

*API.*

- `atkinLehner` (*constructor*) — w_ζ : X₁(N)_{ℤ[1/N,ζ_N]} → X₁(N)_{ℤ[1/N,ζ_N]}.
- `atkinLehner_comp` (*relation*) — w_{ζ^j} ∘ w_ζ = I_j.
- `atkinLehner_sq` (*simp*) — w_ζ ∘ w_ζ = id.
- `atkinLehner_inv_root` (*simp*) — w_{ζ^{−1}} = w_ζ.
- `atkinLehner_galois` (*compatibility*) — g ∘ w_ζ ∘ g^{−1} = w_{g(ζ)} for g ∈ Gal(ℚ(ζ_N)/ℚ).

*Used by.*

- `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.2/weil-pairing-adjointness` — w_ζ conjugates the (·)^*-action to the (·)_*-action
- `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.3/hecke-twisted-self-adjoint` — the twisted pairing [x, y] = (x, w_ζ^* y)
- `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.6/special-fibre-eichler-shimura` — w_ζ^{−1} F w_ζ = ⟨p⟩_*^{−1} F on the special fibre

*Unit tests.* A wrong definition fails one of these.

- `atkinLehner_sq_eq_id` (degenerate) — w_ζ² = id.
- `atkinLehner_analytic` (value) — Over ℂ with ζ = e^{2πi/N}, w_ζ is z ↦ −1/(Nz).
- `not_atkinLehner_over_Z` (non-example) — w_ζ is not defined over ℤ[1/N] for N ≥ 3: Galois moves ζ, and w_{ζ^j} = w_ζ ∘ I_j ≠ w_ζ when j ≠ ±1.
- `atkinLehner_comp_diamond` (compatibility) — w_{ζ^j} ∘ w_ζ = I_j, so conjugation by w_ζ inverts the diamond action.

*Construction.*

1. Well-definedness: E[N]/⟨P⟩ is étale cyclic of order N, and the Weil pairing gives E[N]/⟨P⟩ ≅ μ_N ⊗ Hom(⟨P⟩, ℤ/N). So there is a unique P′ with ⟨P, P′⟩ = ζ, and its image in E/⟨P⟩ has exact order N.
2. w_{ζ^j} w_ζ = I_j: apply the construction twice. The dual isogeny identifies (E/⟨P⟩)/⟨P′⟩ with E, carrying the second P′ to a multiple of P fixed by the two pairings.
3. Descent to ℤ[1/N, ζ_N]^+: w_ζ = w_{ζ^{−1}} and étale descent along ℤ[1/N, ζ_N]^+ → ℤ[1/N, ζ_N].
4. Extension to X₁(N): as for the other operators.

*Acceptance.*

- Over ℂ with ζ = e^{2πi/N}: (ℂ/[1, z], 1/N) ↦ (ℂ/[1/N, z], z/N), since ⟨1/N, z/N⟩ = e^{2πi/N}. Multiplying by N gives (ℂ/[1, Nz], z), and multiplying by −1/(Nz) gives (ℂ/[1, −1/(Nz)], −1/N) ≅ (ℂ/[1, −1/(Nz)], 1/N). This is the point −1/(Nz), the source's identification w_{e^{2πi/N}} = w_N.
- w_ζ² = id is the case j = 1 of w_{ζ^j} w_ζ = I_j.

*Uses.* `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.1/diamond-operators`, `ModularCurvesPartII:R13.2`, `AbelianSchemesAndArithmeticModuli:A3`.

*Planet:* Atkin–Lehner involution.

### Theorems

#### Theorem. Composition relations among the Hecke correspondences, diamond and Atkin–Lehner operators

*Node* `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.1/composition-relations-of-correspondences`.

On X₁(N) over the relevant bases: (i) I_n ∘ I_m = I_{nm} and I_n commutes with every π_i^{(p)}; (ii) for primes p ≠ q, X₁(N, p) ×_{π₂,X₁(N),π₁} X₁(N, q) ≅ X₁(N, pq) ≅ X₁(N, q) ×_{π₂,X₁(N),π₁} X₁(N, p) over ℤ[1/Npq], compatibly with the outer degeneracy maps, so the correspondences T_p and T_q commute; (iii) w_{ζ^j} ∘ w_ζ = I_j.

*Hypotheses.*

- X₁(N, pq) parametrises (E, P, C) with C cyclic of order pq meeting ⟨P⟩ trivially; for p ≠ q, C = C_p ⊕ C_q uniquely.
- How w_ζ interacts with π₁, π₂ holds only up to a diamond operator (it realises w_N T_p w_N as the adjoint of T_p). The source states that relation on the Jacobian, where it is node weil-pairing-adjointness. It is not asserted here on the moduli side.
- The double-coset formula T_p ↔ Γ₁(N) diag(1, p) Γ₁(N) is the analytic side, compared in R14.3; it is not re-proved here.

*Proof outline.*

1. (i): moduli, node diamond-operators.
2. (ii): a point of the fibre product is (E, P, C_p) together with (E/C_p, P, C′_q), C′_q ⊂ E/C_p of order q. Its preimage C_q ⊂ E has order q (p ≠ q) and C_p ⊕ C_q is cyclic of order pq. Conversely (E, P, C) with C cyclic of order pq splits uniquely. Both composites send (E, P, C) to (E, P) and (E/C, P mod C).
3. (iii): moduli computation with the Weil pairing (node atkin-lehner-involution).

*Acceptance.*

- N = 11, p = 2, q = 3: X₁(11, 6) is a fibre product of degree 3·4 = 12 = (2 + 1)(3 + 1) over X₁(11) through either order, as the degrees of the degeneracy maps multiply.
- p = q is excluded: X₁(N, p) ×_{X₁(N)} X₁(N, p) contains the component where C′ is the image of E[p], which gives the relation T_p² = T_{p²} + p⟨p⟩ rather than a cyclic subgroup of order p².

*Uses.* `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.1/degeneracy-maps-and-hecke-correspondence`, `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.1/diamond-operators`, `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.1/atkin-lehner-involution`, `tauceti:HeckeRing.GL2.heckeT_mul_of_coprime`.

*Sources.*

- Lectures on Serre's conjectures (with an appendix by Brian Conrad, The Shimura construction in weight 2, and by Kevin Buzzard), Chapter 5, §5.1, p. 66: “In particular, this ring is commutative (which can be seen directly via cohomological considerations as well).” Commutativity of the Hecke operators; this node gives its moduli form.

#### Comparison. Over ℂ the algebraic correspondences are the classical Hecke, diamond and Atkin–Lehner operators

*Node* `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.1/analytic-comparison-of-correspondences`.

Under (X₁(N) ×_{ℤ[1/N]} ℂ)^an ≅ Γ₁(N)\𝔥 ∪ {cusps}, identifying (ℂ/[1, z], 1/N) with the class of z: π₁^{(p)} is induced by z ↦ z, I_n by any γ_n ∈ SL₂(ℤ) with γ_n ≡ (n^{−1} *; 0 n) mod N, and w_{e^{2πi/N}} by z ↦ −1/(Nz). On H⁰(X₁(N)^an, Ω¹) = S₂(Γ₁(N), ℂ), the pullback–trace (π₁^{(p)})_* ∘ (π₂^{(p)})^* is the classical T_p, the unique double-coset operator Γ₁(N) diag(1, p) Γ₁(N) of Tau Ceti ModularForms layer 2, and I_n^* is ⟨n⟩.

*Hypotheses.*

- The trace (π₁)_* is weighted by ramification indices, (a_y) ↦ Σ e_y a_y on stalks. A naive average over fibres would be wrong at the elliptic points and cusps.
- The Betti form of the same comparison, on H¹(X₁(N)^an, ℤ), is ModularCurvesPartII:R14.3/weight-two-shimura-isomorphism (Conrad's Theorem 5.1). This node is its holomorphic part, which RS-06 assigns to R14.1.
- The comparison of X₁(N)^an with the analytic quotient is R12.6; its proof needs care with the relative theory of elliptic curves over analytic bases.

*Proof outline.*

1. The uniformisations of Y₁(N)^an and Y₁(N, p)^an by (ℂ/[1, z], 1/N) and (ℂ/[1, z], 1/N, ⟨1/p⟩) (p ∤ N) or ⟨z/p⟩ (p | N) identify π₁ with z ↦ z. The moduli formulas identify I_n with γ_n and w_ζ with z ↦ −1/(Nz) (node atkin-lehner-involution).
2. Conrad's Theorem 5.1 identifies T_p ⊕ T̄_p with (π₁)_* (π₂)^* on H¹ ⊗ ℂ; restricting to H⁰(Ω¹) gives the classical T_p on S₂(Γ₁(N), ℂ).
3. Uniqueness of the double-coset normalisation: T_p on S₂ is the operator of Γ₁(N) diag(1, p) Γ₁(N) in the normalisation of Tau Ceti ModularForms layer 2 (request), and Tau Ceti's abstract T(m) satisfies T(m)T(n) = T(mn) for coprime m, n.

#### Construction. The modular Jacobian J₁(N) = Pic⁰_{X₁(N)/ℤ[1/N]} and Picard/Albanese functoriality for finite maps of curves

*Module* `TauCeti/ModularCurves/Jacobian.lean`. *Node* `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.2/jacobian-and-functoriality`.

For N ≥ 5, J₁(N) := Pic⁰_{X₁(N)/ℤ[1/N]} is an abelian scheme over ℤ[1/N] with a canonical principal polarisation, and it is the Néron model of its generic fibre. #### Theorem. Weil-pairing adjoints on J₁(N): (·)^* and (·)_* are dual, and w_ζ exchanges them

*Node* `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.2/weil-pairing-adjointness`.

Let V_ℓ(N) = ℚ_ℓ ⊗ T_ℓ(J₁(N)) with its alternating Weil pairing (·,·)_ℓ from the canonical principal polarisation. For T ∈ T₁(N), the adjoint of T^* is T_*. For p ∤ N the adjoint of T_p (in either action) is ⟨p⟩^{−1}T_p, and that of ⟨n⟩ is ⟨n⟩^{−1}. w_ζ conjugates the (·)^*-action to the (·)_*-action, so with [x, y]_ℓ = (x, w_ζ y)_ℓ every T is self-adjoint. For g ∈ G_ℚ acting on ℚ̄-points, (g^{−1})^* ∘ w_{g(ζ)} ∘ g^* = w_ζ.

*Hypotheses.*

- The Weil pairing and the cup product on H¹ differ by a sign (Conrad's Lemma 5.7), which does not affect adjoints.
- The analytic counterpart with the cup product on H¹(X₁(N)^an, ℤ) is ModularCurvesPartII:R14.3/hecke-twisted-self-adjoint. This node is the Tate-module form used over 𝔽_p and ℚ.

*Proof outline.*

1. Duality of Pic⁰(f) and Alb(f) (node jacobian-and-functoriality) gives (T_p^*)^† = (Alb(π₁)Pic⁰(π₂))^† = Alb(π₂)Pic⁰(π₁) = (T_p)_*, and similarly for ⟨n⟩.
2. Adjoint of T_p for p ∤ N: T_p^† = (T_p)_* = ⟨p⟩^{−1}T_p on V_ℓ, by Conrad's Theorem 5.8 (from the analytic Corollary 5.3 via the comparison of Weil pairing and cup product, Lemma 5.7).
3. w_ζ conjugates the (·)^*-action to the (·)_*-action. The source deduces this on V_ℓ(N) from the analytic Corollary 5.3 (w^*_ζ conjugates each T to its cup-product adjoint), Lemma 5.7 (cup product versus Weil pairing) and the faithfulness of the Tate-module functor. Since w_ζ is self-adjoint, [x, Ty] = [Tx, y].
4. Galois twist: g carries the moduli description of w_ζ to that of w_{g(ζ)}, since the Weil pairing is Galois-equivariant.

#### Theorem. The Tate module of J₁(N): a continuous G_ℚ-representation unramified outside Nℓ, commuting with T₁(N)

*Node* `ModularCurvesPartII:R14.2/tate-module-with-galois-and-hecke-actions`.

- Unramifiedness is Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich: J₁(N) has good reduction at p ∤ N.

3. Commutation: endomorphisms defined over ℚ commute with G_ℚ on ℚ̄-points.
