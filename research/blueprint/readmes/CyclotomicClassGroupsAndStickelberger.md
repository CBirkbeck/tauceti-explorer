# Cyclotomic class groups, Stickelberger and Herbrand–Ribet

This roadmap develops cyclotomic arithmetic for every prime and every prime-power level. Its finite core supplies the class groups, integral annihilators, unit comparisons and character conventions used by Eisenstein-ideal and Iwasawa arguments. Stickelberger annihilation is stated for every conductor, including even and composite conductors. Herbrand–Ribet uses Ribet’s modular construction, independently of a main conjecture. The Iwasawa layer specializes the general growth theorem already owned by IntegralIwasawaTheory.

The definitions use native ideal class groups and full integral unit groups. Every displayed target is a mathematical specification; none asserts an existing formal implementation. The compact suggested file contains only four light definitions, arithmetic milestones directly expressible on pinned carriers, and four proved coefficient examples. The full APIs and checks below are the definitive targets.

## Conventions and dependency boundaries

- Put K_(p,r)=Q(ζ_(p^r)), for p prime and r≥1; ζ is primitive. Write Cl(K)=ClassGroup(O_K), h(K)=NumberField.classNumber K, and A(K)=Cl(K){p}. Class modules in this document use additive notation; the native Lean class group is multiplicative. The primary subgroup and Cl(K)⊗Z_p are compared, rather than identified by a change of definition.
- Arithmetic Frobenius is used. The action is covariant: σ[a]=[σa]. For odd p the tame component A^(i) is the image of the projector with coefficient ω(a)^(−i); on the component σ_a acts by ω(a)^i. Exponents are reduced modulo p−1. The wild p-group action survives at higher levels.
- At p=2 no factor 1/2 is inverted in an integral class module. Real norm kernels, sign kernels and sign quotients are retained separately. The dyadic tower used for Iwasawa growth starts at Q(i), while the conductor-two field Q remains in the finite statements.
- Bernoulli numbers have B₁=−1/2. The nonprincipal generalized value B_(1,χ) is the existing finite-character value. Class-group character 1−k corresponds to B_k. A constant F_p-valued dual inverts characters; a μ_p-valued dual sends i to 1−i.
- Integral smoothings use b−σ_b. Their coefficients are floor(ba₀/m), with b any positive integer prime to m, without reducing b modulo m. The rational element θ_m by itself does not act integrally on an arbitrary class group.
- E_K means the full integral unit group, including torsion. Circular numbers are generated in K^× before intersecting with E_K. The real unit group is the native maximal real subfield’s unit group. Universal Vandiver is a conjecture, never an input theorem or an axiom.

The finite arithmetic follows ClassFieldTheory, GlobalNumberFields and NumberFieldArithmetic. Ribet’s argument also uses ModularForms and the exact representation, finite-flat and good-reduction contracts listed below. Existing analytic continuation, Hilbert 90, class-group functoriality and cyclotomic unit ratios are imported from the libraries. Generic character projectors remain with PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras; generalized Bernoulli values remain with DirichletPadicLFunctions.

The finite circular-unit definition and finite index theorems move from IntegralIwasawaTheory:L0 into C6, so that the consumer imports them. Generic inverse modules, compact Λ-structures, theorems for arbitrary Z_p-extensions and Euler systems retain their existing owners. To package C7 together with the finite core, apply the proposed two-roadmap bundle with IntegralIwasawaTheory, or place C7 and C8’s higher-level applications with that consumer. This ordering choice is explicit in the integration gap below.

## C0. Finite cyclotomic class groups and tame characters

For every prime p and r≥1 use K_(p,r)=Q(ζ_(p^r)), its native ideal class group and p-primary module A_(p,r). Compare Artin conjugation with the covariant ideal action. For odd p specialize integral tame Teichmüller projectors at every level, retain the wild action and prove real transfer. At p=2 preserve the integral sign defects. Identify the unique prime over p as principal and its unramified splitting quotient with A_(p,r).

### C0/native-class-action — Cyclotomic class groups on native carriers

**Comparison.** For every prime p and r≥1, identify A_(p,r)={x∈Cl(O_(K_(p,r))):p^n x=0 for some n} with Cl(O_(K_(p,r)))⊗Z_p. Transport the existing ring-automorphism action, without inversion, along Gal(K_(p,r)/Q)≃(Z/p^r Z)^×. The Artin isomorphism A_(p,r)≃Gal(H_(p,r)/K_(p,r)) is equivariant for conjugation on the right.

**Hypotheses and conventions.** K_(p,r)=Q(ζ_(p^r)); H_(p,r) is its maximal everywhere-unramified abelian p-extension; ordinary ideal classes and arithmetic Frobenius are used.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Use finiteness and the primary decomposition of the native class group to compare p-primary torsion with the tensor product.
2. Restrict ClassGroup.mulEquivHom to p-primary torsion; compute on classes using ClassGroup.mulEquiv_mk0.
3. Apply global Artin functoriality and normality of the Hilbert class field over Q.

**Inputs.** `mathlib:NumberField.classNumber`, `tauceti:ClassGroup.mulEquivHom`, `tauceti:ClassGroup.mulEquiv_mk0`, `mathlib:IsCyclotomicExtension.Rat.galEquivZMod`, `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ClassFieldTheory#layer-11-the-global-class-formation-and-global-artin-reciprocity`, `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ClassFieldTheory#layer-13-norm-theorems-and-class-fields`, `mathlib:NumberField.RingOfIntegers.mapRingEquiv`.

**Acceptance.**

- On an ideal class, σ sends [a] to [σa]; conjugation sends g to σgσ⁻¹.
- At p=2,r=1 the field is Q and A=0.
- The comparison commutes with change of primitive root.

**Sources.** [Sharifi](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/iwasawa.pdf), §1.3, Propositions 1.3.1–1.3.2, pp.14–15. Normality and Artin equivariance supply the Galois-module comparison. [Mazur1977](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1977__47__33_0.pdf), I §2, pp.52–53, before Proposition 2.8. Mazur explicitly compares the ideal and conjugation actions.

### C0/tame-eigenspace — Teichmüller components at every odd-prime level

**Definition.** For odd p and r≥1 let Δ_r be the unique subgroup of order p−1 in (Z/p^r Z)^× and Γ_r the kernel of reduction modulo p. Via the Teichmüller lift ω:Δ_r→Z_p^×, define A_(p,r)^(i)=e_i A_(p,r), e_i=(p−1)⁻¹Σ_(a∈Δ_r)ω(a)^(−i)σ_a, for i∈Z/(p−1). This is a Γ_r-stable Z_p-submodule; it is a specialization of the existing character projector, not a new group algebra.

**Hypotheses and conventions.** p is an odd prime; r≥1; the action is C0/native-class-action.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Identify the prime-to-p torsion subgroup by Teichmüller lifting.
2. Specialize the imported character idempotent to ω^i and take its image.
3. Use commutativity of the cyclotomic Galois group to preserve the wild action.

**Inputs.** `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C0/native-class-action`, `PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras:L4/character-decomposition`, `tauceti:TauCeti.teichmuller`.

**API.**

- `tameEigenspace_mem` (characterisation): x∈A^(i) iff σ_a x=ω(a)^i x for every a∈Δ_r.
- `tameEigenspace_period` (simp): The component depends only on i modulo p−1.
- `tameEigenspace_wild` (structure): The restricted Γ_r action and Z_p-module structure are inherited from A.
- `tameEigenspace_native` (compatibility): The inclusion into the native class group agrees with restriction of the imported projector.

**Discriminating checks and worked examples.**

- `inverse_character`: For the rank-one module Z/p² with Δ acting by ω^3, e_3 is identity and e_(−3) is zero when p=11.
- `wild_not_projected_away`: For r=2 the regular F_p[Γ_r]-module tensored with character ω^i survives in full in component i.

- `period`: For p=5 the labels i and i+4 define identical submodules.

**Uses.**

- `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C8/etale-duality`: Controls the inverse character on a constant-coefficient dual.

**Sources.** [Sharifi](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/iwasawa.pdf), §2.8, Definition 2.8.1 and Lemmas 2.8.3–2.8.7, pp.69–73; §4.4, p.111. Integral tame character projections give the cyclotomic eigenspaces.

### C0/decomposition — Integral tame decomposition and complex conjugation

**Theorem.** For odd p, A_(p,r) is the direct sum of A_(p,r)^(i) over i∈Z/(p−1), with mutually orthogonal projectors summing to one. Complex conjugation acts on component i by (−1)^i. Norm and extension maps between cyclotomic levels preserve i and satisfy Nj=[K_s:K_r] and jN=Σ Gal(K_s/K_r).

**Hypotheses and conventions.** p odd; s≥r≥1; norm and extension are the existing arithmetic maps.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Apply character orthogonality to the tame subgroup and the imported projector evaluation theorem.
2. Evaluate ω at −1 and use centrality.
3. Use functoriality of ideal norm/extension and their group-ring norm identities.

**Inputs.** `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C0/tame-eigenspace`, `PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras:L6/character-idempotent-evaluation`, `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ClassFieldTheory#layer-13-norm-theorems-and-class-fields`.

**Acceptance.**

- The decomposition is integral over Z_p, even when p divides the full Galois degree.
- The identity jN is a group-ring norm, not multiplication by the degree on arbitrary elements.

**Sources.** [Sharifi](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/iwasawa.pdf), §2.8, Lemmas 2.8.3–2.8.7, pp.69–73; §1.3, Definition 1.3.3 and adjacent identities, p.15. These give orthogonal components and norm/extension identities.

### C0/real-and-dyadic — Real class groups and the dyadic boundary

**Comparison.** For odd p and r≥1, extension identifies A(K_(p,r)^+) with the c=+1 part of A_(p,r), and the c=−1 part is ker N_(K/K+); the inverse on the plus part is N/2. For p=2 retain ker N, ker(1+c), and A/(1+c)A as distinct native groups, with comparisons whose kernels/cokernels are killed by 2; no integral sign projector is asserted.

**Hypotheses and conventions.** K^+ is the native maximal real subfield. At p=2,r=1,2 the real field is Q.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Use Nj=2 and jN=1+c.
2. Invert 2 only on an odd-primary module to get plus/minus decomposition.
3. For arbitrary 2-primary modules use (1−c)(1+c)=0 and retain the 2-torsion defects explicitly.

**Inputs.** `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C0/native-class-action`, `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C0/decomposition`, `mathlib:NumberField.maximalRealSubfield`, `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ClassFieldTheory#layer-13-norm-theorems-and-class-fields`.

**Acceptance.**

- Test Z/2 with c=1: it is also killed by 1+c, so a direct sum of signs is invalid.
- For odd p, N/2 composed with j and j composed with N/2 are identities on the asserted domains.

**Sources.** [Sharifi](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/iwasawa.pdf), §1.3, Lemma 1.3.5, p.15; §4.2, pp.105–108. Prime-to-p transfer identifies invariants and the real class group; the odd-prime hypothesis matters.

### C0/prime-above-p — The principal prime and the unramified quotient

**Theorem.** For every prime p and r≥1 there is a unique prime P above p in K_(p,r), generated by 1−ζ_(p^r), and its class is zero. Consequently the quotient of the p-Hilbert class group obtained by killing Frobenius at P is canonically A_(p,r) itself. This quotient classifies everywhere-unramified abelian p-extensions in which P splits completely.

**Hypotheses and conventions.** For p=2,r=1, ζ_2=−1 and P=(2).

**Construction or proof.**

1. Use the cyclotomic integral basis and total ramification formula.
2. Compute the norm of 1−ζ as Φ_(p^r)(1)=p and identify its prime ideal.
3. Use the Artin comparison and the splitting condition as quotient by the Frobenius class.

**Inputs.** `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C0/native-class-action`, `mathlib:IsCyclotomicExtension.Rat.isIntegralClosure_adjoin_singleton`, `mathlib:IsCyclotomicExtension.Rat.ramificationIdxIn_eq`, `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ClassFieldTheory#layer-13-norm-theorems-and-class-fields`.

**Acceptance.**

- Do not quotient out inertia in an already-unramified extension and call that a splitting condition.
- The assertion concerns prime-power conductors; it is not an assertion about every prime of every number field.

**Sources.** [Sharifi](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/iwasawa.pdf), §1.3, Definition 1.3.19 and Remark 1.3.21, pp.22–23; §3.1, cyclotomic example and Proposition 3.1.11, pp.75–77. The S-class quotient kills the prime class; total ramification is combined with the pinned cyclotomic ideal theorem to identify that class as principal. [Mazur1977](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1977__47__33_0.pdf), I §2, pp.50–53. The unramified quotient and its class-group model are the input to Mazur’s argument.


## C1. Hilbert 90–94, relative units and capitulation

Specialize the existing field Hilbert 90 without claiming a unit-valued lift. For arbitrary cyclic prime-degree number-field extensions with no infinite ramification, construct the saturated relative unit lattice and prove Hilbert 91’s independence modulo 1−σ and Hilbert 92’s cohomology count. Prove Hilbert 93’s different/invariant-ideal assertions, construct the capitulation cocycle and prove Hilbert 94’s exact kernel count in the everywhere-unramified case. Supply one-prime p-class control for all finite Galois p-extensions.

### C1/hilbert90-comparison — Hilbert 90 in the cyclic number-field convention

**Comparison.** For a finite cyclic extension L/K with generator σ and x∈L^×, N_(L/K)x=1 iff x=y/σ(y) for some y∈L^×. This is a specialization of the pinned Hilbert 90 theorem. It does not assert y∈O_L^× when x is a unit.

**Hypotheses and conventions.** L/K is finite cyclic; the generator is specified.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Apply the existing cyclic Hilbert 90 theorem.
2. Compute the telescoping norm for the reverse implication.
3. Keep the distinction between field elements and units in the codomain of the coboundary map.

**Inputs.** `mathlib:groupCohomology.exists_div_of_norm_eq_one`.

**Acceptance.**

- The numerator/denominator agrees with the pinned theorem.
- C1/hilbert92 will witness failure of the same statement inside the unit group.

**Sources.** [Hilbert1897](https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/content/titleinfo/2944458), §54, Satz 90, pp.271–272. The field-element norm-one criterion is Hilbert’s theorem 90.

### C1/relative-unit-lattice — Saturated relative unit lattice

**Definition.** For a cyclic prime-degree extension L/K, [L:K]=ℓ, let E_K and E_L be full integral unit groups and E_K^sat={u∈E_L:u^n∈E_K for some n≥1}. Define R_(L/K)=E_L/E_K^sat. The norm relation makes it a torsion-free Z[ζ_ℓ]-module with ζ_ℓ acting as σ. A relative fundamental system means lifts of an F_ℓ-basis of R/(1−ζ_ℓ)R.

**Hypotheses and conventions.** ℓ is prime; σ generates Gal(L/K); no real place of K becomes complex.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Define saturation inside the existing unit group and quotient by it.
2. Prove torsion-freeness from full saturation, and show that its prime-to-ℓ torsion enlargement does not change the quotient modulo 1−σ.
3. Descend σ and the cyclotomic polynomial relation; interpret coinvariants over the Dedekind domain Z[ζ_ℓ].

**Inputs.** `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C1/hilbert90-comparison`, `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ClassFieldTheory#layer-13-norm-theorems-and-class-fields`.

**API.**

- `relativeUnits_quotient` (projection): The quotient kills exactly units some positive power of which belongs to E_K.
- `relativeUnits_action` (structure): σ induces multiplication by ζ_ℓ and Φ_ℓ(σ)=0.
- `relativeUnits_torsionFree` (instance): R has no nonzero Z-torsion.
- `relativeUnits_independence` (characterisation): A lift system is fundamental iff its images form a basis of R/(1−ζ_ℓ)R.

**Discriminating checks and worked examples.**

- `saturated_root`: A unit u∉E_K with u^ℓ∈E_K maps to zero; quotienting only by E_K would retain it.
- `quadratic_real`: For L=Q(√2),K=Q,ℓ=2 the quotient is rank one and σ acts by −1.
- `torsion_prime_to_degree`: For L=Q(ζ_7), K=Q(√−7), ℓ=3 the unit ζ_7 maps to zero; saturation by third powers alone would incorrectly retain 7-torsion.
- `nonprincipal_lattice`: The API permits a projective rank-one Z[ζ_ℓ]-lattice without selecting a free generator.

**Uses.**

- `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C1/hilbert91`: States the full relative fundamental-system theorem.
- `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C1/hilbert92`: Detects unit coboundaries with nontrivial cohomology.

**Acceptance.**

- Use the saturation, not the usually torsion-containing quotient E_L/E_K.
- No freeness over Z[ζ_ℓ] is inferred from torsion-freeness alone.

**Sources.** [Hilbert1897](https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/content/titleinfo/2944458), §55, Satz 91 and its proof, pp.272–275. Hilbert’s relative fundamental units are independence modulo 1−σ after saturation by base-field units.

### C1/hilbert91 — Hilbert 91: relative fundamental systems

**Theorem.** Under C1/relative-unit-lattice, put d=r₁(K)+r₂(K). Then rank_(Z[ζ_ℓ]) R_(L/K)=d and dim_(F_ℓ) R/(1−ζ_ℓ)R=d. There exist d lifted relative units independent modulo 1−σ in Hilbert’s sense: if ∏H_i^(F_i(σ)) times a saturated base unit lies in (1−σ)E_L, each F_i(ζ_ℓ) is divisible by 1−ζ_ℓ.

**Hypotheses and conventions.** Cyclic degree ℓ, prime ℓ; no ramification at infinite places.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Compute the Z-rank by the signatures and Dirichlet’s unit theorem, obtaining d(ℓ−1).
2. Use the torsion-free cyclotomic-module structure and localization at 1−ζ_ℓ to compute the residue dimension.
3. Lift a residue basis and translate the relation back to the unit group.

**Inputs.** `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C1/relative-unit-lattice`, `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ClassFieldTheory#layer-13-norm-theorems-and-class-fields`.

**Acceptance.**

- Include the independence conclusion and its saturated unit condition.
- Do not assert a global Z[ζ_ℓ]-basis.

**Sources.** [Hilbert1897](https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/content/titleinfo/2944458), §55, Satz 91, pp.272–275. The relative system has independence modulo 1−σ, stronger than merely the relative Z-rank.

### C1/hilbert92 — Hilbert 92 and the unit cohomology count

## C2. The integral Stickelberger ideal and annihilation

### C2/stickelberger-element — The rational Stickelberger element

**Definition.** For m≥2, K=Q(ζ_m), G=Gal(K/Q), and σ_a(ζ_m)=ζ_m^a, define θ_m=Σ_(a∈(Z/m)^×)(a₀/m)σ_a⁻¹∈Q[G], where 1≤a₀<m is the positive representative. Use the native monoid algebra and transport along the pinned Galois equivalence.

**Hypotheses and conventions.** m≥2; the chosen root is primitive; G acts covariantly on ideal classes.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Form the finite rational linear combination in the existing group algebra.
2. Compare the sum indexed by units modulo m with the sum over integers prime to m.
3. Check independence under a simultaneous change of the cyclotomic field model and primitive root.

**Inputs.** `mathlib:IsCyclotomicExtension.Rat.galEquivZMod`, `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C0/native-class-action`.

**API.**

- `stickelberger_coeff` (projection): The coefficient of σ_b is the representative of b⁻¹ divided by m.
- `stickelberger_conjugation` (relation): (1+c)θ_m=Σ_(g∈G)g for m>2; m=2 is the direct half-identity case.
- `stickelberger_model` (functoriality): Cyclotomic field isomorphisms carry θ_m to the corresponding element.
- `stickelberger_eval` (compatibility): For nontrivial χ, χ(θ_m)=m⁻¹Σ a₀χ(a)⁻¹.

**Discriminating checks and worked examples.**

### C2/integral-ideals — Denominator and Stickelberger ideals

**Definition.** Inside Z[G]⊂Q[G], define J_m={x:xθ_m∈Z[G]} and I_m={xθ_m:x∈J_m}=Z[G]θ_m∩Z[G]. The last intersection uses the image of the integral group ring under multiplication by θ_m, not the Q[G]-ideal generated by θ_m.

**Hypotheses and conventions.** m≥2; θ_m is C2/stickelberger-element.

**Construction or proof.**

3. Take its image under multiplication by θ_m and compare with the intersection.

**Inputs.** `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C2/stickelberger-element`.

**API.**

**Discriminating checks and worked examples.**

### C2/smoothing — Integral smoothing and the floor coefficients

**Definition.** For b≥1 coprime to m≥2, define s_(m,b)=Σ_(a∈(Z/m)^×) floor(ba₀/m) σ_a⁻¹ in Z[G]. Under coefficient extension to Q[G] it equals (b−σ_b)θ_m. The s_(m,b), with b ranging over all positive coprime integers, generate I_m.

**Construction or proof.**

### C2/gauss-factorization — Gauss-sum principal ideal factorization

**Theorem.** Let ℓ≡1 mod m be prime, l a prime of Q(ζ_m) over ℓ, and choose a primitive root c modulo ℓ and χ(c)=ζ_m with ζ_m mod l=c^(−(ℓ−1)/m). **Hypotheses and conventions.** m≥2; ℓ∤m; the auxiliary character/root compatibility is imposed as written; L is the unique prime above l.

**Construction or proof.**

**Sources.** [Sharifi](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/iwasawa.pdf), §4.5, Lemma 4.5.4, p.115, and proof of Theorem 4.5.6, pp.115–117. Descent of smoothed Gauss sums and their valuations prove the principal ideal identity; use the corrected Gauss product recorded in E2.

### C2/stickelberger-annihilation — Stickelberger annihilation of the full class group

### C3/reflection — Cyclotomic reflection and the two exceptional components

**Theorem.** For every odd prime p and even residue k modulo p−1, r_p(A_(p,1)^(k))≤r_p(A_(p,1)^(1−k))≤r_p(A_(p,1)^(k))+1. Also A_(p,1)^(0)=A_(p,1)^(1)=0; at k=0 the sharper equality of ranks is zero on both sides. Here r_p(M)=dim_(F_p)(M/pM).

**Hypotheses and conventions.** Use full integral units, their p-th-power quotient, and the natural Δ action.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Apply Kummer duality to the radical of everywhere-unramified p-extensions and compare its ideal valuation sequence with units modulo p-th powers.
2. The duality with μ_p sends character i to 1−i; the even nontrivial unit character has rank one by Dirichlet’s theorem.
3. The trivial class component is the p-part of Cl(Q), hence zero; the exceptional radical Q(μ_(p²))/Q(μ_p) is ramified, eliminating its defect.
4. Combine the rank inequalities and use Nakayama for finite p-groups.

**Inputs.** `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C0/decomposition`, `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C0/real-and-dyadic`, `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C3/projected-annihilator`, `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ClassFieldTheory#layer-5-local-coefficients-the-brauer-group-the-local-invariant-and-duality`, `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ClassFieldTheory#layer-13-norm-theorems-and-class-fields`.

**Acceptance.**

**Sources.** [Sharifi](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/iwasawa.pdf), §4.4, Theorem 4.4.2, Lemma 4.4.3 and Corollaries 4.4.4–4.4.5, pp.111–113. Reflection compares complementary characters through the Kummer pairing and the unit contribution.

### C5/kummer-lemma — Kummer’s lemma for regular primes

**Theorem.** For every odd regular prime p and unit u∈O_(Q(ζ_p))^×, if u is congruent to an integer modulo pO_K, then u=v^p for some v∈O_K^×.

## C8. Mazur’s inverse-character obstruction

Prove Mazur’s inverse low odd-character vanishing at conductor p and propagate it to every prime-power level by exact control and compact Nakayama, with the p=691 exception for exponent −11. Show every everywhere-unramified abelian p-quotient of character ω⁻¹ is trivial and that killing the principal conductor prime does not change the Hilbert p-class quotient. Compare constant-coefficient étale H¹ with the inverse-character dual and μ_p coefficients with the twisted dual. Export the precise conditional division-field obstruction to the Eisenstein-ideal roadmap.

### C8/mazur-odd-vanishing — Mazur’s inverse-character vanishing

**Theorem.** For every odd prime p and every r≥1, A_(p,r)^(−1)=0. More generally, for j∈{1,3,5,7,9}, A_(p,r)^(−j)=0 for every odd prime p and every r≥1, and A_(p,r)^(−11)=0 for every r≥1 when p≠691. For p=2 the whole A_(2,r) is zero for every r≥1. Characters are reduced modulo p−1, including the exceptional ω case.

**Hypotheses and conventions.** χ_P in Mazur’s notation is the Teichmüller character ω, not an arbitrary odd character.

**Construction or proof.**

1. Use the projected annihilator and reduce −j modulo p−1.
2. For a nonexceptional character, Kummer’s congruence reduces its Bernoulli scalar to B_(j+1)/(j+1), with the p-denominator boundary treated by the exceptional smoothing.
3. Use B₂=1/6, B₄=−1/30, B₆=1/42, B₈=−1/30, B₁₀=5/66. Reduce the exponent before using a Bernoulli congruence at small p; for p=5 and j=9 the component has index 3 and is controlled by B₂, not by the numerator 5 in B₁₀.
4. For j=11 use B₁₂=−691/2730 and retain the prime 691.
5. For each odd-prime character i, exact control gives X^(i)/(γ−1)X^(i)≃A_(p,1)^(i). The imported finite generation and compact Nakayama imply X^(i)=0 when this base component vanishes; control then gives A_(p,r)^(i)=0 at every r. Use the dyadic theorem for p=2.

**Inputs.** `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C3/projected-annihilator`, `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C3/bernoulli-comparison`, `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C3/reflection`, `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C5/dyadic-tower`, `CyclotomicClassGroupsAndStickelberger:C7/cyclotomic-control`, `IntegralIwasawaTheory:L2/class-module-finiteness-torsion`.

**Acceptance.**

- At p=691 the exponent −11 is the irregular B₁₂ component, so a universal vanishing assertion would contradict Herbrand–Ribet.
- Higher-level vanishing requires the exact-control and compact-Nakayama step; a conductor-p citation alone does not prove it.

**Sources.** [Mazur1977](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1977__47__33_0.pdf), I Proposition 2.8 and Corollaries 2.9–2.10, pp.53–54. The inverse low odd characters vanish, with the Bernoulli-numerator exception at 691. [Sharifi](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/iwasawa.pdf), §3.2, Proposition 3.2.11 and Remark 3.2.12, pp.80–81.
