# Pro-étale cohomology of p-adic curves and of the Drinfeld tower

This roadmap starts with analytic functions on Stein spaces and ends with a cohomological period diagram for a Drinfeld tower type. The rational theory explains why geometric pro-étale cohomology contains much more than its étale bounded vectors. The finite-coefficient theory gives arithmetic profiniteness and finite presentation, and separates the exceptional behavior of the base field Q_p from larger local fields. The first two layers work in arbitrary finite dimension and are independent of tower geometry.

This is a completed **planning pass**, with 75 targets in thirteen layers, 16 definition/construction APIs containing 67 API items and 48 tests, and 50 planets. Every mathematical target is unchecked. All thirteen layers are **planned**, none closed: 20 supplier contracts and twelve explicit gaps must be resolved before claiming dependency closure. A target below includes its hypotheses, proof route, dependencies, source locator and Check; each definition or construction also includes its uses, API and three distinguishing examples. The packet is the structured form of these same targets.

## Scope, conventions and ownership

Let F/Q_p be finite, C the completion of an algebraic closure of F, and L a sufficiently large finite p-adic coefficient field with integers O_L. A geometric analytic space is base changed to C when the notation says so; arithmetic cohomology over a finite K retains its Galois action. Put G=GL₂(F), D× the multiplicative group of the quaternion division algebra over F, and G_F=Gal(Fbar/F). The diagonal central subgroup generated by a uniformizer is divided out only where indicated. A superscript ϖ denotes that quotient; for F=Q_p it is written p. The effective Q_p model Σ_n in the Dospinescu–Le Bras argument and the geometric tower M_n are related by the descent data, rather than identified by notation. Their level conventions differ: the former uses an even quaternionic depth. The exact translation is part of T0.

A prime on a topological vector space denotes its continuous dual, with strong or weak topology stated explicitly; it never silently means its full algebraic dual. Completed tensors, direct limits over tower levels, inverse limits over coefficients, and direct limits over finite fields have distinct roles. In A1 the level n remains fixed, and the field colimit is taken **inside** the coefficient inverse limit. Almost smoothness allows a different open Galois stabilizer modulo every p^k.

Quasi-Stein means a countable dense affinoid exhaustion. Relative compactness is a stronger hypothesis used for strict differential and support arguments. Finite-dimensional means a uniform dimension bound; Swan also needs the indicated rank/generator bounds. Classical strong-dual arguments over a finite p-adic field use spherical completeness there. They do not automatically extend to C_p. The C-valued extension is a precise supplier requirement, G1.

The period convention is Nφ=pφN, with the Tate twist Q_p(1) and φ=p on the positive semistable period space. The special rank-two block has slopes 0 and 1, not a single slope 1/2. The multiplicity functor [M] is Hom **from** JL(M). Galois, G and D× actions, the residual unramified twists, and the contragredients on LL(M) are retained in the target statements.

Generic adic spaces, coherent sheaves, perfectoid spaces, period rings, proper Hyodo–Kato comparison, smooth and locally analytic representations, and the two towers are imported. This roadmap adds their Stein, support, nonproper period and tower-cohomology applications. Classical local Langlands normalizes the geometric computation; the p-adic correspondence consumes that computation. In particular B0 is a theorem about any supplied continuous O-linear map to the function bundle, and does not construct a p-adic-Langlands representation or its map. A1’s separate integral local-Langlands finite-length input is isolated in G10 and must not depend back on A1.

The current TauCetiRoadmap main and Tau Ceti library were checked in addition to the older atlas snapshot. AlgebraicVectorBundles supplies scheme bundle interfaces, but not analytic Swan, analytic P¹ splitting, or the nonproper equivariant bundle argument. OperatorTheory’s real/complex results do not supply this nonarchimedean duality. The current AdicSpaces and RepresentationTheory interfaces are used at their actual scope. The exact library commits, verified declarations, and outstanding supplier contracts are recorded below.

The all-dimensional proper-support definition uses closed pseudoadic supports proper over the base. Such supports need not be closed analytic subvarieties. The extension-by-zero lemma only requires a quasi-compact separated ambient space; it does not add ambient properness. Pure dimension and vector-bundle coefficients are retained in coherent duality, as required by BCGP25 Lemmas 4.6.23–4.6.24, pp.85–86.

Three worked diagnostics govern the curve theory. On an open disc the de Rham first cohomology and the universal period term vanish, but the function quotient by constants is nonzero. On an annulus the logarithmic differential has nonzero residue and gives a Q_p-to-C right vertical map. For a disconnected finite union the kernel of the function differential has one constant per component. These cases prevent identifying étale and pro-étale cohomology, discarding residues, or using only diagonal constants.

The two largest tower checks are also structural. A compact-support fibre is the cone of global-to-boundary sections shifted by −1. It descends only after controlling the rational perfectoid complements and derived inverse limits, not merely the perfectoid affinoids. On a central quotient, Hochschild–Serre uses Γ_i/p^(NZ); using Γ_i introduces extra central group cohomology. The supercuspidal type removes constants, but the compact decomposition must still account for the special terms in its full indexing family.

#### S0/exhaustion: Quasi-Stein and Stein exhaustions

**Definition target.** A separated analytic space X locally of finite type over a complete nonarchimedean field K is quasi-Stein if it has a countable increasing admissible affinoid cover U_i with dense restriction O(U_{i+1})→O(U_i). A relatively compact Stein exhaustion additionally has U_i relatively compact in U_{i+1}. The exhaustion is auxiliary data; the property is existential. Finite-dimensional means a uniform bound on local dimensions. Use the stronger exhaustion for differential closedness and compact-support limits.

**Hypotheses.** K complete and nontrivially valued; X separated and locally of finite type.

**Proof route.**

1. Use the affinoid subspace and restriction notions of AdicSpacesPartII:R0.
2. Form the two exhaustion structures with their cofinal refinements; prove independence of the chosen cofinal sequence.

**Dependencies.** `AdicSpacesPartII:R0`, `AdicSpacesPartII:R3/coherent-sheaf`.

**Check.** A closed affinoid is quasi-Stein by its constant exhaustion, without acquiring the stronger relative-compactness property automatically.

**Uses that determine the API.**

- CDN20, Theorem 3.3: Computes curve cohomology by strict affinoid restriction.
- BCGP25, Lemma 4.6.24: Supplies the all-dimensional Stein input before any tower specialization.

**Required API.**

- `SteinExhaustion.restrict` (projection): Expose each affinoid, its inclusion and its dense restriction map.
- `SteinExhaustion.cofinal` (equivalence): Cofinal refinement gives the same space and the same section topology.
- `SteinExhaustion.finiteEtale` (functoriality): Finite étale inverse images preserve the relevant exhaustion properties, under the finite-dimensional hypotheses used below.
- `SteinSpace.quasiStein` (coercion): Forget relative compactness to obtain a quasi-Stein exhaustion.

**Distinguishing tests.**

- `SteinExhaustion.openDisc`: The open unit disc is covered by closed discs of radii increasing to one, with dense compact transition maps.
- `SteinExhaustion.affinoidBoundary`: A constant closed-disc exhaustion meets quasi-Stein density but does not certify strict relative compactness.
- `SteinExhaustion.projectiveLine`: The proper positive-dimensional projective line is not quasi-Stein: its coherent H¹(O(−2)) contradicts Theorem B.

**Sources.** [DLB17](#source-dlb17), §3.2, pp.338–339 — Defines the dense affinoid exhaustion and explains its coherent Fréchet topology.; [GK04](#source-gk04), Corollary 3.2, pp.11–12 — The closedness proof uses the relatively compact exhaustion.

Atlas planet: **Stein spaces**.

Proposed destination: `TauCeti/Geometry/Adic/Stein`, namespace `TauCeti.Stein`.

#### S0/coherent-limits: Coherent Fréchet limits and Kiehl A/B

**Theorem target.** For a quasi-Stein X and coherent F, F(X)=lim_i F(U_i) with its canonical complete Hausdorff Fréchet topology; the restriction maps F(X)→F(U_i) have dense image. The countable system has R¹lim F(U_i)=0, H^q(X,F)=0 for q>0, and global sections generate every stalk. Exact sequences of coherent sheaves give strict exact sequences of global Fréchet spaces. These assertions are invariant under cofinal exhaustion.

**Hypotheses.** X as S0/exhaustion; each F(U_i) has its finite-affinoid-module Banach topology.

**Proof route.**

1. Apply affinoid coherent equivalence and Tate acyclicity to the cover and its finite intersections.
2. Use the dense-transition Mittag–Leffler approximation argument to solve the difference map on the product and prove R¹lim=0.
3. Apply the exhaustion spectral sequence for B, and approximate local generators with successive correction for A; use the Fréchet open-mapping theorem for strictness.

**Dependencies.** [S0/exhaustion](#s0-exhaustion), `AdicSpacesPartII:R3/tate-kiehl-affinoid`, `AdicSpacesPartII:R3/tate-acyclicity-finite-modules`, `LocallyAnalyticDistributions:L0/banach-open-mapping-lifts`.

**Check.** For an open disc and F=O, the limit is the convergent-on-each-smaller-disc function space, not the Tate algebra of the closed unit disc. Strictness is obtained from a surjection of Fréchet spaces, rather than from algebraic exactness alone.

**Sources.** [BCGP25](#source-bcgp25), §4.6.17–4.6.18, p.84 — Reduces quasi-Stein coherent acyclicity to Kiehl and the dense-transition derived-limit argument.; [DLB17](#source-dlb17), §3.2, p.338 — Specifies the Fréchet structure and coherent consequences used on tower levels.

Atlas planet: **Kiehl’s theorems A and B**.

Proposed destination: `TauCeti/Geometry/Adic/Stein`, namespace `TauCeti.Stein`.

#### S0/analytic-swan: Analytic Serre–Swan with uniform bounds

**Theorem target.** If X is quasi-Stein with uniformly bounded local dimension, global sections give an equivalence between vector bundles whose ranks are uniformly bounded and finitely generated projective O(X)-modules. The inverse is sheafification of a direct summand of a finite free module. The equivalence respects tensor products, duals and pullbacks for which the stated hypotheses hold. The canonical topology on a finite projective module is its direct-summand Fréchet topology.

**Hypotheses.** Uniform bounds on dimension and bundle rank; X quasi-Stein.

**Proof route.**

1. Use Kiehl A/B and the finite-generation theorem under the two uniform bounds, as in Kohlhaase Theorem A.2.
2. Split a finite free sheaf presentation using the vanishing of the appropriate coherent H¹.
3. Identify the two unit/counit maps on affinoids and compare the direct-summand and inverse-limit topologies.

**Dependencies.** [S0/coherent-limits](#s0-coherent-limits), `AdicSpacesPartII:R3/locally-free-sheaf`, `AdicSpacesPartII:R3/tate-kiehl-affinoid`.

**Check.** A disjoint union with unbounded ranks is excluded; a finite free rank-r bundle gives O(X)^r with the product topology.

**Sources.** [KOHL11](#source-kohl11), Theorem A.2, pp.36–37 — Gives the vector-bundle/global-projective equivalence with uniform rank and dimension bounds.

Atlas planet: **Analytic Serre–Swan theorem**.

#### S0/finite-flat-projective: Finite-flat functions over a quasi-Stein base

**Theorem target.** For finite flat f:Y→X between quasi-Stein spaces, assume X has bounded local dimension and Y has finitely many connected components. Then O(Y) is a finite projective O(X)-module in the canonical topologies. If Y is nonempty and X is connected and normal, it is faithfully flat. In particular this applies to a finite étale tower level over the half-plane.

**Hypotheses.** All finiteness and connectedness assumptions are retained; the stronger conclusion requires normal connected X.

**Proof route.**

1. Finite flat pushforward is locally free. Finitely many components upstairs bounds its locally constant degree.
2. Apply the bounded-rank analytic Serre–Swan theorem.
3. For faithfulness use openness and closedness of the nonempty finite-flat image and connectedness of the normal base.

**Dependencies.** [S0/analytic-swan](#s0-analytic-swan), `AdicSpacesPartII:R3/finite-locally-free-morphism`.

**Check.** A disjoint degree-r étale cover gives a free rank-r module; an empty source cannot be faithfully flat.

**Sources.** [KOHL11](#source-kohl11), Proposition A.5, p.37 — Proves the finite-projective and faithful-flatness assertions with precisely these geometric finiteness assumptions.

Proposed destination: `TauCeti/Geometry/Adic/Stein`, namespace `TauCeti.Stein`.

#### S1/proper-supports: Cohomology with proper closed supports

**Construction target.** For separated X locally of finite type over Spa(K,O_K) and an analytic sheaf F, define RΓ_c(X,F) as the filtered colimit of RΓ_Z(X,F) over closed subsets Z of X proper over Spa(K,O_K). Local cohomology is the fibre of RΓ(X,F)→RΓ(X minus Z,F). For coherent sheaves retain the natural locally convex, or solid, derived realization and its comparison to the classical groups when justified. Supports are closed pseudoadic subsets; they need not be closed analytic subspaces.

**Hypotheses.** The support family is the family of proper closed subsets, ordered by inclusion.

**Proof route.**

1. Use local-cohomology fibres and the union of two proper closed supports to form a filtered support system.
2. Construct transition maps and maps induced by coefficient morphisms; prove the localization triangle before taking cohomology.
3. Compare the derived topological realization through the functional-analysis input requested for this layer.

**Dependencies.** `AdicSpacesPartII:R0`, `AdicSpacesPartII:R3/coherent-sheaf`, `LocallyAnalyticDistributions:L0`.

**Check.** On proper X this returns ordinary cohomology; on a nonproper space the support condition remains present.

**Uses that determine the API.**

- BCGP25, Lemmas 4.6.23–4.6.24: Provides the support functor used for extension by zero and coherent duality.
- T2/boundary-complex: Compares curve support cohomology with the boundary-section model.

**Required API.**

- `ProperSupportCohomology.localize` (characterisation): For each allowed Z the defining local-cohomology triangle has third term the complement complex.
- `ProperSupportCohomology.coefficientMap` (functoriality): A coefficient map induces maps on all support complexes and their colimit, respecting identities and composites.
- `ProperSupportCohomology.proper` (compatibility): For proper X the terminal support Z=X identifies RΓ_c with RΓ.
- `ProperSupportCohomology.trace` (projection): Expose the eventual trace H_c^d(X,Ω^d)→K with its support-compatible normalization.

**Distinguishing tests.**

- `ProperSupportCohomology.empty`: The empty support gives the zero complex.
- `ProperSupportCohomology.properPoint`: For a point Spa(K,O_K) and O, H_c^0=K and higher groups vanish.
- `ProperSupportCohomology.openDisc`: On a smooth open disc, H_c^0(O)=0 and H_c^1(O) is the continuous dual of Ω¹, distinguishing proper supports from unrestricted sections.

**Sources.** [BCGP25](#source-bcgp25), Definition 4.6.21, p.85 — Defines compact-support analytic cohomology using proper closed supports, rather than all topologically compact subsets.

#### S1/coherent-duality: Coherent Stein concentration and Serre duality

**Theorem target.** Let K be a finite extension of Q_p, X a smooth Stein space of pure dimension d, and E a finite locally free O_X-module. Then H_c^q(X,E)=0 for q≠d. The pairing H_c^d(X,E) × Γ(X,Hom(E,Ω_X^d))→K obtained by evaluation and trace is perfect in the continuous topological sense: the first factor is the strong continuous dual of the nuclear Fréchet second factor and is an LB space of compact type. A coefficient map E→E′ is transposed to Hom(E′,Ω^d)→Hom(E,Ω^d). The solid realization has this classical realization. The corresponding statement over C requires the separately recorded functional-analysis extension; it is not inferred from spherical completeness.

**Hypotheses.** K finite over Q_p for the classical strong-dual proof; X smooth, pure-dimensional and with relatively compact Stein exhaustion; E finite locally free.

**Proof route.**

1. Apply affinoid residue duality on relatively compact pieces and coherent Theorem B to the dual bundle.
2. Pass to the support colimit and the dual projective limit using compact restriction maps and the imported nuclear/compact-type duality.
3. Identify the residue trace on overlaps; prove independence of the exhaustion and naturality by the evaluation pairing.
4. For the C-valued tower use the explicit scalar-extension/solid-duality request rather than a Hahn–Banach assertion over C.

**Dependencies.** [S0/coherent-limits](#s0-coherent-limits), [S1/extension-by-zero](#s1-extension-by-zero), `AdicSpacesPartII:R3/sheaf-of-continuous-differentials`, `LocallyAnalyticDistributions:L0/compact-type-nuclear-duality`, `LocallyAnalyticDistributions:L0`.

**Check.** For d=0 and a finite set of K-points the pairing is the sum of pointwise evaluations. For d=1, E=O gives H_c¹(O)=Ω¹(X)′; replacing Ω¹ by O gives the wrong dual. Neither singular X nor mixed dimensions is silently included.

**Sources.** [BCGP25](#source-bcgp25), Lemma 4.6.24, pp.85–86 — Gives coherent concentration, compact-type LB topology and the continuous dual description for smooth pure-dimensional Stein spaces.; [GK00](#source-gk00), §4.2–4.6, pp.19–21 — Constructs residue duality and records spherical completeness for the classical functional-analytic argument.

#### S1/de-rham-support-complex: Stein de Rham cohomology and support complex

**Construction target.** For smooth finite-dimensional Stein X/K define RΓ_dR(X)=RΓ(X,Ω_X^•) and RΓ_dR,c(X)=RΓ_c(X,Ω_X^•), with the exterior differential and cohomological indexing starting in degree zero. **Hypotheses.** X smooth of bounded dimension; use continuous analytic differentials already owned by AdicSpacesPartII.

**Dependencies.** [S0/coherent-limits](#s0-coherent-limits), [S1/proper-supports](#s1-proper-supports), `AdicSpacesPartII:R3/sheaf-of-continuous-differentials`.

**Check.** For a curve, ordinary cohomology is computed by O(X)→Ω¹(X), while compact supports have a different total-degree shift.

**Uses that determine the API.**

#### S1/de-rham-duality: Topological de Rham duality

**Theorem target.** For K finite over Q_p and X smooth Stein of pure dimension d, the cup/trace pairings identify H_dR^i(X) with the strong continuous dual of H_dR,c^{2d−i}(X), and conversely, in the strict Fréchet/compact-type categories. In particular on a Stein curve the strict exact sequence 0→H_dR,c¹(X)→H_c¹(X,O)→O(X)′→H_dR⁰(X)′→0 is the transpose of 0→H_dR⁰(X)→O(X)→Ω¹(X)→H_dR¹(X)→0. Extension to the C-valued tower categories is a named missing input, not an unproved algebraic-dual replacement.

**Hypotheses.** X smooth and pure-dimensional; K finite over Q_p; all differentials use their proved strict topologies.

**Proof route.**

1. Dualize the global de Rham complex termwise using coherent Serre duality.
2. Use strictness to commute cohomology with strong duality, with the total-degree shift d from forms and d from coherent supports.
3. Apply reflexivity of nuclear Fréchet/compact-type duality for the reverse identification.

**Dependencies.** [S1/coherent-duality](#s1-coherent-duality), [S1/closed-differentials](#s1-closed-differentials), `LocallyAnalyticDistributions:L0/compact-type-nuclear-duality`.

**Check.** For a connected open disc H_dR⁰=K pairs with H_dR,c²=K, and both middle-degree groups vanish.

**Sources.** [GK00](#source-gk00), Theorem 4.11, p.22 — Gives the complementary-degree de Rham pairing.; [CDN20](#source-cdn20), §4.3.2, p.36 — Uses the exact transpose sequence on tower curves.

#### H0/boundary-mass: Mass-zero boundary distributions

#### B0/dense-image-surjectivity: Dense image implies surjectivity over a Stein space

**Theorem target.** Let X/K be a finite-dimensional quasi-Stein space over a finite p-adic field, A=O(X), N a finite projective A-module with its canonical Fréchet topology, and Y a Fréchet A-module with continuous multiplication by every fixed a∈A. **Hypotheses.** X separated, bounded-dimensional and quasi-Stein; N finite projective; Y complete Hausdorff Fréchet with the stated module continuity.

**Proof route.**

1. Reduce projective N to a finite free ambient module by adding a complement.
2. For rank one choose countably many distinct closed test points meeting the components of successive zero loci on an affinoid exhaustion. Interpolate at finitely many previous points using Kiehl A/B.
3. Build convergent corrections in Y whose image avoids those components; completeness keeps the new section in Im(F). Uniform bounded dimension makes finitely many such sections have no common zero.
4. In rank one the finitely generated ideal is closed by coadmissibility. Its affinoid extension, not intersection with an affinoid ring, is the whole ring by the affinoid Nullstellensatz; exhaustion density gives the whole global ring.
5. For higher rank compose F with its first coordinate. The rank-one result makes this a surjection of Fréchet spaces, hence open. This openness shows the image of its closed kernel is dense in the remaining coordinates: approximate (0,b) by F(y) and correct the small first coordinate by a small lift. Induction on the rank now proves surjectivity; coordinatewise surjectivity without this correction argument would be insufficient.

### E1: DLB17, misprint

**Locator:** Proposition 10.11 proof, printed p.386.

**Source assertion:** The ideal on an exhaustion affinoid is denoted J∩O(U_j).

**Correction:** Use the extended ideal J O(U_j), generated by the restrictions of its finite generators.

**Reason:** The restriction O(X)→O(U_j) points in the opposite direction to a subring intersection; the Nullstellensatz is applied to the ideal of restricted generators.

**Reach:** the proof. **Prior correction/provenance:** Previously identified as PAPER-DOSPINESCU-LEBRAS-17/E31 in the existing extraction; checked in the Annals version here. No separate published correction found in the limited searches recorded here.

**Correction:** Use V(f_i) in that induction step.

**Reason:** The newly constructed f_i is the function nonvanishing at one point of every preceding irreducible component.

### E3: DLB17, gap

**Locator:** Proposition 10.11 proof, printed pp.385–386.

**Source assertion:** After reducing to a finite free target the proof reduces to rank one by working in a basis, without an argument for the kernel.

**Correction:** First prove rank one. For a free target, compose with the first coordinate and use Fréchet openness of that surjection to show that its closed kernel maps densely to the remaining coordinates. Induct on the rank.

**Reason:** Surjectivity of the coordinate projections alone does not imply surjectivity to their product; the open-mapping correction supplies the missing implication.

**Reach:** the proof. **Prior correction/provenance:** No separate published correction found in the limited searches recorded here.
