# Explicit K₂: symbols, residues and reciprocity — blueprint (part from T.5)

This part completes the layer T.5, *tame kernels and explicit arithmetic*, of the roadmap. The layer's targets were planned in the accepted part that begins at T.3, and they are reproduced below as they stand there. The unramified subgroup of K₂ of a field is defined before any localisation theorem. The tame-kernel exact sequences of a ring of integers and of a ring of S-integers are derived from the Dedekind localisation sequence, and so is the relative sequence between them. The layer also contains the real sign symbol, the computation K₂(ℤ) ≅ ℤ/2 with generator {−1, −1}, and the computation K₂(ℚ) ≅ ℤ/2 ⊕ ⊕_{p odd} 𝔽_p^×. The fact K₂(𝔽_q) = 0, which the layer's text also asks for, is the node T.2/k2-finite-field of the earlier layer.

One input was still open: Steinberg's theorem that, for a commutative ring A and n ≥ 3, the kernel of the elementary map restricted to the monomial subgroup W_n(A) ⊆ St_n(A) is central and generated by symbols (Milnor, *Introduction to Algebraic K-Theory*, Corollary 9.3 and Theorem 9.11). The upper bound |K₂(ℤ)| ≤ 2 rests on it. It belongs with the Steinberg symbols of T.2:symbols, and this part plans it there, from Milnor §9. The same section proves that for a field the whole Steinberg kernel is monomial (Theorem 9.12, through the normal form of the upper unipotent subgroup and the cover St_n(F) = T·W·T). This gives, with no appeal to Matsumoto's presentation, that K₂ of a field is generated by symbols and that K₂(𝔽_q) = 1 (Corollary 9.13). Those two statements close the finite-field gaps of T.2. The new nodes are stated for general commutative rings and fields, and T.5 applies them to ℤ and to finite residue fields.

The layer's attachments to the accepted parts are given as routed amendments: exact prerequisite replacements, each checked against the consumer's statement and proof. They are listed after the node sections. They also narrow the citations of KTheoryLowDegrees U.4 (SK₁ of S-integers) to its accepted nodes, and answer the requests that other roadmaps make of T.5.

## Conventions

- St_n(A), n ≥ 3, is the finite-rank Steinberg group of T.1, with generators x_ij(λ) and the Steinberg relations; φ_n : St_n(A) → E_n(A) ⊆ GL_n(A) sends x_ij(λ) to the transvection e_ij(λ). The stable group is St(A) and K₂(A) = ker(St(A) → E(A)).
- w_ij(u) = x_ij(u) x_ji(−u⁻¹) x_ij(u) and h_ij(u) = w_ij(u) w_ij(−1) for a unit u (T.2:symbols). The monomial subgroup W_n(A) is generated by the w_ij(u), and C_n(A) = W_n(A) ∩ ker φ_n.
- Monomial matrices are written P_π·diag(v_1, …, v_n) with P_π e_k = e_{π(k)}. In Tau Ceti's notation P_π = permutationGL π, which is the matrix Mathlib attaches to π⁻¹, and diag(v) = diagGL v.
- Commutators are [a, b] = aba⁻¹b⁻¹, Mathlib's commutatorElement. The finite-rank symbol is {u, v}_n = [h_ij(u), h_ik(v)] for pairwise distinct i, j, k, and its stable image is the Steinberg symbol {u, v} of T.2.
- Tame symbols follow T.3: ∂_v{f, g} = (−1)^{v(f)v(g)} · residue of f^{v(g)}/g^{v(f)}, so ∂_v{u, π} = ū. The localisation boundary is its inverse in the K-book's normalisation; kernels and images do not depend on the sign.
- A ring of S-integers O_{F,S} is Mathlib's Set.integer S F, with S a finite set of nonzero primes of O_F. Its tame-kernel sequence sums over the primes outside S; the relative sequence has its residues at the primes in S.

### `unramified-subgroup` — The unramified subgroup of K_2 of a field ★

*definition* · planet **Unramified subgroup** · `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.5/unramified-subgroup`

Let F be a field with a family (v_i)_{i∈I} of discrete valuations — in the arithmetic case R is a Dedekind domain with fraction field F, I = HeightOneSpectrum R and v_𝔭 is the 𝔭-adic valuation; for a number field R = O_F and I is the set of finite places. The unramified subgroup U_I(F) ⊂ K_2(F) is the intersection over i of the kernels of the tame symbols ∂_{v_i} : K_2(F) → k(v_i)^× of T.3; for S ⊂ I the subgroup unramified outside S is the intersection over i ∉ S. The definition uses no localisation theorem; for a number field its identification with K_2(O_F) is T.5/tame-kernel-sequence.

**Hypotheses.**

- Each v_i is a discrete valuation of F with residue field k(v_i).
- When every element of F^× has nonzero valuation at only finitely many v_i (a Dedekind domain, the places of a function field), U_I(F) is the kernel of the residue sum K_2(F) → ⊕_i k(v_i)^×.

**Construction and proof.**

1. Define U_I(F) as the infimum over i of the kernels of the homomorphisms ∂_{v_i} (T.3/tame-symbol-steinberg).
1. Under finite support (T.3/finite-support) the residue sum is defined and its kernel is U_I(F).
1. Restriction along a finite extension whose family lies over the family of F maps U into U, by the ramification formula (T.3/ramification-formula); the transfer maps U back into U by the norm-residue formula (T.3/transfer-and-norm-residue).
1. For a Dedekind domain R the image of K_2(R) → K_2(F) lies in U: the map factors through K_2(R_𝔭), which is generated by Steinberg symbols of units (K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2/symbols-generate for the local ring R_𝔭), and the tame symbol of two units is trivial.

**API.**

- `unramifiedSubgroup` (data) — For a family v : I → discrete valuations of F, ⨅ i, ker(∂_{v i}) as a subgroup of K_2(F).
- `mem_unramifiedSubgroup_iff` (characterisation) — x ∈ U ↔ ∀ i, ∂_{v i} x = 1.
- `unramifiedOutside` (data) — For S ⊆ I, ⨅ i ∉ S, ker(∂_{v i}); unramifiedOutside ∅ = unramifiedSubgroup, and it is monotone in S.
- `unramifiedSubgroup_eq_ker_residueSum` (characterisation) — Under finite support, U = ker(K_2(F) → ⨁ i, k(v i)ˣ).
- `symbol_mem_unramifiedSubgroup` (simp) — If u and w are units at every v_i then {u, w} ∈ U.
- `range_K2_le_unramifiedSubgroup` (compatibility) — For a Dedekind domain R with fraction field F and I = HeightOneSpectrum R, the image of K_2(R) → K_2(F) lies in U.
- `unramifiedSubgroup_map_le` (functoriality) — Restriction along a finite extension carrying the family into the family maps U into U.
- `transfer_mem_unramifiedSubgroup` (relation) — The transfer of a finite extension of number fields maps the unramified subgroup of the larger field into that of the smaller.
- `unramifiedSubgroup_heightOneSpectrum` (compatibility) — For I = HeightOneSpectrum R, v_𝔭 is Mathlib's HeightOneSpectrum.valuation F and k(v_𝔭) is identified with R ⧸ 𝔭.

**Used by.**

- T.5/s-integer-tame-kernel-sequence and T.5/tame-kernel-sequence — the image of K_2(O_{F,S}) in K_2(F) is the subgroup unramified outside S, and that of K_2(O_F) is this subgroup
- ArithmeticKTheory N.2 — N.2 specialises its all-degree localisation sequence to T.5's degree-two rows, which identify this subgroup with K_2(O_F)
- SpecialValuesBirchTate B.1 and B.7 — the orders #K_2(O_F) and #K_2(O_{F,S}) are those of this subgroup and of its outside-S variant, through T.5/tame-kernel-sequence and T.5/relative-s-integer-sequence

**Unit tests.**

- `neg_one_neg_one_mem` (computation) — For F = ℚ with the family of all primes, {−1, −1} ∈ U, since −1 is a unit at every prime.
- `neg_one_p_not_mem` (non-example) — For an odd prime p, {−1, p} ∉ U over ℚ: its tame symbol at p is −1 ≠ 1 in 𝔽_p^×; a definition testing only symbols of units, or only one place, misses this.
- `three_three_not_mem` (non-example) — {3, 3} ∉ U over ℚ: the sign (−1)^{v(f)v(g)} makes its tame symbol at 3 equal to −1; a symbol without the sign would wrongly put {3, 3} in U.
- `two_neg_one_mem` (degenerate) — {2, −1} = 1 by the Steinberg relation (2 + (−1) = 1); correspondingly its tame symbol at 2 is −1 = 1 in 𝔽_2^× and all others are 1.
- `empty_family` (degenerate) — For the empty family U = K_2(F), and unramifiedOutside I = K_2(F).
- `mem_iff_residueSum_rat` (characterisation) — For ℚ and the primes, x ∈ U exactly when the residue sum of x in ⨁_p 𝔽_p^× vanishes.
- `heightOneSpectrum_int` (compatibility) — For R = ℤ the valuation Mathlib attaches to (p) ∈ HeightOneSpectrum ℤ is the p-adic valuation and ℤ ⧸ (p) ≅ ZMod p, so the tame symbol at (p) lands in (ZMod p)ˣ.

**Acceptance.**

- The symbol of two units of O_F is unramified everywhere.
- The definition does not presuppose the localisation theorem.

### `s-integer-tame-kernel-sequence` — The tame-kernel sequence of the S-integers: residues at the primes outside S

*theorem* · `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.5/s-integer-tame-kernel-sequence`

Let F be a number field, S a finite set of nonzero primes of O_F (S = ∅ allowed) and O_{F,S} = Set.integer S F. The nonzero primes of O_{F,S} are the 𝔭O_{F,S} with 𝔭 ∉ S, with residue fields k(𝔭) = O_F/𝔭 (Tau Ceti's IsDedekindDomain.integerHeightOneSpectrumEquiv). Then 0 → K_2(O_{F,S}) → K_2(F) −(∂_𝔭)_{𝔭∉S}→ ⊕_{𝔭∉S} k(𝔭)^× → 0 is exact: the sum is over the finite primes OUTSIDE S. Equivalently K_2(O_{F,S}) → K_2(F) is injective with image the subgroup unramified outside S (T.5/unramified-subgroup), and the residue sum over the primes outside S is onto. The sequence is derived through the actual maps of the Dedekind localisation sequence, whose boundary is the tame symbol at each prime (T.3/dedekind-localization-boundary, from T.3:localization-comparison): injectivity because K_2 of each finite residue field vanishes, surjectivity because the cokernel of the residue sum is ker(K_1(O_{F,S}) → K_1(F)), which is zero by the Bass–Milnor–Serre theorem SK_1(O_{F,S}) = 0 (KTheoryLowDegrees U.4). The relative sequence comparing O_F with O_{F,S}, whose residues are at the primes IN S, is T.5/relative-s-integer-sequence.

**Hypotheses.**

- F is a number field and S a finite set of nonzero primes of O_F.
- K_2 is the classical group of T.1, identified with Quillen's K_2 by T.1/k2-pi2; the sign of the boundary is fixed in T.3/localization-boundary and does not affect kernels or images.

**Proof.**

1. O_{F,S} is a Dedekind domain with fraction field F, a localisation of O_F; its height-one primes are the 𝔭O_{F,S} with 𝔭 ∉ S (IsDedekindDomain.integerHeightOneSpectrumEquiv), and O_{F,S}/𝔭O_{F,S} = O_F/𝔭.
1. Apply T.3/dedekind-localization-boundary to R = O_{F,S}: ⊕_{𝔭∉S} K_2(k(𝔭)) → K_2(O_{F,S}) → K_2(F) −∂→ ⊕_{𝔭∉S} k(𝔭)^× → K_1(O_{F,S}) → K_1(F) is exact, the 𝔭-component of ∂ being the inverse of the tame symbol at 𝔭.
1. Injectivity: K_2(k(𝔭)) = 0 for the finite fields k(𝔭) (K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2/k2-finite-field).
1. Image: ker ∂ is the subgroup unramified outside S, a tame symbol and its inverse having the same kernel (T.5/unramified-subgroup, unramifiedOutside S).
1. Surjectivity: coker ∂ ≅ ker(K_1(O_{F,S}) → K_1(F)), and this map is injective because SK_1(O_{F,S}) = 0 and O_{F,S}^× ⊆ F^× (KTheoryLowDegrees U.4). The surjectivity of each tame symbol and finite support do not suffice (Example III.1.5.4).

**Acceptance.**

- For F = ℚ and S = {p}: 0 → K_2(ℤ[1/p]) → K_2(ℚ) → ⊕_{ℓ≠p} 𝔽_ℓ^× → 0; the residue at p is not in the sum.
- For S = ∅ it is T.5/tame-kernel-sequence.
- Indexing test: {5, 2} ∈ K_2(ℚ) has nonzero tame symbol only at 5 (value 3 with T.3's normalisation), so it lies in the image of K_2(ℤ[1/5]) and not in that of K_2(ℤ[1/2]).

### `tame-kernel-sequence` — The tame-kernel exact sequence for the ring of integers

*theorem* · `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.5/tame-kernel-sequence`

For a number field F with ring of integers O_F the sequence 0 → K_2(O_F) → K_2(F) −⊕∂_𝔭→ ⊕_𝔭 k(𝔭)^× → 0 is exact, the sum over all nonzero primes of O_F and the third map the residue sum of the tame symbols (sign fixed in T.3/localization-boundary): K_2(O_F) → K_2(F) is injective with image the unramified subgroup of T.5/unramified-subgroup, and the residue sum is onto. It is the case S = ∅ of T.5/s-integer-tame-kernel-sequence: injectivity from K_2(k(𝔭)) = 0, surjectivity from SK_1(O_F) = 0 through the Dedekind localisation sequence (T.3/dedekind-localization-boundary and KTheoryLowDegrees U.4). ArithmeticKTheory N.2 imports this row and specialises its all-degree localisation sequence to it (RT-AREA-ktheory-1/9); T.5 does not import it from N.2. Surjectivity does not follow from the surjectivity of the individual tame symbols.

**Hypotheses.**

- F is a number field; 𝔭 runs over the nonzero primes of O_F and k(𝔭) = O_F/𝔭.
- K_2 is the classical group of T.1, identified with Quillen's K_2 by T.1/k2-pi2.

**Proof.**

1. Take S = ∅ in T.5/s-integer-tame-kernel-sequence: Set.integer ∅ F consists of the elements of F integral at every prime of O_F, which is O_F (mathlib:NumberField.RingOfIntegers).
1. State the sequence with the residue sum of T.5/unramified-subgroup as third map; exactness in the middle says that the image of K_2(O_F) is the unramified subgroup.
1. Record where each half comes from: injectivity from K_2(k(𝔭)) = 0 (K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2/k2-finite-field); surjectivity from SK_1(O_F) = 0 (KTheoryLowDegrees U.4) through the exact segment ⊕_𝔭 k(𝔭)^× → K_1(O_F) → K_1(F) of T.3/dedekind-localization-boundary. The surjectivity of each tame symbol does not suffice: for the Dedekind domain ℝ[x, y]/(x² + y² − 1) every tame symbol is onto, but the cokernel of the residue sum is its SK_1, which is nonzero (Example III.1.5.4).

**Acceptance.**

- For F = ℚ it is 1 → K_2(ℤ) → K_2(ℚ) → ⊕_p 𝔽_p^× → 1 (Application III.6.5.1).
- The third map is onto, but not because each tame symbol is: Example III.1.5.4 gives surjective tame symbols with a nonzero cokernel.
- The same sequence holds for every Dedekind domain whose fraction field is a global field (Soulé's Theorem V.6.8, n = 2).

### `relative-s-integer-sequence` — The relative sequence comparing K₂(O_F) with K₂(O_{F,S}): residues at the primes in S

*theorem* · `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.5/relative-s-integer-sequence`

Let F be a number field and S a finite set of nonzero primes of O_F. Then 0 → K_2(O_F) → K_2(O_{F,S}) −(∂_𝔭)_{𝔭∈S}→ ⊕_{𝔭∈S} k(𝔭)^× → 0 is exact, the first map induced by O_F ⊆ O_{F,S} and the residues taken at the primes IN S (read on the image of K_2(O_{F,S}) in K_2(F)). The tame-kernel sequence of O_{F,S} itself, T.5/s-integer-tame-kernel-sequence, has its residues at the primes OUTSIDE S; the two are distinct statements. Consequently #K_2(O_{F,S}) = #K_2(O_F)·∏_{𝔭∈S}(N𝔭 − 1) whenever K_2(O_F) is finite. This is the stage text's 'exact sequence comparing their tame kernel with the integral one'.

**Hypotheses.**

- F is a number field and S a finite set of nonzero primes of O_F.

**Proof.**

1. By T.5/tame-kernel-sequence and T.5/s-integer-tame-kernel-sequence, K_2(O_F) and K_2(O_{F,S}) inject into K_2(F) with images the unramified subgroup U and the subgroup U_S unramified outside S (T.5/unramified-subgroup); K_2(O_F) → K_2(O_{F,S}) → K_2(F) is K_2(O_F) → K_2(F) by functoriality, so the first map is injective with image corresponding to U ⊆ U_S.
1. U is the kernel of the residues at S restricted to U_S, which is exactness in the middle.
1. Surjectivity: given (y_𝔭)_{𝔭∈S}, extend it by 1 at the primes outside S and lift it through the surjective residue sum of T.5/tame-kernel-sequence; the lift is unramified outside S, so it lies in U_S, the image of K_2(O_{F,S}).

**Acceptance.**

- For F = ℚ and S = {p}: 0 → K_2(ℤ) → K_2(ℤ[1/p]) → 𝔽_p^× → 0, so K_2(ℤ[1/p]) has order 2(p − 1); ArithmeticKTheory N.8 demonstrates this sequence and imports it.
- Indexing: the residues are at the primes in S; for S = ∅ the sequence is the identity of K_2(O_F).

### `silvester-word-reduction` — Silvester’s monotone integer word lemma

*lemma* · `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.5/silvester-word-reduction`

For n≥2, a signed standard basis vector β∈ℤ^n and z∈S_n, there exist signed unit generators g₁,…,g_r and w∈W_n with z=g₁⋯g_r w and 1≤|βg₁|₁≤⋯≤|βg₁⋯g_r|₁. The word equality holds in S_n, not just after elementary matrices.

**Hypotheses.**

- S_n, W_n and the right row action are integer-steinberg-word-model; β=±e_i; n≥2.

**Proof.**

1. Take a signed unit word followed by W_n. Let σ_j=|βg₁⋯g_j|₁, σ₀=1. If a descent occurs, let λ=max{σ_j:σ_j>σ_(j+1)} and μ the last index attaining that maximum at a descent. Order (λ,μ) lexicographically. It is a pair of natural numbers, so strict decreases terminate; word length itself need not decrease.
1. Conjugate/renumber to make g_μ=x₁₂(1). Write the vector after g_μ as (a,b,c,…), so the preceding vector is (a,b−a,c,…). The maximality choice gives |b−a|≤|b|, hence |a|≤2|b| and a≠0 implies ab>0. Analyze g_(μ+1)=x_ij(ε), ε=±1. These are the source’s seven exhaustive index cases.
1. Cases 1–2: if i=1,j≥3, or both i,j≥3, commute g_(μ+1) left across x₁₂; only the peak norm drops (or its final occurrence moves earlier). Case 3: the same root x₁₂ must have ε=−1 and cancels; ε=+1 contradicts a descent and |b−a|≤|b|.
1. Case 4: i≥3,j=2 (take i=3). The two roots commute, but a plain swap need not reduce the norm. Writing x_ij=x_ij(1) and x_ij^ε=x_ij(ε), use x₁₂x₃₂^ε=x₃₂^εx₁₂=x₁₃^εx₃₂^εx₁₃^(−ε)=x₃₁^εx₁₂x₃₁^(−ε), verified by the Steinberg relations (Milnor p. 88). One decreases the peak according as |b−a|>|b−a+εc|, |c|>|c+εa|, or |a|>|a+εc|. The descent makes b and εc have opposite signs; if a≠0 it has the sign of b, forcing one of the last two inequalities; if a=0 the first holds.
1. Case 5: i=2,j=1. The sign ε=+1 contradicts the descent, so ε=−1. Replace x₁₂(1)x₂₁(−1) by x₂₁(1)w₂₁(−1), move w right by conjugation, and compare (a,b−a)→(b,b−a) with the old peak. Case 6: i=2,j≥3. Use x₁₂x₂₃^ε=x₁₃^εx₂₃^εx₁₂=x₂₃^εx₁₃^εx₁₂=x₂₁x₁₃^εx₁₂^(−1)w₁₂(1) on pp. 89–90; the possible reductions are |c|>|c+εa|, |c|>|c+εb−εa|, or |a|>|b|. The descent forces c and εb to have opposite signs and |b|<2|c|; split a=0 and a≠0 to obtain one of the three reductions.
1. Case 7: i≥3,j=1. Use x₁₂x₃₁^ε=x₃₁^εx₁₂x₃₂^(−ε)=x₃₁^εx₁₃^(−ε)x₃₂^(−ε)x₁₃^ε (p. 90). The sufficient inequalities are |b+εc|≤|b| or |c|≥|a|. Both are NONSTRICT; the original strict descent combines with them to reduce the peak pair, so equality must not be discarded. The descent gives opposite signs for a and εc and |c|<2|a|; together with |a|≤2|b| and ab>0, either |c|≤2|b| gives the first inequality, or |c|≥2|b| gives the second. Verify every replacement in S_n by its defining relations; W factors preserve the norm and conjugate subsequent signed generators to signed generators.
1. Each replacement strictly decreases (λ,μ), leaving the represented z fixed and every peak above λ untouched. Well-founded induction therefore yields the monotone word. In rank two only cases 3 and 5 occur; case 5 uses precisely the rank-two conjugation relation.

**Acceptance.**

- The result preserves the Steinberg word, not merely the resulting integer vector.
- At a=0 in Case 4 the first inequality is required; omitting that branch leaves a gap.
- The algorithm terminates by (λ,μ), not by a claimed decrease in word length.

**Depends on.** `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.5/integer-steinberg-word-model`.

**Source.** `Milnor.1971`, Lemma 10.6 and its seven-case proof, pp. 85–90. Silvester word reduction, with a well-founded peak pair and actual Steinberg-word rewrites.

### `integer-kernel-in-monomial-subgroup` — The integer Steinberg kernel is contained in the monomial subgroup

*lemma* · `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.5/integer-kernel-in-monomial-subgroup`

For every n≥1, ker(φ_n:S_n→E(n,ℤ))⊆W_n in the auxiliary finite-rank integer models.

**Hypotheses.**

- The auxiliary S₂ has Milnor’s Definition 10.4; no injectivity of stabilization is assumed.

**Proof.**

1. Induct on n, starting with S₁=1. For z in the kernel and n≥2 choose β=e_n and the monotone word g₁⋯g_r w from silvester-word-reduction. Since φ_n(z)=1 and w preserves |·|₁, the first and last norms are 1, hence every prefix norm is 1.
1. A signed elementary transvection taking a signed standard vector to a vector of norm 1 must fix it: adding its nonzero coordinate to a different zero coordinate would raise the norm to 2. Induct through the prefixes to see every g_j fixes e_n. Thus none has first index n, and w fixes e_n too.
1. Use Steinberg commutators to move the factors x_in(±1) left: z=x·ι(y)·w, x=∏_(i<n)x_in(a_i), y∈S_(n−1). Since w’s signed monomial matrix fixes e_n, choose w′∈W_(n−1) with the same upper-left monomial matrix and write w=ι(w′)c with c∈W_n∩ker φ_n. The determinant-one monomial image is generated by the w_ij, by Milnor Lemma 9.1; the n=2 fixed-e₂ case has identity monomial image and w′=1.
1. The matrices of x and ι(yw′) have respectively only last-column off-diagonal entries and an upper-left block with last column e_n. Their product being 1 forces both matrices to be 1. The commuting last-column root subgroups have injective matrix map (entries are a_i), so x=1. Also yw′ lies in ker φ_(n−1), hence in W_(n−1) by induction. Therefore z=ι(yw′)c∈W_n.

**Acceptance.**

- The induction includes n=2 using the auxiliary group; omitting this base does not prove the stable bound.
- The x=1 step uses the injective matrix map on commuting last-column root subgroups, not injectivity of φ_n on all S_n.

### `monomial-kernel/monomial-subgroup` — The monomial subgroup W_n(A) of the Steinberg group and its kernel C_n(A)

*definition* · `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2:symbols/monomial-kernel/monomial-subgroup`

Let A be a commutative ring and n ≥ 3, let St_n(A) be the finite-rank Steinberg group of T.1/steinberg-group-finite-rank and φ_n : St_n(A) → E_n(A) ⊆ GL_n(A) its elementary map (T.1:classical/to-elementary). The monomial subgroup W_n(A) ⊆ St_n(A) is the subgroup generated by the Weyl lift words w_ij(u) = x_ij(u) x_ji(−u⁻¹) x_ij(u), for i ≠ j and u ∈ A^× (T.2:symbols/diagonal-lift-words). It contains every diagonal lift h_ij(u) = w_ij(u) w_ij(−1) (T.2:symbols/diagonal-lift). The monomial kernel is C_n(A) = W_n(A) ∩ ker φ_n. Its image is described by Milnor's Lemma 9.1: φ_n(W_n(A)) is exactly the group of monomial matrices of determinant one, the matrices P_π·diag(v_1, …, v_n) with π a permutation, P_π e_k = e_{π(k)}, every v_k ∈ A^× and sign(π)·v_1⋯v_n = 1.

**Hypotheses.**

- A is a commutative ring: the determinant condition and the diagonal-lift calculus use commutativity.
- n ≥ 3, the range in which T.1 defines St_n(A); i, j are distinct indices in {1, …, n} and u ranges over A^×.

**Construction and proof.**

1. Define W_n(A) as the subgroup closure (mathlib:Subgroup.closure) of the set of all w_ij(u), and C_n(A) as its intersection with the kernel of φ_n. Each h_ij(u) is the product of the generators w_ij(u) and w_ij(−1), so it lies in W_n(A).
1. φ_n(w_ij(u)) has u in position (i, j), −u⁻¹ in position (j, i), 1 in the other diagonal positions and 0 elsewhere (T.2:symbols/diagonal-lift-words). Its 2 × 2 block [[0, u], [−u⁻¹, 0]] has determinant 1, so it is a monomial matrix of determinant one. φ_n(h_ij(u)) is diagonal, with u at i and u⁻¹ at j (T.2:symbols/diagonal-lift).
1. Lemma 9.1: these matrices generate every monomial matrix of determinant one. Write g = P_π D. A product of signed transpositions φ_n(w_ij(1)) equals P_π times a diagonal matrix of signs. The remaining diagonal factor has determinant one, so it is a product of the φ_n(h_1k(u_k)) = diag(u_k at 1, u_k⁻¹ at k), k = 2, …, n. On the matrix side, P_π D is tauceti:TauCeti.permutationGL π times tauceti:TauCeti.diagGL v. Mathlib's permutation matrix of σ sends e_j to e_{σ⁻¹(j)}, and Tau Ceti's permutationGL σ is the matrix of σ⁻¹, so P_π = permutationGL π. Its determinant is sign(π)·∏ v_k (mathlib:Matrix.det_permutation, tauceti:TauCeti.det_diagGL).
1. A ring map f : A → B sends w_ij(u) to w_ij(f(u)), hence W_n(A) into W_n(B) and C_n(A) into C_n(B). The stabilisation St_n(A) → St_{n+1}(A) of T.1/stabilisation sends generators to generators, hence W_n(A) into W_{n+1}(A) and C_n(A) into C_{n+1}(A).
1. For A = ℤ the units are ±1 and w_ij(−1) = w_ij(1)⁻¹ (weyl-conjugation (c)). So W_n(ℤ) is generated by the w_ij(1), and it is the monomial subgroup of T.5/integer-steinberg-word-model for n ≥ 3.

### `monomial-kernel/weyl-conjugation` — Conjugation by the monomial subgroup permutes the root generators (Milnor 9.2–9.5)

*lemma* · `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2:symbols/monomial-kernel/weyl-conjugation`

Let A be commutative and n ≥ 3. For w ∈ W_n(A) write φ_n(w) = P_π·diag(v_1, …, v_n) (monomial-subgroup). For i ≠ j, λ ∈ A and u ∈ A^×: (a) w x_ij(λ) w⁻¹ = x_{π(i)π(j)}(v_i λ v_j⁻¹); (b) w w_ij(u) w⁻¹ = w_{π(i)π(j)}(v_i u v_j⁻¹) and w h_ij(u) w⁻¹ = h_{π(i)π(j)}(v_i u v_j⁻¹)·h_{π(i)π(j)}(v_i v_j⁻¹)⁻¹; (c) w_ij(u)⁻¹ = w_ij(−u) and w_ij(u) = w_ji(−u⁻¹); (d) the subgroup H_n(A) generated by the h_ij(u) is normal in W_n(A); (e) C_n(A) lies in the centre of St_n(A). These are Milnor's Lemma 9.2, Corollaries 9.3 and 9.4 and Lemma 9.5.

**Hypotheses.**

- A is commutative and n ≥ 3; i ≠ j, λ ∈ A, u ∈ A^×.
- P_π e_k = e_{π(k)} and φ_n(w) = P_π D with D = diag(v_1, …, v_n); this factorisation exists by monomial-subgroup.

**Proof.**

1. If A is the zero ring, every root generator is x_ij(0) = 1 by additivity. Thus St_n(A), W_n(A) and C_n(A) are trivial, and all the assertions follow directly. In the remaining argument assume 0 ≠ 1, so a monomial matrix determines its permutation and diagonal units. Lemma 9.2: conjugation by one generator w_kl(u) carries each x_ij(λ) to a single root generator. Split into Milnor's seven cases, according to which of i, j equal k or l. Each case is a calculation with the Steinberg relations in St_n(A) (T.1/steinberg-group-finite-rank). For example, if i = k and j ∉ {k, l} then w_kl(u) x_kj(λ) w_kl(u)⁻¹ = x_lj(−u⁻¹λ). If (i, j) = (k, l) then w_ij(u) x_ij(λ) w_ij(u)⁻¹ = x_ji(−u⁻²λ); T.2:symbols/field-proof/root-word-normalization records this case for fields. A general w is a word in the generators, and the statement follows by induction along the word.
1. Corollary 9.4: by step 1 the conjugate is some root generator x_ab(μ); apply φ_n. On matrices, P_π D e_ij(λ) D⁻¹ P_π⁻¹ = e_{π(i)π(j)}(v_i λ v_j⁻¹) (tauceti:TauCeti.permutationGL_mul_diagGL_mul_inv relabels the diagonal). A transvection with λ ≠ 0 determines its index pair and its parameter (tauceti:TauCeti.transvectionUnit, which is injective in the parameter), so (a, b, μ) = (π(i), π(j), v_i λ v_j⁻¹); the case λ = 0 is trivial. Multiplying out the defining words of w_ij and h_ij gives (b).
1. (c), Lemma 9.5: w_ij(u) w_ij(−u) = 1 follows from x_ij(a) x_ij(b) = x_ij(a + b) by cancelling the middle factors. Apply (b) to w = w_ij(u) itself: its permutation is the transposition (i j), with v_i = −u⁻¹ and v_j = u, so w_ij(u) = w w_ij(u) w⁻¹ = w_ji(v_i u v_j⁻¹) = w_ji(−u⁻¹).
1. (d): by the formula for h in (b), every conjugate of a generator h_ij(u) by an element of W_n(A) is a product of generators of H_n(A).
1. (e), Corollary 9.3: if φ_n(w) = 1 then π = 1 and every v_k = 1, so by (a) w commutes with every generator x_ij(λ). Hence w lies in the centre of St_n(A) (mathlib:Subgroup.center).

**Acceptance.**

- Over ℤ: w_12(1) x_12(λ) w_12(1)⁻¹ = x_21(−λ) and w_12(1) x_13(λ) w_12(1)⁻¹ = x_23(−λ).
- w_12(1)⁴ = h_12(−1)⁻² lies in C_n(ℤ), so it is central in St_n(ℤ).
- Over a field, the same-root and opposite-root cases agree with T.2:symbols/field-proof/root-word-normalization.

**Depends on.** `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2:symbols/monomial-kernel/monomial-subgroup`, `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1/steinberg-group-finite-rank`, `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.1:classical/to-elementary`, `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2:symbols/diagonal-lift-words`, `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2:symbols/diagonal-lift`, `mathlib:Subgroup.center`, `mathlib:Subgroup.Normal`, `tauceti:TauCeti.transvectionUnit`, `tauceti:TauCeti.transvectionUnit_injective`, `tauceti:TauCeti.permutationGL_mul_diagGL_mul_inv`.

**Source.** `Milnor.1971`, Lemma 9.2 with its seven-case proof, pp. 71–73. Conjugation by an element of W sends each root generator to a root generator; the proof treats a generator w_kl(u) case by case through the Steinberg relations.

### `monomial-kernel/finite-rank-symbol` — The finite-rank Steinberg symbol {u, v}_n in C_n(A)

*definition* · `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2:symbols/monomial-kernel/finite-rank-symbol`

Let A be commutative and n ≥ 3. For u, v ∈ A^× and pairwise distinct indices i, j, k put {u, v}_n = [h_ij(u), h_ik(v)] = h_ij(u) h_ik(v) h_ij(u)⁻¹ h_ik(v)⁻¹ ∈ St_n(A). Then: (a) {u, v}_n = h_ik(uv) h_ik(u)⁻¹ h_ik(v)⁻¹ (Lemma 9.6); (b) {u, v}_n ∈ C_n(A), so it is central; (c) it does not depend on the triple (i, j, k); (d) it is bimultiplicative and skew-symmetric, {v, u}_n = ({u, v}_n)⁻¹ (Lemma 9.7); (e) {u, 1 − u}_n = 1 when u and 1 − u are units, and {u, −u}_n = 1 (Lemma 9.8); (f) stabilisation sends {u, v}_n to {u, v}_{n+1}, and its image in the stable group St(A) is the Steinberg symbol {u, v} of T.2/steinberg-symbol; (g) a ring map f sends {u, v}_n to {f(u), f(v)}_n. Milnor calls this the sharper, finite-rank meaning of the symbol.

**Hypotheses.**

- A is commutative and n ≥ 3; u, v ∈ A^×; i, j, k pairwise distinct.
- Commutators are [a, b] = aba⁻¹b⁻¹, the convention of T.2/steinberg-symbol and of T.2:symbols/field-proof/root-word-normalization.

**Construction and proof.**

1. (a): apply weyl-conjugation (b) to w = h_ij(u). Its image is diagonal with v_i = u, v_j = u⁻¹ and v_k = 1, so h_ij(u) h_ik(v) h_ij(u)⁻¹ = h_ik(uv) h_ik(u)⁻¹. Multiply on the right by h_ik(v)⁻¹.
1. (b): φ_n(h_ij(u)) and φ_n(h_ik(v)) are diagonal, so they commute and the commutator lies in W_n(A) ∩ ker φ_n = C_n(A). It is central by weyl-conjugation (e).
1. (d), bimultiplicativity for a fixed triple: by (a) at the triple (i, k, j), h_ij(uu′) = c·h_ij(u′)h_ij(u) with c = [h_ik(u), h_ij(u′)] central. The commutator identity [xy, z] = x[y, z]x⁻¹[x, z] and centrality then give {uu′, v}_n = {u, v}_n {u′, v}_n. The second variable is symmetric (Milnor Lemmas 8.1–8.2 and 9.7).
1. (c) and skew-symmetry: conjugate by an element w of W_n(A) whose permutation carries (i, j, k) to another triple and whose diagonal entries ε at those indices are ±1. By weyl-conjugation (b), h_ab(u) becomes h(εu)h(ε)⁻¹ at the new indices. By bimultiplicativity at the new triple the sign factors cancel in the commutator, and the conjugate of a central element is itself. Swapping j and k gives {v, u}_n = ({u, v}_n)⁻¹.
1. (e), Lemma 9.8: for v = 1 − u, show h_12(u) h_12(v) = h_12(uv) by rewriting w_12(u) w_12(−1) w_12(v) = w_12(uv) through w_12(−1) = w_21(1) = x_21(1) x_12(−1) x_21(1) and the conjugation formulas; this is the computation of T.2/steinberg-identity, carried out using the chosen three indices in St_n(A), or mapped there along the rank-raising homomorphism St_3(A) → St_n(A); no injectivity of that homomorphism is assumed. The hypothesis is symmetric in (u, v), so also h_12(v) h_12(u) = h_12(vu); since uv = vu these h's commute, and (a) gives {u, v}_n = 1. For v = −u, w_12(u) w_12(−1) w_12(−u) = w_12(u) w_12(−1) w_12(u)⁻¹ = w_12(−u²) by weyl-conjugation (b), (c), and the same argument applies.
1. (f): the stabilisation map of T.1/stabilisation sends x_ij(λ) to x_ij(λ), hence w, h and commutators to the same words. T.2/steinberg-symbol defines the stable symbol as this commutator in St(A). (g) is the same remark for a ring map.

### `monomial-kernel/generated-by-symbols` — Steinberg's theorem: the monomial kernel C_n(A) is generated by symbols (Milnor 9.11)

*theorem* · `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2:symbols/monomial-kernel/generated-by-symbols`

Let A be a commutative ring and n ≥ 3. The monomial kernel C_n(A) = W_n(A) ∩ ker φ_n is the subgroup generated by the finite-rank symbols {u, v}_n, u, v ∈ A^× (Milnor's Theorem 9.11, due to Steinberg). In particular C_n(A) is abelian and central in St_n(A), and the description is compatible with stabilisation and with ring maps. For A = ℤ, C_n(ℤ) is generated by {−1, −1}_n = w_12(1)⁴ and has at most two elements.

**Hypotheses.**

- A is commutative and n ≥ 3.
- The theorem concerns W_n(A) ∩ ker φ_n only. That the whole kernel of φ_n lies in W_n is a separate statement: field-kernel-is-monomial proves it for fields, and T.5/integer-kernel-in-monomial-subgroup for ℤ.

**Proof.**

1. If A is the zero ring, every root generator is x_ij(0) = 1 by additivity. Thus St_n(A), W_n(A) and C_n(A) are trivial, and all the assertions follow directly. In the remaining argument assume 0 ≠ 1, so a monomial matrix determines its permutation and diagonal units. Let H = H_n(A) ⊆ W_n(A) be the subgroup generated by the h_ij(u); it is normal in W_n(A) (weyl-conjugation (d)).
1. C_n(A) ⊆ H. Modulo H, w_ij(u) ≡ w_ij(1), since w_ij(u) w_ij(1)⁻¹ = w_ij(u) w_ij(−1) = h_ij(u). Write w̄_ij for the common class; w̄_ij = w̄_ji by weyl-conjugation (c), and w̄_ij² = 1 since w_ij(1)² = h_ij(−1)⁻¹. By weyl-conjugation (b), w̄_ij w̄_kl ≡ w̄_{τ(k)τ(l)} w̄_ij with τ the transposition (i j). Take c ∈ C_n(A) as a word in the w_ij(u). Push every w̄_1l to the left past the w̄_ij with i, j > 1 (these fix the index 1). Then cancel w̄_1l w̄_1l ≡ 1 and rewrite w̄_1j w̄_1l ≡ w̄_1l w̄_lj for j ≠ l, removing the w̄_1l one or two at a time, until at most one remains on the left. A single remaining w̄_1l is impossible, because the permutation part of φ_n(c) is the identity and the rest of the word fixes 1. Repeat with the index 2, and so on, to get c ≡ 1 modulo H (Milnor's word reduction).
1. Lemma 9.10: h_jk(u) = h_ik(u) h_ij(u)⁻¹ for pairwise distinct i, j, k. This comes from evaluating the commutator h_ik(u) w_jk(1) h_ik(u)⁻¹ w_jk(1)⁻¹ in two ways with weyl-conjugation (b). Hence h_jk(u) h_kj(u) = 1, and H is generated by the h_1k(u), k = 2, …, n.
1. Let C* ⊆ C_n(A) be the subgroup generated by the symbols; it is central (finite-rank-symbol (b)), hence normal. Modulo C*, h_1l(uv) ≡ h_1l(u) h_1l(v) by finite-rank-symbol (a) at a triple (1, j, l), j ∉ {1, l}, which needs n ≥ 3. Also h_1j(u) h_1l(v) ≡ h_1l(v) h_1j(u), by finite-rank-symbol (a)–(c) when j ≠ l and, when j = l, by h_1j(uv) ≡ h_1j(u) h_1j(v) ≡ h_1j(v) h_1j(u) modulo C*, since uv = vu. So c ≡ h_12(u_2) h_13(u_3) ⋯ h_1n(u_n) modulo C*.
1. Apply φ_n: φ_n(c) = diag(u_2⋯u_n, u_2⁻¹, …, u_n⁻¹) (tauceti:TauCeti.diagGL). Since φ_n(c) = 1, every u_k = 1, so c ∈ C*. The reverse inclusion C* ⊆ C_n(A) is finite-rank-symbol (b).
1. Stabilisation and ring maps send C_n to C_n and symbols to symbols (monomial-subgroup, finite-rank-symbol (f), (g)), so the description is compatible with both.
1. A = ℤ: the units are ±1 and symbols with an entry 1 are trivial, so C_n(ℤ) is generated by {−1, −1}_n. Moreover ({−1, −1}_n)² = {1, −1}_n = 1 by bimultiplicativity, and {−1, −1}_n = w_12(1)⁴ by finite-rank-symbol.

**Acceptance.**

- Over ℤ, C_3(ℤ) ⊆ {1, w_12(1)⁴}. With T.5/integer-kernel-in-monomial-subgroup this bounds K₂(ℤ), which is its use in T.5/integer-kernel-upper-generation.
- Over a finite field, together with finite-rank-symbol (d), (e) and the cyclic-annihilator argument (T.2:symbols/field-proof/cyclic-annihilator), C_n(𝔽_q) = 1: Milnor's Corollary 9.9.
- The reduction modulo H uses only the permutation part of φ_n: for a nonzero ring A, W_n(A)/H_n(A) maps onto the symmetric group, and the word reduction shows that this map has trivial kernel.

- For the zero ring the Steinberg group is trivial; no permutation quotient is asserted in that case.

### `monomial-kernel/upper-unipotent-normal-form` — The upper unipotent subgroup T_n(A) maps isomorphically onto U_n(A) (Milnor 9.14)

*construction* · `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2:symbols/monomial-kernel/upper-unipotent-normal-form`

Let A be a commutative ring and n ≥ 3, and let T_n(A) ⊆ St_n(A) be the subgroup generated by the x_ij(λ) with i < j. Every element of T_n(A) is a product ∏_{i<j} x_ij(λ_ij), with the factors in lexicographic order of (i, j) and uniquely determined λ_ij ∈ A. Consequently φ_n restricts to an isomorphism of T_n(A) onto the upper unitriangular group U_n(A) (Tau Ceti's upperUnitriangularGroup). Moreover, for each i < n the subgroup T′_i generated by the x_kl(μ), k < l, (k, l) ≠ (i, i + 1), is normal in T_n(A), and T_n(A) = x_{i,i+1}(A)·T′_i.

**Hypotheses.**

- A is commutative and n ≥ 3; the order on pairs (i, j), i < j, is lexicographic.

**Proof.**

1. Existence of the ordered form: induct on the height l − k, collecting factors with the Steinberg relations of St_n(A) (T.1/steinberg-group-finite-rank). These are [x_ij(λ), x_jl(μ)] = x_il(λμ) for distinct i, j, l, and commutation for non-chaining pairs. A commutator of two positive-root generators involves only a root of larger height, so the collection terminates.
1. Uniqueness: put R_i = ∏_{j>i} x_ij(λ_ij). The factors of R_i commute, and φ_n(R_i) = I + Σ_{j>i} λ_ij E_ij changes only row i. Write M = φ_n(R_1⋯R_{n−1}) and N_{i+1} = φ_n(R_{i+1}⋯R_{n−1}). Row i of M equals e_i + Σ_{j>i} λ_ij·(row j of N_{i+1}), and the rows j > i of N_{i+1} form an invertible unitriangular block. So the λ_ij are recovered from M row by row, from the last row up.
1. The same triangular system shows that every upper unitriangular matrix is such a product, so φ_n maps T_n(A) bijectively onto U_n(A) (tauceti:TauCeti.upperUnitriangularGroup; tauceti:TauCeti.UpperUnitriangularGroup.ext determines its elements by their entries above the diagonal). The matrix-side relations are tauceti:TauCeti.commutatorElement_transvectionUnit and tauceti:TauCeti.commute_transvectionUnit.
1. Normality of T′_i: the commutator of x_{i,i+1}(λ) with x_kl(μ), (k, l) ≠ (i, i + 1), is trivial, or equals x_il(λμ) when k = i + 1, or x_{k,i+1}(−μλ) when l = i. Each of these lies in T′_i, so T_n(A) = x_{i,i+1}(A)·T′_i.

**API.**

- `Steinberg.upperUnipotent` (data) — T_n(A) is the subgroup generated by x_ij(a) with i < j.
- `Steinberg.upperUnipotentComplement` (data) — For i < n, T′_i is generated by all positive roots except (i, i+1); T′_i is normal in T_n(A).
- `Steinberg.orderedUpperProduct` (constructor) — A family (a_ij) for i < j gives the lexicographically ordered product in T_n(A). Every element is obtained uniquely.
- `Steinberg.toElementary_upperUnipotent` (equivalence) — The restriction of φ_n is a group isomorphism T_n(A) ≃ U_n(A), with U_n(A) Tau Ceti’s upperUnitriangularGroup.
- `Steinberg.upperUnipotent_ext` (extensionality) — Elements of T_n(A) with equal elementary images are equal; their unique ordered coordinates agree.
- `Steinberg.upperUnipotent_map` (functoriality) — A ring map sends ordered coordinates to their images and carries T_n and T′_i into their counterparts. Identity and composition hold on generators.
- `Steinberg.upperUnipotent_simpleRoot_factor` (characterisation) — For fixed i, every t ∈ T_n(A) is x_{i,i+1}(a)t′ with unique a ∈ A and t′ ∈ T′_i; a is the (i,i+1) entry of φ_n(t).

**Unit tests.**

- `upperUnipotent_rank_three_cross_term` (computation) — Over ℤ, x_12(1)x_13(0)x_23(1) has (1,3) entry 1. Replacing ordered coordinates by raw matrix entries misses this cross term.
- `upperUnipotent_not_abelian` (non-example) — Over ℤ, [x_12(1),x_23(1)] = x_13(1) ≠ 1; the positive-root subgroup is not a product with coordinatewise addition.
- `upperUnipotent_simpleRoot_complement` (characterisation) — For n=3 and i=1, T′_1 consists of x_13(b)x_23(c), with (1,2) entry zero. It contains x_13(1), and excludes x_12(1) over ℤ.

**Acceptance.**

### `monomial-kernel/field-bruhat-cover` — For a field, St_n(F) = T·W·T (Milnor 9.15)

*lemma* · `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2:symbols/monomial-kernel/field-bruhat-cover`

Let F be a field and n ≥ 3. Every element of St_n(F) lies in T_n(F)·W_n(F)·T_n(F), where T_n(F) is the upper unipotent subgroup of upper-unipotent-normal-form and W_n(F) the monomial subgroup.

**Hypotheses.**

- F is a commutative field and n ≥ 3. Milnor states the lemma for skew fields as well; the atlas uses fields only.

**Proof.**

1. St_n(F) is generated by T_n(F) and the w_{i,i+1}(1), i < n. The x_ij(λ) with |i − j| = 1 generate St_n(F) by the commutator relations, and w_ij(1) x_ij(λ) w_ij(1)⁻¹ = x_ji(−λ) (weyl-conjugation (a)) turns each lower one into a conjugate of an upper one.
1. So it suffices that T W T is closed under right multiplication by w = w_{i,i+1}(1). Given t₁ w′ t₂, write t₂ = x_{i,i+1}(λ) t′ with t′ ∈ T′_i (upper-unipotent-normal-form). Then t₁ w′ t₂ w = t₁ (w′ x_{i,i+1}(λ) w)(w⁻¹ t′ w), and w⁻¹ t′ w ∈ T_n(F): conjugation by w_{i,i+1}(1)⁻¹ = w_{i,i+1}(−1) permutes the positive roots other than (i, i + 1) (weyl-conjugation (a), (c)).
1. Let π be the permutation of φ_n(w′). Case 1, π(i) < π(i + 1): w′ x_{i,i+1}(λ) w = x_{π(i)π(i+1)}(λ′) w′ w ∈ T W.
1. Case 2, π(i) > π(i + 1) and λ ≠ 0. Write x_{i,i+1}(λ) = w_{i,i+1}(λ) x_{i,i+1}(−λ) x_{i+1,i}(λ⁻¹), so w′ x_{i,i+1}(λ) w = (w′ w_{i,i+1}(λ) x_{i,i+1}(−λ))(x_{i+1,i}(λ⁻¹) w). The permutation of w′ w_{i,i+1}(λ) sends (i, i + 1) to (π(i + 1), π(i)), a positive root, so the first factor lies in T W. The second is w x_{i,i+1}(−λ⁻¹) ∈ W T, by conjugation with w_{i,i+1}(−1).
1. Case 3, λ = 0: the factor is w′ w ∈ W. Every nonzero λ is a unit because F is a field; over a ring, a nonzero non-unit parameter falls outside cases 2 and 3, and this is where the field hypothesis is used.

**Acceptance.**

- The matrix shadow is the Bruhat decomposition SL_n(F) = ⋃ U·N·U, and the upper-triangular monomial matrices are diagonal (tauceti:TauCeti.UpperTriangularGroup.mem_diagonalTorus_of_mem, over fields with a unit other than 1).
- For n = 3 and F = 𝔽₂, every element of St_3(𝔽₂) is t₁ w t₂ with t₁, t₂ upper unipotent and w in the group generated by the w_ij(1).
- Only cases 1–3 occur, and case 2 needs λ invertible: the lemma is stated for fields.

### `monomial-kernel/field-kernel-is-monomial` — For a field, the whole Steinberg kernel is monomial and generated by symbols (Milnor 9.12)

*theorem* · `K2SymbolsBrauer:T.2:symbols/monomial-kernel/field-kernel-is-monomial`

Let F be a field and n ≥ 3. The kernel of φ_n : St_n(F) → E_n(F) is contained in W_n(F), hence equals C_n(F). So it is central in St_n(F) and is generated by the finite-rank symbols {u, v}_n (generated-by-symbols): St_n(F) → E_n(F) is a central extension whose kernel is generated by symbols, for every n ≥ 3. Passing to the colimit, K₂(F) is generated by the stable Steinberg symbols {u, v} (Milnor's Theorem 9.12 and Corollary 9.13); this is the field clause of T.2/symbols-generate.

**Hypotheses.**

- F is a commutative field and n ≥ 3.
- For skew fields Milnor proves the containment of the kernel in W_n but not generation by symbols, which needs commutativity (generated-by-symbols). The atlas states the field case; see the source issue K2SymbolsBrauer/E-T5-division-ring-attribution.

**Proof.**

1. Write x ∈ ker φ_n as t₁ w t₂ with t₁, t₂ ∈ T_n(F) and w ∈ W_n(F) (field-bruhat-cover).
1. Then φ_n(w) = φ_n(t₁)⁻¹ φ_n(t₂)⁻¹ is a monomial matrix that is upper unitriangular. A monomial matrix P_π D that is upper triangular has π = 1: column j has its single nonzero entry v_j in row π(j) ≤ j, and the only such permutation is the identity. Unitriangularity then forces D = 1. Tau Ceti's tauceti:TauCeti.UpperTriangularGroup.mem_diagonalTorus_of_mem gives this argument for the diagonal normalizer over fields with a unit other than 1; it holds over every nonzero commutative ring.
1. Hence φ_n(w) = 1, so w ∈ C_n(F); and φ_n(t₂) = φ_n(t₁)⁻¹, so t₂ = t₁⁻¹ because φ_n is injective on T_n(F) (upper-unipotent-normal-form).
1. Therefore x = t₁ w t₁⁻¹ = w, since w is central (weyl-conjugation (e)). So ker φ_n ⊆ W_n(F), equality with C_n(F) holds by definition, and generation by symbols is generated-by-symbols.
1. Stable consequence: an element of K₂(F) = ker(St(F) → E(F)) comes from some St_n(F), n ≥ 3 (T.1/stabilisation), with trivial image in E_n(F) since E_n(F) → E(F) is injective. So it is a product of finite-rank symbols, whose stable images are the Steinberg symbols {u, v} (finite-rank-symbol (f)).

**Acceptance.**

- For F = 𝔽_q every finite-rank symbol is 1, by finite-rank-symbol (d), (e) and the cyclic-annihilator argument. So St_n(𝔽_q) → E_n(𝔽_q) is an isomorphism for n ≥ 3, and K₂(𝔽_q) = 1 (Milnor Corollaries 9.9 and 9.13), with no appeal to Matsumoto's theorem.
- For F = ℝ the kernel is nontrivial: {−1, −1}_3 is a nontrivial central element of St_3(ℝ), detected after stabilisation by the real sign symbol.
- Its stable consequence is the field clause of T.2/symbols-generate (routed row R3 of restructure).

## Source corrections

- **K2SymbolsBrauer/E-T5-division-ring-attribution** (gap; `Weibel.KBook.III`, Theorem III.5.10.5 and the sentence before it, p. 43 of the author's Chapter III file). The text says that the generation of K₂ by Steinberg symbols was proved for fields and division rings in §9 of Milnor's book. Theorem 5.10.5 then states it for fields, division rings, local rings and semilocal rings. *Correction:* Milnor §9 proves generation by symbols for fields only. Corollary 9.13 (p. 78) draws the generation conclusion for fields. Theorem 9.12 is the only statement of the section that covers skew fields, and its conclusion is the containment of the kernel in W. (Affects the attribution’s proof reference; independently confirmed by REV-K2SymbolsBrauer--T.5. The division-ring theorem itself is not rejected.)

Five proved examples check the integral Weyl matrix, its characteristic-two permutation, the two sign-pairing value patterns, normalization with an entry one, and pullback at the real embedding of ℚ. No example uses an admitted roadmap theorem. The remaining boundary checks are named theorem targets: `weylGL_int_square`, `weylGL_int_fourth`, `diagLiftGL_zmod_five_not_signed`, `monomialImage_card_zmod_three`, `orderedTransvection_three_coe` and `orSign_not_steinberg`. They are matrix or pairing forms of the checks above; the definitive general statements remain in this document. The general monomial predicate is used over arbitrary commutative rings: the pinned normalizer theorem’s extra field and nontrivial-unit hypotheses do not supply it, notably over 𝔽₂.
