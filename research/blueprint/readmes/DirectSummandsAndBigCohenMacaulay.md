# Direct summands and big Cohen–Macaulay algebras

Hochster's direct summand conjecture (1969, published 1973) asks whether every finite extension R ⊆ S of a Noetherian regular ring is split as a map of R-modules: whether there is an R-linear ρ : S → R with ρ(1) = 1. Equivalently, every finite extension of R is pure, or S is a generator of the category of R-modules, or every ideal I of R is contracted from every integral extension. Rings with this property are called splinters. Hochster proved the conjecture in characteristic p, the trace settles it for rings containing ℚ, Auslander–Buchsbaum settles dimension at most two, and Heitmann settled dimension three in mixed characteristic. André proved it in general in 2016 using perfectoid spaces, and Bhatt gave a second, shorter perfectoid proof that also yields a derived variant conjectured by de Jong.

This roadmap plans both proofs and the theory around them. It starts where [Perfectoid rings and spaces, Part II](PerfectoidSpacesPartII.md) (PerfectoidSpacesPartII) stops. That roadmap supplies the analytic inputs: purity and almost purity, André's Krull detection lemma, the normalized Kummer tower over W(k)⟦T_1,…,T_n⟧ with André's flatness theorem and its consequence modulo p^m (the fragment of the perfectoid Abhyankar lemma that the direct summand theorem consumes), the perfectoid Abhyankar lemma itself, and Bhatt's quantitative Riemann extension theorem. What is built here is the commutative algebra that turns those inputs into splittings, the big Cohen–Macaulay algebras that come out of the same constructions, and their consequences.

**André's route** (DS.2, DS.4, DS.5). For A = W(k)⟦T_1,…,T_n⟧ and a finite extension B that is étale away from a discriminant pg, the completed Kummer tower gives a faithfully flat integral perfectoid algebra over A in which g has all p-power roots; over it the extension B becomes almost finite étale modulo every p^m, in an almost setup generated by the roots of g. Ext¹ commutes with this flat base change, so the class of 0 → A/p^m → B/p^m → B/A ⊗ A/p^m → 0 becomes almost zero after base change, and André's Krull detection lemma shows it was zero. Splittings modulo all p^m give a splitting. The same almost Cohen–Macaulay algebra, B° with π = ϖg, is fed into Hochster's partial algebra modifications to produce balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebras for every Noetherian local ring of mixed characteristic. Over a regular ring a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebra is faithfully flat, so every finite cover of a regular ring is dominated by a faithfully flat algebra, which reproves the direct summand theorem for every regular ring without Hochster's 1983 reduction to the unramified case.

**Bhatt's route** (DS.3). Every p-torsion-free regular local ring A_0 of residue characteristic p admits an almost faithfully flat map to an integral perfectoid algebra, also in the ramified case. After adjoining the p-power roots of a discriminant g (André's flatness lemma in Bhatt's form), the splitting obstruction of A_0 → B_0 survives modulo p^m up to a controlled root of pg. On the rational annuli {|p^n| ≤ |g|} of the associated perfectoid space the extension is finite étale after inverting p, so almost purity splits it almost; Bhatt's quantitative Riemann extension theorem says the annuli recover A_∞/p^m up to an almost-pro-isomorphism with bounds sufficient for each perfect dual’s finite filtration and the relevant Hom and lim¹, so the obstruction is almost zero, hence zero. The argument is linear in the extension and works verbatim for the cone of A_0 → RΓ(X_0, O) for a proper surjective X_0 → Spec A_0, once proper maps over perfectoid algebras are shown to be almost split: this is the **derived direct summand theorem** (DS.7).

## Conventions

Rings are commutative with 1, and p is a fixed prime. A **splitting** of an R-algebra S is an R-linear map ρ : S → R with ρ(1) = 1; it is never required to be multiplicative, and "R → S splits" means a splitting exists. A **splinter** is a reduced Noetherian ring over which every finite injective algebra splits (the convention of Bhatt–Ma–Patakfalvi–Schwede–Tucker–Waldron–Witaszek, Definition 2.12; Bhatt's 2012 scheme version through finite surjective maps agrees for reduced Noetherian rings).

For a Noetherian local ring (R, m) of dimension d, a **system of parameters** is a list of d elements of m generating an m-primary ideal (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra R03.3). A list x is **weakly regular** on a module M if each x_{i+1} is a nonzerodivisor on M/(x_1, …, x_i)M (Mathlib `IsWeaklyRegular`). An R-module M, not necessarily finitely generated, is **big Cohen–Macaulay for x** if x is weakly regular on M and M ≠ mM, and **balanced big Cohen–Macaulay** if this holds for every system of parameters. André's "algèbre de Cohen–Macaulay" is a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebra.

Almost mathematics is always taken with respect to a named ideal generated by a compatible system of p-power roots: p^{1/p^∞}, (pg)^{1/p^∞}, π^{1/p^∞} with π = ϖg, or g^{1/p^∞}K°°_∞. A root system of x is a sequence (x^{1/p^k}) with x^{1/p^0} = x and (x^{1/p^{k+1}})^p = x^{1/p^k}; it is data. Almost statements are never upgraded to exact ones without an explicit argument (Krull detection, faithful flatness of (·)_{!!}).

K = Q_p(p^{1/p^∞})^ in DS.3 and DS.7, with t = p. In DS.2 and DS.4, k is a perfect field of characteristic p, A = W(k)⟦T_1,…,T_n⟧, K_∞ = W(k)[1/p](ζ_{p^∞}), and Â°_∞∞ is the completed Kummer tower of PerfectoidSpacesPartII RA.5. For a ring map u : A → K in D(A), cone(u) and the splitting obstruction α_u ∈ Hom_{D(A)}(cone u, A[1]) are as in DS.0.

## Layer DS.0: Splittings, splinters and big Cohen–Macaulay modules

Everything later rests on four notions. A *split algebra* is the conclusion of the direct summand theorem; for finite algebras over Noetherian rings splitting is controlled by the ideal ev(Hom_R(S, R)) ⊆ R, which commutes with flat base change, so it is local and descends along faithfully flat maps — the reason the problem reduces to complete local rings with any residue field. A *splinter* packages the theorem. *Big Cohen–Macaulay modules* are the non-Noetherian substitutes for maximal Cohen–Macaulay modules that the perfectoid constructions produce; completion makes them balanced, and over a regular local ring balanced ones are faithfully flat, which links big Cohen–Macaulay algebras back to splittings. The *splitting obstruction* is the extension class that both André and Bhatt kill; defining it in the derived category from the start lets the derived theorem of DS.7 reuse DS.3 unchanged. The layer ends with the cases that need no perfectoid input: rings containing ℚ (trace) and dimension at most two.

**Targets of the layer.**

Commutative algebra of splittings and of big Cohen–Macaulay modules, with no perfectoid input. Pure and almost pure maps and André's Krull detection lemma are imported from PerfectoidSpacesPartII RA.5; systems of parameters, injective hulls and Matlis duality from DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra R03.3. Targets:

- **Split algebras**: an R-algebra S is split if some R-linear ρ : S → R has ρ(1) = 1; for S finite over a Noetherian R this is local on R, descends along faithfully flat maps and is equivalent to purity.
- **Splinters**: reduced Noetherian rings over which every finite injective algebra splits.
- **Big Cohen–Macaulay modules** over a Noetherian local ring (some system of parameters weakly regular and M ≠ mM, no finiteness) and **balanced** ones (every system of parameters); m-adic completion turns the first into the second; over a regular local ring balanced big Cohen–Macaulay modules are faithfully flat (André B Remarque 4.2.1).
- **The splitting obstruction** α_u ∈ Hom_{D(A)}(cone u, A[1]) of a unit map u : A → K, its flat base change and its reduction modulo powers of an ideal.
- **Reductions** of the splitting problem to a domain finite over a complete regular local ring with algebraically closed residue field and, in mixed characteristic, a discriminant g nonzero modulo p.
- The **trace splitting** (every Noetherian regular ring containing ℚ is a splinter) and the case of **dimension ≤ 2**.

### Split algebras (R-linear retractions of the unit)

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/split-algebra` (definition).** Let R be a commutative ring and S a commutative R-algebra with structure map ι : R → S. A splitting of S over R is an R-linear map ρ : S → R with ρ ∘ ι = id_R, equivalently ρ(1) = 1. S is split over R (ι splits) if a splitting exists. No multiplicativity of ρ is required. Writing ev : Hom_R(S, R) → R for evaluation at 1, S is split iff ev is surjective, and the image of ev is an ideal of R (the trace ideal of 1).

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Algebra.IsSplit`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Splittings are R-module maps, never ring maps: ℝ → ℂ is split by the real part, which is not multiplicative.
- When S is a finitely presented R-module, splitting is equivalent to purity of ι (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map; in the proof of André B Lemme A.2.1(4) Hom_S(S, R) is read as Hom_R(S, R), misprint DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay/E19).

**Construction.**

1. The image of ev is an ideal: for f ∈ Hom_R(S, R) and r ∈ R, (r·f)(1) = r·f(1). If ev(f) = u is a unit, u⁻¹f is a splitting.
2. Composition: splittings of R → S and S → T (the latter S-linear) compose to an R-linear splitting of R → T; if R → S → T splits then the restriction of a splitting of R → T to S splits R → S.
3. Base change: if ρ splits ι then R′ ⊗_R ρ splits R′ → R′ ⊗_R S for every R-algebra R′.
4. Finite case: if R is Noetherian and S is a finite R-module, S is finitely presented, so Hom_R(S, R) is finite and its formation commutes with flat base change; hence the ideal ev(Hom_R(S, R)) commutes with flat base change, so splitting can be checked after localising at every maximal ideal, and descends along any faithfully flat R → R′ (an ideal I with IR′ = R′ is R).

**Inputs.** `mathlib:Module.Finite`, `mathlib:Module.FaithfullyFlat`, `mathlib:Module.Dual`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map`.

**API.**

- `TauCeti.Algebra.IsSplit` (characterisation): IsSplit R S ↔ ∃ ρ : S →ₗ[R] R, ρ 1 = 1.
- `TauCeti.Algebra.IsSplit.iff_eval_surjective` (characterisation): IsSplit R S ↔ the evaluation map Hom_R(S, R) → R, f ↦ f(1), is surjective.
- `TauCeti.Algebra.IsSplit.comp` (structure): If S splits over R and T splits over S then T splits over R.
- `TauCeti.Algebra.IsSplit.of_comp` (relation): If T splits over R and R → S → T, then S splits over R.
- `TauCeti.Algebra.IsSplit.baseChange` (functoriality): If S splits over R then R′ ⊗_R S splits over R′ for every commutative R-algebra R′.
- `TauCeti.Algebra.IsSplit.isPure` (compatibility): A split algebra is pure (PerfectoidSpacesPartII RA.5 IsPure); the converse holds when S is finitely presented as an R-module.
- `TauCeti.Algebra.IsSplit.injective` (relation): The structure map of a split algebra is injective.
- `TauCeti.Algebra.IsSplit.of_localization_maximal` (other): For R Noetherian and S finite over R: S splits over R iff S_m splits over R_m for every maximal ideal m.
- `TauCeti.Algebra.IsSplit.of_faithfullyFlat_baseChange` (other): For R Noetherian, S finite over R and R → R′ faithfully flat: if R′ ⊗_R S splits over R′ then S splits over R.

**Unit tests.**

- `isSplit_polynomial` (computation): R → R[X] is split by evaluation at 0.
- `isSplit_self` (degenerate): The identity R → R is split, and so is R → R × R by the first projection.
- `isSplit_gaussian` (computation): ℤ → ℤ[i] is split: the coefficient of 1 in the basis {1, i} is a ℤ-linear retraction.
- `not_isSplit_node_normalisation` (non-example): For a field K, K[x, y]/(xy) → K[x] × K[y] is injective and finite but not split; a definition requiring only injectivity of R → S fails this test.
- `not_isSplit_int_rat` (non-example): ℤ → ℚ is injective and flat but not split: Hom_ℤ(ℚ, ℤ) = 0.
- `isSplit_iff_isPure_of_finitePresentation` (compatibility): If S is finitely presented as an R-module, S is split over R iff R → S is pure in the sense of PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map.

**Uses.**

- André B Conjecture 0.1.1 (proved in the paper) and Bhatt Theorem 1.1 (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/direct-summand-theorem): the conclusion of the direct summand theorem
- André B §3.4 (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.2/adic-splitting-criterion): splittings modulo p^m assemble to a splitting through the ideal ev(Hom_R(S, R))
- Bhatt proof of Theorem 5.4 (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/direct-summand-reductions): splitting is local on R and descends along faithfully flat completion
- André B §0.2 and Bhatt Example 1.1 [Bhatt12] (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/trace-retraction): the normalised trace is a splitting

**Checks.**

- For K a field, K → L splits for every nonzero K-algebra L (extend 1 to a basis).
- For R = K[x, y]/(xy) and its normalisation S = K[x] × K[y], R → S is finite and injective but not split (André B §0.2): its splitting would make R → S pure, but the ideal (x + y) of R is not contracted from S.

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, §0.1, Conjecture 0.1.1 and the sentence after it, p. 71. States the direct summand conjecture as the existence of an R-linear retraction of R → S and notes its equivalence with purity of R → S and with S being a generator of R-modules.

- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Theorem 1.1, p. 1. Formulates the direct summand theorem as splitting of the inclusion A_0 → B_0 as a map of A_0-modules.

### Splinters

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/splinter` (definition).** A commutative ring R is a splinter if it is Noetherian and reduced, and every finite injective ring map R → S (S commutative) is split in the sense of DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/split-algebra. Equivalently (R Noetherian and reduced) every integral injective ring map R → S is pure (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map), since an integral extension is the filtered union of its finite subalgebras.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.IsSplinter`.

**Planet.** Splinter.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The convention follows Bhatt–Ma–Patakfalvi–Schwede–Tucker–Waldron–Witaszek, Definition 2.12. Bhatt [Bhatt12] Definition 0.1 instead asks splitting of O_S → f_*O_X for every finite surjective f : X → Spec S; for Noetherian rings the two agree once R is reduced, and the scheme version forces reducedness (apply it to R_red → R).
- Reducedness is part of the definition: without it the Artinian Gorenstein ring k[ε]/(ε²), which is self-injective, would satisfy the splitting condition.

**Construction.**

1. Integral version: an integral extension R ⊆ S is the filtered union of finite subalgebras S_α; each R → S_α splits hence is pure, and pure maps are stable under filtered colimits (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map).
2. Contracted ideals: for a pure extension R → S and every ideal I of R, R/I → S/IS is injective, so IS ∩ R = I.
3. A splinter domain is integrally closed: if a/b ∈ Frac(R) is integral over R, then R → R[a/b] is finite and injective; a splitting gives R[a/b] ≅ R ⊕ R[a/b]/R, so the quotient, which is killed by powers of b, is also torsion-free as a submodule of Frac(R), hence zero, and a/b ∈ R (Bhatt [Bhatt12] Example 1.1).

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/split-algebra`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map`, `mathlib:IsNoetherianRing`, `mathlib:IsReduced`, `mathlib:Algebra.IsIntegral`.

**API.**

- `TauCeti.IsSplinter` (characterisation): IsSplinter R ↔ IsNoetherianRing R ∧ IsReduced R ∧ ∀ S finite injective over R, Algebra.IsSplit R S.
- `TauCeti.IsSplinter.isSplit` (projection): For a splinter R and a finite injective R-algebra S, S splits over R.
- `TauCeti.IsSplinter.isPure_of_isIntegral` (other): For a splinter R, every injective integral R-algebra S is pure over R.
- `TauCeti.IsSplinter.contracted_ideal` (relation): For a splinter R, an injective integral R-algebra S and an ideal I of R, IS ∩ R = I.
- `TauCeti.IsSplinter.isIntegrallyClosed` (relation): A splinter domain is integrally closed.
- `TauCeti.IsSplinter.of_ringEquiv` (functoriality): Being a splinter is invariant under ring isomorphism.
- `TauCeti.isSplinter_of_isRegularRing` (compatibility): Every Noetherian regular ring is a splinter (the direct summand theorem, node DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/direct-summand-theorem).

**Unit tests.**

- `isSplinter_field` (degenerate): A field K is a splinter.
- `isSplinter_int` (computation): ℤ is a splinter: a finite injective ℤ → S splits because the image of 1 in S modulo torsion is primitive (an element s with ns ≡ 1 would make 1/n integral over ℤ).
- `not_isSplinter_dual_numbers` (non-example): k[ε]/(ε²) is not a splinter because it is not reduced, although every finite injective map out of it splits (it is self-injective); a definition without reducedness fails this test.
- `not_isSplinter_node` (non-example): K[x, y]/(xy) is reduced but not a splinter: its normalisation does not split.
- `not_isSplinter_cusp` (non-example): K[t², t³] is not a splinter: K[t²,t³] → K[t] is finite and injective but the quotient is torsion of generic rank 0 and nonzero, so no splitting exists.
- `isSplinter_iff_isIntegrallyClosed_of_charZero` (compatibility): A Noetherian domain containing ℚ is a splinter iff it is integrally closed (Mathlib IsIntegrallyClosed).

**Uses.**

- André B Conjecture 0.1.1 (proved in the paper) (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/direct-summand-theorem): every Noetherian regular ring is a splinter
- BMPSTWW Definition 2.12 and the Part II of SchemeAndStackFoundations routed from PAPER-BHATT-ETAL-23: splinters are the singularities whose +-regularity and Cohen–Macaulayness are compared
- André B §0.2 and A.3 (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/absolute-integral-closure-splits): the integral form: R → R⁺ is pure for a splinter

**Checks.**

- Every field is a splinter.
- A Noetherian domain containing ℚ is a splinter iff it is normal (Bhatt [Bhatt12] Example 1.1; DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/trace-retraction).

Defines a splinter as a reduced Noetherian ring every finite extension of which splits as a module map, and notes that in characteristic 0 normal rings are splinters by the trace and that local splinters in positive or mixed characteristic are Cohen–Macaulay.
- Bhargav Bhatt, *Derived splinters in positive characteristic*, Definition 0.1 and Example 1.1, pp. 1–2. Defines splinters through finite surjective maps of schemes and shows that a connected Noetherian ℚ-scheme is a splinter iff it is normal.

### Big Cohen–Macaulay modules and algebras

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/big-cm-module` (definition).** Let (R, m) be a Noetherian local ring of Krull dimension d and x = (x_1, …, x_d) a system of parameters of R (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/systems-of-parameters, applied to M = R). An R-module M, not necessarily finitely generated, is big Cohen–Macaulay for x if M ≠ mM and x is weakly regular on M: for each 0 ≤ i < d, x_{i+1} is a nonzerodivisor on M/(x_1, …, x_i)M. M is big Cohen–Macaulay if it is big Cohen–Macaulay for some system of parameters. An R-algebra C is a big Cohen–Macaulay algebra (for x) if it is so as an R-module.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.IsBigCohenMacaulayFor`.

**Planet.** Big Cohen–Macaulay module.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Because (x) is m-primary, M ≠ mM is equivalent to M ≠ (x)M; so for a system of parameters the condition is exactly Mathlib's RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular M x.
- No finiteness is imposed on M; for finitely generated M the notion is that of a maximal Cohen–Macaulay module.
- In dimension 0 the empty sequence is the system of parameters and every module with M ≠ mM qualifies.

**Construction.**

1. The equivalence M ≠ mM ⟺ M ≠ (x)M: m^n ⊆ (x) for some n, so M = mM gives M = m^n M ⊆ (x)M.
2. Hence big Cohen–Macaulay for x is RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular M x with x a system of parameters, and the Mathlib API for regular sequences applies.

**Inputs.** `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/systems-of-parameters`, `mathlib:RingTheory.Sequence.IsWeaklyRegular`, `mathlib:RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular`, `mathlib:IsLocalRing`, `mathlib:ringKrullDim`.

**API.**

- `TauCeti.IsBigCohenMacaulayFor` (characterisation): IsBigCohenMacaulayFor x M ↔ IsWeaklyRegular M x ∧ M ≠ m • ⊤, for x a system of parameters.
- `TauCeti.IsBigCohenMacaulayFor.iff_isRegular` (compatibility): For x a system of parameters, IsBigCohenMacaulayFor x M ↔ RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular M x.
- `TauCeti.IsBigCohenMacaulay` (characterisation): IsBigCohenMacaulay M ↔ ∃ x a system of parameters, IsBigCohenMacaulayFor x M.
- `TauCeti.IsBigCohenMacaulayFor.of_linearEquiv` (functoriality): The predicate is invariant under R-linear isomorphism.
- `TauCeti.IsBigCohenMacaulayFor.quotient` (other): If M is big Cohen–Macaulay for (x_1, …, x_d), then M/x_1M is big Cohen–Macaulay over R/x_1R for (x_2, …, x_d).
- `TauCeti.IsBigCohenMacaulayFor.monomial_not_mem` (relation): If M is big Cohen–Macaulay for x then (x_1⋯x_d)^{t−1}M ⊄ (x_1^t, …, x_d^t)M for every t ≥ 1; in particular for an algebra the element (x_1⋯x_d)^{t−1} is not in (x^t)C.

**Unit tests.**

- `isBigCM_self_regular` (computation): For R = K⟦X, Y⟧ and x = (X, Y), R is big Cohen–Macaulay for x.
- `isBigCM_dim_zero` (degenerate): If dim R = 0 then M is big Cohen–Macaulay iff M ≠ mM; for R a field, iff M ≠ 0.
- `not_isBigCM_rat_over_zp` (non-example): Over R = ℤ_(p) with x = (p), the module ℚ is not big Cohen–Macaulay: p is a nonzerodivisor on ℚ but ℚ = pℚ. A definition requiring only weak regularity fails this test.
- `not_isBigCM_embedded_prime` (non-example): R = K⟦X, Y⟧/(XY, Y²) has dimension 1 and depth 0 (Y is killed by the maximal ideal), so R is not big Cohen–Macaulay over itself for the system of parameters (X).
- `isBigCM_iff_isRegular` (compatibility): For a system of parameters x, IsBigCMFor x M ↔ RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular M x (Mathlib).

**Uses.**

- André B Proposition 4.1.2 (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.4/almost-cm-to-big-cm): the colimit of partial modifications is big Cohen–Macaulay for the fixed x before completion
- Hochster 2002 Theorem 4.2 (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.4/modification-criterion): the B-side of the modification criterion is big Cohen–Macaulay for one system of parameters
- BMPSTWW §2.2 and Theorem 2.5: R⁺ and its p-adic completion are compared with these predicates

**Checks.**

- A regular local ring is big Cohen–Macaulay over itself for a regular system of parameters.

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, Définition 4.1.1(1), p. 84. Calls a not necessarily Noetherian B-algebra C Cohen–Macaulay for (B, x) when C ≠ m_B C and the system of parameters x becomes a regular sequence in C.
- B. Bhatt, L. Ma, Z. Patakfalvi, K. Schwede, K. Tucker, J. Waldron and J. Witaszek, *Globally +-regular varieties and the minimal model program for threefolds in mixed characteristic*, §2.2, conditions (a)–(d) and the definitions after them, p. 12. For an arbitrary module over a Noetherian local ring, big Cohen–Macaulay means some system of parameters is a regular sequence (in the weak sense) and M/mM ≠ 0; the algebra version is the module version for the underlying module.

### Balanced big Cohen–Macaulay modules and algebras

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/balanced-big-cm-module` (definition).** Let (R, m) be a Noetherian local ring. An R-module M is balanced big Cohen–Macaulay if M ≠ mM and every system of parameters of R is weakly regular on M, i.e. M is big Cohen–Macaulay (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/big-cm-module) for every system of parameters. An R-algebra C is a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebra if it is so as an R-module; André calls these Cohen–Macaulay B-algebras.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.IsBalancedBigCohenMacaulay`.

**Planet.** Balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebra.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- For finitely generated nonzero M, balanced and big agree. For arbitrary modules they can differ; m-adic completion passes from big to balanced (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/completion-balances), with the arbitrary-module completion input recorded in G3.

**Construction.**

1. Unfold: balanced means IsBigCohenMacaulayFor x M for all systems of parameters x.
2. Change of rings: a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebra for B is one for any local subring A ⊆ B over which B is finite with the same systems of parameters (used in André B Remarque 4.2.1).

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/big-cm-module`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/systems-of-parameters`.

**API.**

- `TauCeti.IsBalancedBigCohenMacaulay` (characterisation): IsBalancedBigCohenMacaulay M ↔ M ≠ m • ⊤ ∧ ∀ x a system of parameters, IsWeaklyRegular M x.
- `TauCeti.IsBalancedBigCohenMacaulay.isBigCohenMacaulayFor` (projection): A balanced big Cohen–Macaulay module is big Cohen–Macaulay for every system of parameters.
- `TauCeti.IsBalancedBigCohenMacaulay.restrictScalars_finite` (functoriality): If A → B is a finite injective local map of Noetherian local rings of the same dimension and C is a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay B-algebra, then C is a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay A-algebra.
- `TauCeti.IsBalancedBigCohenMacaulay.faithfullyFlat_of_isRegularLocalRing` (compatibility): Over a regular local ring, balanced big Cohen–Macaulay modules are faithfully flat (node DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/regular-big-cm-flat).

**Unit tests.**

- `isBalancedBigCM_regular_self` (computation): A regular local ring R is a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay R-module (every system of parameters of a Cohen–Macaulay local ring is regular).
- `isBalancedBigCM_field` (degenerate): Over a field every nonzero module is balanced big Cohen–Macaulay.
- `isBalancedBigCM_of_finite_cm` (compatibility): A nonzero finitely generated module is balanced big Cohen–Macaulay iff it is big Cohen–Macaulay iff it is maximal Cohen–Macaulay (depth = dim R).
- `not_isBalancedBigCM_quotient` (non-example): Let R be regular local of positive dimension and x_1 the first member of a regular system of parameters. The nonzero finite module R/x_1R has depth dim R − 1, so it is neither big nor balanced big Cohen–Macaulay over R. In particular the system of parameters beginning with x_1 fails weak regularity.

**Uses.**

- André B Théorème 0.7.1 (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.4/big-cm-existence): the existence statement is for balanced algebras
- André B Remarque 4.2.1 (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/regular-big-cm-flat): balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebras over regular rings are faithfully flat
- André B Théorème 0.7.2 (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/finite-cover-flat-domination): the dominating flat algebra is a product of balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebras

**Checks.**

- Over a regular local ring a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebra is faithfully flat (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/regular-big-cm-flat).

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, Définition 4.1.1(2) and footnote 5, p. 84. A B-algebra is Cohen–Macaulay if it is Cohen–Macaulay for (B, x) for every system of parameters x; the footnote identifies this with balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebras.
- B. Bhatt, L. Ma, Z. Patakfalvi, K. Schwede, K. Tucker, J. Waldron and J. Witaszek, *Globally +-regular varieties and the minimal model program for threefolds in mixed characteristic*, §2.2, condition (c) and the definition of balanced, p. 12. Balanced big Cohen–Macaulay means every system of parameters is a regular sequence on M and M/mM ≠ 0.

### Completion turns big Cohen–Macaulay modules into balanced ones

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/completion-balances` (theorem).** Let (R, m) be a Noetherian local ring and M an R-module with M ≠ mM on which some system of parameters x of R is weakly regular. Then the m-adic completion M̂ = lim_n M/m^n M is a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay R-module (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/balanced-big-cm-module). If M is an R-algebra, M̂ is an R-algebra and M → M̂ is an algebra map.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.IsBigCohenMacaulayFor.adicCompletion_isBalanced`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- M̂ is the adic completion of M as an R-module (Mathlib AdicCompletion); M itself need not be separated.
- The source states the arbitrary-module theorem, but its proof and the Ext/Čech comparison remain G3; the Bruns–Herzog book cited there was not independently read.

**Proof.**

1. A system of parameters weakly regular on M implies H^i_m(M) = 0 for i < d by the direct limit of the Koszul complexes on its powers. This needs the Čech/Koszul comparison with Mathlib’s Ext-colimit localCohomology; the baseline definition alone does not provide that comparison.
2. Apply the arbitrary-module completion/change-of-parameters theorem recalled in André §4.1, p. 85, and BMPSTWW §2.2, p. 12: this vanishing together with M/mM ≠ 0 makes the m-adic completion balanced. G3 records this non-routine input; it does not follow from the finite-module depth criterion or from merely defining a Koszul complex.
3. Completion is functorial, and for an algebra the inverse limit of the quotient algebras carries the required algebra structure. No finite-generation or separatedness assumption on M is inserted.

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/big-cm-module`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/balanced-big-cm-module`, `mathlib:AdicCompletion`, `mathlib:localCohomology`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/local-cohomology-depth-and-top-tensor`, `DerivedDeRhamCohomology:DD.1/koszul-complex`.

**Checks.**

- For M finitely generated, M̂ = M ⊗_R R̂ and the statement reduces to the classical fact that a maximal Cohen–Macaulay module stays maximal Cohen–Macaulay after completion.

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, §4.1, sentence after Définition 4.1.1, p. 85. One passes from Cohen–Macaulay for (B, x) to Cohen–Macaulay for every system of parameters by m_B-adic completion, citing Bartijn–Strooker Théorème 1.7.
- B. Bhatt, L. Ma, Z. Patakfalvi, K. Schwede, K. Tucker, J. Waldron and J. Witaszek, *Globally +-regular varieties and the minimal model program for threefolds in mixed characteristic*, §2.2, paragraph after conditions (a)–(d), p. 12. If M satisfies the local cohomology vanishing (a) and M/mM ≠ 0, then the m-adic completion satisfies (c), citing Bruns–Herzog Exercise 8.1.7 and Theorem 8.5.1.
- Raymond Heitmann and Linquan Ma, *Big Cohen–Macaulay algebras and the vanishing conjecture for maps of Tor in mixed characteristic*, §1.2, discussion after Theorem 1.6, p. 4. Uses the same completion step (Bruns–Herzog Corollary 8.5.3) to make the modification algebras balanced.

### Balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebras over regular local rings are faithfully flat

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/regular-big-cm-flat` (theorem).** Let A be a Noetherian regular local ring and C a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay A-module (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/balanced-big-cm-module). Then C is faithfully flat over A. In particular a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay A-algebra is a faithfully flat A-algebra.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.IsBalancedBigCohenMacaulay.faithfullyFlat_of_isRegularLocalRing`.

**Planet.** Big Cohen–Macaulay algebras over regular rings are flat.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Use the corrected exact Tor segment in E18 and choose the maximal regular sequence by ht P, not by the descending Tor degree i; these are independent indices.

**Proof.**

1. Every finitely generated A-module M has projective dimension at most n + 1 = dim A (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/regular-local-finite-pd), so Tor^A_i(M, C) = 0 for i > dim A.
2. For the descending induction on the Tor degree i > 0, reduce by a prime filtration to M = A/P. Choose a maximal A-regular sequence x_1,…,x_h in P, of length h = ht P, independently of i; it extends to a system of parameters. For N = A/(x_1,…,x_h), the prime P is associated to N: N is Cohen–Macaulay and its localisation at P has dimension zero. Thus A/P embeds in N. The Koszul resolution of N and weak regularity on C give Tor_j(N,C)=0 for all j>0.
3. The exact segment Tor_{i+1}(N/M, C) → Tor_i(M, C) → Tor_i(N, C) has vanishing outer terms (the first by the induction hypothesis), so Tor_i(M, C) = 0: C is flat.
4. mC ≠ C gives faithful flatness for the local ring A (Mathlib Module.FaithfullyFlat).

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/balanced-big-cm-module`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/regular-local-finite-pd`, `DerivedDeRhamCohomology:DD.1/koszul-complex`, `mathlib:IsRegularLocalRing`, `mathlib:Module.FaithfullyFlat`.

**Checks.**

- For A = C a regular local ring the statement is trivial; for A a DVR it says a torsion-free module with C ≠ ϖC is faithfully flat.

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, Remarque 4.2.1 and footnote 7, pp. 86–87. Every Cohen–Macaulay B-algebra is Cohen–Macaulay over the regular subring A and hence faithfully flat over A; the footnote proves flatness by descending induction on Tor using finite projective dimension and prime filtrations.
- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, §0.7, sentence before Théorème 0.7.2, p. 75. Over a regular ring an algebra is Cohen–Macaulay iff it is faithfully flat.

### The splitting obstruction of a unit map

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/splitting-obstruction` (construction).** Let A be a commutative ring and u : A → K a morphism in the derived category D(A) (Mathlib DerivedCategory of A-modules), for example the unit A → B of a finite A-algebra or A → RΓ(X, O_X) for an A-scheme X. Complete it to a distinguished triangle A → K → Q_u → A[1] and let α_u ∈ Hom_{D(A)}(Q_u, A[1]) be the connecting morphism. Then u has a left inverse in D(A) iff α_u = 0 (EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E1/splitting-through-comparison). For a finite injective algebra A → B, Q_u = B/A in degree 0 and α_u is the class of the extension 0 → A → B → B/A → 0 in Ext¹_A(B/A, A); it vanishes iff B is split over A (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/split-algebra).

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.DirectSummand.splittingObstruction`.

**Planet.** Splitting obstruction.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- α_u depends on the triangle only up to automorphisms of Q_u fixing the triangle; its vanishing does not.
- For A → A′ any ring map the derived base change carries α_u to α_{u′} for u′ = u ⊗^L_A A′; vanishing of α_{u′} implies vanishing of α_u only when the base change map on Hom groups is injective, for example for A → A′ faithfully flat and Q_u pseudo-coherent with A Noetherian.

**Construction.**

1. Existence and characterisation of α_u: the triangle obstruction lemma EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E1/splitting-through-comparison.
2. Flat base change: if Q_u is pseudo-coherent (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:P7/pseudo-coherent-object) and A → A′ is flat, Hom_{D(A)}(Q_u, A[1]) ⊗_A A′ ≅ Hom_{D(A′)}(Q_u ⊗^L A′, A′[1]) (Stacks 0A6A), compatibly with α.
3. For A Noetherian and I-adically complete, represent pseudo-coherent Q_u by a bounded-above complex of finite free modules. In each fixed cohomological degree its dual uses only finitely many adjacent finite modules. Artin–Rees exactness of I-adic quotient pro-systems gives {H^j RHom(Q_u,A)/I^m}_m ≅ {H^j RHom(Q_u,A/I^m)}_m as pro-systems, with lim¹ zero. Taking j=1 gives the required Hom inverse limit. This is a degreewise argument extending the finite-module supplier; no uniform assertion for unbounded complexes is needed.

**Inputs.** `EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E1/splitting-through-comparison`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/split-algebra`, `mathlib:DerivedCategory`, `mathlib:CategoryTheory.Abelian.Ext`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:P7/pseudo-coherent-object`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.1/adic-hom-pro-comparison`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.1/adic-hom-lim-one`.

**API.**

- `splittingObstruction` (data): The class α_u ∈ Hom_{D(A)}(cone u, A[1]) attached to u : A → K in D(A).
- `splittingObstruction_eq_zero_iff` (characterisation): α_u = 0 ↔ u has a left inverse in D(A).
- `splittingObstruction_eq_extClass` (compatibility): For a finite injective algebra A → B, α_u is the Yoneda class of 0 → A → B → B/A → 0 in Ext¹_A(B/A, A).
- `splittingObstruction_baseChange` (functoriality): Derived base change along A → A′ maps α_u to α_{u ⊗ A′}.
- `splittingObstruction_flat_baseChange_injective` (other): For A Noetherian, Q_u pseudo-coherent and A → A′ faithfully flat, α_u = 0 iff α_{u ⊗ A′} = 0.
- `splittingObstruction_eq_lim` (other): For A Noetherian and I-adically complete and Q_u pseudo-coherent, α_u = 0 iff its reduction in Hom_{D(A)}(Q_u, A/I^m[1]) vanishes for every m.

**Unit tests.**

- `splittingObstruction_eq_zero_iff_isSplit` (characterisation): For a finite injective A-algebra B, the obstruction of A → B vanishes iff Algebra.IsSplit A B.
- `splittingObstruction_id` (degenerate): For u = id_A the cone is 0 and the obstruction is 0.
- `splittingObstruction_cusp_ne_zero` (computation): For A = K[t², t³] and B = K[t], B/A ≅ K (spanned by t) and the obstruction is a nonzero element of Ext¹_A(K, A); a definition through Hom instead of Ext¹ would give 0.
- `splittingObstruction_int_mul_two` (non-example): For the module map 2 : ℤ → ℤ (not an algebra unit), the class of 0 → ℤ → ℤ → ℤ/2 → 0 is the generator of Ext¹_ℤ(ℤ/2, ℤ) = ℤ/2: injectivity of u does not make α vanish.
- `splittingObstruction_baseChange` (compatibility): For flat A → A′ and pseudo-coherent cone, the obstruction of u ⊗ A′ is the image of α_u under Hom_{D(A)}(Q, A[1]) ⊗_A A′ ≅ Hom_{D(A′)}(Q ⊗ A′, A′[1]).

**Uses.**

- André B §3.3 (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.2/mod-p-power-splitting): the class e of the extension modulo p^m is killed after flat base change and detected by Lemme 1.1.2
- Bhatt proof of Theorem 5.4 (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/obstruction-detection): α_0 is detected modulo p^m and after almost faithfully flat base change
- Bhatt proof of Theorem 6.1 (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-direct-summand-theorem): the same obstruction for A_0 → RΓ(X_0, O)

**Checks.**

- For a nonzero finite free A-algebra B, the element 1 is primitive at every maximal ideal (Nakayama applied to B); therefore B/A is finite locally free, hence projective, and Ext¹_A(B/A,A)=0. In the concrete case ℤ → ℤ[i], the quotient is free of rank one. Global freeness of B/A is not required.

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Proof of Theorem 5.4, p. 10. Introduces the boundary map α_0 ∈ Hom_{A_0}(Q_0, A_0[1]) of the triangle A_0 → B_0 → Q_0 as the obstruction to splitting, and reduces its vanishing to vanishing modulo p^m using p-adic completeness and Lemma 5.3.
- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, §3.3, p. 84. Uses the class e of the extension 0 → Ā → B̄ → B̄/Ā → 0 modulo p^m and shows it vanishes.
- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Proof of Theorem 6.1, p. 11. Runs the same obstruction argument for A_0 → RΓ(X_0, O_{X_0}).

### Reductions of the splitting problem

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/direct-summand-reductions` (theorem).** Let A_0 → B_0 be a finite injective ring map with A_0 Noetherian regular. For every maximal ideal m of A_0 and every field extension K of A_0/m there is a complete Noetherian regular local ring A, faithfully flat over (A_0)_m, with m A = m_A and residue field K; and B = B_0 ⊗_{A_0} A has a prime 𝔮 with 𝔮 ∩ A = 0. A_0 → B_0 splits (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/split-algebra) as soon as, for every m, the finite injective map A → B/𝔮 splits for one such A and one such 𝔮. If A has mixed characteristic (0, p), then B/𝔮 is a domain finite over the normal domain A and there is g ∈ A, outside every minimal prime of pA (so g ≠ 0 in A/p), such that B/𝔮[1/(pg)] is finite étale over A[1/(pg)].

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.DirectSummand.isSplit_of_complete_local_domain`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The completion is taken before passing to the domain quotient: completion need not preserve the domain property (source issue DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay/E10, after PAPER-BHATT-18/E14).
- The domain reduction is needed before choosing g (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay/E9, after PAPER-BHATT-18/E9).

**Proof.**

1. Localise: splitting of a finite algebra over a Noetherian ring is checked at maximal ideals (API Algebra.IsSplit.of_localization_maximal).
2. Complete and extend the residue field: DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.1/completed-residue-extension-exists gives a complete Noetherian local A faithfully flat over (A_0)_m with m A = m_A and residue field K; A is regular because m_A is generated by dim A elements. Splitting descends along faithfully flat maps (Algebra.IsSplit.of_faithfullyFlat_baseChange).
3. Domain quotient: A → B is injective and integral, so some prime 𝔮 of B lies over 0 (lying over for the minimal prime 0 of the domain A, DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/regular-local-domain); a splitting of A → B/𝔮 composed with B → B/𝔮 splits A → B.
4. Discriminant: B/𝔮 is a domain finite over the normal domain A of characteristic 0, so Frac(B/𝔮)/Frac(A) is finite separable and A → B/𝔮 is generically étale (Mathlib Algebra.Etale). The non-étale locus in Spec A[1/p] has closure Z of codimension ≥ 1 whose codimension-one components are height-one primes not containing p; a minimal prime P of pA has height one and contains p, so P ∉ Z, and prime avoidance gives g in the ideal of Z outside every minimal prime of pA.

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/split-algebra`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.1/completed-residue-extension-exists`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.1/faithful-flat-algebra-completion`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/regular-local-domain`, `mathlib:IsRegularRing`, `mathlib:IsRegularLocalRing`, `mathlib:Algebra.Etale`, `mathlib:Localization.AtPrime`.

**Checks.**

- The reductions preserve the conclusion in all three characteristic types: equal characteristic 0, equal characteristic p and mixed characteristic (0, p).

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Proof of Proposition 5.2, first sentences, p. 8. Replaces A_0 by faithfully flat regular local extensions, making it complete with algebraically closed residue field.
- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Proof of Theorem 5.4, first paragraph, p. 10. Reduces to a Noetherian regular local A_0 of mixed characteristic and chooses g coprime to p with A_0 → B_0 finite étale after inverting pg.

### Trace splittings and the characteristic-zero direct summand theorem

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/trace-retraction` (theorem).** Let R be a Noetherian integrally closed domain and S a domain containing R and finite over R, with d = [Frac(S) : Frac(R)]. ### The direct summand theorem in dimension at most two

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/low-dimension-direct-summand` (theorem).** Let A be a Noetherian regular local ring of dimension at most 2 and A → S a finite injective ring map. Then A → S splits. More precisely, for a domain S finite over A containing A, the reflexive hull S** = Hom_A(Hom_A(S, A), A) is a finite free A-algebra containing S, so A → S** splits and hence A → S splits.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.isSplinter_of_ringKrullDim_le_two`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Any characteristic. The statement is the case André B §0.2 calls easy: finite flat extensions split, and in dimension ≤ 2 normal (reflexive) finite extensions are flat.

**Proof.**

1. Reduce to S a domain containing A (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/direct-summand-reductions, the domain step).

3. **Checks.**

- In dimension 1 (A a DVR) every torsion-free finite A-module is free, so every finite extension domain splits.
- For dimension 3 in mixed characteristic the statement is Heitmann's theorem (André B §0.2), here a special case of DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/direct-summand-theorem.

**Sources.**

## Layer DS.1: Hochster's purity criteria and the characteristic-p theorem

Hochster's two purity criteria (André B Annexe A.3) convert weak information into splittings. The first uses a contracting endomorphism σ (Frobenius in characteristic p, x_i ↦ x_i^q on V⟦x⟧) to turn any nonzero R-linear map S → R into a retraction; with σ = Frobenius it proves the direct summand theorem in characteristic p in a few lines. The second says pure maps out of complete local rings split, by Matlis duality. Both statements need the corrections recorded at the end of this document: the first needs the separating element to be regular (or zero), and the second is false without completeness, by an example of Datta and Murayama.

**Targets of the layer.**

Hochster's criteria turning purity or a nonzero dual into a splitting (André B Annexe A.3, corrected). Targets:

- **Hochster's endomorphism criterion**: for a local ring R, separated for a regular (or zero) element r ∈ m, with a local endomorphism σ making R free over σ(R) and ∩σ^j(m)R ⊆ rR, and an extension S on which σ extends injectively, Hom_R(S, R) ≠ 0 implies R → S splits.
- **Pure maps out of complete Noetherian local rings split** (by Matlis duality); completeness cannot be weakened to excellent Henselian (Datta–Murayama).
- **The direct summand theorem in characteristic p**: every Noetherian regular 𝔽_p-algebra is a splinter.

### Hochster's endomorphism criterion for splitting

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.1/endomorphism-retraction-criterion` (theorem).** Let (R, m) be a nonzero local ring, not necessarily Noetherian, and r ∈ m such that ∩_{n≥1} r^n R = 0 and either r = 0 or r is a nonzerodivisor on R. Let σ : R → R be a local ring endomorphism such that R is a free module over σ(R) and ∩_{j≥0} σ^j(m)R ⊆ rR. Let R ⊆ S be a ring extension and τ : S → S an injective ring endomorphism restricting to σ on R. If Hom_R(S, R) ≠ 0, then R → S splits (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/split-algebra).

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.DirectSummand.isSplit_of_dual_ne_zero_of_endomorphism`.

**Planet.** Hochster's endomorphism criterion.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The hypothesis 'r = 0 or r a nonzerodivisor' is added to André's statement, which only asks R to be r-adically separated: dividing a functional by a power of r needs it (source issue DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay/E2, after PAPER-ANDRE-18-B/E4).
- The linear form in the proof is defined on the finite free summand M, not on N (misprint DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay/E1, after PAPER-ANDRE-18-B/E2).

**Proof.**

1. Take λ ∈ Hom_R(S, R) nonzero. Since ∩ r^nR = 0 and r is regular (or zero), divide λ by the largest power of r dividing all its values, then precompose with multiplication by some s ∈ S, to get λ(1) ∉ rR.
2. Then λ(1) ∉ σ^j(m)R for some j, by the hypothesis on ∩σ^j(m)R.
3. R is free over σ^j(R), a local ring with maximal ideal σ^j(m); choose a finite free σ^j(R)-summand M of R with λ(1) ∈ M ∖ σ^j(m)M and, by Nakayama, a σ^j(R)-linear form μ on M with μ(λ(1)) = 1, extended by 0 on a complement.
4. μ ∘ λ restricted to τ^j(S) is a σ^j(R)-linear retraction of σ^j(R) → τ^j(S); transporting along the isomorphisms σ^j : R → σ^j(R) and τ^j : S → τ^j(S) gives an R-linear retraction of R → S.

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/split-algebra`, `mathlib:IsLocalRing`, `mathlib:Module.Free`, `mathlib:Module.Dual`.

**Checks.**

- With r = 0 and σ = Frobenius this is the characteristic-p splitting criterion (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.1/frobenius-direct-summand).
- With R = V⟦x_2, …, x_d⟧ for a complete DVR V with uniformiser r and σ(x_i) = x_i^q (q ≥ 2), R is free over σ(R) with monomial basis of exponents < q and ∩σ^j(m)R = rR; this is the case used for R → R⁺ (Hochster 1983 6.1, André A.3 Applications).

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, Annexe A.3, Proposition A.3.1, implication (c) ⇒ (b) and its proof, pp. 90–91. For a local ring R, r-adically separated, with a local endomorphism σ making R free over σ(R) and ∩σ^m(m)R ⊆ rR, and an extension S on which σ extends injectively, a nonzero element of Hom_R(S, R) yields an R-linear retraction, by producing a retraction of σ^m(R) → σ^m(S) and transporting it.
- Melvin Hochster, *Contracted ideals from integral extensions of regular rings*, §1, Theorem 2, statement p. 31 and first characteristic-p proof p. 32. Historical special case: for a module-finite domain extension of a complete regular local ring of characteristic p, a nonzero dual functional and iterated Frobenius contradict a bad monomial relation. The abstract endomorphism criterion here is André Proposition A.3.1, not a theorem stated on Hochster p. 30.

### Pure maps out of complete local rings split

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.1/complete-pure-split` (theorem).** Let (R, m) be a complete Noetherian local ring and S a commutative R-algebra such that R → S is pure (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map). Then R → S splits (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/split-algebra). No finiteness, integrality or endomorphism of S is needed.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.isSplit_of_isPure_of_isAdicComplete`.

**Planet.** Pure maps from complete local rings split.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Completeness is essential: Datta–Murayama Proposition 5.4.1(iii) gives an excellent Henselian DVR V of characteristic p with Hom_V(V^{1/p}, V) = 0, so V → V^{1/p} is faithfully flat, hence pure, but not split. This corrects André B Proposition A.3.1, implication (a) ⇒ (b) for regular R and integral S, and its application to V⟦T⟧ → V⟦T⟧⁺ for an arbitrary DVR V (source issues DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay/E3 and E4, after PAPER-ANDRE-18-B/E5 and E21).

**Proof.**

1. Let E be the injective hull of the residue field (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/residue-injective-hull). Purity makes E = E ⊗_R R → E ⊗_R S injective.
2. E is injective, so restriction Hom_R(E ⊗_R S, E) → Hom_R(E, E) is surjective.
3. Hom_R(E, E) = R̂ = R for complete R (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/matlis-duality), and by adjunction Hom_R(E ⊗_R S, E) = Hom_R(S, Hom_R(E, E)) = Hom_R(S, R); under these identifications the restriction map is evaluation at 1.
4. Hence evaluation at 1 on Hom_R(S, R) is surjective and R → S splits.

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/split-algebra`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/residue-injective-hull`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/matlis-duality`, `mathlib:IsAdicComplete`.

**Checks.**

- R = K a field: every nonzero K-algebra splits.
- The Datta–Murayama DVR is a non-example showing that 'complete' cannot be replaced by 'excellent Henselian'.

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, Annexe A.3, Proposition A.3.1, implication (a) ⇒ (c) and its proof, p. 91. Proves nonvanishing of the dual for regular R and integral pure S through top local cohomology and local duality; for complete R the duality step gives the splitting directly, which is the corrected form used here.

- Melvin Hochster, *Local cohomology (Math 615 lecture notes, Winter 2011)*, §5, Theorem 5.1, p. 19 (End(E)=R̂), and Theorem 5.4, p. 21 (Matlis duality). Matlis duality over a complete local ring, with End(E) = R.

## Layer DS.3: Perfectoid covers of regular local rings and Bhatt's proof

Bhatt's proof works for every complete regular local ring of mixed characteristic, ramified or not, so together with DS.0 and DS.1 it proves the direct summand theorem for all regular rings. Its inputs are a perfectoid cover of A_0 (constructed by hand in the ramified case through the Cohen presentation W(k)⟦X⟧/(p − f)), Bhatt's root extension of the discriminant (PerfectoidQuotients Q3), almost purity on the annuli A_∞⟨p^n/g⟩ where g divides p^n, and the quantitative Riemann extension theorem (PerfectoidSpacesPartII RA.1). The obstruction-detection and limit nodes are stated for an arbitrary perfect cone Q_0, which covers the finite case Q_0 = B_0/A_0 and the derived case of DS.7 at once.

**Targets of the layer.**

Bhatt's proof of the direct summand theorem for every complete regular local ring of mixed characteristic, ramified or not, replacing the Abhyankar lemma by the quantitative Riemann extension theorem of PerfectoidSpacesPartII RA.1. Targets:

- **Cohen presentations** of complete regular local rings: W(k)⟦x_2, …, x_d⟧ when p ∉ m², W(k)⟦X_1, …, X_d⟧/(p − f) when p ∈ m².
- **Perfectoid covers** A_0 → A of p-torsion-free regular local rings by integral perfectoid algebras over Q_p(p^{1/p^∞})^, almost faithfully flat (Tor almost zero, detection of nonzero modules), faithfully flat when unramified (Bhatt Proposition 5.2).

### Cohen presentations of complete regular local rings of mixed characteristic

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/regular-local-cohen-presentation` (theorem).** Let (A_0, m, k) be a complete Noetherian regular local ring of mixed characteristic (0, p) and dimension d, with k perfect, and fix a continuous local coefficient map ι : W(k) → A_0 inducing the identity of k (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.1/cohen-coefficient-map). All presentations are W(k)-algebra maps for this ι. (1) Unramified case p ∉ m²: A_0 ≅ W(k)⟦x_2, …, x_d⟧. (2) Ramified case p ∈ m²: for any generators x_1, …, x_d of m, the surjection ψ : W(k)⟦X_1, …, X_d⟧ → A_0, X_i ↦ x_i, has kernel generated by one element p − f with f ∈ (p, X_1, …, X_d)², f having no constant term and p ∤ f.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.DirectSummand.regularLocal_cohenPresentation`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Only this regular-local corollary is planned here; the coefficient ring and its presentations are DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra R03.1's.

**Proof.**

1. (1) p is part of a regular system of parameters (p, x_2, …, x_d); the map W(k)⟦X_2, …, X_d⟧ → A_0 is surjective (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.1/cohen-series-presentation) between regular local rings of the same dimension, hence an isomorphism (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/regular-surjection-kernel).
2. (2) ψ is a surjection of regular local rings of dimensions d + 1 and d, so its kernel is generated by one element h that is part of a regular system of parameters (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/regular-surjection-kernel). Since p ∈ m² is in the image of (p, X)², p ≡ f′ modulo ker ψ for some f′ ∈ (p, X)², and h can be taken as p − f with f ∈ (p, X)².
3. Write the initial generator as p − f_0 with f_0 ∈ (p,X)² and constant term c ∈ p²W(k). Put u = 1 − c/p, a unit of W(k), and f = (f_0 − c)/u. Multiplying the generator by u⁻¹ gives p − f; now f has zero constant term and is still in (p,X)². If p divides f then p − f is p times a unit, forcing characteristic p, contrary to mixed characteristic.

**Inputs.** `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.1/cohen-coefficient-map`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.1/cohen-series-presentation`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/regular-surjection-kernel`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/regular-local-domain`, `mathlib:WittVector`, `mathlib:MvPowerSeries`, `mathlib:IsRegularLocalRing`.

**Checks.**

- A_0 = ℤ_p[√p]⟦x_2⟧ ≅ ℤ_p⟦X_1, X_2⟧/(p − X_1²) is a ramified example with f = X_1².

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Proof of Proposition 5.2, pp. 8–9. In the unramified case A_0 ≅ W⟦x_2, …, x_d⟧; in the ramified case a surjection from W⟦x_1, …, x_d⟧ has kernel generated by p − f with f ∈ (p, x_1, …, x_d)² a power series without constant term and not divisible by p.

### Perfectoid covers of regular local rings

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/regular-local-perfectoid-cover` (construction).** Let A_0 be a p-torsion-free Noetherian regular local ring with residue characteristic p, and K = Q_p(p^{1/p^∞})^ with t = p. ### The perfectoid cover is almost faithfully flat

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/perfectoid-cover-almost-faithfully-flat` (theorem).** For A_0 → A as in DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/regular-local-perfectoid-cover and every A_0-module M: (a) Tor_i^{A_0}(M, A) is almost zero (killed by every p^{1/p^n}) for i > 0; (b) if M ⊗_{A_0} A is almost zero then M = 0. In the unramified case A_0 → A is faithfully flat.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.DirectSummand.perfectoidCover_almostFaithfullyFlat`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Almost mathematics is with respect to p^{1/p^∞}A, which equals π^{1/p^∞}A and the radical of pA.

**Proof.**

1. Factor A_0 → A′ → A_{!!} → A, where A_{!!} is the left adjoint of almostification applied to A (PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-algebra-lower-shriek-shriek); A_{!!} → A is an injective almost isomorphism, A_{!!} is p-adically complete and p-torsion-free.
2. Formation of (·)_{!!} commutes with reduction modulo p^m, and A′ → A is almost faithfully flat modulo π, so A′ → A_{!!} is faithfully flat modulo p^m (PerfectoidSpaces:P0/lower-shriek-shriek-faithful-flatness); by Bhatt's Proposition 5.1 (DerivedDeRhamCohomology:DD.1/ordinary-flatness-from-complete-flatness) A_0 → A_{!!} is faithfully flat. This gives (a).
3. (b): reduce by faithful flatness and a filtration to M = A_0/I; then π^{1/p^n} ∈ IA_{!!} for all n, so p = π^p v^{-1} ∈ I^{p^n}A_{!!} and, by faithful flatness, p ∈ I^{p^n} for all n. Krull's intersection theorem (Mathlib Ideal.iInf_pow_eq_bot_of_isLocalRing) forces I = A_0 since p ≠ 0.

### Detection of the splitting obstruction after perfectoid base change

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/obstruction-detection` (theorem).** Let A_0 be a complete Noetherian regular local ring of mixed characteristic (0, p), Q_0 a perfect complex of A_0-modules and α_0 ∈ Hom_{D(A_0)}(Q_0, A_0[1]); put P = RHom_{A_0}(Q_0, A_0). **Named signature.** `TauCeti.DirectSummand.splittingObstruction_detect`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- For the direct summand theorem Q_0 = B_0/A_0 in degree 0, which is perfect because A_0 is regular (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/regular-local-finite-pd); for the derived theorem Q_0 is the cone of A_0 → RΓ(X_0, O) (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/proper-cohomology-perfect).
- m ≥ 3 is used so that p²g ≠ 0 in A_0/p^m.

**Proof.**

1. For P = RHom(Q_0,A_0), the triangle for multiplication by p^m gives 0 → H¹(P)/p^m → H¹(P ⊗^L A_0/p^m) → H²(P)[p^m] → 0. Transitions on the last term are multiplication by p. The p-primary torsion in finite H²(P) is bounded, so that tower is pro-zero. This proves the needed complex comparison directly from finite cohomology, rather than attributing a complex theorem to the finite-module Hom supplier.
2. Krull's intersection theorem for the finite module H¹(P) gives m ≥ 3 with α_0/p^m ≠ 0, so Ann(α_0/p^m) ≠ A_0/p^m; Krull again gives k with p²g ∉ (Ann(α_0/p^m))^{p^k}.
3. Choose a bounded finite free representative of Q_0. Tensor its dual modulo p^m along the two almost faithfully flat maps. The finite filtration, or its Tor spectral sequence, identifies cohomology after tensoring up to an almost isomorphism. This proves the almost Hom comparison for this perfect Q_0; arbitrary complexes are not covered by a module-only flatness assertion.
4. Annihilators therefore satisfy pg ∉ (Ann(α_∞/p^m))^{p^k}, losing one power of p in passing to almost mathematics, hence (pg)^{1/p^k} ∉ Ann(α_∞/p^m).

### Almost splitting on the annuli A_∞⟨p^n/g⟩

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/annular-almost-splitting` (theorem).** In the setting of DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/obstruction-detection, let A_0 → B_0 be finite and injective with B_0[1/(pg)] finite étale over A_0[1/(pg)], and let A_∞⟨p^n/g⟩ be the annular tower of A_∞ (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/annular-tower, with t = p). **Named signature.** `TauCeti.DirectSummand.annular_almostSplit`.

**Planet.** Annular almost splitting.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- g divides p^n in A_∞⟨p^n/g⟩, so after inverting p the base change of A_0 → B_0 is finite étale.

**Proof.**

1. Since g | p^n in A_∞⟨p^n/g⟩, the algebra B_0 ⊗ A_∞⟨p^n/g⟩[1/p] is finite étale over the perfectoid Tate ring A_∞⟨p^n/g⟩[1/p].
2. ### From the annuli back to A_∞: the limit argument

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/obstruction-limit` (theorem).** In the setting of DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/obstruction-detection, put Q_∞ = Q_0 ⊗^L_{A_0} A_∞. **Named signature.** `TauCeti.DirectSummand.splittingObstruction_almostZero_of_annuli`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Q_0 is perfect, so RHom_{A_∞}(Q_∞, A_∞⟨p^n/g⟩/p^m) = K ⊗^L_{A_∞/p^m} A_∞⟨p^n/g⟩/p^m with K = RHom_{A_∞}(Q_∞, A_∞/p^m).

**Proof.**

1. Represent K by a bounded complex of finite projective A_∞/p^m-modules, using perfection of Q_0. RA.1’s general quantitative Riemann comparison applies to the coefficient modules. The bound may depend on the finite amplitude; no statement uniform over all complexes is used.
2. Consequently each relevant cohomology tower, including degree zero, is almost-pro-isomorphic to a constant tower. The degree-zero tower has almost-zero lim¹ by RA.0’s derived-limit theorem; the resulting map Hom(Q_∞,A_∞/p^m[1]) → lim_n Hom(Q_∞,A_∞⟨p^n/g⟩/p^m[1]) is an almost isomorphism for (pg)^{1/p^∞}.

## Layer DS.4: Big Cohen–Macaulay algebras

Balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebras for every Noetherian local ring. In mixed characteristic André's construction gives an *almost* Cohen–Macaulay algebra D = B° with π = ϖg, whose colon modules are killed by all roots of π; Hochster's partial algebra modifications turn it into an honest one. The mechanism: an algebra on which a system of parameters is weakly regular exists iff no finite sequence of partial modifications is bad (Hochster 2002 Theorem 4.2), and maps into D[1/π] extend along partial modifications with denominators bounded by a fixed root of π (Hochster 2002 Lemma 5.1), so badness would force a root of π into m_B D. The same double-sequence argument gives weak functoriality from squares of almost Cohen–Macaulay algebras. Equal characteristic is due to Hochster and Huneke: in characteristic p the absolute integral closure R⁺ is itself balanced big Cohen–Macaulay, and characteristic 0 reduces to it.

**Targets of the layer.**

Existence of balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebras for every Noetherian local ring, and weak functoriality from almost Cohen–Macaulay squares. Targets:

- **Almost Cohen–Macaulay algebras** for (B, x, π^{1/p^∞}) (André B Définition 4.1.1(3)) and their **criteria** by almost isomorphism with faithfully flat algebras (Lemme 4.1.3).
- **Hochster's pointed partial algebra modifications** (E21–E22; finite-witness comparison G4), his **modification criterion** (a big Cohen–Macaulay square exists iff no double sequence is bad) and his **key lemma** extending maps with controlled denominators.
- **Almost Cohen–Macaulay ⇒ balanced big Cohen–Macaulay** (Proposition 4.1.2) and **weak functoriality from almost Cohen–Macaulay squares** (Proposition 4.4.1).
- **Reductions** (completion, minimal primes, reduced quotient, perfect residue field).
- **Existence**: André in mixed characteristic (Théorème 0.7.1), Hochster–Huneke in characteristic p (R⁺ is balanced big Cohen–Macaulay) and in equal characteristic 0.

### Almost Cohen–Macaulay algebras

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.4/almost-big-cm-algebra` (definition).** Let (B, m_B) be a Noetherian local ring of residue characteristic p and dimension d, x = (x_1, …, x_d) a system of parameters, and D a B-algebra containing a nonzerodivisor π with a compatible system of p-power roots π^{1/p^h} (π^{1/p^0} = π, (π^{1/p^{h+1}})^p = π^{1/p^h}). Put J = Σ_h π^{1/p^h}D. D is almost Cohen–Macaulay for (B, x, π^{1/p^∞}) if J ⊄ m_B D and J annihilates ((x_1, …, x_i)D :_D x_{i+1}) / (x_1, …, x_i)D for every 0 ≤ i < d.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.IsAlmostCohenMacaulay`.

**Planet.** Almost Cohen–Macaulay algebra.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Almostness is with respect to the ideal J generated by the roots of π, which need not be a valuation ideal: in André's application π = ϖg (source footnote 6).
- The roots are data; the condition depends on the root system only through J.

**Construction.**

1. The condition J ⊄ m_B D says that D is not almost equal to m_B D in the setup (D, J).
2. If D is big Cohen–Macaulay for x and π^{1/p^h} ∉ m_B D for some h, D is almost Cohen–Macaulay; conversely almost Cohen–Macaulay with J = D (π a unit) is big Cohen–Macaulay.

### Hochster's partial algebra modifications

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.4/partial-algebra-modification` (construction).** Let (R,m) be Noetherian local, x a system of parameters, and (M,e) an R-module with a distinguished element e. For 0 ≤ k < length x, a relation x_{k+1}u_{k+1} = Σ_{j≤k} x_j u_j in M, and N ≥ 1, set M[X] = M ⊗_R R[X_1,…,X_k], F = u_{k+1} − Σ_{j≤k} x_j(e X_j), and M′ = M[X]_{≤N}/F·R[X]_{≤N−1}. Constants give a pointed map (M,e) → (M′,e′). A finite sequence starting at an algebra T carries e=1 and is bad when the final image of e lies in m times the final module. A double sequence along a local map (R,m) → (S,n) first modifies over R and then over S after tensoring, carrying 1 ⊗ e; badness is tested against n at the end.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.PartialModification`.

**Planet.** Partial algebra modification.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The distinguished element supplies the coefficient of each formal variable. An unpointed arbitrary module has no element 1. The notation in Hochster §4.1 p. 8 and Heitmann–Ma §1.2 p. 3 suppresses this datum (E22).
- Only scalar polynomials multiply F; there is no multiplication on M. This also repairs André’s coefficient-module slip E11.
- At a fixed relation the N-colimit is M[X]/F·R[X], as a module. For an algebra T this need not equal T[X]/(F·T[X]); E21 gives a counterexample. The comparison of finite bad witnesses with full algebra modifications is the separate gap G4.

**Construction.**

1. F is now an element of M[X] of degree at most one. Multiplication by polynomials of degree at most N−1 has image in degree at most N, so the quotient and its pointed constant map are well defined.
2. The class of F vanishes, so the image of u_{k+1} equals Σ x_j times the class of eX_j. Pointed maps compatible with the relation induce maps of these quotients.
3. Taking the filtered colimit over N gives exactly M[X]/F·R[X]. Do not infer an algebra structure or the full modification criterion from this fixed-relation colimit; the required finite-witness comparison is G4.

**Inputs.** `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/systems-of-parameters`, `mathlib:IsLocalRing`, `mathlib:LinearMap.lTensor`.

**API.**

- `TauCeti.PartialModification` (constructor): The pointed module (M′,e′) attached to (M,e,k,relation,N), with its pointed R-linear constant map.
- `TauCeti.PartialModification.mem_span` (characterisation): The image of u_{k+1} in M′ lies in (x_1, …, x_k)M′.
- `TauCeti.PartialModification.lift` (universal-property): For α:M → T[1/c] with α(e)=1, evaluation at solutions of α(u_{k+1})=Σ x_jv_j induces an extension M′ → T[1/c] preserving the distinguished element. The key lemma supplies denominator bounds.
- `TauCeti.ModificationSequence.IsBad` (data): Badness means that the transported distinguished element, initially 1 in T, belongs to the maximal ideal times the final module; tensoring for a double sequence transports e to 1 ⊗ e.
- `TauCeti.PartialModification.fixedRelationColimit` (other): The N-colimit at a fixed pointed relation is M[X]/F·R[X] as an R-module. An algebra structure on an iterated universal construction requires the additional comparison G4.
- `TauCeti.PartialModification.map` (functoriality): A pointed R-linear map carrying each u_j to the corresponding u_j induces a map on partial quotients; identity and composition laws hold. Quotient maps and lifts are determined by their values on the degree-bounded monomial generators.

**Unit tests.**

- `partialModification_trivialises` (characterisation): In the pointed quotient M′, the image of u_{k+1} equals Σ_{j≤k} x_j[eX_j], hence belongs to (x_1,…,x_k)M′.

### Hochster's modification criterion for (weakly functorial) big Cohen–Macaulay algebras

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.4/modification-criterion` (theorem).** Let (R, m) → (S, n) be a local homomorphism of Noetherian local rings with systems of parameters x of R and y of S, and T an R-algebra. **Named signature.** `TauCeti.exists_bigCM_square_iff_not_bad`.

**Planet.** Hochster's modification criterion.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The intended criterion is stated in Hochster Theorem 4.2 and Heitmann–Ma Theorem 1.6. Its comparison with the explicitly pointed quotient in the preceding node is G4, not a consequence of taking a fixed-relation N-colimit.

**Proof.**

1. Construct B′ as the direct limit of all finite sequences of algebra modifications of T over R, and C′ as the direct limit of algebra modifications of S ⊗_R B′ over S; x and y are weakly regular on B′ and C′ by construction.
2. The nontrivial finite-witness comparison must show that any bad witness in the full algebra-modification construction is realised by a finite pointed partial sequence (and similarly for double sequences). This is G4. The fixed-relation colimit claim is false (E21) and cannot prove this step. Conversely a map to a big Cohen–Macaulay algebra extends along partial modifications by evaluating the formal variables, so no such sequence is bad.

### Hochster's key lemma: extending maps along partial modifications

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.4/hochster-key-lemma` (theorem).** Let R be a local ring, (M,e) a pointed R-module, T an R-algebra, c ∈ T a nonzerodivisor and α : M → T[1/c] an R-linear map with α(e)=1 and α(M) ⊆ c^{-N}T. Let M → M′ be a partial algebra modification with respect to x_1, …, x_{k+1} with degree bound D. Suppose that for every relation x_{k+1}t_{k+1} = Σ_{j≤k} x_j t_j with t_j ∈ T one has c·t_{k+1} ∈ (x_1, …, x_k)T. Then α extends to an R-linear β : M′ → T[1/c] with β(M′) ⊆ c^{-(ND+D+N)}T. The extension satisfies β(e′)=1 for the transported distinguished element.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.PartialModification.extend_of_colon_killed`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The map is pointed from the outset. With F=u_{k+1}−Σ x_j(eX_j), the evaluation argument kills F because α(e)=1; this is the convention required by E22.

**Proof.**

1. Apply α to the defining relation: x_{k+1}v_{k+1} = Σ x_j v_j with v_j = α(u_j) ∈ c^{-N}T, so x_{k+1}(c^N v_{k+1}) = Σ x_j (c^N v_j) and the hypothesis gives c^{N+1}v_{k+1} = Σ x_j w_j with w_j ∈ T.

### Almost Cohen–Macaulay algebras give balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebras

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.4/almost-cm-to-big-cm` (theorem).** Let B be a Noetherian local ring of residue characteristic p. **Named signature.** `TauCeti.exists_balancedBigCM_of_almostCM`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The proof gives maps M_ℓ → π^{-1/p^h}D ⊂ D[1/π] depending on the choices, not a B-algebra map from the colimit to D: André's parenthetical claim that B → D factors through the big Cohen–Macaulay algebra is dropped (source issue DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay/E12, after PAPER-ANDRE-18-B/E19).

**Proof.**

1. Fix a finite sequence B = M_1 → ⋯ → M_ℓ of partial modifications of degree ≤ n and h ≥ 0. ### Hochster–Huneke: R⁺ is big Cohen–Macaulay in characteristic p

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.4/char-p-absolute-integral-closure` (theorem).** Let (R, m) be a Noetherian local domain of characteristic p > 0 that is a homomorphic image of a Gorenstein local ring (for example a complete local domain), and R⁺ its absolute integral closure (the integral closure of R in an algebraic closure of Frac R). Then H^i_m(R⁺) = 0 for i < dim R, and every system of parameters of R is a regular sequence on R⁺; R⁺ ≠ mR⁺, so R⁺ is a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay R-algebra.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.absoluteIntegralClosure_isBalancedBigCM_of_charP`.

**Planet.** Hochster–Huneke theorem on R⁺.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Hochster–Huneke prove it for excellent R; Huneke–Lyubeznik for homomorphic images of Gorenstein local rings, with a simpler proof; complete local domains satisfy both.

**Proof.**

1. Apply the strong form of Huneke–Lyubeznik Theorem 2.1 to every finite intermediate domain R′ ⊆ R⁺: for each i<dim R there is a finite R′-extension inside R⁺ killing H^i_m(R′). Finite extensions have the same dimension; the theorem’s stated finite-extension form supplies this step. Its proof uses Frobenius, local duality over the Gorenstein ring and the equational Lemma 2.2.
2. Every class in the filtered colimit of H^i_m(R′) thus dies at a later finite stage, so H^i_m(R⁺)=0. This uses local cohomology commuting with filtered colimits and its Čech comparison; merely killing the classes coming from R would not suffice.
3. Corollary 2.3(b): induction on j shows x_1, …, x_j is regular on R⁺, using the local cohomology vanishing and the identification of the colon modules.
4. R⁺ ≠ mR⁺ because R⁺ is integral over R (lying over).

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/balanced-big-cm-module`, `mathlib:localCohomology`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/local-cohomology-depth-and-top-tensor`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/matlis-duality`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/kunz-and-henselian-f-finite`, `mathlib:AlgebraicClosure`, `mathlib:integralClosure`, `mathlib:frobenius`.

**Checks.**

**Sources.**

- Craig Huneke and Gennady Lyubeznik, *Absolute integral closure in positive characteristic*, Theorem 2.1, Lemma 2.2 and Corollary 2.3, pp. 2–6. ## Layer DS.5: The direct summand theorem and flat domination

The final theorems for regular rings. The direct summand theorem is stated once, with both mixed-characteristic proofs as alternative routes. Flat domination of finite covers follows from DS.4 and the flatness of balanced big Cohen–Macaulay algebras over regular rings, glued over all maximal ideals by Chase's theorem on products of flat modules over Noetherian rings. Purity of R → R⁺ and the complete pure-to-split theorem give a splitting of R → R⁺ for complete regular local rings; contraction of ideals from integral extensions and the monomial theorem are immediate consequences.

**Targets of the layer.**

The final theorems for regular rings. Targets:

- **The direct summand theorem**: every Noetherian regular ring is a splinter (André Conjecture 0.1.1, proved in the paper, Bhatt Theorem 1.1), with André's and Bhatt's mixed characteristic proofs as alternative routes.
- **Products of flat modules over a Noetherian ring are flat** (needed for the global product).
- **Flat domination of finite covers**: every finite extension of a Noetherian regular ring is dominated by a faithfully flat algebra (Théorème 0.7.2, corrected to dominating components).

### The direct summand theorem

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/direct-summand-theorem` (theorem).** Every Noetherian regular ring is a splinter: for a Noetherian regular ring R and a commutative R-algebra S that is finite and faithful as an R-module, R → S admits an R-linear retraction (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/split-algebra). Equivalently, every finite extension of R is pure, and S is a generator of the category of R-modules.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.isSplinter_of_isRegularRing`.

**Planet.** Direct summand theorem.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The retraction is R-linear and is not required to be multiplicative.
- The theorem is stated once; it has two proofs in mixed characteristic, André's and Bhatt's, recorded as alternative routes in the proof steps. Both routes use the same reductions.

**Proof.**

1. Reduce to A a complete regular local ring with algebraically closed residue field and S a domain finite over A (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/direct-summand-reductions).
2. A contains ℚ: trace splitting (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/trace-retraction). A has characteristic p: Frobenius (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.1/frobenius-direct-summand).
3. Mixed characteristic, Bhatt's route: DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/bhatt-direct-summand, valid for ramified and unramified A.
4. Mixed characteristic, André's route: A ⊆ S is dominated by a faithfully flat algebra (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/finite-cover-flat-domination, built from André's big Cohen–Macaulay algebras over the unramified ring W(k)⟦T⟧ of DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.4/mixed-char-big-cm-existence); then A → S is pure as a left factor of a faithfully flat map, hence split since S is finite (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map). For A unramified this is also André's direct theorem DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.2/andre-unramified-direct-summand.

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/splinter`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/direct-summand-reductions`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/trace-retraction`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.1/frobenius-direct-summand`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/bhatt-direct-summand`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/finite-cover-flat-domination`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.2/andre-unramified-direct-summand`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map`, `mathlib:IsRegularRing`.

**Checks.**

- Dimension ≤ 2 (any characteristic) is DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/low-dimension-direct-summand; dimension 3 in mixed characteristic is Heitmann's theorem, a special case.
- Regularity cannot be weakened to normality: in characteristic p or mixed characteristic there are normal non-splinters, and K[x, y]/(xy) is a reduced non-splinter (André B §0.2).

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, Conjecture 0.1.1, §0.2 and Théorème 0.2.1, pp. 71–72. States Hochster's direct summand conjecture for Noetherian regular rings and proves it, the general case following from the case W(k)⟦T_1, …, T_n⟧ by Hochster's reduction.
- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, §0.7, after Théorème 0.7.2, p. 75. Flat domination of finite covers gives purity of R → S, hence the conjecture again by a detour.
- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 5.4, pp. 1 and 10. Proves the direct summand theorem for every regular A_0 by a proof avoiding the perfectoid Abhyankar lemma.

### Products of flat modules over a Noetherian ring are flat

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/flat-products-noetherian` (theorem).** Let R be a Noetherian ring (more generally a coherent ring) and (M_i)_{i∈I} any family of flat R-modules. Then ∏_i M_i is flat. If moreover each M_i is an R-algebra and for every maximal ideal m of R some M_i satisfies mM_i ≠ M_i, then ∏_i M_i is faithfully flat.

**Named signature.** `Module.Flat.pi_of_isNoetherianRing`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Tau Ceti has the finite case (Module.Flat.pi); the infinite case needs coherence (Chase's theorem), and fails over non-coherent rings.

**Proof.**

1. Flatness is tested on finitely generated ideals J: J is finitely presented since R is Noetherian, so J ⊗ ∏ M_i ≅ ∏ (J ⊗ M_i) (tensor product with a finitely presented module commutes with arbitrary products).
2. J ⊗ M_i → M_i is injective for every i, hence so is the product map.
3. Faithful flatness: m(∏ M_i) ⊆ ∏ mM_i ≠ ∏ M_i when some factor has mM_i ≠ M_i, and flat with mT ≠ T for all maximal m is faithfully flat (Mathlib Module.FaithfullyFlat).

**Inputs.** `tauceti:Module.Flat.pi`, `mathlib:Module.Flat`, `mathlib:Module.FaithfullyFlat`, `mathlib:Module.FinitePresentation`, `mathlib:IsNoetherianRing`.

**Checks.**

- For I finite this is Tau Ceti's Module.Flat.pi.
- Over a non-coherent ring the statement fails (Chase); the Noetherian hypothesis is used exactly in the finite presentation of ideals.

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, §4.3, p. 87. Uses that, R being Noetherian, the product T = ∏ T_{m,i} of flat R-algebras is still flat over R.

### Finite covers of regular rings are dominated by faithfully flat algebras

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/finite-cover-flat-domination` (theorem).** Let R be a Noetherian regular ring and R ⊆ S a finite extension. There is a commutative S-algebra T such that the composite R → S → T is faithfully flat.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.exists_faithfullyFlat_of_finite_of_isRegularRing`.

**Planet.** Flat domination of finite covers.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The product is taken only over components that receive R̂_m injectively: André's proof takes all factors of the normalisation of S ⊗_R R̂_m, which can include components of smaller dimension over which Remarque 4.2.1 does not apply (source issue DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay/E14, after PAPER-ANDRE-18-B/E20). No normalisation is needed: a domain quotient of maximal dimension suffices.

**Proof.**

1. For each maximal ideal m of R, R̂_m is a complete regular local domain and S ⊗_R R̂_m is a finite R̂_m-algebra, a finite product of complete local rings (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.1/finite-algebra-completion-product); R̂_m → S ⊗ R̂_m is injective.
2. By lying over for the minimal prime 0 of R̂_m, some factor has a prime P lying over 0; S_m := factor/P is a complete local domain, finite over R̂_m and containing it, of the same dimension.
3. Let T_m be a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay S_m-algebra (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.4/big-cm-existence). It is a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay R̂_m-algebra (same systems of parameters), hence faithfully flat over R̂_m (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/regular-big-cm-flat), hence flat over R with mT_m ≠ T_m.
4. T = ∏_m T_m is an S-algebra, flat over R (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/flat-products-noetherian) and faithfully flat because each maximal ideal stays proper in its own factor.

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.4/big-cm-existence`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/regular-big-cm-flat`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/balanced-big-cm-module`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/flat-products-noetherian`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.1/finite-algebra-completion-product`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/regular-local-domain`, `mathlib:IsRegularRing`, `mathlib:AdicCompletion`.

**Checks.**

- For R a field the statement says every nonzero finite K-algebra S has a K-algebra map to a nonzero K-algebra: take a residue field of S.
- Consequence: R → S is pure, hence split since S is finite (André B §0.7).

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, Théorème 0.7.2, p. 75. For a Noetherian regular ring R and a finite extension R ⊆ S there is S → T with R → T faithfully flat; in particular R → S is pure.
- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, §4.3, Preuve du théorème 0.7.2, p. 87. Works over each completion R̂_m, takes Cohen–Macaulay algebras T_{m,i} for the complete local domain factors, uses Remarque 4.2.1 for faithful flatness over R̂_m, and takes the product over all m and i.

### R → R⁺ splits for complete regular local rings

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/absolute-integral-closure-splits` (theorem).** Let R be a complete Noetherian regular local ring and R⁺ the integral closure of R in an algebraic closure of Frac(R). Then R → R⁺ admits an R-linear retraction. In particular W(k)⟦T_1, …, T_n⟧ → W(k)⟦T_1, …, T_n⟧⁺ splits for every perfect field k of characteristic p.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.isSplit_absoluteIntegralClosure`.

**Planet.** Splitting of R → R⁺.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Completeness of R is needed for the passage from purity to splitting: the corresponding statement for an arbitrary DVR V in place of W(k), asserted in André B Annexe A.3 Applications, fails by the Datta–Murayama example (source issue DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay/E4, after PAPER-ANDRE-18-B/E21).

**Proof.**

1. R⁺ is the filtered union of its finite R-subalgebras S_α, each split over R by the direct summand theorem (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/direct-summand-theorem), hence pure.
2. Filtered colimits of pure maps are pure (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map), so R → R⁺ is pure.
3. R is complete, so the pure map R → R⁺ splits (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.1/complete-pure-split).

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/direct-summand-theorem`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.1/complete-pure-split`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map`, `mathlib:integralClosure`, `mathlib:AlgebraicClosure`, `mathlib:IsAdicComplete`.

**Checks.**

- For R a complete DVR the retraction exists (R⁺ is the union of DVRs finite over R, each free); the theorem is substantial from dimension 2 on.
- In characteristic p the splitting of R → R⁺ also follows from DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/regular-big-cm-flat and DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.4/char-p-absolute-integral-closure, which make R⁺ faithfully flat over R.

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, §0.2, last paragraph, p. 73. The embedding of W(k)⟦T_1, …, T_n⟧ into its integral closure in an algebraic closure of its fraction field admits a W(k)⟦T⟧-linear retraction (cf. A.3).

### Contracted ideals and the monomial theorem

### Purity survives completion and perfect residue fields

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.6/pure-local-completion` (theorem).** Let β : B → B′ be a pure local homomorphism of Noetherian local rings. Then the induced map B̂ → B̂′ of maximal-ideal-adic completions is pure. If B and B′ have mixed characteristic (0, p) and the residue extension k → k′ is separable, then for compatible coefficient maps Λ → B̂, Λ′ → B̂′ the map (B̂ ⊗_Λ W(k^{1/p^∞}))^ → (B̂′ ⊗_{Λ′} W(k′^{1/p^∞}))^ is pure.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.RingHom.IsPure.adicCompletion`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Compatible coefficient rings exist because k → k′ is separable (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.1/cohen-relative-separable-compatibility).
- Request R2 to R03.1 is needed for the common coefficient extension; the current individual perfect-residue-basechange statement does not assert this comparison.

**Proof.**

1. Put C=completion_{m_B B′}(B′). This is Noetherian local, and its maximal-adic completion is B̂′, faithfully flat over C. To prove B̂ → C pure, test every finite B̂-module P, rather than only those descending to B.
2. Each P/m_B^nP is a finite B-module and is unchanged by tensoring B → B̂ at that level. Purity of B → B′ gives its injection into (P/m_B^nP) ⊗_B B′ = (P/m_B^nP) ⊗_{B̂} C. An element in the kernel of P → P ⊗_{B̂} C therefore lies in every m_B^nP; Krull intersection for the finite B̂-module P makes it zero. Compose with C → B̂′.
3. For the perfect-residue assertion, compatibility of the two completed coefficient extensions is an additional supplier need R2. Separability makes k′ ⊗_k k^perf a field, but the existence and faithful flatness of the resulting map of completed Cohen extensions still need proof. Given that comparison, base change preserves purity and the first part handles completion. Individual faithfully flat extensions of source and target alone do not establish purity of the comparison map.

### Cohen–Macaulayness descends along pure maps to regular rings with a big Cohen–Macaulay square

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.6/cm-descent-from-regular-target` (theorem).** Let β : B → B′ be a pure local homomorphism of Noetherian local rings with B′ regular. Suppose there are a B-algebra C on which some system of parameters x = (x_1, …, x_d) of B is a regular sequence, a balanced big Cohen–Macaulay B′-algebra C′ and a ring map C → C′ compatible with β. Then x is a regular sequence on B; in particular B is Cohen–Macaulay.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.isCohenMacaulay_of_isPure_of_bigCM_square`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- André calls this 'a known argument' and cites Hochster–Huneke 1995 (2.3), which was not read in this job; the steps below are a complete argument from this roadmap's nodes.

**Proof.**

1. Let 0 ≤ i < d and b ∈ B with x_{i+1}b ∈ (x_1, …, x_i)B. As x is regular on C, b ∈ (x_1, …, x_i)C, hence b ∈ (x_1, …, x_i)C′.
2. C′ is faithfully flat over the regular local ring B′ (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/regular-big-cm-flat), so (x_1, …, x_i)C′ ∩ B′ = (x_1, …, x_i)B′ and b ∈ (x_1, …, x_i)B′.
3. B → B′ is pure, so (x_1, …, x_i)B′ ∩ B = (x_1, …, x_i)B (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map) and b ∈ (x_1, …, x_i)B. Since x is a system of parameters, B ≠ (x)B, and B is Cohen–Macaulay.

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/regular-big-cm-flat`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/balanced-big-cm-module`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map`, `mathlib:RingTheory.Sequence.IsRegular`, `mathlib:IsRegularLocalRing`.

**Checks.**

## Layer DS.7: The derived direct summand theorem

Bhatt's derived direct summand theorem: for a Noetherian regular ring A_0 and a proper surjective X_0 → Spec A_0, the unit A_0 → RΓ(X_0, O) splits in D(A_0); regular rings are derived splinters. In mixed characteristic the proof of DS.3 runs unchanged on the perfect cone of the unit, with almost purity on the annuli replaced by the almost splitting of proper maps over integral perfectoid algebras that are finite étale and surjective after inverting p (Bhatt Proposition 6.2, corrected). In characteristic p, splinters and derived splinters coincide because higher coherent cohomology of proper maps dies on finite covers (Bhatt 2012). In characteristic 0 the theorem is Kovács' characterisation of rational singularities, whose prerequisites (resolution of singularities, Grauert–Riemenschneider vanishing) are not planned anywhere in the atlas; this is recorded as gap G2.

**Targets of the layer.**

Bhatt's Theorem 1.2: for a Noetherian regular ring A_0 and a proper surjective X_0 → Spec A_0, A_0 → RΓ(X_0, O) splits in D(A_0). Targets:

- **Derived splinters**.
- **Perfectness of proper coherent cohomology** over regular local rings, with flat base change; coherence is imported from current StableReduction and boundedness comes from a finite affine Čech cover.
- **Reductions** of the derived obstruction to complete regular local rings and generically finite integral covers.
- **Almost splitting of proper maps over integral perfectoid algebras** that are finite étale and surjective after inverting p (Proposition 6.2, corrected).
- **Characteristic p**: killing higher coherent cohomology by finite covers and the equality of splinters and derived splinters (Bhatt 2012).
- **Characteristic 0**: the rational-singularity route, with Kovács' exact projective hypotheses and the missing extensions recorded in G2.
- **The derived direct summand theorem**.

### Derived splinters

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-splinter` (definition).** A Noetherian ring A is a derived splinter if for every proper surjective morphism of schemes f : X → Spec A the unit A → RΓ(X, O_X) has a left inverse in the derived category D(A), i.e. its splitting obstruction (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/splitting-obstruction) vanishes.

**Named signature.** `IsDerivedSplinter`.

**Planet.** Derived splinter.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Bhatt's Definition 0.2 [Bhatt12] asks splitting of O_S → Rf_*O_X in D(Coh(S)) for schemes S; for affine S = Spec A this is the definition above, RΓ(X, O_X) being computed by the enhanced pushforward (EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E1/ringed-pullback-pushforward).
- Every finite injective ring map A → B gives a finite surjective Spec B → Spec A (lying over), with RΓ = B; so a reduced derived splinter is a splinter.

**Construction.**

1. RΓ(X, O_X) is the derived pushforward of O_X along X → Spec A (EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E1/ringed-pullback-pushforward), an object of D(A) with H^i = H^i(X, O_X) (Tau Ceti Scheme.Modules.Cohomology).
2. Composition: if A → RΓ(Y, O_Y) splits for some Y → X → Spec A with Y → X proper surjective, then A → RΓ(X, O_X) splits, since the unit for Y factors through the one for X.

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/splitting-obstruction`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/splinter`, `EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E1/ringed-pullback-pushforward`, `tauceti:TauCeti.AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.Modules.Cohomology`, `mathlib:AlgebraicGeometry.IsProper`, `mathlib:AlgebraicGeometry.Surjective`.

**API.**

- `IsDerivedSplinter` (characterisation): IsDerivedSplinter A ↔ ∀ X proper surjective over Spec A, the unit A → RΓ(X, O_X) has a left inverse in D(A).
- `IsDerivedSplinter.isSplinter` (compatibility): A reduced Noetherian derived splinter is a splinter.
- `IsDerivedSplinter.split` (projection): For a derived splinter A and a proper surjective X → Spec A, a chosen left inverse RΓ(X, O_X) → A in D(A).
- `IsDerivedSplinter.of_flat_local` (other): If A is Noetherian and A → A_m^ splits the unit for X ×_A A_m^ for every maximal m, then A splits it for X (node DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-obstruction-reductions).
- `IsDerivedSplinter.of_isRegularRing` (compatibility): Every Noetherian regular ring is a derived splinter (node DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-direct-summand-theorem).

**Unit tests.**

- `isDerivedSplinter_field` (degenerate): A field K is a derived splinter: for X proper over K and nonempty, K → H⁰(X, O_X) splits as K-vector spaces and the higher cohomology is a direct summand of RΓ.
- `isSplinter_of_isDerivedSplinter` (compatibility): A reduced Noetherian derived splinter is a splinter (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/splinter): take X = Spec B for B finite and injective over A.
- `not_isDerivedSplinter_elliptic_cone` (non-example): The cone C = ℂ[x, y, z]/(x³ + y³ + z³) is a normal domain containing ℚ, hence a splinter, but not a derived splinter: its vertex is not a rational singularity (H¹ of the structure sheaf of the blow-up of the vertex is H¹(E, O_E) ≠ 0 for the elliptic curve E), so by Kovács the unit for a resolution does not split. A definition using only finite maps fails this test.
- `isDerivedSplinter_iff_isSplinter_charP` (characterisation): For a Noetherian 𝔽_p-algebra A, IsDerivedSplinter A ↔ IsSplinter A ([Bhatt12] Theorem 0.3).

**Uses.**

- Bhatt Theorem 1.2 (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-direct-summand-theorem): the conclusion of the derived direct summand theorem
- Bhatt [Bhatt12] Theorem 0.3 (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/char-p-derived-splinter): splinters and derived splinters agree in characteristic p
- Bhatt §1.1 Remark 1.3(2): passage to alterations of a regular affine scheme is lossless for coherent cohomology

**Checks.**

- In characteristic 0 a variety is a derived splinter iff it has rational singularities (Kovács, as recalled in [Bhatt12] Example 1.2); in characteristic p derived splinters are exactly the splinters ([Bhatt12] Theorem 0.3).

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *Derived splinters in positive characteristic*, Definition 0.2 and Theorem 0.3, p. 1. Defines a derived splinter by splitting of O_S → Rf_*O_X in the derived category of coherent sheaves for every proper surjective f, and shows that over 𝔽_p derived splinters are the splinters.
- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Theorem 1.2, p. 1. The derived direct summand theorem: regular Noetherian rings are derived splinters in this sense.

### Coherent cohomology of proper schemes over regular rings is perfect

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/proper-cohomology-perfect` (theorem).** Let A_0 be a Noetherian ring and f : X → Spec A_0 proper. Then RΓ(X, O_X) is a bounded complex with finitely generated cohomology modules, hence pseudo-coherent (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:P7/pseudo-coherent-object), and so is the cone of A_0 → RΓ(X, O_X). If A_0 is a regular local ring, these complexes are perfect. For flat A_0 → A_0′, RΓ(X, O_X) ⊗^L_{A_0} A_0′ ≅ RΓ(X ×_{A_0} A_0′, O).

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.DerivedSplinter.isPerfect_globalSections_of_isProper`.

**Planet.** Perfectness of proper coherent cohomology.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Bhatt uses these facts implicitly in the proof of Theorem 6.1; they are stated here because the localisation of the derived obstruction and its flat base change need them.

**Proof.**

1. Import coherent proper pushforward and R^i f_* from the current StableReduction Layer 2; no extension of that roadmap is requested. Since X is separated and quasi-compact, choose a finite affine cover of t opens. Their intersections are affine and the alternating Čech complex computes quasi-coherent cohomology, so H^i(X,O_X)=0 for i≥t (Stacks Lemma 30.4.2). This finite-cover bound does not require finite Krull dimension of the Noetherian base.
2. Bounded with finitely generated cohomology over Noetherian A_0 means pseudo-coherent (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:P7/pseudo-coherent-object).
3. Over a regular local ring every finitely generated module has finite projective dimension (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/regular-local-finite-pd), so a pseudo-coherent complex with bounded cohomology is perfect (DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:P7/perfect-complexes-tor-amplitude-and-minimal-models).
4. Flat base change for quasi-coherent cohomology (SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.2/tor-independent-base-change).

**Inputs.** `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/StableReduction#layer-2-coherent-curve-theory-duality-and-positivity`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:P7/pseudo-coherent-object`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:P7/perfect-complexes-tor-amplitude-and-minimal-models`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.3/regular-local-finite-pd`, `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.2/tor-independent-base-change`, `EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E1/ringed-pullback-pushforward`, `mathlib:AlgebraicGeometry.IsProper`.

**Checks.**

- For X = Spec B with B finite over A_0, RΓ(X, O_X) = B; over a regular local ring B has finite projective dimension, consistent with perfectness.

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Proof of Theorem 6.1, p. 11. Assumes A_0 regular local and repeats the argument of Theorem 5.4 for the cone of A_0 → RΓ(X_0, O_{X_0}); the localisation needs a pseudo-coherent cone and the flat base change of its Hom along A_0 → A_∞ needs a perfect cone.
- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, §1.2, last paragraph, p. 3. Outlines the derived theorem as the same argument applied to RΓ of a proper map.
- The Stacks Project Authors, *The Stacks Project*, Lemma 30.4.2, Section 30.4 (tag 01XH); Proposition 30.19.1 and Lemma 30.19.2 (tag 02O3). A finite affine cover of a quasi-compact scheme with affine diagonal bounds quasi-coherent cohomology; proper coherent pushforward over a locally Noetherian base has finite cohomology over an affine base.

### Reductions of the derived splitting problem

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-obstruction-reductions` (theorem).** Let A_0 be a Noetherian regular ring and f_0 : X_0 → Spec A_0 proper surjective, with splitting obstruction α ∈ Hom_{D(A_0)}(Q, A_0[1]) of A_0 → RΓ(X_0, O_{X_0}) (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/splitting-obstruction). Then α = 0 iff its image vanishes over the completion of the localisation at every maximal ideal; each such completion is a complete regular local ring of equal characteristic 0, of equal characteristic p, or of mixed characteristic (0, p), and the base-changed map is again proper surjective. Over a complete regular local A_0 one may moreover replace X_0 by an integral closed subscheme Z, proper, surjective and generically finite over A_0; in mixed characteristic there is then g ∈ A_0 nonzero in A_0/p with Z → Spec A_0 finite étale over A_0[1/(pg)].

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.DerivedSplinter.splittingObstruction_eq_zero_of_local`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The pseudo-coherence of Q makes Hom_{D(A_0)}(Q, A_0[1]) commute with flat base change (Stacks 0A6A), so the passage to completed local rings is lossless (source issue DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay/E16, after PAPER-BHATT-18/E15).

**Proof.**

1. Q is pseudo-coherent (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/proper-cohomology-perfect), so for flat A_0 → A_0′, Hom(Q, A_0[1]) ⊗ A_0′ ≅ Hom(Q ⊗^L A_0′, A_0′[1]), and Q ⊗^L A_0′ is the cone for X_0 ×_{A_0} A_0′ by flat base change. A finitely generated module vanishing at every completed local ring vanishes.
2. Multisection: choose a closed point of the generic fibre of X_0 → Spec A_0 (A_0 a domain) and let Z be its closure, integral, proper, surjective and generically finite; the unit for Z factors through the unit for X_0, so splitting for Z implies splitting for X_0.
3. Discriminant: Z → Spec A_0 is generically finite étale in characteristic 0, and as in DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/direct-summand-reductions g can be chosen outside the minimal primes of pA_0.

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/splitting-obstruction`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/direct-summand-reductions`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/proper-cohomology-perfect`, `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.2/tor-independent-base-change`, `DeformationAndDerivedPatchingAlgebra:R03.1/faithful-flat-algebra-completion`, `mathlib:AlgebraicGeometry.IsIntegral`, `mathlib:AlgebraicGeometry.IsProper`.

**Checks.**

- For X_0 = Spec B_0 with B_0 finite this reduces to DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/direct-summand-reductions.

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Proof of Theorem 6.1, first sentences, p. 11. We may assume A_0 regular local; by taking the closure of a suitable generically defined multisection, X_0 integral and f_0 generically finite; choose g coprime to p with f_0 finite étale after inverting pg.

### Proper maps over integral perfectoid algebras are almost split

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/perfectoid-proper-almost-split` (theorem).** Let K be a perfectoid field of characteristic 0, A an integral perfectoid K°-algebra and f : Y → Spec A a proper morphism such that f[1/p] is finite étale and surjective. Then A → RΓ(Y, O_Y) has a left inverse in the almost derived category with respect to p^{1/p^∞}.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.DerivedSplinter.almostSplit_of_perfectoid`.

**Planet.** Almost splitting over perfectoid bases.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Surjectivity of f[1/p] is added to Bhatt's Proposition 6.2, which only asks f[1/p] finite étale: if the generic fibre is empty (Y lying over p = 0), the statement fails (source issue DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay/E17, after PAPER-BHATT-18/E1). In the application f[1/p] is surjective because f_0 is.
- A may be non-Noetherian and Spf A need not be an admissible locally finite-type formal scheme. AdicSpacesPartII R2’s formal-model theorems therefore do not supply this factorisation; use the scheme blow-up charts directly. Integrality of H⁰(Y,O_Y) over A is used here without claiming finite generation over A.

**Proof.**

1. B = H⁰(Y, O_Y) is integral over A and finite étale after inverting p; almost purity (PerfectoidSpaces:P3/almost-purity-theorem, PerfectoidSpaces:P3/integral-closure-almost-finite-etale) gives B → C, an isomorphism after inverting p, with A → C almost finite étale, faithful by surjectivity, hence almost split (trace, PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-dual-and-trace).
2. After almost purity replace A by the integral perfectoid closure C of the generic finite étale algebra, and Y by Y ×_{Spec B} Spec C; this reduces to the generic-isomorphism case, and the almost finite étale faithful trace supplies descent of the eventual retraction. Dominate Y by a scheme blow-up of Spec A in a finitely generated ideal containing p^N, using SF.4/modification-domination over a quasi-compact quasi-separated arbitrary base.
3. Write the blow-up ideal (f_0,…,f_r). The rational domains |f_j|≤|f_i|, with some f_i nonzero since p^N lies in the ideal, cover Spa(A[1/p],A). On each, the bounded fractions f_j/f_i give a map to the corresponding scheme blow-up chart; these glue to a morphism of ringed spaces (Spa(A[1/p],A),O⁺) → Y. This is Bhatt’s footnote 8 argument. No locally finite-type formal model or general Raynaud equivalence is used.
4. The composite is an almost isomorphism by Scholze's almost vanishing of H^i(S_η, O⁺) for i > 0 (PerfectoidSpaces:P2/sheaf-theorem-and-almost-acyclicity), so the first map is almost split.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpaces:P3/almost-purity-theorem`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P3/integral-closure-almost-finite-etale`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-dual-and-trace`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P2/sheaf-theorem-and-almost-acyclicity`, `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.4/modification-domination`, `EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E1/ringed-pullback-pushforward`.

**Checks.**

- For Y = Spec B finite over A this is the almost splitting of an almost finite étale cover, as in DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/annular-almost-splitting.

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Proposition 6.2 and proof, with footnote 8, p. 11. Proposition 6.2 gives almost splitting over an integral perfectoid base; add generic surjectivity as in E17. Footnote 8 constructs the factorisation through a scheme blow-up using bounded rational functions, without an admissible finite-type formal model.

### Killing higher coherent cohomology by finite covers in characteristic p

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/char-p-cohomology-killing` (theorem).** Let f : X → S be a proper morphism of Noetherian 𝔽_p-schemes. There exist a proper morphism g : Y → S and a finite surjective morphism π : Y → X such that π* : τ^{≥1}Rf_*O_X → τ^{≥1}Rg_*O_Y is zero.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.DerivedSplinter.exists_finite_cover_killing_cohomology`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The source is Bhatt [Bhatt12] Theorem 0.4, whose proof follows the method of Hochster–Huneke and Huneke–Lyubeznik (equational lemma and Frobenius); it is cited here as a key theorem with that outline.

**Proof.**

1. Over an affine Noetherian base, each positive coherent cohomology module is finite and Frobenius-stable. For finitely many generators, monic additive Frobenius equations allow finite integral extensions killing the classes (Bhatt 2012 Proposition 3.2 and Corollary 3.5, pp. 7–9). The equational lemma supplies the method; ordinary affine cohomology is not local cohomology.
2. Choose a finite affine cover of X. Its base changes remain affine, giving a fixed finite Čech bound for the higher cohomology at every later finite cover. Killing all cohomology maps is insufficient by itself to kill a derived morphism: iterate the covers through that finite amplitude, using Bhatt Lemma 2.2, pp. 5–6, to make τ^{≥1}Rf_* → τ^{≥1}Rg_* zero.
3. For a general Noetherian S choose finitely many affine opens; Proposition 3.1, pp. 6–7, extends and dominates the local finite covers to obtain a common finite surjective cover. The same bounded-complex argument yields the global zero morphism. No induction on potentially infinite Krull dimension is used.

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.4/char-p-absolute-integral-closure`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/proper-cohomology-perfect`, `EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E1/ringed-pullback-pushforward`, `mathlib:frobenius`.

**Checks.**

- For an elliptic curve E over a field of characteristic p, the finite surjective map [p]:E → E acts as multiplication by p on H¹(E,O_E), hence as zero. Since τ^{≥1}RΓ(E,O_E) has only H¹, its induced map is zero. In contrast, X=Spec R already has zero positive ordinary coherent cohomology and cannot test local-cohomology killing.

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *Derived splinters in positive characteristic*, Theorem 0.4, p. 1; Lemma 2.2 and Propositions 3.1–3.2 with Corollary 3.5, pp. 5–9. For f : X → S proper between Noetherian 𝔽_p-schemes there are a proper g : Y → S and a finite surjective π : Y → X with π* zero on τ^{≥1}Rf_*O_X; the proof is inspired by Hochster–Huneke and Huneke–Lyubeznik.

### Splinters are derived splinters in characteristic p

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/char-p-derived-splinter` (theorem).** A Noetherian 𝔽_p-algebra is a splinter if and only if it is a derived splinter (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-splinter). In particular every Noetherian regular 𝔽_p-algebra is a derived splinter.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.isDerivedSplinter_iff_isSplinter_of_charP`.

**Planet.** Bhatt's derived splinter theorem in characteristic p.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The 'in particular' combines this with DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.1/frobenius-direct-summand, as Bhatt cites [Bhatt12] Theorem 1.4 and Example 2.3 for the characteristic-p case of Theorem 1.2.

**Proof.**

1. Derived splinter ⇒ splinter: finite surjective maps are proper surjective (API isSplinter of DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-splinter).
2. Given X → Spec A proper surjective, choose Y → X finite surjective killing the map on τ^{≥1}. The composite RΓ(X,O_X) → RΓ(Y,O_Y) → τ^{≥1}RΓ(Y,O_Y) is zero. Exactness of Hom applied to the truncation triangle H⁰(Y,O_Y) → RΓ(Y,O_Y) → τ^{≥1}RΓ(Y,O_Y) therefore lifts the first map to RΓ(X,O_X) → H⁰(Y,O_Y).
3. The lift preserves the unit: Hom(A,H⁰(Y,O_Y)) → Hom(A,RΓ(Y,O_Y)) is an isomorphism by the standard t-structure. The finite algebra H⁰(Y,O_Y) has surjective spectrum over A by proper surjectivity of Y → Spec A; since a splinter is reduced, its structure map is injective. Its A-linear splitting composed with the lift retracts A → RΓ(X,O_X).

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-splinter`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/char-p-cohomology-killing`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.1/frobenius-direct-summand`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.0/splinter`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/proper-cohomology-perfect`, `EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E1/ringed-pullback-pushforward`.

**Checks.**

- 𝔽_p⟦x, y⟧ is a derived splinter, by DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.1/frobenius-direct-summand and this theorem.

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *Derived splinters in positive characteristic*, Theorem 0.3, p. 1, proof p. 9; Example 1.3, pp. 2–3. A Noetherian 𝔽_p-scheme is a splinter iff it is a derived splinter; regular affine 𝔽_p-schemes are splinters.
- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, §1, paragraph after Theorem 1.2, p. 1. The characteristic p case of Theorem 1.2 follows from [Bhatt12] Theorem 1.4 and Example 2.3.

### The derived direct summand theorem for regular rings containing ℚ

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/char-zero-derived-splitting` (theorem).** Every Noetherian regular ring containing ℚ is a derived splinter (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-splinter).

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.isDerivedSplinter_of_isRegularRing_of_algebraRat`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Kovács Theorem 1, p. 187, is descent of rational singularities assuming the source already has rational singularities. Theorem 2, p. 188, gives splitting for a surjective morphism of projective complex varieties when both varieties have rational singularities. Neither theorem by itself states the arbitrary proper, arbitrary characteristic-zero-base assertion needed here.
- Kovács' theorem rests on resolution of singularities in characteristic 0 and on Grauert–Riemenschneider vanishing, which no roadmap of the atlas plans: gap G2.

**Proof.**

1. Reduce to A_0 a complete regular local ring containing ℚ (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-obstruction-reductions); the obstruction lies in Ext¹ of a bounded coherent complex and commutes with the faithfully flat completion.
2. Use Popescu approximation and finite-presentation limits to seek a smooth finite-type stage carrying the proper cover and its obstruction; descent of surjectivity and compatibility of the retraction require explicit limit arguments, retained in G2 rather than inferred solely from the algebra colimit.

### The derived direct summand theorem

**Target `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-direct-summand-theorem` (theorem).** Let A_0 be a Noetherian regular ring and f_0 : X_0 → Spec A_0 a proper surjective morphism. Then A_0 → RΓ(X_0, O_{X_0}) has a left inverse in D(A_0); that is, every Noetherian regular ring is a derived splinter (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-splinter).

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.isDerivedSplinter_of_isRegularRing`.

**Planet.** Derived direct summand theorem.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- In mixed characteristic the theorem was new even in dimension 2 when Bhatt proved it; it implies the direct summand theorem for reduced finite covers and is orthogonal to it for birational f_0 (blow-ups).

**Proof.**

1. Reduce to A_0 complete regular local of one of the three characteristic types, and to Z integral, generically finite, finite étale over A_0[1/(pg)] (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-obstruction-reductions).
2. Equal characteristic 0: DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/char-zero-derived-splitting. Equal characteristic p: DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/char-p-derived-splinter.
3. Mixed characteristic: the cone Q_0 is perfect (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/proper-cohomology-perfect). If α_0 ≠ 0, DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/obstruction-detection gives m, k with (pg)^{1/p^k} ∉ Ann(α_∞/p^m).
4. For fixed m, n the base change of Z to A_∞⟨p^n/g⟩ is proper and finite étale and surjective after inverting p (g divides p^n), so A_∞⟨p^n/g⟩/p^m → RΓ(Z, O) ⊗^L A_∞⟨p^n/g⟩/p^m is almost split (DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/perfectoid-proper-almost-split).
5. DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/obstruction-limit makes α_∞/p^m almost zero for (pg)^{1/p^∞}, a contradiction; hence α_0 = 0.

**Inputs.** `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-splinter`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/derived-obstruction-reductions`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/char-zero-derived-splitting`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/char-p-derived-splinter`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/proper-cohomology-perfect`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.7/perfectoid-proper-almost-split`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/obstruction-detection`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/obstruction-limit`, `DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.3/regular-local-perfectoid-cover`, `PerfectoidQuotients:Q3/bhatt-perfectoid-root-extension`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/annular-tower`.

**Checks.**

- For f_0 finite and A_0 → B_0 injective the theorem is the direct summand theorem DirectSummandsAndBigCohenMacaulay:DS.5/direct-summand-theorem.
- For f_0 the blow-up of a regular local ring of dimension d at its maximal ideal, RΓ(X_0, O) = A_0 and the splitting is trivial; for alterations of regular schemes it says coherent cohomology loses nothing.

**Sources.**

For a regular Noetherian A_0 and proper surjective f_0, A_0 → RΓ(X_0, O) splits in D(A_0); the mixed characteristic proof repeats that of Theorem 5.4 with Proposition 6.2 replacing almost purity on the annuli.
