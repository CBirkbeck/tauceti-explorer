# Foundations for p-adic differential equations and rigid cohomology

This reader supplies the remaining analytic and lifting inputs to RD.0. Its four threads are Laurent topologies and coefficient extension; weak Amice convergence and Frobenius substitution; valuation p-bases and overconvergent tensor descent; and controlled weak lifting with analytic divisor theory. The parent reader already specifies the Laurent, Cohen, bounded, integral and extended Robba carriers, their basic algebra, and the equal-reduction Frobenius homotopy. Those definitions are inputs here.

The end results are radius-sensitive Frobenius continuity, injectivity of completed-coefficient base change under the actual valuation-p-basis hypothesis, endpoint-preserving weak homotopies with rational de Rham integration, and the closed-ideal/divisor descriptions on analytic charts and Robba germs. A proof sketch below is a formalisation plan, not a claim that the corresponding Lean theorem has been proved.

## Conventions and ownership

K denotes a complete nontrivially nonarchimedean field, with multiplicative absolute value. For discretely valued K, O is its valuation ring, π a uniformiser, and k its residue field. Annular radii are positive real numbers; compact intervals may be singletons or have endpoints outside the value group. A disc containing zero has only nonnegative exponents. When comparing with Colmez's additive radii, ρ=p^(−r); the centre r=+∞ becomes ρ=0.

A_K(I) means Laurent coefficients convergent at every radius in I. The parent Robba ring R_K is their union over (r,1), with r<1. R_K^bd has bounded coefficients, R_K^int has integral coefficients, E_K is the complete bounded-coefficient Amice field with negative tail tending to zero, and O_E is its integral ring. The chart topology, Robba final seminorm topology, E_K norm topology, O_E π-adic topology and O_E weak topology are different structures. Every continuity statement names its structure. In particular O_E is the π-adic completion of O[[t]][t⁻¹], while finite Laurent polynomials are dense only for its weak topology.

Γ is the parent Cohen/Frobenius ring with its chosen ramified coefficient ring O, residue valuation, Frobenius lift σ and enough-units radii. Γ\_con is its overconvergent henselian DVR. Γ^perf and Γ^alg use valuation-completed perfect and algebraic residue coefficients. They do not mean merely the union of finite residue extensions. Partial valuations and Teichmüller expansions are the parent's definitions. A π-adic expansion and a finite algebraic monomial expansion must not be confused.

### Laurent topology and weak Frobenius substitution

#### Laurent Fréchet charts and the Robba inductive limit

Target `laurent-topology-interface` (construction).

On the parent Laurent carrier define A_K(I) for a nonempty interval I of positive multiplicative radii by convergence at every radius in I. At radius ρ use |f|\_ρ = sup_n |a_n|ρ^n. On each compact window J=[α,β] inside I use p_J=max(|·|\_α,|·|\_β). A_K(I) carries the separated complete topology generated by these seminorms and is their compatible projective limit; a compact I gives its Banach norm p_I. For a disc containing the centre use only n≥0 and include evaluation at zero. R_K is the parent union of A_K((r,1)), with the nonarchimedean locally convex inductive-limit topology of LocallyAnalyticDistributions:L0 (the finest locally convex topology making all chart inclusions continuous); each A_K(I) is the L0 projective limit of its compact-window Banach charts. A cofinal sequence r_j increasing to 1 gives the same locally convex inductive limit (LF) topology. No strictness, bounded-set factorisation or completeness of this inductive limit is assumed.

Hypotheses. K is complete, nontrivially nonarchimedean, of either characteristic. Compact positive-radius annuli may have arbitrary real endpoints; no assumption that the value group is discrete. Locally convex is meant in the nonarchimedean sense of LocallyAnalyticDistributions:L0/nonarchimedean-locally-convex-spaces (ultrametric seminorms and open lattices), which rests on the pinned WithSeminorms API rather than on Mathlib’s real convexity predicates. The Gauss seminorms |·|\_ρ and p_J are ultrametric.

Proof route. Use the parent coefficient carrier and convolution. Ultrametric summability gives the submultiplicative Gauss bounds; truncation by finite Laurent polynomials converges on every compact positive-radius window. Construct the compact completion as weighted two-sided null sequences. Compatible coefficient limits on a countable compact exhaustion give completeness and metrisability of A_K(I); the topology does not depend on the exhaustion. For discs use power-series truncations. Give the existing Robba union the locally convex inductive-limit topology of LocallyAnalyticDistributions:L0/locally-convex-limits (i): a lattice is open exactly when its preimage in every chart is open, and a linear map out of R_K is continuous exactly when its restrictions to all charts are. Each chart is the projective limit of part (ii) over its compact windows. Chart restriction is injective and continuous; multiplication is continuous on every fixed chart and separately continuous on the union.

Uses. RD.1 and parent Frobenius-lift-on-robba-ring: Radius-sensitive continuity of Frobenius pullback. Lazard Proposition 10; RD.0 ideal theory: Closure of an ideal is defined in the projective chart topology. PadicHodgeTheory:P7 and PhiGammaModulesAndIwasawaCohomology:PG.0: Import these topologies on the one RD.0 coefficient carrier.

#### Finite coefficient extension of Laurent and Robba rings

Target `finite-coefficient-extension-interface` (construction).

Let L/K be a finite isometric extension of complete nonarchimedean fields. For every annulus interval I, the coefficient map A_K(I)⊗\_K L→A_L(I) is a K-algebra isomorphism and a homeomorphism for the finite-dimensional tensor topology. A fixed K-basis e_1,…,e_d of L gives the inverse by taking the d coefficient-coordinate series. It also gives R_K⊗\_K L≅R_L for the LF topologies and E_K⊗\_K L≅E_L for the bounded-coefficient Amice norm topologies. No completed tensor is needed for finite L/K. Integral coefficient lattices are compared by uniform bounds; do not assert O_{E_L}=O_{E_K}⊗\_{O_K}O_L without a separate integral-basis theorem.

Hypotheses. L/K is finite, with its given norm extending the norm of K; this is coefficient extension, not the parent finite separable residue-field extension Γ\_L/Γ. No separability assumption is needed for this coefficient-coordinate argument; it applies in equal characteristic.

Proof route. Read the finite-basis coordinate equivalence in Mathlib at the pin. Its coordinates and inverse have uniform norm bounds independent of the Laurent index. Apply those bounds to |a_n|ρ^n at every radius, and to boundedness and the negative-tail limit for E. Reconstruct the inverse finite sum Σ\_j e_j f_j and verify multiplication coefficientwise using convergent convolution. Apply the same uniform bounds on every compact window. For the Robba union, finitely many coordinate series share a common inner radius; the final linear topology proves continuity in both directions.

Uses. RD.1 finite-coefficient base change: Transport Laurent convergence and Frobenius/differential matrices to a finite coefficient extension without constructing a second analytic carrier. Compact Laurent preparation and divisor evaluations: Evaluate polynomial roots after finite coefficient extension and use coordinate bounds to descend analytic factorisation statements.

API.

- `LaurentChart.coefficientExtension` (equivalence): The coefficient algebra equivalence A_K(I)⊗\_K L≃ₐ[K]A_L(I), with inverse given by the finite K-basis coordinates.

- `LaurentChart.coefficientExtension_homeomorph` (compatibility): The equivalence and its inverse are continuous for the finite-coordinate topology; it commutes with restriction of radius intervals.

- `RobbaRing.coefficientExtension` (equivalence): The finite-coefficient equivalence R_K⊗\_K L≃ₐ[K]R_L is a homeomorphism for the final seminorm topologies, since finitely many coordinate series share an inner radius.

- `AmiceRing.coefficientExtension` (equivalence): The equivalence E_K⊗\_K L≃ₐ[K]E_L is a homeomorphism for the bounded-coefficient norms. Its inverse coordinates have uniform bounds; this is not a claim that every basis is orthogonal.


Worked tests.

- `coefficientExtension_identity` (compatibility): For L=K and the basis 1, every comparison is the identity coefficient map.

- `coefficientExtension_coordinates` (computation): For a quadratic L/K with basis 1,α, f=Σ\_n(a_n+b_nα)t^n maps back to f_a⊗1+f_b⊗α; both coordinate series converge on the same I.

- `coefficientExtension_inseparable` (compatibility): For K=F_p((z)), L=K(z^{1/p}), the finite p coordinates still reconstruct all Laurent series; separability must not be added.

- `coefficientExtension_basis_bounds` (non-example): Uniform two-sided coordinate bounds do not assert exact orthogonality: for a quadratic extension of Q_p choose α∉Q_p with 0<|pα|<1. The basis 1,1+pα has two vectors of norm 1 whose difference has norm |pα|<1. Using the maximum of their unweighted coordinate norms as an exact norm is wrong.


Checks. L=K is the identity comparison. The degree-d coordinate decomposition holds with an arbitrary basis, without demanding exact orthogonality. For L=K(z^{1/p}) over K=F_p((z)), coefficient extension is still an isomorphism despite inseparability.

#### The weak topology on the integral Amice ring

Target `integral-amice-weak-topology` (construction).

For K a complete discretely valued field with integers O and uniformiser π, give the parent O_E the inverse-limit topology of O_E/π^m≅(O/π^m)((t)), where the latter has the additive t-adic topology: a series is small when every coefficient below a specified index is zero. Thus a fundamental neighbourhood of zero is U(m,N)={f: a_i∈π^m O for all i<N}. Equivalently f_j→0 iff for each m its reduction eventually has t-order tending to +∞. This topology is complete, Hausdorff and a ring topology, and is coarser than the π-adic topology. Finite Laurent polynomials are dense for it. The inverse-limit reductions are ring isomorphisms, not just bijections of coefficient sets.

Hypotheses. K may have either characteristic; O is a complete DVR. The coefficients of every O_E series are integral and tend π-adically to zero towards −∞. For each m a reduction has only finitely many negative terms, but may have infinitely many positive terms.

Proof route. Reduction of convolution is formal Laurent multiplication because only finitely many summands survive in each coefficient modulo π^m. Choose compatible coefficient lifts in O to identify O_E with the inverse limit of these Laurent series rings. This proves completeness and Hausdorffness of the levelwise topology. The t-adic multiplication estimates on each quotient give the ring topology. Truncate both tails modulo π^m to prove weak density. Every U(m,N) contains π^mO_E, so the identity from the π-adic topology to the weak topology is continuous.

Uses. Parent FrobeniusLift.ofImage and .ext: Convergence and uniqueness are proved in this topology. Kedlaya Lemma 2.2.19: The analogous levelwise topology allows extension to completed perfect coefficients.

API.

- `AmiceRing.integral_reduce` (equivalence): For every m≥1, O_E/π^m≅(O/π^m)((t)), compatibly with coefficients, multiplication and transition maps.

- `AmiceRing.weak_nhds_zero` (characterisation): The sets U(m,N), m≥1 and N∈Z, form a neighbourhood basis at zero.

- `AmiceRing.weak_complete` (instance): O_E is complete and separated for the inverse-limit t-adic uniformity.

- `AmiceRing.laurentPolynomial_weak_dense` (relation): Finite Laurent polynomials over O are weak-dense in O_E.

- `AmiceRing.integralRobba_reduce` (compatibility): R^int/πR^int≅k((t)); surjectivity is obtained by bounded lifts with finitely many negative indices, and the kernel is πR^int.


Worked tests.

- `weak_shift` (computation): t^n→0 weakly as n→∞, but t^n never lies in πO_E, so it does not tend to zero π-adically.

- `weak_geometric_tail` (compatibility): Σ\_{i=0}^N t^i converges weakly to (1−t)⁻¹ in O_E; the same sequence is not π-adic Cauchy.

- `weak_negative_tail` (non-example): The family Σ\_{i<0}t^i is not in O_E; reduction must be a Laurent series with a finite negative tail.

- `weak_nilpotent_coefficients` (computation): For m=2, reduction is over O/π², not merely over k; πt^N is nonzero there and still tends to zero t-adically.


Checks. Do not use the π-adic completion universal property to justify weak-continuous substitution.

Inputs. `PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology:RD.0/frobenius-lift-on-robba-ring`, `PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology:RD.0/laurent-topology-interface`.

Sources. [slopes](#sources), Definition 2.2.7, p.454; Convention 2.3.1 and Definition 2.3.2, p.458: Levelwise topology and the explicit Laurent description. The U(m,N) model is its coefficient-coordinate realisation.

#### Weak convergence of semilinear Laurent substitution

Target `weak-substitution-convergence` (theorem).

Let σ\_K:K→K be an isometric ring endomorphism preserving O and inducing q-power Frobenius on k, q=p^a with a≥1. For u∈R^int with u≡t^q mod π, the series Σ\_i σ\_K(a_i)u^i converges in the weak topology for every f=Σ\_i a_i t^i∈O_E and defines the unique weak-continuous σ\_K-semilinear ring endomorphism with t↦u. Modulo π its action on k((t)) is exactly the q-power map. It is π-adically continuous as well, but weak continuity is essential to its classification.

Hypotheses. Mixed characteristic for the parent Frobenius object; the weak-convergence lemma itself also applies in equal characteristic when the stated coefficient lift exists. u is overconvergent, not an arbitrary unit of O_E, when extending to analytic charts.

Proof route. Modulo π^m write u=t^q(1+h) with h∈π(O/π^m)((t)). The inverse of 1+h is the finite nilpotent geometric sum. Positive powers u^i have t-order at least qi−C_m, since only powers h^j with j<m survive; this estimate is uniform in i. For the positive tail use that estimate, including σ\_K on coefficients; for the negative tail a_i vanish modulo π^m once i is sufficiently negative. Thus both tails converge in every quotient. Use finite polynomial substitution and continuity to obtain additivity, multiplicativity and uniqueness on the weak completion. For reduction, raising a Laurent series to q in characteristic p applies q-power to coefficients and multiplies every exponent by q.

Checks. σ(t⁻¹)=u⁻¹. For u=t^q the formula sends a_i t^i to σ\_K(a_i)t^{qi}. For K=Q_p and u=(1+t)^p−1 the reduction is t^p, although u is not equal to t^p.

Inputs. `PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology:RD.0/integral-amice-weak-topology`, `PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology:RD.0/frobenius-lift-on-robba-ring`.

Sources. [slopes](#sources), Convention 2.3.1 and Definitions 2.2.3, 2.2.7, pp.454,458: Semilinear Frobenius and its levelwise topology; the quotient-wise substitution estimate spells out the convergence behind the displayed substitution.

#### Frobenius substitution and radius comparison

Target `frobenius-radius-comparison` (theorem).

Under weak-substitution-convergence, there is r_\*<1 such that for r_\*<s<1, |u|\_s=s^q and |u/t^q−1|\_s<1. If f is analytic at ρ=s^q and on a surrounding annulus near 1, its substitution is analytic at s and |σ(f)|\_s=|f|\_{s^q}. Consequently substitution maps A_K((r,1)) continuously into A_K((r^{1/q},1)) after increasing r sufficiently, and induces the parent extensions to R^int, R^bd, E and R. The radius threshold depends on u; it cannot be omitted or replaced by a fixed-chart endomorphism.

Hypotheses. K is complete discretely valued with isometric σ\_K. The norm identity is asserted at radii of convergence, not for arbitrary coefficient families.

Proof route. Overconvergence of π⁻¹(u−t^q) gives an exponential bound on its negative tail. Its positive integral coefficients and the factor π imply |u/t^q−1|\_s<1 for all s sufficiently close to 1. Apply the comparison of truncated naive partial valuations with the σ-defined partial valuations (Slopes Lemmas 2.3.3 and 2.3.5). This proves equality, rather than just the easy upper bound from submultiplicativity; it excludes cancellation between dominant terms. Laurent truncations and the chart completion extend the equality to arbitrary analytic f. Uniform bounds on each compact window give continuity. Inverting π gives the bounded/Amice maps; the final seminorm topology gives the Robba map.

Checks. For standard substitution, |σ(t⁻¹)|\_{ρ^{1/q}}=ρ⁻¹. Over Q_p, Σ\_{n≥1}p^n t⁻ⁿ is analytic for p⁻¹<ρ<1 but its standard Frobenius pullback diverges for p⁻¹<ρ≤p^{−1/p}. Cyclotomic substitution gives σ(log(1+t))=p log(1+t), with both sides interpreted on the open unit disc.

Inputs. `PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology:RD.0/weak-substitution-convergence`, `PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology:RD.0/laurent-topology-interface`, `PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology:RD.0/witt-partial-valuations`, `PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology:RD.0/robba-ring`.

Sources. [slopes](#sources), Lemmas 2.3.3 and 2.3.5, pp.458–459; Corollary 2.3.7, p.459; Definition 2.4.2, p.460: Naive/true valuation comparison near the boundary and extension to analytic completions.

### Valuation p-bases and coefficient descent

#### Valuation bases

Target `valuation-basis` (definition).

For a normed field F and a normed F-vector space V, an existing algebraic basis b indexed by ι is a valuation basis when for every finitely supported coefficient family c, the norm of Σ\_i c_i b_i equals max_i |c_i|·|b_i|, with the empty maximum equal to zero. Use the NNReal finite supremum for this maximum. In additive valuation notation this is v(Σ\_i c_i b_i)=min_i v(c_i b_i), with v(0)=+∞. This condition augments Module.Basis; it does not replace its finite-coordinate representation.

Hypotheses. The uses here have ultrametric norms; the norm equality is meaningful on any normed space, and may fail there. All sums are finite algebraic sums, not completed or Schauder expansions.

Proof route. Use Module.Basis.repr to express the norm equality as a real-bodied predicate on an existing basis. Translate max norms to min additive valuations by the fixed logarithmic normalisation. The zero family must be treated explicitly. Transport the equality under reindexing and nonzero diagonal rescaling; each coordinate summand is bounded by the norm of the reconstructed vector.

Uses. Slopes Definition 2.2.17: The monomial family of a p-basis must satisfy the valuation formula. Slopes Lemma 2.2.19: Prevent cancellation in the coefficient-of-one projection.

API.

- `IsValuationBasis` (data): Predicate on Module.Basis: the NNReal norm of its inverse coordinate map is the finite supremum of the weighted coordinate norms.

- `IsValuationBasis.coordinate_le` (relation): For every x and i, |(b.repr x)\_i|·|b_i|≤|x|.

- `IsValuationBasis.reindex_iff` (functoriality): Reindexing by an equivalence preserves the predicate in both directions.

- `IsValuationBasis.rescale_iff` (compatibility): Replacing b_i by d_i b_i, with every d_i a unit of F (Module.Basis.unitsSMul), gives a valuation basis exactly when b is one; the i-th coordinate is divided by d_i.


Worked tests.

- `valuationBasis_zero` (computation): The zero coordinate family reconstructs zero and its empty weighted maximum is zero.

- `valuationBasis_singleton` (compatibility): The singleton basis (1) of F over F is a valuation basis and its sole coordinate equals x.

- `valuationBasis_bad_change` (non-example): In F² with the sup norm and 0<|π|<1, the basis (1,0),(1,π) is not a valuation basis: their difference has norm |π| while both summands have norm 1.

- `valuationBasis_archimedean` (non-example): The standard basis of Euclidean R² fails the condition at coefficients (1,1), since sqrt(2)≠1.


Checks. The predicate must distinguish a basis from a valuation basis and must not admit infinite coordinate sums.

Inputs. `mathlib:Module.Basis`.

Sources. [slopes](#sources), Definition 2.2.17, p.456: Valuation bases are characterised by the minimum formula for finite algebraic coordinates.

#### Valuation p-bases and their finite monomials

Target `valuation-p-basis` (definition).

For a valued field κ of characteristic p, a subset S is a valuation p-basis when its finite-support monomials ∏\_s s^{e_s}, 0≤e_s<p, form an algebraic valuation basis of κ over the Frobenius-image subfield κ^p. The empty monomial is 1. Coordinates have finite support in this monomial index set, even if S is infinite; each coefficient lies in κ^p. Equivalently, every x has a unique finite expansion and its valuation is the minimum of the valuations of its nonzero summands.

Hypotheses. p is prime and κ has characteristic p. κ^p is the image subfield of x↦x^p, carrying the restricted valuation; S contains no zero element.

Proof route. Index monomials by finitely supported functions S→{0,…,p−1}. Use the existing basis carrier together with valuation-basis, requiring its vectors to be exactly these monomial products. Uniqueness and spanning are algebraic requirements independent of norm orthogonality. Iterating Frobenius gives the analogous q^n monomial bases over κ^{q^n}. Use the valuation formula to identify coordinate projections as nonexpanding on weighted summands, which is the ingredient of Lemma 2.2.19.

Uses. Slopes Lemma 2.2.19: Build compatible coefficient-of-one projections through the perfection tower. Slopes Proposition 2.2.21 and §7.5: The tensor descent theorem carries the actual valuation-p-basis hypothesis.

API.

- `ValuationPBasis.monomialBasis` (projection): Return the algebraic basis over κ^p with the specified finite monomial vectors.

- `ValuationPBasis.expand` (data): Every x has a unique finitely supported coordinate family in κ^p.

- `ValuationPBasis.min_formula` (characterisation): The additive valuation of x equals the minimum over its finitely many nonzero monomial summands; for x=0 both sides are +∞.

- `ValuationPBasis.iterate` (compatibility): The q^n monomials, exponent <q^n in each of finitely many factors, give the valuation basis over κ^{q^n}.

- `ValuationPBasis.perfect_iff_empty` (characterisation): The empty set is a valuation p-basis exactly when κ is perfect.


Worked tests.

- `pBasis_perfect` (computation): For a perfect valued κ, S=∅ has the single monomial 1; the expansion of x is x·1.

- `pBasis_laurent_perfect` (computation): For κ=F_p((t)), S={t}; x=Σ\_{j=0}^{p−1}t^j y_j^p is a finite expansion by residue classes of exponents modulo p.

- `pBasis_imperfect_constants` (compatibility): For κ=F_p(s)((t)), S={s,t}; the p² monomials have unique finite coordinates over κ^p and satisfy the t-adic minimum formula.

- `pBasis_infinite_coefficients` (non-example): For k=F_p(s_1,s_2,…) and κ=k((t)), S={t,s_1,s_2,…} does not span algebraically: Σ\_{n≥1}s_n t^{pn} needs infinitely many distinct constant p-monomials. Finite truncations spanning densely do not fix this failure.


Checks. An ordinary p-basis is insufficient without the valuation minimum formula. Topological spanning is insufficient without finite algebraic coordinates.

Inputs. `PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology:RD.0/valuation-basis`, `mathlib:Module.Basis`.

Sources. [slopes](#sources), Definition 2.2.17 and Lemma 2.2.19, p.456: Finite monomials, finite algebraic coordinates and the minimum valuation formula.

#### The Laurent example with finite coefficient p-rank

Target `finite-p-basis-laurent-example` (theorem).

If k has a finite p-basis B, then {t}∪B is a valuation p-basis of κ=k((t)) with its t-adic valuation. This includes arbitrary perfect k, whose p-basis is empty. Without the finite-p-rank hypothesis, this particular monomial family need not be an algebraic basis of k((t)); the theorem does not assert that no other valuation p-basis exists.

Hypotheses. k has characteristic p, and B is an ordinary finite p-basis of k. The valuation on k is trivial and the valuation on κ is t-adic.

This interpolation implies that every finitely generated ideal of A_K(U) is closed, whether or not K is spherically complete.

Hypotheses. No uniform bound on the jets is imposed. This is interpolation without a growth bound, unlike spherical prescribed-divisor realization. For inseparable P use the polynomial jet quotient, not derivative data at distinct roots.

Proof route. Group the poles by packets escaping every compact subdomain. Each rational principal part whose poles lie outside a fixed compact window has a convergent power or inverse-power expansion there. Subtract a finite Laurent-polynomial truncation to make its norm arbitrarily small on that window. Summing the corrected packets converges on every compact window and retains all principal parts. Choose the nonzero divisor majorant f. Extend the requested jets to orders div(f), including arbitrary zero data at the additional zeros of f. Apply the principal-parts construction to their local quotients by f; multiply the resulting meromorphic function by f to obtain a global analytic function with the required jets (Lazard (8.11)). For generators f_0,…,f_m and g vanishing along their common divisor, compact division gives local coefficients on each circle. Interpolate the coefficients for i>0 modulo the zero divisor of f_0. The remaining difference is globally divisible by f_0, so g is in the generated ideal. This identifies the ideal with its closed divisor ideal.

Checks. For two distinct closed points finite interpolation agrees with polynomial CRT. Arbitrarily large jets escaping the boundary are allowed; no boundedness hypothesis is introduced. The argument shows principal ideals are closed over any complete K, but not that every closed ideal is principal.

#### Closed ideals and effective analytic divisors

Target `closed-ideal-divisor-correspondence` (theorem).

For any complete nonarchimedean K, the nonzero closed ideals of A_K(U) correspond order-reversingly to effective locally finite divisors on U. For a nonzero ideal I define D_I(P)=min_{0≠f∈I}div(f)(P); for a divisor D put I_D={0}∪{f≠0:D≤div(f)}. Then I_D is nonzero and closed, D_{I_D}=D, and closure(I)=I_{D_I}. The zero ideal is an additional closed ideal, with no finite-multiplicity divisor. The unit ideal corresponds to D=0. On compact U every such ideal is principal; on noncompact U principality requires the separate spherical-completeness theorem.

Hypotheses. Divisors and closure use the exact carrier and topology specified above. No spherical completeness is needed for this classification alone.

Proof route. Choose a nonzero member of I; its divisor bounds D_I, proving local finiteness. Vanishing to a given order is a closed condition on every compact chart, so I_D is closed. The divisor-majorant theorem supplies a nonzero member. On a compact window, select finitely many members of I which attain the minimum at each of its finitely many relevant points. Compact PID theory produces their gcd on that window. Laurent-polynomial approximation to the local coefficients makes I dense in every function vanishing along D_I. Use jet interpolation to make each minimum multiplicity sharp for I_D. Passing over the compact exhaustion proves the inverse correspondence and the closure formula. Treat I=0 separately. Over characteristic zero transport the polynomial divisor to the Galois-invariant geometric form, correcting the omission of the zero-ideal exception in Colmez’s statement.

Checks. I_D=the unit ideal exactly when D=0. The zero ideal is closed but is outside the displayed bijection. At a point a in the disc, the ideal (T−a)^m has divisor multiplicity m at a.

#### Prescribing exact zeros under spherical completeness

Target `spherical-prescribed-divisors` (theorem).

If K is spherically complete, every locally finite effective divisor D on a connected annulus or disc U is div(f) for some nonzero analytic f. The stronger construction includes Lazard’s norm-controlled finite-zero interpolation on increasing compact windows. Conversely, exact realization of every divisor on an open disc detects spherical completeness; completeness alone does not imply it. Spherical completeness means that every nested family of nonempty closed balls in K has nonempty intersection.

Hypotheses. K is complete, nontrivially nonarchimedean and spherically complete for the forward statement. The inverse implication concerns all effective locally finite divisors, not just finite polynomial divisors.

Proof route. Finite-zero interpolation with specified norm inequalities constrains successive coefficient choices to nested closed balls (Lazard Lemmas 4–5 and Proposition 8). Spherical completeness supplies compatible choices when their radii need not tend to zero. Pass along an exhaustion with the controlled corrections, preserving the exact polynomial divisor rather than allowing extra zeros. Apply the disc argument in both Laurent directions for an annulus. An empty nested family of balls gives the obstruction divisor of Lazard Proposition 6. Its realization would provide an element in their intersection; this is the necessity argument in Theorem 2.

Checks. Complete discretely valued K is spherically complete, so the theorem applies to the coefficient fields of the parent Γ theory. C_p is complete but not spherically complete: exclude it from the unconditional noncompact exact-divisor claim. Finite divisors on compact windows are already realised without this hypothesis.

#### Bézout and principal closed ideals on open annuli and discs

Target `noncompact-analytic-bezout` (theorem).

If K is spherically complete, A_K(U) is a Bézout domain for any connected nonempty open or half-open annulus or disc U. An ideal is closed iff it is principal. Its nonzero closed ideals are generated by functions realizing their effective divisors. On compact U the stronger PID conclusion holds for any complete K. Principal and finitely generated ideals are closed over any complete K; the spherical hypothesis is needed to turn every divisor ideal into a principal ideal on a noncompact domain.

Hypotheses. Use IsBezout, the existing Mathlib class, for the finite-generation conclusion. The domain U and the chart topology are fixed; the LF Robba germ topology is a different case.

Proof route. Analytic-jet-interpolation identifies every finitely generated ideal with its closed common-divisor ideal. Spherical-prescribed-divisors realises the common divisor by f. The divisor divisibility criterion shows I_D=(f). Thus every finitely generated ideal is principal. For an arbitrary nonzero closed ideal use closed-ideal-divisor-correspondence and the same generator. Conversely principal ideals are finitely generated and closed by interpolation. The zero ideal is separately principal and closed.

Checks. For Q_p on the open unit disc, the ideal generated by two nonzero functions is generated by a function with their pointwise minimum zero divisor. Do not assert this spherical version for C_p using completeness alone. This theorem does not imply that every algebraic ideal is closed.

#### Closed ideals of the Robba germ ring

Target `robba-closed-ideals` (theorem).

For the parent Robba ring R_K with the final seminorm topology, the only closed ideals are 0 and R_K. This closed-ideal assertion holds over any complete nontrivially nonarchimedean K by the compact-window argument. For spherically complete K the ring is also Bézout by noncompact-analytic-bezout and taking a common chart. Neither assertion says R_K is a field: over Q_p, log(1+t) is nonzero with infinitely many zeros tending to |t|=1, is not a unit, and generates a proper dense ideal.

Hypotheses. Closed means closed for the Robba LF topology, not for a single chart or for the Amice norm topology. The additional Bézout assertion retains spherical completeness; the parent DVR coefficient result is an instance.

Proof route. Take nonzero f∈I and a chart A_K((r,1)) containing it. On each compact window J_n of an exhaustion, compact preparation gives the finite polynomial zero factor P_n of f. P_n is a unit in the Robba germ ring: each irreducible factor has all its roots at one radius below 1, beyond which its leading term dominates, so it is invertible there by Lazard Proposition 4(1); raising the inner radius past the largest root radius inverts P_n. Over discretely valued K this is also an instance of the parent unit theorem. Thus h_n=f/P_n still lies in I and in the original analytic chart, and has no zeros on J_n. The inverse of h_n on J_n can be approximated arbitrarily well by a Laurent polynomial Q_n. Choose the error so that h_n Q_n→1 on every compact window. Every h_n Q_n lies in I∩A_K((r,1)); continuity of the chart inclusion shows 1 belongs to closure(I) in R_K. This proof uses no spherical completeness. For finite generators choose a common chart and apply noncompact-analytic-bezout when K is spherically complete, then pass its gcd to the union. For log(1+t), the zeros ζ\_{p^n}−1 have radii tending to 1, so no Robba chart removes all of them. An inverse cannot exist, yet the preceding density result applies to its principal ideal.

Checks. For f=t−a with 0<|a|<1, increase the inner radius above |a|; f becomes a unit of R_K. For log(1+t) over Q_p, the principal ideal is proper but dense, demonstrating why closed ideals do not classify all ideals. The ideal zero is closed because the chart coefficient functionals separate points and remain continuous for the final seminorm topology.

Inputs. `PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology:RD.0/compact-laurent-preparation`, `PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology:RD.0/laurent-topology-interface`, `PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology:RD.0/noncompact-analytic-bezout`, `PadicDifferentialEquationsAndRigidCohomology:RD.0/units-of-robba-ring`.

Sources.
