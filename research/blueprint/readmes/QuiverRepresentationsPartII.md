# Quiver representations, Part II: multisegment duality

This roadmap builds the graded-operator and interval combinatorics behind the ramified multisegment used in general-linear newforms. Its endpoint is the identity ram(m)=ram(maximalPart(m))+ram(remainder(m)) for the canonical one-copy maximal split. Finite type-A representations, interval indecomposables and Krull–Schmidt come from the parent QuiverRepresentations Layers 1,2,5. The integer grading adapter and the opposite-degree commuting theory are the additions here.

All intervals are nonempty, closed and integral; all multisegments are finite multisets. Copies matter. All operator geometry is over ℂ and finite dimensional, with finitely many nonzero integer grades. VN raises degree by one; WL lowers it by one. V(c)_a=V_(a−c), so the image of a VN block [b,e] is [b+1,e] before shifting and [b,e−1] after the shift −1. A singleton is removed. WL right truncation uses the unshifted image.

The topology in every genericity claim is generated by polynomial nonvanishing conditions in complex coordinates. It is independent of basis and is not the usual complex topology. Coordinate spaces in the suggested file model arbitrary finite graded spaces after choosing homogeneous bases. An image model contains an actual injective embedding onto the range, grade-by-grade, together with an intertwining equation; it cannot be supplied merely by guessing a multisegment.

The generic-rank locus, its conormal symmetry and simultaneous genericity on iterated images are separate targets. The adjacent Knight–Zelevinsky formula has an independent proof through commuting block coordinates and a finite occurrence-poset matching; it does not assume the full higher-power rank formula, Fourier transform, canonical bases or a finite-field variant. Grid paths locate the adjacent formula in the source’s monotone minimum, and supply a second way to compute its chain maximum. Dual and ram are not claimed additive on arbitrary multisegment sums.

The representation comparison uses the existing smooth-representation category and early segment classification. Current upstream SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups §SR.5.3 already plans the general segment, Z/L and Zelevinsky-involution objects; ET.6 must reuse those targets. The geometry here has no local-field dependency. Only the final representation adapter refers to the unramified degree-one cuspidal line. General Aubert duality, the conductor tuple definition, local Langlands and unipotent induction are not added here.

The pinned baseline is Tau Ceti f790474821cf4256814db967cb154e7af3d0c369 and Mathlib 082e2d37e8b0463410cdb532e111cd43d5a66174. The reviewed library audit and the current read-only upstream/library were checked before defining targets. No existing named generic commuting multisegment API was found. Each target below gives its precise statement, prerequisites and source; definitions carry the API and discriminating tests that the suggested signatures implement.

The planning pass is complete: all six stages are **planned**, not closed. G1 names the algebraic geometry bridge for conormal symmetry, while G2 names the absent baseline-ready representation interface. Parent layers are explicit supplier requests. Nothing below is claimed implemented.

### Integral intervals

An interval is a pair of integers b≤e representing every integer from b through e. Its length is e−b+1. Inclusion is c≤b and e≤d for [b,e]⊆[c,d]. Translation by t adds t to both endpoints. Right shortening returns [b,e−1] if b<e and no interval if b=e.

Suggested declaration: `TauCeti.MultisegmentDuality.Interval`.

Proof or construction. Use the parent’s type-A interval representation as the semantic source; this adapter permits arbitrary integer translation and explicitly excludes empty intervals.

Prerequisites: `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/RepresentationTheory/QuiverRepresentations#layer-5-gabriels-theorem-the-ade-classification`.

Source: AKY, §2.1 p.7; §2.3 p.8; §3.1 pp.14–15 — Inclusive integral segment and right-shortening conventions.

- `TauCeti.MultisegmentDuality.Interval.contains_shift`: a+t belongs to the translate of s by t exactly when a belongs to s.
- `TauCeti.MultisegmentDuality.Interval.shorten_singleton`: The right shortening of [a,a] is absent.
- `TauCeti.MultisegmentDuality.Interval.length_positive`: Every interval has strictly positive length.

### Multisegments and right truncation

A multisegment is a finite multiset of integral intervals. Its degree-a dimension counts all occurrences containing a; its total length sums all interval lengths. Right truncation binds each occurrence to its right shortening, deleting singletons and retaining repetitions. Translation acts occurrence by occurrence.

Suggested declaration: `TauCeti.MultisegmentDuality.rightTruncate`.

Proof or construction. Use finite multisets rather than finite sets; form dimension by filtering occurrences, length by a sum and truncation by multiset bind.

Prerequisites: `QuiverRepresentationsPartII:MS.0/interval`.

### Canonical maximal decomposition

Suggested declaration: `TauCeti.MultisegmentDuality.maximalPart`.

Proof or construction. Filter the distinct support by absence of a strict containing interval. Give each survivor multiplicity one and use multiset subtraction. This finite antichain is not the submultisegment of all maximal occurrences.

Prerequisites: `QuiverRepresentationsPartII:MS.0/multisegment`.

### Reconstruction from the VN image

Let VN pairs P,Q have equal degree dimensions. If their unshifted image VN pairs are graded-isomorphic, then P and Q are graded-isomorphic.

Suggested declaration: `TauCeti.MultisegmentDuality.reconstruct_from_image`.

Proof or construction. For i≤j let r_(i,j)=rank(N^(j−i):V_i→V_j); take r_(i,i)=dim V_i. The rank data of Im N determine r_(i,j) for i<j by evaluating N^(j−i−1) on N(V_i). For a pair (V,T) of degree ε=±1, centralizer(P) is the complex vector subspace of endomorphisms S of degree −ε satisfying ST=TS. A member gives the opposite pair on V. ### Commuting block coordinates

Choose occurrence labels for m. A degree −1 commuting map from the VN interval [t,u] to [s,v] exists with one free scalar precisely when s<t≤v+1≤u; otherwise that block is zero. Within an allowed block its scalar is constant wherever the target basis vector of degree a−1 exists. Consequently centralizer(standardPair m) is linearly equivalent to one independent complex coordinate for each allowed ordered occurrence pair. At degree a+1→a the coordinate occupies exactly the row of the target occurrence and column of the source occurrence.

Suggested declaration: `TauCeti.MultisegmentDuality.centralizer_coordinates`.

Proof or construction. In interval bases extend matrix coefficients by zero outside both blocks. Commutativity equates the coefficient at degree a with that at degree a+1. The two boundary zero equations force the strict endpoint and overlap inequalities. Conversely a constant scalar on a permitted overlap satisfies all equations. Apply this independently to every ordered occurrence pair, including repeated intervals with different labels.

Prerequisites: `QuiverRepresentationsPartII:MS.2/centralizer`, `QuiverRepresentationsPartII:MS.1/standard`.

Source: MW, Lemma II.4 pp.160–161; II.1 p.148 — Explicit scalar parameterization and the precedence inequalities.

Acceptance: A single interval has no allowed self block. Two adjacent singletons give a single permitted coefficient. Two copies in degree a and three in degree a+1 give six independent coefficients, rather than one.

### Polynomial Zariski topology

On C^n use the topology generated by polynomial nonvanishing loci. On a finite dimensional vector space transport this topology through a complex basis; prove independence of the basis and compatibility with linear maps and subspaces. ZariskiOpenDense(P,U) means U is both open and dense in centralizer(P) for this topology, including the zero dimensional case.

Suggested declaration: `TauCeti.MultisegmentDuality.polynomialTopology`.

Proof or construction. Invertible linear coordinate changes pull polynomials back to polynomials. A nonzero polynomial over the infinite field C has a nonzero evaluation, by induction on the number of variables. Products of finitely many nonzero polynomials remain nonzero, so nonempty principal opens meet. This proves irreducibility and finite intersection of dense opens. For a surjective linear map choose a section and a kernel complement: the preimage of a nonempty open dense set is open dense. Do not install the Euclidean topology for these predicates.

Prerequisites: `QuiverRepresentationsPartII:MS.1/space`, `mathlib:MvPolynomial.eval`, `mathlib:TopologicalSpace.generateFrom`, `mathlib:IsIrreducible`, `mathlib:Module.finBasis`, `mathlib:Module.Basis.ofVectorSpace`.

Source: AKY, §3.1 p.16 and Lemma 3.4 p.17 — Zariski open dense loci in finite dimensional commuting spaces.; MW, Remark II.6.1 p.163 — Nonempty intersection with a dense open set in an affine subspace remains dense there.

### Truncation respects maximal peeling

For every m, maximalPart(rightTruncate(m))=rightTruncate(maximalPart(m)) and remainder(rightTruncate(m))=rightTruncate(remainder(m)).

Suggested declaration: `TauCeti.MultisegmentDuality.truncate_maximalPart`.

Proof or construction. On nonsingleton intervals, right truncation is injective and preserves and reflects inclusion: both right endpoints decrease by one while left endpoints stay fixed. A nonsingleton contained in a larger original interval is still contained in its nonempty truncation. A maximal singleton disappears. It cannot contain a nonsingleton or contribute to a surviving maximal interval. These facts identify the surviving distinct maxima with their correct one-copy multiplicities. Use truncation additivity and cancellation to identify the remainders.

Prerequisites: `QuiverRepresentationsPartII:MS.0/maximal`, `QuiverRepresentationsPartII:MS.0/multisegment`.

### Weighted chain decrement under peeling

If cutPart(m,a) is nonempty, then E_a(m)=E_a(remainder(m))+1 and E_a(maximalPart(m))=1. If it is empty all three quantities are zero.

Suggested declaration: `TauCeti.MultisegmentDuality.chainWeight_peeling`.

Proof or construction. A chain contains at most one distinct maximal interval, since distinct maximal intervals are incomparable. Removing one copy per global maximum reduces a chain weight by at most one, so E_a(m)≤E_a(remainder(m))+1. Choose a maximum full chain in the remainder. Its largest interval lies in some inclusion-maximal interval of m, which also meets the cut. If that maximal interval already belongs to the chain, restore its one removed copy; otherwise append it. This gives a chain of weight at least E_a(remainder)+1. If the remainder cut is empty, use a maximal interval meeting the cut instead. The maximal part’s cut is a nonempty inclusion antichain, with each interval weighted one, so its chain maximum is one.

Prerequisites: `QuiverRepresentationsPartII:MS.0/maximal`, `QuiverRepresentationsPartII:MS.4/cut`.

Source: AKY, Proof of Proposition 2.7 pp.21–22 — Multiplicity-sensitive chain decrement.

Acceptance: If cutPart(m,a) is nonempty, then E_a(m)=E_a(remainder(m))+1 and E_a(maximalPart(m))=1. If it is empty all three quantities are zero.
