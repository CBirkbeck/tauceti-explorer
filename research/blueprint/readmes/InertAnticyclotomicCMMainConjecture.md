# Inert anticyclotomic CM main conjecture

This roadmap plans the global arithmetic consequences of the integral signed decomposition at an inert prime. Its first endpoint is BKO21 Theorem 6.1 (p.962): the dual with sign opposite the functional equation is torsion, with integral characteristic ideal generated by Rubin’s anticyclotomic p-adic L-function. Its second endpoint is Theorem 6.2 (pp.962–963), a finite-character CM rank bound, with a possible extra local dimension in one parity.

The parent is **ModularIwasawaMainConjectures**. This is a separate inert-CM branch: no ordinary theorem, split-prime hypothesis, epsilon conjecture or Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer equality is imported as an endpoint. The standing hypothesis is p≥5 inert and p∤h_K. The main-conjecture theorem permits the Gross Q-curve only over H. The finite-rank theorem adds E/K. Neither general statement assumes 2 splits.

This is a completed planning pass, with every layer planned and implementation unchecked. Six explicit source/proof gaps and the supplier requests below prevent mathematical closure. In particular the exact integral Rubin input and the full Gross normalization argument remain open; the endpoint statements are targets, not claims of completed proofs.

## Conventions and the arithmetic objects

The standing hypotheses for every target are:

1. p≥5 is prime and inert in the imaginary quadratic field K; p does not divide h_K. H is the Hilbert class field.
2. φ has infinity type (1,0), conductor f prime to p, φ((α))=α for α≡1 mod f, and φ(conjugate(a))=conjugate(φ(a)). The character φ∘N_(H/K) belongs to a maximal-CM Gross Q-curve E/H with good reduction above p.
3. Φ=K_p is unramified quadratic over Q_p, O its integers, π=−p, and F/O is the imported Lubin–Tate formal O-module. The fixed complex and p-adic embeddings, smooth model and differential give a period lattice Ω O_K.
4. R is the integer ring of a finite extension L/Φ containing the p-adic avatar φ_p. T=R(φ_p), W=T⊗_O(Φ/O)≃(T⊗_R L)/T. Λ_R=R[[Γ⁻]], Γ⁻=Gal(K∞/K)≃Z_p; ε=W(φ)∈{+1,−1}.

For every finite-rank/control target the extra hypothesis is: For finite-level rank/control statements E descends to K, and the chosen CM component of V_pE is T⊗_R L. Character values lie in a finite extension Lχ/L; dimensions are over Lχ.

Write H for the Hilbert class field only in the arithmetic setup; in the local-cohomology comparison write H_Iw for the compact local limit (the target formulas abbreviate that module as H). T′ always denotes the Cartier-dual lattice, with the inverse-different coefficient trace pairing. It is not silently T. The local O-module and its signed lattices are the ones supplied by AnticyclotomicLocalUnits. Λ_R is the coefficient ring throughout. Restriction to O uses the norm of a characteristic ideal; it does not leave its generator unchanged.

The trivial character has exponent c=0 and least layer K_0. For any χ, use n≥max(c−1,0); BKO’s source indexing K_c is also a permissible deeper layer. One must not require a degree-p^c extension to be the least field of definition. For interpolation, δ_1=(1+p⁻¹)δ is used as supplied by ALU. At ε=− the trivial derivative of the signed line is zero, so a nontrivial-character quotient formula cannot be extended to χ=1 by substitution.

## How the proof is assembled

The first two layers identify arithmetic towers, coefficients, points and Selmer kernels before any global rank is asserted. The primitive class is then put in its signed line and its integral coordinate is defined. The global all-auxiliary module remains a distinct object: interpolation of one local vector does not prove equality with its localized global lattice.

The integral two-variable theorem gives char(X_str)=char(S_rel/C), after the specialization correction divisor is proved common to both sides. Global duality gives the signed exact sequence. Its rank argument proves opposite signed torsion and vanishing of the compact strict kernel; its two quotient sequences then give char(X_(−ε))=char(H_Iw^ε/loc(C)). The final normalization target identifies this last ideal with the primitive (L_p), integrally over Λ_R. Each of these equalities is a separate target, so the missing integral input cannot hide inside a restatement of the final theorem.

For finite ranks, the signed control polynomials split the finite character sets only after finite integral errors are accounted for. Matching characters are bounded using the torsion opposite signed dual. Other characters use the strict characteristic divisor and the one-dimensional CM local correction. A Kummer injection supplies the lower bound by the Mordell–Weil CM rank. None of this asserts rank equality, a leading-term formula or an epsilon-factor statement.

## Worked consistency examples

Take a character χ of order p. Its conductor exponent is two, so χ∈Ξ⁺. If ε=+, the primitive class is in V⁺ and the torsion dual is X_−. The local minus point condition kills Ξ⁻, hence retains χ. The finite Selmer bound has no extra dimension. If ε=−, this same χ is in the other parity and only the strict-control argument gives the extra one. For order p² the conductor exponent is three and both branches reverse. These tests reject the tempting uniform sign reversal when translating AH’s character-set notation.

At χ=1, ω_n⁻ has a zero and ω_n⁺ does not. The minus formal-point condition retains the ordinary rational base-field component. Thus ε=+ again uses X_− without the extra one. For ε=− the bound allows the extra one. In the latter case δ vanishes on V⁻ and no division by δ(v_−) is permitted in an interpolation formula. This does not imply L_p(1)=0.

For a lattice test let ξ=πv in a free signed line with basis v. Then L_p=π, the quotient is Λ_R/(π), and the characteristic ideal retains (π). Replacing v by uv for a unit u replaces the coordinate by u⁻¹π and preserves that ideal. Inverting p makes the quotient disappear; consequently an equality proved only after inverting p would lose exactly the information the integral endpoint needs.

For a coefficient test let R/O have rank d and let f already have coefficients in Λ_O. The Λ_R cyclic module Λ_R/(f) has characteristic ideal (f) over Λ_R, whereas its restriction to Λ_O has ideal (f^d). For general f use its norm. This rejects a coefficient-blind equality with the same element over both rings.

For a specialization test, at a characteristic-zero character prime with parameter t, a localized module A/(t^k) has specialization dimension one and characteristic multiplicity k. Thus the rank inequality is dimension≤order, and equality is not automatic for k>1. A finite R-module can have a nonzero integral control error while contributing no rational character dimension. These two observations explain why the control proof tracks finite errors and characteristic lengths separately.
