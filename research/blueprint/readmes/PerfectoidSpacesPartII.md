# Perfectoid rings and spaces, Part II: ramification and Riemann extension

This roadmap starts where [Perfectoid rings and spaces](../packages/PerfectoidSpaces/README.md) (PerfectoidSpaces) stops. PerfectoidSpaces builds almost mathematics over an arbitrary basic setup (P0), perfectoid Tate rings, tilting and untilting (P1), rational localisation of perfectoid affinoids with Scholze's explicit almost integral models and the approximation lemma (P2), and the almost purity theorem for finite étale covers (P3). All of that concerns covers that are étale wherever p is invertible. This Part II studies what happens along a divisor g = 0 on which a cover may ramify, in the two forms that the direct summand theorem needs.

The first form is a **Riemann extension theorem**: bounded functions on the complement of g = 0 in a perfectoid space extend, up to almost zero error, across g = 0. Bhatt's quantitative version (RA.0–RA.1) is a statement about the tower of rational annuli X⟨t^n/g⟩ = {|t^n| ≤ |g|}: the comparison of A/t^m with the bounded functions modulo t^m on these annuli is an almost-pro-isomorphism whose error is controlled uniformly in n. Uniformity is what lets the estimate survive derived tensor products and Ext, which is how the direct summand theorem and its derived variant use it. André's version (RA.2–RA.3) identifies the limit exactly: lim_j A{ϖ^j/g}° is the complete integral closure g^{-1/p^∞}A° of A° in A[1/g]. It rests on a calculus of uniform Banach algebras (closures, localisations, uniform limits and colimits, root algebras) and on almost perfectoid algebras.

The second form is the **perfectoid Abhyankar lemma** (RA.4): for a perfectoid A containing compatible p-power roots of g, and B′ finite étale over A[1/g], the largest perfectoid A-algebra B in B′ has B° almost finite étale over A° modulo every power of p, in the almost setup generated by the roots of g and of a pseudouniformizer. This extends the almost purity theorem from g = 1 to arbitrary ramification. Its proof restricts B′ to the annuli A{ϖ^j/g}, where almost purity applies, and takes the uniform limit, controlling finite étaleness through Galois theory of almost algebras.

The last layer (RA.5) applies these results to the **normalized Kummer tower** over A = W(k)⟦T_1,…,T_n⟧, obtained by adjoining p-power roots of unity, of the coordinates and of a nonzero g ∈ A, and taking integral closures. André's flatness theorem says the completed tower Â°_∞∞ is almost faithfully flat over the coordinate tower A°_∞0. It implies purity of all Kummer extensions of A and, with the Abhyankar lemma, almost faithful flatness modulo p^m of any finite extension of A that is étale away from pg. These are the inputs of André's proof of the direct summand theorem; Bhatt's proof uses the quantitative Riemann extension theorem instead of the Abhyankar lemma. Both proofs, the derived direct summand theorem and big Cohen–Macaulay algebras belong to the roadmap Direct summands and big Cohen–Macaulay algebras, which builds on this one.

## Conventions

p is a fixed prime. A **root system** of an element x of a ring is a sequence (x^{1/p^k})_{k≥0} with x^{1/p^0} = x and (x^{1/p^{k+1}})^p = x^{1/p^k}; it is part of the data. For such x, almost mathematics "with respect to x^{1/p^∞}" is the basic setup whose ideal is generated by all x^{1/p^k}; in RA.0–RA.1 almost statements are elementwise (killed by every x^{1/p^k}) and need no flatness of the ideal. Products of root systems are root systems, so (tg)^{1/p^k} = t^{1/p^k}g^{1/p^k}.

K denotes a perfectoid field (RA.1, RA.3–RA.5) or a complete nonarchimedean field (RA.2), K° its valuation ring, K°° its maximal ideal, and ϖ ∈ K°° a pseudouniformizer with |p| ≤ |ϖ| < 1 and a root system; in characteristic 0 it is chosen as ϖ = (ϖ♭)^♯. Bhatt's t is a pseudouniformizer with a root system and |t| = |p| in characteristic 0. An **integral perfectoid K°-algebra** (Bhatt) is K°-flat, t-adically complete, equal to its almost elements, with Frobenius bijective from A/t^{1/p} to A/t; these correspond to perfectoid K-algebras through A ↦ A[1/t] and R ↦ R°.

**Uniform Banach K-algebras** are complete Tate K-algebras whose power-bounded subring A° is bounded, with continuous homomorphisms; equivalently their norm is equivalent to the spectral seminorm. A° is the unit ball of the spectral norm. B{f/λ} and A{λ/g} are the Weierstrass and Laurent localisations; the superscript u is separated completion for the spectral seminorm. The almost setups (K°, K°°), (K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩, T^{1/p^∞}K°°) and (K°[T^{1/p^∞}], (ϖT)^{1/p^∞}) are distinguished throughout; "almost" is always relative to a stated setup, and an almost statement is never upgraded to an exact one. For a K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩-algebra, g denotes the image of T.

In RA.5, k is a perfect field of characteristic p (not to be confused with the root index k of the Kummer tower, which is always a subscript), A = W(k)⟦T_1,…,T_n⟧, K_j = W(k)[1/p](ζ_{p^j}), and infinite indices mean directed unions; a hat means p-adic completion.

Generic Banach completions use the Gauss topology: complete an integral unit ball p-adically and then invert p. They are not algebraic (p)-adic completions of rings in which p is already a unit. Kummer root algebras retain all branches and their integral closures are relative to the specified ambient algebras; these models can have zero divisors.

## Layer RA.0: Almost-pro-zero towers and uniform annihilation

Almost-pro-zero towers are Bhatt's device for statements that hold "almost, after passing far enough in a tower", where how far may depend on the precision t^{1/p^k}. They are weaker than being zero as a pro-object of almost modules (Bhatt Example 3.3), but strong enough to make derived limits almost zero and to be preserved by linear functors. The uniform variant fixes the distance c(k) independently of the tower index; it is the form in which Riemann extension enters the direct summand theorems, because it survives derived tensor products. The cone argument proving that is written out here: Bhatt calls it formal.

**Targets of the layer.**

Fix a ring A and an element t with a chosen compatible system of p-power roots t^{1/p^k} (the root ideal of PerfectoidSpaces P0). Targets:

- **Almost-pro-zero towers** (Bhatt Definition 3.2): a tower {M_n}_{n≥1} of A-modules is almost-pro-zero when for every k and n there is m ≥ n with the image of M_m → M_n killed by t^{1/p^k}; equivalently {M_n[t^{1/p^k}]} → {M_n} is a pro-isomorphism for every k. A morphism of towers in D^b(A) is an **almost-pro-isomorphism** when every cohomology tower of its cone is almost-pro-zero. Example 3.3 (the tower A/t^{1/p^n} with transitions multiplication by t^{1/p^n−1/p^{n+1}}) is almost-pro-zero but not pro-zero in the almost category.
- **Uniform almost-pro-zero towers** relative to a second element g with compatible roots: for every k there is c with the c-step transitions killed by g^{1/p^k} in t-almost modules, for all n at once.
- **Derived limits** (Bhatt Lemmas 3.4–3.5): R lim of an almost-pro-zero tower is almost zero, and an almost-pro-isomorphism induces an almost isomorphism on R lim.
- **Linear functors** (Bhatt Lemma 3.6): an additive A-linear functor sends almost-pro-zero towers to almost-pro-zero towers.
- **Uniform annihilation survives derived tensor products** (the amplitude-zero cone argument behind Bhatt Remark 4.3): if R → R_n (R = A/t^m) has almost-zero kernels and uniformly g^{1/p^k}-annihilated cokernel transitions with constant c, then for every K ∈ D(R) and every i the kernel and cokernel towers of H^i(K) → H^i(K ⊗^L_R R_n) have c-step transitions killed by (tg)^{1/p^k}, with the same c.

Ordinary pro-zero towers, R lim and the Milnor sequence are imported from EnhancedDerivedSheaves E2; the derived tensor product of modules from E1.

### Almost-pro-zero towers and almost-pro-isomorphisms

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/almost-pro-zero-tower` (definition).** Let A be a commutative ring, p a prime, t ∈ A and (t^{1/p^k})_{k≥0} a chosen compatible system of p-power roots (t^{1/p^0} = t, (t^{1/p^{k+1}})^p = t^{1/p^k}); write m_t for the ideal generated by all t^{1/p^k}. A tower M = (M_n)_{n≥1} of A-modules, with transition maps ρ_{n′,n}: M_{n′} → M_n for n′ ≥ n satisfying ρ_{n,n} = id and ρ_{n′,n}∘ρ_{n″,n′} = ρ_{n″,n}, is almost-pro-zero (with respect to t^{1/p^∞}) if for every k ≥ 0 and every n ≥ 1 there is n′ ≥ n such that t^{1/p^k} annihilates the image of ρ_{n′,n}. Equivalently, for every k the inclusion of towers (M_n[t^{1/p^k}])_n → (M_n)_n, where M_n[t^{1/p^k}] is the t^{1/p^k}-torsion, is an isomorphism of pro-objects. A morphism of towers of A-modules is an almost-pro-isomorphism if its kernel and cokernel towers are almost-pro-zero; a morphism of towers in D^b(A) is an almost-pro-isomorphism if for every i the tower of i-th cohomology modules of its cone is almost-pro-zero.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Almost.IsAlmostProZero`.

**Planet.** Almost-pro-zero tower.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The index n′ may depend on k as well as on n. This is strictly weaker than the condition that the tower be zero as a pro-object of almost modules, where one n′ must serve every k (Bhatt Example 3.3).
- No hypothesis on t is needed for the definition. The derived-limit statements of this layer assume t is a nonzerodivisor, as in Bhatt Notation 3.1.

**Construction.**

1. If for each k the torsion subtower is pro-isomorphic to M, then for each n some transition ρ_{n′,n} factors through M_n[t^{1/p^k}], so its image is killed by t^{1/p^k}; conversely, if the image of ρ_{n′,n} is killed by t^{1/p^k}, the transition factors through the torsion submodule, which defines the inverse pro-morphism.
2. Almost-pro-zero towers form a Serre class among towers: subtowers and quotient towers inherit the property directly. For an extension 0 → M′ → M → M″ → 0, choose transitions into M″_n killed by t^{1/p^{k+1}} and then into M′ killed by t^{1/p^{k+1}}; the composite transition into M_n is killed by t^{2/p^{k+1}}, which divides t^{1/p^k} since p ≥ 2.
3. Hence almost-pro-isomorphisms of module towers satisfy two-out-of-three, and so do almost-pro-isomorphisms of towers in D^b(A), by the long exact cohomology sequences of cones.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/root-ideal-basic-setup`, `EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E2/pro-towers`.

**API.**

- `IsAlmostProZero` (characterisation): IsAlmostProZero t M holds iff for all k and n there is n′ ≥ n with t^{1/p^k} · ρ_{n′,n}(x) = 0 for every x ∈ M_{n′}.
- `IsAlmostProZero.iff_torsion_proIso` (equivalence): IsAlmostProZero t M iff for every k the inclusion of the t^{1/p^k}-torsion subtower is a pro-isomorphism.
- `IsAlmostProZero.of_isProZero` (compatibility): An ordinary pro-zero tower (EnhancedDerivedSheaves E2 IsProZero) is almost-pro-zero.
- `IsAlmostProZero.iff_isProZero_of_isUnit` (compatibility): If t is a unit of A then IsAlmostProZero t M ↔ IsProZero M.
- `IsAlmostProZero.of_almostZero` (other): A tower whose terms are almost zero (killed by m_t) is almost-pro-zero, with n′ = n.
- `IsAlmostProZero.subtower` (structure): Subtowers and quotient towers of an almost-pro-zero tower are almost-pro-zero.
- `IsAlmostProZero.extension` (structure): In a short exact sequence of towers, the middle tower is almost-pro-zero if the outer two are.
- `IsAlmostProZero.reindex` (compatibility): The property is invariant under cofinal monotone reindexing of the tower.
- `IsAlmostProIso` (characterisation): A morphism of module towers is an almost-pro-isomorphism iff its kernel and cokernel towers are almost-pro-zero; in D^b(A) iff all cohomology towers of the cone are.
- `IsAlmostProIso.comp` (structure): Almost-pro-isomorphisms are closed under composition and satisfy two-out-of-three.

**Unit tests.**

- `isAlmostProZero_bhatt_example` (computation): For A = K° the valuation ring of a perfectoid field K and t = ϖ with chosen roots, the tower M_n = A/ϖ^{1/p^n} with transitions multiplication by ϖ^{1/p^n − 1/p^{n+1}} is almost-pro-zero: every M_{n′} with n′ ≥ k is killed by ϖ^{1/p^k}.
- `bhatt_example_not_proZero_almost` (non-example): For the same tower, no single n′ works for all k: the image of M_{n+r} → M_n contains the class of ϖ^{1/p^n − 1/p^{n+r}}, which is killed by ϖ^{1/p^{n+r}} but is not killed by ϖ^{1/p^{n+r+1}}. So the pro-object of almost modules is not zero; a definition with n′ independent of k fails this test.
- `isAlmostProZero_zero` (degenerate): The zero tower, and any tower of almost zero modules with arbitrary transitions, is almost-pro-zero.
- `not_isAlmostProZero_const` (non-example): For A = K° and t = ϖ ≠ 0, the constant tower (A, id) is not almost-pro-zero: the image of every transition is A, which ϖ^{1/p^k} does not annihilate.
- `isAlmostProZero_iff_isProZero_unit` (compatibility): When t = 1 (so every t^{1/p^k} may be taken to be 1), IsAlmostProZero coincides with EnhancedDerivedSheaves E2 IsProZero.

**Uses.**

- Bhatt Theorem 4.2 (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/quantitative-riemann-extension): the cokernel tower of A/t^m → A⟨t^n/g⟩/t^m is almost-pro-zero, which makes the annular tower an almost-pro-isomorphism
- Bhatt Lemmas 3.4–3.5 (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/almost-pro-zero-derived-limit): R lim of an almost-pro-zero tower is almost zero
- Bhatt proof of Theorem 5.4 (direct summand theorem, in Direct summands and big Cohen–Macaulay algebras): the Hom tower Hom(Q_∞, A_∞⟨p^n/g⟩/p^m) is almost-pro-isomorphic to a constant tower, so its lim¹ is almost zero
- Bhatt proof of Theorem 6.1 (derived direct summand theorem): the same argument applied to RΓ of a proper map, through Remark 4.3

**Checks.**

- Bhatt Example 3.3 (computation test) and the comparison with ordinary pro-zero towers (compatibility test) pin the definition.

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Notation 3.1 and Definition 3.2, pp. 5–6. Defines almost-pro-zero pro-modules by the condition that for every k and n some later transition has image killed by t^{1/p^k}, gives the equivalent pro-isomorphism formulation, and defines almost-pro-isomorphisms in D^b(A) through cohomology of cones.
- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Example 3.3, p. 6. Exhibits the tower A/(t^{1/p^n}) with transitions multiplication by t^{1/p^n−1/p^{n+1}}, which is almost-pro-zero although the associated pro-object of almost modules is not zero.

### Uniformly almost-pro-zero towers relative to a second root system

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/uniformly-almost-pro-zero` (definition).** Let A and t be as in PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/almost-pro-zero-tower, and let g ∈ A carry a compatible system of p-power roots g^{1/p^k}. A tower (N_n)_{n≥1} of A-modules is uniformly almost-pro-zero with respect to g^{1/p^∞} (in t-almost modules), with constants c = (c(k))_{k≥0}, if for every k and every n ≥ 1 the image of the transition N_{n+c(k)} → N_n is annihilated by g^{1/p^k}·t^{1/p^l} for every l ≥ 0. A morphism from a constant tower f_n : L → L_n (compatible with the transitions of (L_n)) is a uniform almost-pro-isomorphism with respect to g^{1/p^∞}, with constants c, if every ker f_n is annihilated by m_t and the cokernel tower (coker f_n) is uniformly almost-pro-zero with constants c.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Almost.IsUniformlyAlmostProZero`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The constant c(k) must not depend on n; this uniformity is what survives derived tensor products (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/uniform-annihilation-derived-tensor).
- Annihilation by g^{1/p^k} is measured in t-almost modules: the image must be killed by g^{1/p^k}t^{1/p^l} for all l, not by g^{1/p^k} alone.

**Construction.**

1. A uniformly almost-pro-zero tower is almost-pro-zero with respect to the root system of tg: the image of N_{n+c(k)} → N_n is killed by g^{1/p^k}t^{1/p^k} = (tg)^{1/p^k}.
2. Enlarging any c(k) preserves the property, and subtowers and quotient towers inherit it with the same constants.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/almost-pro-zero-tower`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/root-ideal-basic-setup`.

**API.**

- `IsUniformlyAlmostProZero` (characterisation): IsUniformlyAlmostProZero t g c N holds iff for all k, n, l and x ∈ N_{n+c k}: g^{1/p^k} t^{1/p^l} · ρ(x) = 0 in N_n.
- `IsUniformlyAlmostProZero.isAlmostProZero_mul` (compatibility): A uniformly almost-pro-zero tower is almost-pro-zero with respect to the root system (tg)^{1/p^k} = t^{1/p^k}g^{1/p^k}.
- `IsUniformlyAlmostProZero.mono` (structure): If c ≤ c′ pointwise, constants c may be replaced by c′.
- `IsUniformlyAlmostProZero.subtower` (structure): Subtowers and quotient towers are uniformly almost-pro-zero with the same constants.
- `IsUniformAlmostProIso` (characterisation): A morphism from a constant tower is a uniform almost-pro-isomorphism iff its kernels are almost zero and its cokernel tower is uniformly almost-pro-zero.

**Unit tests.**

- `isUniformlyAlmostProZero_zero` (degenerate): The zero tower is uniformly almost-pro-zero with all constants 0.
- `isUniformlyAlmostProZero_zero_transitions` (computation): A tower with zero transition maps is uniformly almost-pro-zero with c(k) = 1 for every k, whatever its terms.
- `bhatt_example_not_uniform` (non-example): For g = 1 the tower A/ϖ^{1/p^n} of Bhatt Example 3.3 over A = K° is almost-pro-zero but not uniformly almost-pro-zero: the c-step image into M_n contains ϖ^{1/p^n−1/p^{n+c}}, which is not killed by ϖ^{1/p^l} once p^{-l} < p^{-(n+c)}; no constant independent of n works.
- `uniform_implies_almostProZero_tg` (compatibility): Every uniformly almost-pro-zero tower with respect to g^{1/p^∞} is almost-pro-zero in the sense of PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/almost-pro-zero-tower for the root system of tg.

**Uses.**

- Bhatt Theorem 4.2(2) (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/quantitative-riemann-extension): the cokernel tower of the annular comparison is uniformly almost-pro-zero with c(k) = p^k m
- Bhatt Remark 4.3 (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/quantitative-riemann-extension-derived): uniformity lets the estimate pass through K ⊗^L − for arbitrary complexes K with the same constants

**Checks.**

- The condition at k = 0 with g = 1 says the c(0)-step images are almost zero; Bhatt's Theorem 4.2 provides c(k) = p^k m.

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Theorem 4.2(2), p. 6, and Remark 4.3, p. 7. ### Derived limits of almost-pro-zero towers are almost zero

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/almost-pro-zero-derived-limit` (theorem).** Let A be a ring with a nonzerodivisor t carrying compatible p-power roots. (1) If (M_n)_{n≥1} is an almost-pro-zero tower of A-modules, then lim_n M_n and lim¹_n M_n are almost zero, i.e. annihilated by every t^{1/p^k}; hence R lim_n M_n has almost zero cohomology. (2) If (K_n) → (L_n) is an almost-pro-isomorphism of towers in D^b(A), then R lim K_n → R lim L_n is an almost isomorphism: the cohomology of its cone is annihilated by every t^{1/p^k}.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Almost.IsAlmostProZero.limit_isAlmostZero`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Towers are indexed by n ≥ 1 (a countable cofiltered index), so R lim has amplitude [0, 1] on module towers.
- For (2), the towers in D^b(A) are coherent towers in the sense of EnhancedDerivedSheaves E2, so that R lim and cones commute.

**Proof outline.**

1. Fix k. The inclusion of the t^{1/p^k}-torsion subtower is a pro-isomorphism, and pro-isomorphic towers have isomorphic lim and lim¹ (EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E2/pro-zero-derived-limit). The torsion subtower has lim and lim¹ annihilated by t^{1/p^k}, so the same holds for M.
2. For (2), R lim commutes with cones; the cohomology towers of the cone are almost-pro-zero by hypothesis, and the Milnor sequence (EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E2/tower-milnor) expresses each cohomology of R lim of the cone through lim and lim¹ of these towers, which are almost zero by (1).

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/almost-pro-zero-tower`, `EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E2/pro-zero-derived-limit`, `EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E2/tower-milnor`, `EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E2/coherent-towers-and-roos`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-zero-limits-and-colimits`.

**Checks.**

- For Bhatt Example 3.3 the limit is zero and lim¹ is almost zero.
- If t is a unit, the statement reduces to the vanishing of lim and lim¹ of pro-zero towers.

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Lemma 3.4, p. 6. Shows that R lim of an almost-pro-zero pro-module has almost zero cohomology, by replacing the tower with its t^{1/p^k}-torsion subtower.
- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Lemma 3.5, p. 6. Deduces that almost-pro-isomorphisms in D^b(A) induce almost isomorphisms on R lim, by applying Lemma 3.4 to the cone.

### Linear functors preserve almost-pro-zero towers

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/almost-pro-zero-linear-functor` (theorem).** Let A and t be as in PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/almost-pro-zero-tower, (M_n) an almost-pro-zero tower of A-modules and F : Mod_A → Mod_A an additive A-linear functor (F(a·φ) = a·F(φ) for a ∈ A and every A-linear map φ). Then (F(M_n))_n, with transitions F(ρ_{n′,n}), is almost-pro-zero. In particular this holds for Hom_A(N, −), Ext^i_A(N, −), N ⊗_A − and Tor_i^A(N, −) for any A-module N, and, termwise, a tower almost-pro-isomorphic to a constant tower stays so after applying such a functor.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Almost.IsAlmostProZero.map_linear`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- F need not be exact; only A-linearity on Hom groups is used.

**Proof outline.**

1. Fix k and n and choose n′ with ρ_{n′,n} factoring through M_n[t^{1/p^k}] ⊂ M_n. Then F(ρ_{n′,n}) factors through F(M_n[t^{1/p^k}]) → F(M_n).
2. The identity of M_n[t^{1/p^k}] multiplied by t^{1/p^k} is zero, so by A-linearity the identity of F(M_n[t^{1/p^k}]) multiplied by t^{1/p^k} is zero; hence the image of F(M_n[t^{1/p^k}]) → F(M_n) is killed by t^{1/p^k}.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/almost-pro-zero-tower`, `mathlib:CategoryTheory.Functor.Linear`.

**Checks.**

- Applied to Hom_A(Q, −) for a finitely presented Q this gives the almost vanishing of lim¹ of Hom towers used in Bhatt's proof of Theorem 5.4.

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Lemma 3.6, p. 6. Shows that A-linear endofunctors of A-modules carry almost-pro-zero pro-modules to almost-pro-zero pro-modules, by factoring transitions through torsion submodules.

### Uniform annihilation survives derived tensor products

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/uniform-annihilation-derived-tensor` (theorem).** Let R be a commutative ring with elements t and g carrying compatible p-power roots, and let (f_n : R → R_n)_{n≥1} be maps of R-modules into a tower (R_n) compatible with the transitions. Suppose every ker f_n is annihilated by m_t and the cokernel tower is uniformly almost-pro-zero with respect to g^{1/p^∞} with constants c(k) (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/uniformly-almost-pro-zero). Then for every complex K ∈ D(R) and every i ∈ Z, the kernel tower and the cokernel tower of H^i(K) → H^i(K ⊗^L_R R_n) have c(k)-step transitions annihilated by g^{1/p^k}t^{1/p^l} for every l, in particular by (tg)^{1/p^k}, for all n. The constants are independent of n, K and i.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Almost.uniformAlmostProIso_derivedTensor`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The R_n need only be R-modules; in the application they are the R-algebras A⟨t^n/g⟩/t^m over R = A/t^m.
- The constant c(k) is the one of the cokernel tower; no constant is lost.

**Proof outline.**

1. Let C_n be the cone of f_n, a complex with H^{−1}(C_n) = ker f_n and H^0(C_n) = coker f_n, and τ : C_{n+c} → C_n the transition, c = c(k).
2. The composite of γτ, with γ = g^{1/p^k}t^{1/p^l}, and C_n → H^0(C_n)[0] vanishes, because it factors through H^0 of the transition multiplied by γ. By the truncation triangle, γτ factors through H^{−1}(C_n)[1] → C_n.
3. Multiplication by t^{1/p^{l′}} kills H^{−1}(C_n), so t^{1/p^{l′}}γτ = 0 in D(R). Since m_t is idempotent, g^{1/p^k}m_t annihilates τ as a morphism of D(R) (use t^{1/p^l} = t^{1/p^{l+1}}·t^{(p−1)/p^{l+1}}).
4. Tensoring with K, g^{1/p^k}m_t annihilates K ⊗^L τ, hence its effect on cohomology. In the long exact sequence of K → K ⊗^L R_n → K ⊗^L C_n, the cokernel of H^i(K) → H^i(K ⊗^L R_n) embeds in H^i(K ⊗^L C_n) and the kernel is a quotient of H^{i−1}(K ⊗^L C_n), compatibly with transitions; so their c-step transitions are annihilated by g^{1/p^k}m_t.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/uniformly-almost-pro-zero`, `EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E1/presentability-and-derived-tensor`, `mathlib:DerivedCategory`.

**Checks.**

- For K = R the statement returns the hypothesis.
- For K a Koszul complex on an R-regular sequence it gives uniform almost-pro-vanishing of the higher Koszul homology of the R_n (Bhatt Remark 4.3).

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Remark 4.3, p. 7. Asserts that, because the constant in Theorem 4.2 is independent of n, the kernel and cokernel pro-systems of H^i(K) → H^i(K ⊗^L A⟨t^n/g⟩/t^m) are uniformly almost-pro-zero with the same constants for every complex K, and records the Koszul consequence; the cone argument written here supplies the justification Bhatt calls formal.

## Layer RA.1: The quantitative Riemann extension theorem

Throughout, A is an integral perfectoid K°-algebra and g ∈ A has a root system. The annular tower consists of the bounded functions on the rational subsets {|t^n| ≤ |g|}, which exhaust {g ≠ 0}. The layer proves that, modulo t^m, A is almost-pro-isomorphic to this tower with uniform constants c(k) = p^k m. The proof is a short computation on Scholze's explicit almost presentation of the annuli.

**Targets of the layer.**

Let K be a perfectoid field, t ∈ K° with compatible p-power roots and |t| = |p| in characteristic 0 (0 < |t| < 1 in general), A an integral perfectoid K°-algebra (Bhatt §1.4) with perfectoid space X = Spa(A[1/t], A), and g ∈ A with compatible p-power roots. Targets:

- **The annular tower** (Bhatt Notation 4.1): A⟨t^n/g⟩ = O⁺_X(X⟨t^n/g⟩) for the rational subsets X⟨t^n/g⟩ = {|t^n| ≤ |g|}, n ≥ 1, with restriction maps A⟨t^{n+1}/g⟩ → A⟨t^n/g⟩, and its explicit almost presentation A⟨t^n/g⟩/t^m ≅^a A[u_n^{1/p^∞}]/(t^m, (u_n g)^{1/p^k} − t^{n/p^k}) from PerfectoidSpaces P2.
- **Quantitative Riemann extension** (Bhatt Theorem 4.2, Theorem 1.6), for g an almost nonzerodivisor modulo t^m: the kernels of f_n : A/t^m → A⟨t^n/g⟩/t^m are almost zero, the cokernel tower is uniformly almost-pro-zero with respect to g^{1/p^∞} with constant c = p^k m, and {f_n} is an almost-pro-isomorphism with respect to (tg)^{1/p^∞}.
- **Derived version** (Bhatt Remark 4.3): for every K ∈ D(A/t^m) the comparison towers H^i(K) → H^i(K ⊗^L A⟨t^n/g⟩/t^m) are uniformly almost-pro-isomorphisms with the same constants; Koszul homology of a regular sequence becomes uniformly almost-pro-zero in nonzero degrees.
- **Removal of the regularity hypothesis** (Bhatt Remark 4.4), through the universal case K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩, g = T.
- **Riemann extension after limits** (Bhatt Remark 1.7): A → lim_n A⟨t^n/g⟩ is an almost isomorphism with respect to (tg)^{1/p^∞}, the principal case of Scholze's Hebbarkeitssatz in mixed characteristic.

### The annular tower A⟨t^n/g⟩ of an integral perfectoid algebra

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/annular-tower` (construction).** Let K be a perfectoid field and t ∈ K° a pseudouniformizer (0 < |t| < 1, with |t| = |p| if char K = 0) carrying compatible p-power roots. Let A be an integral perfectoid K°-algebra in Bhatt's sense: K°-flat, t-adically complete, equal to its almost elements A_* (if f ∈ A[1/t] and t^{1/p^k}f ∈ A for all k then f ∈ A), with Frobenius inducing a bijection A/t^{1/p} → A/t; let X = Spa(A[1/t], A) be its affinoid perfectoid space. Let g ∈ A carry compatible p-power roots g^{1/p^k}. For n ≥ 1 put X⟨t^n/g⟩ = {x ∈ X : |t^n(x)| ≤ |g(x)|}, a rational subset, and A⟨t^n/g⟩ = O⁺_X(X⟨t^n/g⟩). Since X⟨t^n/g⟩ ⊂ X⟨t^{n+1}/g⟩, restriction gives maps A⟨t^{n+1}/g⟩ → A⟨t^n/g⟩; with f_n : A → A⟨t^n/g⟩ they form a tower under A. In A⟨t^n/g⟩ the functions u_n^{1/p^k} := (t^n/g)^{1/p^k} are bounded, so g^{1/p^k}u_n^{1/p^k} = t^{n/p^k} and g divides t^n. For each m ≥ 0 the map A[u_n^{1/p^∞}]/(t^m, (u_n g)^{1/p^k} − t^{n/p^k} for all k) → A⟨t^n/g⟩/t^m is an almost isomorphism with respect to t^{1/p^∞}, and under these presentations the transition sends u_{n+1}^e to t^e u_n^e for e ∈ Z[1/p]_{≥0}.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Perfectoid.annularTower`.

**Planet.** Annular tower of a perfectoid algebra.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Correction PAPER-BHATT-18/E16: t must be a pseudouniformizer (0 < |t| < 1), which matters in characteristic p.
- The explicit almost presentation is Scholze's Lemma 6.4, supplied by PerfectoidSpaces P2; it holds as an almost isomorphism, not on the nose.
- Integral perfectoid K°-algebras and perfectoid K-algebras correspond through A ↦ A[1/t] and R ↦ R° (PerfectoidSpaces P1).

**Construction.**

1. Rational subsets of affinoid perfectoid spaces are affinoid perfectoid with explicit almost integral model (PerfectoidSpaces:P2/rational-localization-of-perfectoid-affinoids, P2/almost-integral-model-of-untilted-rational-localization). Since g has compatible roots, the generators t^n and g of X⟨t^n/g⟩ are already of the form required there, so no approximation is needed.
2. The model of A⟨t^n/g⟩ is the t-adic completion of A[u^{1/p^∞}]/((ug)^{1/p^k} − t^{n/p^k})_k, up to almost isomorphism; reducing modulo t^m gives the stated presentation.
3. Restriction from X⟨t^{n+1}/g⟩ to X⟨t^n/g⟩ sends t^{n+1}/g to t·(t^n/g), hence u_{n+1}^e ↦ t^e u_n^e on the presentations.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpaces:P1/integral-perfectoid-comparison`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P2/rational-localization-of-perfectoid-affinoids`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P2/almost-integral-model-of-untilted-rational-localization`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P2/perfectoid-pair-rational-restriction`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/root-ideal-basic-setup`.

**API.**

- `annularTower` (constructor): For (A, t, g) as in the statement and n ≥ 1, the K°-algebra A⟨t^n/g⟩ = O⁺_X(X⟨t^n/g⟩).
- `annularTower.restrict` (data): The restriction map A⟨t^{n′}/g⟩ → A⟨t^n/g⟩ for n′ ≥ n.
- `annularTower.restrict_comp` (functoriality): restrict n′ n ∘ restrict n″ n′ = restrict n″ n and restrict n n = id.
- `annularTower.toAnnular` (projection): The structure map f_n : A → A⟨t^n/g⟩, compatible with restrict.
- `annularTower.g_mul_u` (characterisation): In A⟨t^n/g⟩, g^{1/p^k}·u_n^{1/p^k} = t^{n/p^k}; in particular g divides t^n.
- `annularTower.almostPresentation` (equivalence): For m ≥ 0, A[u^{1/p^∞}]/(t^m, (ug)^{1/p^k} − t^{n/p^k}) → A⟨t^n/g⟩/t^m is an almost isomorphism with respect to t^{1/p^∞}.
- `annularTower.restrict_u` (simp): Under the presentations, restrict (n+c) n sends u_{n+c}^e to t^{ce}u_n^e.
- `annularTower.generic` (compatibility): A⟨t^n/g⟩[1/t] is the rational localisation O_X(X⟨t^n/g⟩) of PerfectoidSpaces P2.

**Unit tests.**

- `annularTower_unit` (degenerate): If g is a unit of A then X⟨t^n/g⟩ = X, A⟨t^n/g⟩ = A and every f_n is the identity.
- `annularTower_zero` (degenerate): If g = 0 then X⟨t^n/g⟩ is empty (t^n is invertible on X), so A⟨t^n/0⟩ = 0.
- `annularTower_disc` (computation): For A = K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩ and g = T, A⟨t^n/T⟩ is almost the t-adic completion of K°[T^{1/p^∞}, U^{1/p^∞}]/((TU)^{1/p^k} − t^{n/p^k})_k.
- `annularTower_not_injective_zero_divisor` (non-example): For A = K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩ × K° and g = (T, 0), X⟨t^n/g⟩ lies in the first component, so (0, 1) lies in the kernel of every f_n and is not almost zero: the hypothesis on g in Theorem 4.2(1) cannot be dropped for kernels.
- `annularTower_generic_eq_rational` (compatibility): A⟨t^n/g⟩[1/t] agrees with PerfectoidSpaces P2's rational localisation of A[1/t] at (t^n, g)/g.

**Uses.**

- Bhatt Theorem 4.2 (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/quantitative-riemann-extension): the tower compared with the constant tower A/t^m
- Bhatt proof of Theorem 5.4: on each A_∞⟨p^n/g⟩ the discriminant g divides p^n, so almost purity splits the base-changed finite extension almost
- Bhatt proof of Theorem 6.1: the same annuli for the derived direct summand theorem
- André A §4.2 (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.3/perfectoid-riemann-extension): A{ϖ^j/g}° is the same tower in the Banach-algebra normalisation

**Checks.**

- For A = K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩ and g = T the tower is that of closed annuli |t^n| ≤ |T| ≤ 1, and A⟨t^n/T⟩ is almost K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}, (t^n/T)^{1/p^∞}⟩ (compare André A (3.12)).

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, §1.4 Notation, p. 3, and Notation 4.1, p. 6. Defines integral perfectoid K°-algebras and the rings A⟨t^n/g⟩ of bounded functions on X⟨t^n/g⟩ for g with compatible roots.

### Bhatt's quantitative Riemann extension theorem

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/quantitative-riemann-extension` (theorem).** In the setting of PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/annular-tower, fix m ≥ 0 and assume that g is an almost nonzerodivisor modulo t^m (the kernel of multiplication by g on A/t^m is annihilated by m_t). Let f_n : A/t^m → A⟨t^n/g⟩/t^m. Then, in almost mathematics with respect to t^{1/p^∞}: (1) every ker f_n is almost zero; (2) for every k ≥ 0, with c = p^k m, for every n ≥ 1 the image of the transition coker f_{n+c} → coker f_n is annihilated by g^{1/p^k}; that is, the cokernel tower is uniformly almost-pro-zero with respect to g^{1/p^∞} with constants c(k) = p^k m; (3) consequently (f_n)_n is an almost-pro-isomorphism from the constant tower A/t^m with respect to (tg)^{1/p^∞}.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Perfectoid.quantitative_riemann_extension`.

**Planet.** Quantitative Riemann extension theorem.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The almost-regularity of g modulo t^m is used only for (1); PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/quantitative-riemann-extension-general removes it for the conclusion (3).
- The constant c = p^k m depends on k and m only.

**Proof outline.**

1. Replace A⟨t^n/g⟩/t^m by its almost presentation M_n = A[u_n^{1/p^∞}]/(t^m, (u_ng)^{1/p^k} − t^{n/p^k}) (annular-tower).
2. Kernels: since g, hence each g^{1/p^k}, is an almost nonzerodivisor on A/t^m, comparing coefficients of the monomials u_n^e shows A/t^m → M_n is almost injective.
3. Cokernels: the c-fold transition sends g^{1/p^k}u_{n+c}^e to g^{1/p^k}t^{ce}u_n^e. If e ≥ 1/p^k then ce ≥ m and the term is zero modulo t^m. If e < 1/p^k it equals g^{1/p^k−e}t^{ce}(gu_n)^e = g^{1/p^k−e}t^{(n+c)e}, which lies in the image of A/t^m. So g^{1/p^k} kills the image of coker f_{n+c} → coker f_n.
4. (3) follows from (1), (2) and PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/uniformly-almost-pro-zero.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/annular-tower`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/uniformly-almost-pro-zero`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.0/almost-pro-zero-tower`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P2/almost-integral-model-of-untilted-rational-localization`.

**Checks.**

- The constant c = p^k m works for every n.
- For m = 0 all rings are zero and the statement is empty.
- For A = K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩ and g = T, (3) is the integral almost form of the statement that bounded functions on the punctured perfectoid disc extend.
- The non-example of PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/annular-tower shows (1) fails without the hypothesis on g.

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Theorem 4.2 and its proof, pp. 6–7. States and proves the almost injectivity of A/t^m → A⟨t^n/g⟩/t^m, the uniform annihilation of cokernel transitions by g^{1/p^k} with c = p^k m, and the resulting almost-pro-isomorphism with respect to (tg)^{1/p^∞}, under the almost-regularity of g modulo t^m.
- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Theorem 1.6, p. 2. Introductory form of the same statement for A_∞, with t = p.

### Derived form of the quantitative Riemann extension theorem

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/quantitative-riemann-extension-derived` (theorem).** In the setting of PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/quantitative-riemann-extension (g almost regular modulo t^m, m ≥ 1), for every complex K ∈ D(A/t^m) and every i ∈ Z the kernel and cokernel towers of H^i(K) → H^i(K ⊗^L_{A/t^m} A⟨t^n/g⟩/t^m) have p^k m-step transitions annihilated by g^{1/p^k} in t-almost modules, hence by (tg)^{1/p^k} in modules, for every k and n; the constants do not depend on n, K or i. ### Quantitative Riemann extension without regularity of g

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/quantitative-riemann-extension-general` (theorem).** Let A be an integral perfectoid K°-algebra and g ∈ A with compatible p-power roots, with no regularity hypothesis on g. For every m ≥ 0 the map of towers (A/t^m)_n → (A⟨t^n/g⟩/t^m)_n is an almost-pro-isomorphism with respect to (tg)^{1/p^∞}.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Perfectoid.quantitative_riemann_extension_general`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Only the conclusion (3) of Theorem 4.2 survives; almost injectivity of each f_n can fail (see the non-example of PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/annular-tower).

**Proof outline.**

1. Universal case: R = K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩ with g = T. Here R/t^m = (K°/t^m)[T^{1/p^∞}], on which T is a nonzerodivisor, so Theorem 4.2 applies: (R/t^m) → (R⟨t^n/T⟩/t^m) is an almost-pro-isomorphism with respect to (tT)^{1/p^∞}.
2. There is a unique continuous K°-algebra map R → A sending T^{1/p^k} to g^{1/p^k} (the universal property of the perfected Tate algebra, PerfectoidSpaces:P1/perfected-tate-algebra, at the integral level). Base change along R/t^m → A/t^m preserves almost-pro-isomorphisms by the derived form (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/quantitative-riemann-extension-derived with K = A/t^m regarded over R/t^m).
3. The almost presentations of annular-tower are compatible with base change, so R⟨t^n/T⟩ ⊗_R A/t^m is almost A⟨t^n/g⟩/t^m; taking H^0 gives the claim.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/quantitative-riemann-extension-derived`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/annular-tower`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P1/perfected-tate-algebra`.

**Checks.**

- For g a nonzerodivisor modulo t^m this recovers Theorem 4.2(3).
- For g = 0 the root ideal of tg is zero, every module is almost zero for it, and the statement is empty; this matches A⟨t^n/0⟩ = 0.

**Sources.**

- Bhargav Bhatt, *On the direct summand conjecture and its derived variant*, Remark 4.4, p. 7. Drops the hypothesis that g be a nonzerodivisor modulo t^m by treating the universal case K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩, g = T, and base-changing along the map determined by the roots of g, using the explicit description of the annuli.

### Riemann extension after limits (principal Hebbarkeitssatz)

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.1/riemann-extension-almost-limit` (theorem).** Let A be an integral perfectoid K°-algebra and g ∈ A with compatible p-power roots. ### p-root closure and the localised complete integral closure

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/p-root-closure` (definition).** Let R → S be an injective map of commutative rings and p a prime. An element s ∈ S is p-radicial over R if s^{p^n} ∈ R for some n ≥ 0, and R is p-root closed in S if s ∈ S and s^p ∈ R imply s ∈ R. The p-root closure R^†_S is the smallest subring of S that contains R and is p-root closed in S; it is the increasing union of R_0 = R and R_{i+1} = the subring generated by the elements p-radicial over R_i. When R has characteristic p, or S = R[1/p], the p-radicial elements already form a subring, equal to R^†_S. For g ∈ R a nonzerodivisor of S with S = R[1/g], the localised complete integral closure is R^*_S = {s ∈ S : there is m with g^m s^n ∈ R for all n ≥ 0}; it is the set of s whose powers lie in a finitely generated R-submodule of S. One has R ⊂ R^†_S ⊂ R^+_S ⊂ R^*_S, where R^+_S is the integral closure.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.pRootClosure`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- André's complete integral closure is defined by finitely generated submodules of S; Mathlib's completeIntegralClosure uses a multiplier from the nonzerodivisors of R. For S = R[1/g] with g ∈ R⁰, André's is contained in Mathlib's (take the multiplier g^m); for a Noetherian domain and its fraction field both equal the integral closure.

**Construction.**

1. The union of the R_i is p-root closed (if s^p ∈ R_i then s ∈ R_{i+1}), and any p-root closed intermediate ring contains every R_i by induction; so the union is the minimum.
2. In characteristic p, (a + b)^{p^n} = a^{p^n} + b^{p^n} and (ab)^{p^n} = a^{p^n}b^{p^n}, so p-radicial elements form a subring; for S = R[1/p] André cites Roberts for the same conclusion.
3. A p-radicial element is integral, and an integral element of R[1/g] has its powers in the finitely generated module spanned by its first d powers, so R^† ⊂ R^+ ⊂ R^*.
4. A finitely generated R-submodule of R[1/g] lies in g^{-m}R for some m, which gives the description of R^*_S.

**Inputs.** `mathlib:integralClosure`, `mathlib:completeIntegralClosure`, `mathlib:IsAlmostIntegral`, `mathlib:Localization.Away`.

**API.**

- `pRootClosure` (constructor): pRootClosure p R S : Subalgebra R S, the smallest p-root closed intermediate ring.
- `IsPRootClosed` (characterisation): IsPRootClosed p T ↔ ∀ s, s^p ∈ T → s ∈ T, for a subalgebra T of S.
- `pRootClosure_isPRootClosed` (characterisation): pRootClosure p R S is p-root closed and is contained in every p-root closed subalgebra.
- `mem_pRootClosure_of_charP` (characterisation): If S has characteristic p, s ∈ pRootClosure p R S ↔ ∃ n, s^{p^n} ∈ R.
- `pRootClosure_le_integralClosure` (relation): pRootClosure p R S ≤ integralClosure R S.
- `completeIntegralClosureAway` (constructor): For g ∈ R, the subalgebra {s ∈ R[1/g] : ∃ m, ∀ n, g^m s^n ∈ R} of Localization.Away g.

- `completeIntegralClosureAway_le_completeIntegralClosure` (compatibility): If g ∈ R⁰, completeIntegralClosureAway R g ≤ Mathlib's completeIntegralClosure R R[1/g].

### The norm–unit-ball dictionary for uniform Banach algebras

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/unit-ball-dictionary` (theorem).** Let K be complete for a nontrivial nonarchimedean absolute value, ϖ ∈ K°° nonzero and Γ ⊂ R with |ϖ|^Γ = |K^×|. (a) For a K°-flat K°-algebra A, the gauge |a| = |ϖ|^r with r = sup{s ∈ Γ : ϖ^{-s}a ∈ A} is a seminorm on A[1/ϖ], a norm iff A has no nonzero infinitely ϖ-divisible element; its unit ball is A when Γ is discrete and A_* = ⋂_{s ∈ Γ_{>0}} ϖ^{-s}A otherwise; a ring map φ : A[1/ϖ] → A′[1/ϖ] with φ(A) ⊂ A′ is continuous, and φ^{-1}(A′) = A is sufficient for it to be isometric. The exact criterion is φ^{-1}(A′_{≤1}) = A_{≤1} for the actual gauge unit balls (equal to A_* and A′_* in the nondiscrete case). (b) If |K| is dense in the norm values of a normed K-algebra A with unit ball A_{≤1}, then A^†_A ⊂ A^+_A ⊂ A^*_A = A° for the closures of A_{≤1}, and (A^†_A)_* is the unit ball of the spectral seminorm. (c) Under (b) the following are equivalent: the norm is spectral (power-multiplicative); A_{≤1} = A°; A_{≤1} is completely integrally closed in A; it is integrally closed in A; it is p-root closed in A; and, when |ϖ| ≥ |p| and 1/p ∈ Γ, Frobenius A_{≤1}/ϖ^{1/p} → A_{≤1}/ϖ is injective. (d) If |K| is dense in R_{>0}, B ↦ B° is an equivalence from uniform Banach K-algebras with continuous homomorphisms to K°-algebras B that are K°-flat, ϖ-adically complete, completely integrally closed (equivalently integrally closed, or p-root closed) in B[1/ϖ], and equal to Hom_{K°}(K°°, B); a quasi-inverse is B ↦ (B[1/ϖ], gauge norm). The same holds relative to a fixed uniform Banach K-algebra.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.UniformBanach.unitBall_dictionary`.

**Planet.** Norm–unit-ball dictionary.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Uniform means that A° is bounded, equivalently that the norm is equivalent to the spectral seminorm (with the corrected constant of PAPER-ANDRE-18/E20).
- Density of |K| in R_{>0} holds when the valuation of K is not discrete, for instance for perfectoid K.

**Proof outline.**

1. Construct the gauge using flatness. A linear map is an isometry exactly when it preserves and reflects every norm bound, equivalently the actual gauge unit ball and its scalar dilates. Equality φ^{-1}(A′) = A implies this after taking the intersections defining A_*; the converse for arbitrary lattices is not asserted.
2. (b) An element is power-bounded iff its powers lie in a finitely generated A_{≤1}-submodule, so A^*_A = A°. The gauge of the p-root closure is lim |a^{p^n}|^{1/p^n}, the spectral seminorm, by Fekete's lemma.
3. (c) combines (a), (b) and the inclusion of closures; Frobenius injectivity modulo ϖ is p-root closedness read on reductions.
4. (d) The four conditions characterise unit balls of spectral norms by (a) and (c); continuous maps of uniform algebras preserve power-bounded elements.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/p-root-closure`, `AdicSpacesPartII:R0/spectral-seminorm`, `AdicSpacesPartII:R3/uniform-spectral-seminorm`, `AdicSpacesPartII:R3/uniform-iff-power-bound`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/valuation-ring-basic-setup`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-hom-and-adjoints`.

**Checks.**

- André's example K°[ϖT, …, ϖ^{i+1}T^{p^i}, …] ⊂ K°[T] (Remarque 2.3.3(2)) shows A^+ ⊂ A^* = A° ⊂ spectral unit ball can all be strict, and that a complete integral closure need not be completely integrally closed.
- For densely valued K, take A = K° + K°°T K°[T] ⊂ A′ = K°[T]. Both generic fibres are K[T] and both gauge norms are Gauss, since scalar factors of norm just above 1 clear each of the finitely many coefficients. Thus the identity is isometric although T ∈ A′ ∖ A. This distinguishes a gauge unit ball from the original lattice.

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *Le lemme d’Abhyankar perfectoïde*, Sorite 2.3.1 with proof, pp. 16–19. Gives the dictionary between flat K°-algebras and norms: gauge norm and its unit ball, continuity and isometry criteria, the chain of closures equal to A° and the spectral unit ball, and six equivalent characterisations of spectrality.

### The root saturation g^{-1/p^∞}B° and its closedness

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/root-saturation` (construction).** Let K have |K| dense in R_{>0} and let B be a uniform Banach K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩-algebra, with g^{1/p^m} the image of T^{1/p^m}; equivalently a uniform Banach K-algebra with a compatible system of p-power roots of g ∈ B°. The root saturation is g^{-1/p^∞}B° = ⋂_m g^{-1/p^m}B° = {b ∈ B°[1/g] : g^{1/p^m}b ∈ B° for all m}. In the almost setup (V, m) = (K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩, T^{1/p^∞}K°°V) it is the module of almost elements: (B°ᵃ)_* = lim along multiplication by g^{1/p^m − 1/p^{m+1}} of B°, which maps injectively to B°[1/g] with image g^{-1/p^∞}B°; this image is ϖ-adically complete, and B° → g^{-1/p^∞}B° is injective iff g is a nonzerodivisor of B. ### Laurent localisation A{λ/g} of a uniform Banach algebra

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/laurent-localisation` (construction).** Let K be complete nonarchimedean and A a uniform Banach K-algebra. For f_1, …, f_n, f ∈ A generating the unit ideal, A{f_1,…,f_n/f} = A⟨U_1,…,U_n⟩/(fU_i − f_i)^−, the quotient by the closure of the ideal; it is the universal Banach A-algebra in which f is invertible and the f_i/f are power-bounded. For 0 ≠ λ ∈ K° and g = λf ∈ A°, A{λ/g} := A{1/f} = A⟨U⟩/(fU − 1)^−, regarded as a K⟨T⟩-algebra through T ↦ g. Properties: the ideal (fU − 1) is closed when A is uniform, so the closure is unnecessary; A{λ/g} ≅ A ⊗̂_{K⟨T⟩} K⟨T⟩{λ/T} isometrically; A{λ/g} = 0 when g ∈ λA°°; for |λ| ≤ |μ| there are canonical maps A{λ/g} → A{μ/g} and A{μ/g} ≅ A{λ/g}{μ/g}; if |g − h| < |λ| then A{λ/g} ≅ A{λ/h}; the image of a ∈ A has quotient norm inf_{h ∈ A⟨U⟩} ‖a + (fU − 1)h‖_Gauss, not in general inf_m |(g/λ)^m a|. The uniformization A{λ/g}^u (AdicSpacesPartII R0) is the left adjoint, on uniform K⟨T⟩-algebras, of the inclusion of those in which T/λ is invertible with power-bounded inverse. Gabber's invariance: if g is a nonzerodivisor and A ⊂ A′ ⊂ A[1/g] is a Banach algebra extension, then A{λ/g} → A′{λ/g} is an isomorphism. If moreover A is uniform, A° → lim_{λ→0} A{λ/g}° is injective.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.UniformBanach.laurentLocalisation`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The lower-bound slip in Lemme 2.6.3 and the stronger false norm formula on pp. 28–29 are separate issues (E3 and E11). The limit-injectivity conclusion is retained using a spectral-point argument. Restriction maps between individual annuli need not be injective (E4).
- A{λ/g} need not be uniform; only its uniformization is used in the uniform category.

**Construction.**

1. A{λ/g} is the rational localisation of the Huber pair (A, A°) at {λ, g}/g (AdicSpaces Layer 3 and AdicSpacesPartII R0), so its universal property is the one of rational localisation.
2. For closedness (André A Lemme 2.6.1, after Mihara), replace the uniform norm by the equivalent spectral norm. At each bounded multiplicative point x, extend to the Gauss seminorm on A⟨U⟩: ‖(fU−1)h‖_x = max(1,|f(x)|)‖h‖_x ≥ ‖h‖_x. Taking the supremum over x gives the spectral Gauss norm and bounds multiplication by fU−1 below. Its image is therefore complete and closed. The same closedness holds for the original equivalent norm.
3. The completed-tensor presentation and transition maps follow from their presentations and universal properties. If ‖g−h‖ < |λ|, use the convergent expansion 1/h = Σ_{m≥0}(g−h)^m/g^{m+1} inside A{λ/g}; the difference 1/h−1/g starts at m = 1. The norm is the quotient norm, obtained by varying all h ∈ A⟨U⟩.
4. Gabber's invariance: by Baire and the open mapping theorem, multiplication by a power g^N maps A′ continuously into A, which gives a continuous inverse after inverting g in A{λ/g}.
5. For nonzero a and a nonzerodivisor g, ag ≠ 0. The spectral seminorm of a uniform Banach algebra is a norm, so choose a bounded multiplicative seminorm x with |a(x)g(x)| > 0 (AdicSpacesPartII R3). Choose nonzero λ ∈ K° with |λ| ≤ |g(x)|. This point extends to A{λ/g}, where it detects the image of a. Hence A → lim_{λ→0} A{λ/g}, and therefore A° → lim A{λ/g}°, is injective. No lower bound uniform in all points is needed.

**Inputs.** `AdicSpacesPartII:R0/uniformization`, `AdicSpacesPartII:R0/uniform-completed-tensor-product`, `AdicSpacesPartII:R0/spectral-seminorm`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/unit-ball-dictionary`, `AdicSpacesPartII:R3/uniform-spectral-seminorm`.

**API.**

- `laurentLocalisation` (constructor): A{λ/g} for 0 ≠ λ ∈ K° and g ∈ A°, as the Banach quotient A⟨U⟩/((g/λ)U−1), with its K⟨T⟩-algebra structure T ↦ g.

### Weierstrass localisation B{f/λ} and its unit ball

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/weierstrass-localisation-unit-ball` (theorem).** Let K be complete for a nontrivial valuation, 0 ≠ λ ∈ K°, B a uniform Banach K-algebra and f ∈ B_{≤1}. Then B{f/λ} = B⟨U⟩/(λU − f), the ideal being closed; if f is a nonzerodivisor of B then so is λU − f in B⟨U⟩. Suppose |K| is dense in |B| and multiplication by f is isometric (e.g. the norm is multiplicative and |f| = 1). Then B_{≤1}⟨U⟩/(λU − f) is torsion-free over K°, (λU − f)B_{≤1}⟨U⟩ = ((λU − f)B⟨U⟩)_{≤1}, and B{f/λ}_{≤1} = B_{≤1}⟨U⟩/(λU − f) if the valuation is discrete, B{f/λ}_{≤1} = (B_{≤1}⟨U⟩/(λU − f))_* otherwise. Moreover (λU − f)B_{≤1}⟨U⟩ is closed, B_{≤1}⟨U⟩/(λU − f) is ϖ-adically separated and equals the ϖ-adic completion of B_{≤1}[f/λ] ⊂ B, through the exact sequence 0 → (λU − f)B_{≤1}[U] → B_{≤1}[U] → B_{≤1}[f/λ] → 0 and its completion. Finally B_{≤1}⟨U⟩/(λU − f) does not change, up to isomorphism, when f is replaced by f′ with |f − f′| ≤ |λ| or λ by λ′ with |λ′| = |λ|; the map from the new presentation to the old one sends U_new ↦ (λ/λ′)U_old + (f′−f)/λ′.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.UniformBanach.weierstrass_unitBall`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- André's argument for torsion-freeness is written for λ-torsion; the same computation with an arbitrary nonzero μ ∈ K° gives torsion-freeness over K°, which is what is used (the extraction's E14 records this reading; its review rejected it as an error).
- The conditional in formula (2) of André B reads 'if the valuation of K is discrete' (misprint PAPER-ANDRE-18-B/E15).

**Proof outline.**

1. Closedness of the ideal is the Weierstrass case of André A Lemme 2.6.1 (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/laurent-localisation).
2. If μ·Σ b_m U^m = (λU − f)Σ a_m U^m with a_m ∈ B_{≤1}, comparing coefficients and using that multiplication by f is injective modulo μ (it is isometric) shows μ divides every a_m; this gives torsion-freeness and the equality of unit balls, then (2)/(3) by the gauge dictionary (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/unit-ball-dictionary).
3. The polynomial sequence is exact by the same torsion-freeness; its completion is right exact with dense image of (λU − f)B_{≤1}⟨U⟩ in the kernel, and closedness gives exactness, whence (4).
4. The substitution U_new ↦ (λ/λ′)U_old + (f′−f)/λ′ is bounded on integral Tate algebras and sends λ′U_new−f′ to λU_old−f. Its inverse is U_old ↦ (λ′/λ)U_new+(f−f′)/λ; this proves invariance in the indicated direction.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/laurent-localisation`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/unit-ball-dictionary`, `mathlib:IsAdicComplete`.

**Checks.**

- For B = K⟨T⟩ (Gauss norm), f = T and λ = ϖ, B{T/ϖ} = K⟨T/ϖ⟩ is the disc of radius |ϖ| and its unit ball is K°⟨U⟩ with T = ϖU.
- The invariance under |f − f′| ≤ |λ| is what allows André B to replace (T − g)^{1/p^k} by an element of a Noetherian stage (formula (14)).

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, §1.2, formula (1), p. 76. Defines B{f/λ} as B⟨U⟩ modulo the closure of (λU − f), notes the ideal is closed, and that λU − f is a nonzerodivisor when f is.
- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, §1.2.1, formulas (2)–(3), pp. 76–77. Under density and isometric multiplication by f, proves torsion-freeness of B_{≤1}⟨U⟩/(λU − f) and identifies the unit ball of B{f/λ} with it (discrete case) or its almost elements (otherwise).
- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, §1.2.2, formulas (4)–(6), p. 77. Shows the quotient is separated and equals the completion of B_{≤1}[f/λ], via the exact sequence (5) and its completion (6).
- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, §1.2.3, p. 77. States the invariance of the integral Weierstrass algebra under approximation of f and rescaling of λ.

### Uniform limits of uniform Banach algebras

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/uniform-limit` (construction).** Let K be complete nonarchimedean. The category K-uBan of uniform Banach K-algebras with continuous homomorphisms has all small limits. For a projective system (A_α) indexed by a (not necessarily filtered) ordered set, the uniform limit is ulim A_α = {a = (a_α) ∈ lim A_α : sup_α |a_α|_sp < ∞} with the norm sup_α |a_α|_sp; it is a spectral Banach K-algebra and (ulim A_α)° = lim A_α°. Consequently the four conditions of the unit-ball dictionary are preserved by limits. The map (ulim A_α)°/ϖ → lim(A_α°/ϖ) is injective.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.UniformBanach.ulim`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The uniform limit is computed with spectral norms; it differs in general from the limit in perfectoid algebras (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.3/uniform-limit-perfectoid-criterion).
- |K| dense in R_{>0} is needed for the unit-ball statements.

**Construction.**

1. Equalizers are closed subalgebras and products exist (bounded families in spectral norm), so the category is complete; the explicit formula is the limit of sets cut down by boundedness.
2. The unit ball for the sup of spectral norms is the set of families with every a_α power-bounded, i.e. lim A_α°.
3. Injectivity modulo ϖ: each A_α° is ϖ-torsion-free, so 0 → lim A_α° —ϖ→ lim A_α° → lim(A_α°/ϖ) is exact.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/unit-ball-dictionary`, `mathlib:CategoryTheory.Limits.limit`.

**API.**

- `ulim` (constructor): ulim A_α : a uniform (spectral) Banach K-algebra for a projective system of uniform Banach K-algebras.
- `ulim.π` (projection): The projections ulim A_α → A_α, continuous homomorphisms compatible with the system.
- `ulim.lift` (universal-property): Compatible continuous maps B → A_α from a uniform Banach algebra B factor uniquely through ulim A_α.
- `ulim.norm_eq` (simp): |a| = sup_α |a_α|_sp.
- `ulim.powerBounded_eq` (characterisation): (ulim A_α)° = lim A_α° as subrings of lim A_α.
- `ulim.reduction_injective` (relation): (ulim A_α)°/ϖ → lim(A_α°/ϖ) is injective.
- `ulim.map` (functoriality): A morphism of projective systems induces a map of uniform limits, with map_id and map_comp.

**Unit tests.**

- `ulim_const` (degenerate): The uniform limit of a constant tower (or a constant diagram on a nonempty connected category) A with identity maps is A with its spectral norm.
- `ulim_annuli` (computation): ulim_j K⟨T, ϖ^j/T⟩ = K⟨T⟩ (André A Exemple 2.8.2(2)).
- `ulim_discs` (computation): The uniform limit of the closed discs of radius r_i ↑ 1 is K°⟦T⟧ ⊗_{K°} K.
- `ulim_ne_lim` (non-example): For the discs of radius r_i ↑ 1 the algebraic limit contains unbounded series such as Σ T^n/ϖ^{⌊√n⌋}, which are not in the uniform limit: the boundedness condition cannot be dropped.

**Uses.**

- André A Théorème 4.2.2 (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.3/perfectoid-riemann-extension): ulim A{ϖ^j/g} = g^{-1/p^∞}A when multiplication by g is isometric
- André A Théorème 4.4.2 and §5 (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.3/nested-systems-theorem, RA.4): the uniform limit is the inverse of localisation on nested systems and builds the Abhyankar envelope B = ulim B′ ⊗ A[j]

**Sources.**

## Layer RA.3: Almost perfectoid algebras and the perfectoid Riemann extension theorem

The perfectoid coreflection ♮ = ♯∘♭ makes perfectoid algebras a coreflective subcategory of uniform Banach algebras. Almost perfectoid algebras are the objects that become perfectoid in the almost category generated by the roots of T, and they are the natural output of limits. The central theorem is André's perfectoid Riemann extension: the uniform limit of the annular localisations A{ϖ^j/g} is the root saturation g^{-1/p^∞}A°, which is the complete integral closure of A° in A[1/g]. Combined with Scholze's limit lemma and a lim¹ estimate, it shows that nested systems of perfectoid algebras on the annuli are equivalent to (a coreflective part of) almost perfectoid algebras.

**Targets of the layer.**

K is a perfectoid field of residue characteristic p and ϖ ∈ K°° with pK° ⊂ ϖK° and compatible p-power roots. Targets:

- **The perfectoid coreflection** ♮ = ♯∘♭, right adjoint to the inclusion of perfectoid K-algebras into uniform Banach K-algebras (André A Proposition 3.3.3).
- **Perfectoid root algebras**: K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩-algebras classify compatible root systems (Exemple 3.2.3(1)); A⟨g^{1/p^∞}⟩ is perfectoid with g = (g♭)♯ for A perfectoid (§3.6.1).
- **Almost perfectoid K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩-algebras** (Définition 3.5.2, Proposition 3.5.4, Lemme 3.5.5, Proposition 3.5.6) in the almost setup (K°⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩, T^{1/p^∞}K°°).
- **Annular localisations** A{ϖ^j/g} of a perfectoid K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩-algebra are perfectoid with integral model (3.16) and tilt A♭{(ϖ♭)^j/g♭} (Proposition 3.6.1).
- **Scholze's limit lemma** (Proposition 4.2.1): R → lim_j R[j] is an almost isomorphism in (K°[T^{1/p^∞}], (ϖT)^{1/p^∞}).
- **The perfectoid Riemann extension theorem** (Théorème 4.2.2, Lemme 4.2.3): for A (almost) perfectoid and g ∈ A° a nonzerodivisor with compatible roots, lim_j A{ϖ^j/g}° = g^{-1/p^∞}A°, the complete integral closure of A° in A[1/g]; the **largest perfectoid subalgebra** of A[1/g] (Corollaire 4.2.4).
- **Uniform limits versus perfectoid limits** (Proposition 4.3.1, Remarque 4.3.1), **lim¹-vanishing for nested systems** (Proposition 4.4.1) and the **nested-systems theorem** (Théorème 4.4.2): uniform limits of compatible systems of perfectoid A{ϖ^j/g}-algebras are almost perfectoid, and ulim is left quasi-inverse and right adjoint to localisation.

### The perfectoid coreflection A ↦ A^♮

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.3/perfectoid-coreflection` (construction).** Let K be a perfectoid field. For a uniform Banach K-algebra A, A♭ = lim_{x↦x^p} A is a perfectoid K♭-algebra (PerfectoidSpaces P1, tilt of a uniform Tate ring), and A^♮ := (A♭)^♯ is the perfectoid K-algebra corresponding to it under the tilting equivalence. There is a canonical map A^♮ → A; with spectral norms it is isometric, and it is an isomorphism iff A is perfectoid. The functor ♮ is right adjoint to the inclusion of perfectoid K-algebras into uniform Banach K-algebras, so perfectoid K-algebras form a coreflective full subcategory, and ♭ is right adjoint to ι∘♯. In characteristic p, A^♮ = ulim_F A. Limits of perfectoid algebras exist and are computed as (ulim)^♮.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Perfectoid.coreflection`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The counit A^♮ → A need not be surjective, and A^♮°/ϖ → A°/ϖ is injective.
- In characteristic p, Frobenius is semilinear over K, not generally K-linear. The tower ulim_F is formed on underlying normed rings with the successive K-actions twisted by Frobenius; it then has the compatible K-algebra structure required by the coreflection.

**Construction.**

1. A^♮°/ϖ ≅ A♭°/ϖ♭ → A°/ϖ is injective (André A Proposition 3.3.1), which gives the isometry of the counit; ♮ restricted to perfectoid algebras is the identity, which gives the adjunction.
2. ♭ restricted to perfectoid algebras has ♯ as quasi-inverse, so ♭ = ♭|∘♮ is right adjoint to ι∘♯ by composing adjunctions.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpaces:P1/tilt-of-uniform-tate-ring`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P1/tilting-equivalence-and-explicit-tilt`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P1/perfectoid-tate-rings-and-algebras`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/uniform-limit`.

**API.**

- `coreflection` (constructor): coreflection A : the perfectoid K-algebra (A♭)^♯ for a uniform Banach K-algebra A.

- `coreflection.counit_isometry` (relation): For spectral norms the counit is isometric.
- `coreflection.counit_isIso_iff` (characterisation): The counit is an isomorphism iff A is perfectoid.
- `coreflection.adjunction` (universal-property): Hom_{Perf}(P, A^♮) ≅ Hom_{uBan}(P, A) for perfectoid P, naturally.
- `coreflection.tilt` (compatibility): (A^♮)♭ = A♭, and in characteristic p A^♮ = ulim_F A.

**Unit tests.**

- `coreflection_perfectoid` (degenerate): For a perfectoid K-algebra A, coreflection A ≅ A via the counit.

### Scholze's limit lemma for root localisations

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.3/scholze-limit-lemma` (theorem).** Let K be perfectoid of residue characteristic p, ϖ ∈ K°° with pK° ⊂ ϖK° and roots ϖ^{1/p^i} as above, r ∈ N[1/p], and R a (K°/ϖ^r)[T^{1/p^∞}]-algebra. Put R[j] = R ⊗_{K°[T^{1/p^∞}]} K°[T^{1/p^∞}, (ϖ^j/T)^{1/p^∞}] for j ∈ N, with transition maps sending u^s_{[j+1]} = 1 ⊗ (ϖ^{j+1}/T)^s to ϖ^s u^s_{[j]}; note T^s u^s_{[j]} = ϖ^{js}. Then the canonical map R → lim_j R[j] is an almost isomorphism in the setup (K°[T^{1/p^∞}], (ϖT)^{1/p^∞}K°[T^{1/p^∞}]).

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Perfectoid.scholze_limit_lemma`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Lim and lim¹ over an ordered set may be computed on a cofinal subset; the statement is algebraic (no completion).

**Proof outline.**

1. Introduce the submodules R[j]_k = Σ_{s ∈ N[1/p] ∩ [0, 1/p^k]} R·u^s_{[j]}. Since u^s_{[j′]} maps to 0 in R[j] when s(j′ − j) ≥ r, the transition R[j′] → R[j] factors through R[j]_k for j′ ≥ j + rp^k; hence lim_j R[j] = lim_{j,k} R[j]_k.
2. T^{1/p^k} maps R[j]_k into the image S_j of R, so (S_j)ᵃ = lim_k (R[j]_k)ᵃ.
3. The kernel of R → S_j is killed by T^{r/j} for j = p^ℓ, by comparing coefficients of a presentation. Combining gives Rᵃ = lim_j (S_j)ᵃ = lim_j R[j]ᵃ.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/root-ideal-basic-setup`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-zero-limits-and-colimits`.

**Checks.**

- For R = K°/ϖ^r with T acting by 1 every R[j] is R and the statement is trivial.

### André's perfectoid Riemann extension theorem

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.3/perfectoid-riemann-extension` (theorem).** Let K be a perfectoid field of residue characteristic p, A a perfectoid (or almost perfectoid) K-algebra, and g ∈ A° a nonzerodivisor admitting a compatible system of p-power roots in A. Moreover, for every perfectoid K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩-algebra B with g the image of T, g^{-1/p^∞}B° is completely integrally closed in B[1/g] (André A Lemme 4.2.3). If multiplication by g is isometric, ulim_j A{ϖ^j/g} = g^{-1/p^∞}A, the complete integral closure of A in A[1/g].

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Perfectoid.riemann_extension`.

**Planet.** Perfectoid Riemann extension theorem.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- The roots of g are part of the hypothesis; without them, André only shows the limit is contained in the complete integral closure, under an Ax–Sen–Tate type invariance (Remarque 4.2.5).

**Proof outline.**

1. Reduce the almost perfectoid case to the perfectoid case by replacing A with A^♮, which still contains the roots of g.
2. 3. Apply almost elements, which commute with limits, and the root-saturation formula (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/root-saturation): g^{-1/p^∞}A° = lim_j A{ϖ^j/g}°.

### When is a uniform limit of perfectoid algebras perfectoid

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.3/uniform-limit-perfectoid-criterion` (theorem).** Let K be perfectoid of characteristic 0, ϖ with pK° ⊂ ϖK° and ϖ_{1/p} of norm |ϖ|^{1/p}. For a projective system (A_α) of perfectoid K-algebras with uniform limit A (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/uniform-limit) and perfectoid limit A^♮, consider: (1) A is perfectoid; (2) A^♮ → A is an isomorphism; (3) A^♮°/ϖ → lim(A_α°/ϖ) is almost surjective; (4) the Frobenius-induced map lim(A_α°/ϖ) ⊗_{K°/ϖ, F} K°/ϖ → lim(A_α°/ϖ) is almost surjective; (5) lim(A_α°/ϖ) → lim(A_α°/ϖ_{1/p}) is almost surjective. Then (1) ⇔ (2) ⇐ (3) ⇔ (4) ⇔ (5). The same argument in the setup (K°[T^{1/p^∞}], T^{1/p^∞}K°°K°[T^{1/p^∞}]) shows: if (A_α) are perfectoid K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩-algebras and lim(A_α°/ϖ) → lim(A_α°/ϖ_{1/p}) is almost surjective, then A is almost perfectoid and A°/ϖ → lim(A_α°/ϖ) is an almost isomorphism.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Perfectoid.ulim_isAlmostPerfectoid_of`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- A uniform limit of perfectoid algebras need not be perfectoid (André A Exemple prophylactique 3.8.5).

**Proof outline.**

1. (A^♮)♭° = A♭° = lim_F L with L = lim(A_α°/ϖ) (formula (3.17)).
2. (4) ⇔ (5): the Frobenius map factors through L/ϖ_{1/p} → L_{1/p} and the compatible Frobenius isomorphisms of the A_α.
3. (4) ⇒ (3): almost surjectivity of Frobenius gives surjectivity of F on K°°L, hence, by Mittag-Leffler for F_p-vector spaces, almost surjectivity of lim_F L → L.
4. (3) + (4) ⇒ (1): A°/ϖ → L is injective (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/uniform-limit), hence an almost isomorphism, and Frobenius on A°/ϖ is almost surjective; (1) ⇔ (2) by the coreflection.

### Vanishing of lim¹ for nested root-localisation systems

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.3/nested-lim-one-vanishing` (theorem).** In the setting of PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.3/scholze-limit-lemma, work in (K°[T^{1/p^∞}], (ϖT)^{1/p^∞}K°[T^{1/p^∞}]). Let (R_i)_{i∈N} be a projective system of (K°/ϖ^r)[T^{1/p^∞}]-algebras such that for all i ≥ j the canonical maps R_i[j] → R_j[j] ← R_j are almost isomorphisms. Then lim¹_j (R_j)ᵃ = 0.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Perfectoid.nested_limOne_almostZero`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Only the nested condition R_i[j] ≅ R_j ≅ R_j[j] (almost) is used.

**Proof outline.**

1. Introduce the triple system R_i[j]_k of submodules spanned by the u^s for s ≤ 1/p^k; as in the limit lemma, lim¹_j (R_j)ᵃ = lim¹_{i,j,k}(R_i[j]_k)ᵃ.
2. Bound cokernels of transitions: R_i[j+1]_k → R_i[j]_k by ϖ^{1/p^k}, R_i[j]_{k+1} → R_i[j]_k by T^{1/p^k}, and, for i′ ≥ i ≥ j + rp^{k+1}/(p−1), an image comparison giving almost containment.
3. On a cofinal diagonal j = k, i_k ≥ k + rp^{k+1}/(p−1), the transition cokernels in the almost category are killed by s^{3/p^k}, where s = ϖT. The remaining argument is stronger than termwise almost-zero limit vanishing and is given in the next two steps.
4. Apply almost elements (·)_*: the cokernel bounds become s^{6/p^k} on the ordinary representative tower N_k, allowing the extra factor needed to lift an almost epimorphism. The comparison (lim¹ N_k)^a ≅ lim¹ (N_k^a) is the module-tower comparison in Gabber–Ramero Lemma 2.4.2(iii), using the Roos product complex. Thus it suffices to show each s^{1/p^l} kills the ordinary Roos cokernel.
5. Fix ε = 1/p^l and a tail k ≥ N with b_k = 12/p^k ≤ ε. For arbitrary y_k ∈ N_k solve x_k−ψ_k(x_{k+1}) = s^ε y_k by x_k = s^{b_k}z_k: choose z_N = 0 and lift s^{b_k−b_{k+1}}(z_k−s^{ε−b_k}y_k) through ψ_k to obtain z_{k+1}. Such a lift exists because b_k−b_{k+1} ≥ 6/p^k, the cokernel-annihilator exponent. Extend x backwards through the finite head. All powers have exponents in Z[1/p]_{≥0}. Every root of s therefore kills lim¹, proving the almost vanishing.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.3/scholze-limit-lemma`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-zero-limits-and-colimits`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-hom-and-adjoints`, `EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E2/coherent-towers-and-roos`.

**Checks.**

- For a constant nested system lim¹ is zero on the nose.

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *Le lemme d’Abhyankar perfectoïde*, Proposition 4.4.1 with proof, pp. 60–61. Proves almost vanishing of lim¹ for nested systems of root localisations by explicit annihilation estimates on a triple system of submodules.
- Ofer Gabber and Lorenzo Ramero, *Almost ring theory*, Lemma 2.4.2(iii) and proof, pp. 22–23 (arXiv v3). The almost-elements comparison doubles the transition-cokernel annihilators; a Cauchy product of these ideals makes lim¹ almost zero. ## Layer RA.4: The perfectoid Abhyankar lemma

The Abhyankar lemma is proved by restricting a finite étale A[1/g]-algebra B′ to the annuli A{ϖ^j/g}, where almost purity applies, and taking the uniform limit. Finite étaleness is not an equational condition, but being Galois is: the layer therefore plans almost Galois extensions and the classical Galois closure and rigidity lemmas, and reduces every statement to the Galois case. The output is the ramified almost purity equivalence and the perfectoid Abhyankar lemma in its corrected form.

**Targets of the layer.**

K perfectoid, ϖ as in RA.3, almost setup (V, m) = (K°[T^{1/p^∞}], (ϖT)^{1/p^∞}V), A a perfectoid (or almost perfectoid) K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩-algebra and g = T·1. Targets:

- **Annular envelopes of finite étale algebras** (André A Proposition 4.4.4): for B′ finite étale over A[1/g], the system B′ ⊗ A{ϖ^j/g} lies in the nested category, its localisations are recovered, and its uniform limit B satisfies B[1/g] ≅ B′.
- **Integral almost étaleness** (Proposition 5.2.3): for a perfectoid A-algebra B with g a nonzerodivisor and B[1/g] finite étale over A[1/g], B°ᵃ/p^m is finite étale over A°ᵃ/p^m in (V, m) for every m, and B°[1/g] is almost finite étale over A°[1/g] in (K°, K°°).
- **Ramified almost purity** (Théorème 5.2.1): equivalences between complete A°ᵃ-algebras finite étale modulo every p^m and finite étale after inverting g, almost perfectoid A-algebras finite étale after inverting g, and finite étale A[1/g]-algebras.
- **The perfectoid Abhyankar lemma** (Théorème 0.3.1 with Proposition 5.3.1, in the corrected form of the packet): the largest perfectoid A-algebra B in B′, B[1/g] = B′, B° almost equal to the integral closures of A° and of g^{-1/p^∞}A° in B′, B°/p^m almost finite étale over A°/p^m, and almost surjectivity of the trace onto g^{-1/p^∞}A° when B′ is faithful.
- **Almost perfectoid envelopes of reduced affinoid algebras** from a fixed finite Noether-normalisation and a chosen nonzero g making the localisation étale (§5.4).

### Almost Galois extensions of almost algebras

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.4/almost-galois-extension` (definition).** Let (V, m) be a basic setup with m ⊗_V m flat (PerfectoidSpaces P0), A → B a monomorphism of commutative almost V-algebras (an extension) and G a finite group acting on B by A-algebra automorphisms. For a set X of A-homomorphisms B → C into a further extension C there is a canonical C-algebra map B ⊗_A C → Π_{χ∈X} C, b ⊗ c ↦ (χ(b)c)_χ; for X = G and C = B this is B ⊗_A B → Π_G B, b ⊗ b′ ↦ (γ(b)b′)_γ. The extension A → B is G-Galois if B^G = A (the invariants being the kernel of B → Π_G B, b ↦ (γb − b)_γ) and B ⊗_A B → Π_G B is an isomorphism; equivalently (B_*)^G = A_* and (B ⊗_A B)_* → Π_G B_* is an isomorphism. Properties (André A Proposition 1.9.1): a G-Galois extension is finite étale of rank |G| with trace the sum of conjugates; G-Galois extensions are stable under base change and descend along faithfully flat maps; for an intermediate extension C with C → B H-Galois for a subgroup H, C ⊗_A B ≅ Π_{G/H} B and C is finite étale of rank [G : H] over A.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Almost.IsGalois`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Galois for almost algebras does not imply that B_* is Galois over A_*, nor that B_{!!} is Galois over A_{!!} (André A footnote 6): almost elements do not commute with tensor products and (·)_{!!} not with finite products.

**Construction.**

1. Finite projectivity: for η ∈ m choose e_η ∈ B_* ⊗ B_* with e_η² = ηe_η killing the kernel of multiplication and mapping to η; writing e_η = Σ b_i ⊗ b′_i gives Σ γ(b_i)b′_i = 0 for γ ≠ 1 and Σ b_ib′_i = η, so ηb = Σ t(bb_i)b′_i, a factorisation of η·id through a free module (PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-flat-projective-finiteness). Rank and trace follow by faithfully flat descent from B to A.
2. Base change: (B ⊗_A A′) ⊗_{A′} (B ⊗_A A′) ≅ (B ⊗_A B) ⊗_A A′ ≅ Π_G B ⊗_A A′, and invariants are computed after faithfully flat base change; the converse is faithfully flat descent (PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-faithfully-flat-descent).
3. Intermediate extensions: after ⊗_C B the canonical map becomes a composite of isomorphisms; descend from B to C.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-algebra-lower-shriek-shriek`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-finite-projective-and-finite-etale-algebras`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-flat-projective-finiteness`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-faithfully-flat-descent`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-dual-and-trace`.

**API.**

- `IsGalois` (characterisation): IsGalois G A B ↔ B^G = A and B ⊗_A B → Π_G B is an isomorphism of almost algebras.

- `IsGalois.baseChange` (functoriality): If A → B is G-Galois, so is A′ → B ⊗_A A′; conversely for faithfully flat A → A′.

### Galois closures of finite étale extensions and Galois rigidity

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.4/galois-closure-and-rigidity` (lemma).** (1) Let R → S be a finite étale extension of rings of constant rank r ≥ 1. There is a Galois extension R → T with group S_r factoring through S such that S → T is Galois with group S_{r−1}: take T the ring of the complement Z of all partial diagonals in the r-fold fibre product Spec S ×_{Spec R} ⋯ ×_{Spec R} Spec S, with S_r permuting the factors. (2) Let G be a finite group of automorphisms of a ring S, R = S^G, and R ⊂ S′ ⊂ S a G-stable intermediate ring that is G-Galois over R. Then S′ = S.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.galoisClosure_of_finiteEtale`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- Both statements are classical ring theory. In (1), finite étale extension means faithful, so r ≥ 1; connectedness of Spec R is not required. The construction and proof are supplied by André A §1.9.3, p. 10.

**Proof outline.**

1. (1) Since S is étale over R the partial diagonals are open and closed, so Z is finite étale over Spec R and over Spec S; S_r × Z → Z ×_X Z (resp. S_{r−1} × Z → Z ×_Y Z) is an isomorphism, checked on geometric fibres where S is a product of r copies of the residue field.
2. (2) The idempotents e_γ splitting S′ ⊗_R S′ ≅ Π_G S′ also split S ⊗_R S′ ≅ Π_G e_γ(S ⊗_R S′); faithful flatness of S′ over R and taking G × 1 invariants give S ⊗_R S′ = S′ ⊗_R S′, hence S = S′.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.4/almost-galois-extension`, `mathlib:Algebra.Etale`, `mathlib:Module.FaithfullyFlat`.

**Checks.**

- For r = 1, T = S = R and S_1 is trivial.
- For r = 2 and R → S = R[x]/(x² − a) with 2a invertible, T = S.
- Rank zero is excluded: over a nonzero ring R, R → 0 cannot factor a faithfully flat Galois extension R → T through 0. For a general finite étale algebra split off its open-and-closed zero-rank locus first.

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *Le lemme d’Abhyankar perfectoïde*, §1.9.3, Lemmes 1.9.2–1.9.3 with proofs, p. 10. Constructs an S_r-Galois closure of a finite étale extension of constant rank through the complement of partial diagonals, and proves that a G-stable Galois intermediate ring of S over S^G is all of S.

### Annular envelopes of finite étale algebras over A[1/g]

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.4/annular-envelope` (theorem).** Let K be a perfectoid field, ϖ ∈ K°° with |p| ≤ |ϖ| and compatible p-power roots, (V, m) = (K°[T^{1/p^∞}], (ϖT)^{1/p^∞}V) the almost setup, A a perfectoid (or almost perfectoid) K⟨T^{1/p^∞}⟩-algebra, g = T·1 ∈ A° and A[j] = A{ϖ^j/g}. Let B′ be a finite étale A[1/g]-algebra. (1) The algebras B_j = B′ ⊗_{A[1/g]} A[j] form an object of 2-lim A[j]-Perf (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.3/compatible-annular-system); let B = ulim B_j. (2) The counits B[j] = B{ϖ^j/g} → B_j are isomorphisms. (3) They come from an isomorphism B[1/g] ≅ B′.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Perfectoid.annularEnvelope_inv_g`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- A may be replaced by g^{-1/p^∞}A°[1/ϖ] without changing the almost category, so A may be assumed completely integrally closed in A[1/g].

**Proof outline.**

1. (1) Each B_j is finite étale over the perfectoid A[j], hence perfectoid (PerfectoidSpaces:P3/finite-etale-over-perfectoid-is-perfectoid); B_i ⊗_{A[i]} A[j] ≅ B_j and, B_i being finite projective over A[i], the tensor product is already complete, equal to B_i{ϖ^j/g}.
2. (2) The integral closure of A° in B′ maps to the integral closure of A[j]° in B_j, which is B_j° (almost purity: PerfectoidSpaces:P3/integral-closure-almost-finite-etale); in the limit this gives δ : B′ → B[1/g], and δ_j is a right inverse of η_j. The adjunction of PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.3/nested-systems-theorem forces δ_jη_j = id.
3. (3) δ is injective: its kernel I has trace zero in every A[j], hence in A[1/g], which embeds into the algebraic limit lim_j A[j] (PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/laurent-localisation, injectivity into the limit); I = 0 as B′ is étale. Surjectivity: for B′ Galois with group G, B[1/g]^G = A[1/g] and Galois rigidity (André A Lemme 1.9.3) concludes; in general embed B′ in an S_r-Galois closure (André A Lemme 1.9.2) and take S_{r−1}-invariants.
4. For the trace argument, first use Laurent limit injectivity to embed A[1/g] in the algebraic projective limit lim_j A[j], where g is invertible. It generally does not embed in the bounded-family uniform limit: 1/T has unbounded annular norms. If I = ker(B′ → lim_j B_j), then tr(xy) maps to zero for x ∈ I and every y ∈ B′, so tr(xy) = 0 by this algebraic-limit embedding. Nondegeneracy of the finite étale trace pairing gives I = 0. On constant-rank components use the Galois closure only for positive rank; the rank-zero algebra is treated separately.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.3/compatible-annular-system`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.3/nested-systems-theorem`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.2/laurent-localisation`, `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.4/galois-closure-and-rigidity`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P3/finite-etale-over-perfectoid-is-perfectoid`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P3/integral-closure-almost-finite-etale`, `PerfectoidSpaces:P0/almost-finite-projective-and-finite-etale-algebras`.

**Checks.**

- For B′ = A[1/g]^n the envelope is the product of n copies of the Riemann extension of A.

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *Le lemme d’Abhyankar perfectoïde*, Proposition 4.4.4 with proof, pp. 62–63. For a finite étale A[1/g]-algebra B′, shows the base changes to the annuli form a nested perfectoid system whose uniform limit B satisfies B[j] ≅ B_j and B[1/g] ≅ B′, using almost purity, the adjunction of Théorème 4.4.2, a trace argument and a Galois reduction.

### André's perfectoid Abhyankar lemma

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.4/perfectoid-abhyankar-lemma` (theorem).** Let A be a perfectoid algebra over a perfectoid field K of residue characteristic p, and suppose A contains a compatible system of p-power roots of a nonzerodivisor g ∈ A°; view A° as a K°[T^{1/p^∞}]-algebra via T^{1/p^i} ↦ g^{1/p^i} and work in the setup (K°[T^{1/p^∞}], T^{1/p^∞}K°°[T^{1/p^∞}]). (1) There is a largest perfectoid A-algebra B contained in B′ such that the structure map A → B is continuous; B[1/g] = B′, and B° is contained in the integral closure of g^{-1/p^∞}A° in B′ and almost isomorphic to it, and also almost isomorphic to the integral closure of A° in B′. (2) For every m ∈ N, B°/p^m is almost finite étale over A°/p^m, and B°[1/g] is almost finite étale over A°[1/g] in the setup (K°, K°°). 1. Take B = B̃^♮ from PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.4/abhyankar-envelope, which gives (1) and maximality.

## Layer RA.5: Purity and André’s flatness theorem for Kummer towers

Purity (universal injectivity) is the commutative-algebra content of the direct summand theorem, and almost purity its almost analogue. The normalized Kummer tower adjoins to A = W(k)⟦T_1,…,T_n⟧ the roots of unity, the coordinate roots and the roots of g, then takes integral closures. André's flatness theorem thickens the completed tower into a colimit of tubes around T = g, descends each tube to a Noetherian stage and proves faithful flatness there. One step of André's Noetherian argument is replaced by Bhatt's complete flatness criterion. The theorem gives purity of Kummer extensions and, with the Abhyankar lemma, almost faithful flatness modulo p^m of finite extensions ramified along pg.

### Pure (universally injective) module and ring maps

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map` (definition).** Let R be a commutative ring. An R-linear map u : M → N is pure if P ⊗_R u : P ⊗_R M → P ⊗_R N is injective for every R-module P; it suffices to test finitely presented P. A ring map R → S is pure if R → S is pure as a map of R-modules, equivalently R′ → S ⊗_R R′ is injective for every R-algebra R′ (universally injective). Properties (André B Annexe A, after Lazard): M ⊂ N is pure iff Hom(P, N) → Hom(P, N/M) is surjective for all finitely presented P; if N/M is finitely presented, M ⊂ N is pure iff M is a direct summand; pure maps are the filtered colimits of split injections; pure maps are Tor-injective; if N is flat, M ⊂ N is pure iff N/M is flat; pure maps of S-modules are pure over R. Pure ring maps are stable under composition, products, base change and filtered colimits; if R → S → T is pure so is R → S; if R → S → T is flat and S → T pure then R → S is flat; R → S is pure iff base change Mod_R → Mod_S is faithful; faithfully flat iff pure and flat; if S is finitely presented as an R-module, R → S is pure iff it has an R-linear retraction iff S is a generator.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.Module.IsPure`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- In the generator criterion the dual is Hom_R(S, R) (misprint PAPER-ANDRE-18-B/E1).

**Construction.**

1. Lazard's characterisation via finitely presented modules and filtered colimits of split monomorphisms; Tor-injectivity follows since filtered colimits commute with Tor.
2. Ring-level statements follow from the module-level ones by taking symmetric algebras and the faithfulness criterion as in André B Lemme A.2.1.
3. For the generator criterion use the trace ideal generated by f(s), with f ∈ Hom_R(S,R). If it is R, choose finitely many pairs (f_i,s_i) with Σ_i f_i(s_i) = 1. Then t ↦ Σ_i f_i(s_i t) is an R-linear retraction S → R. The generator property does not require a single evaluation f(s) to equal 1.

**Inputs.** `mathlib:LinearMap.lTensor`, `mathlib:Module.Flat`, `mathlib:Module.FaithfullyFlat`, `mathlib:Module.FaithfullyFlat.lTensor_injective_iff_injective`.

### Krull detection along faithfully flat algebras with an idempotent ideal

**Target `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/krull-detection-lemma` (lemma).** Let R be a Noetherian ring, M a finitely generated R-module, S a faithfully flat R-algebra and K an idempotent ideal of S with K·(M ⊗_R S) = 0. Then for every s ∈ R whose image lies in K there is r ∈ Ann_R(M) with (1 − r)s = 0. In particular, if R is local and the preimage of K in R is nonzero, then M = 0.

**Named signature.** `TauCeti.krull_detection_of_idempotent`.

**Hypotheses and conventions.**

- This is the published statement; arXiv v1 has a different version with an ideal J and K ⊂ JS (both versions are proved the same way).

**Proof outline.**

1. Let J = Ann_R M. By flatness J S = Ann_S(M ⊗ S), so K ⊂ JS and K = K^n ⊂ J^nS for all n.
2. By purity of R → S (faithful flatness), R ∩ J^nS = J^n, so the preimage of K lies in ⋂_n J^n.
3. Krull's intersection theorem (Mathlib Ideal.mem_iInf_smul_pow_eq_bot_iff) gives r ∈ J with (1 − r)s = 0; in the local case 1 − r is a unit unless J is the unit ideal, and then M = 0.

**Inputs.** `PerfectoidSpacesPartII:RA.5/pure-map`, `mathlib:Ideal.mem_iInf_smul_pow_eq_bot_iff`, `mathlib:Module.FaithfullyFlat`, `mathlib:Module.annihilator`.

**Checks.**

- If K = 0 the statement is empty.
- Noetherianity of R is necessary: for R = S = K° (K perfectoid), M = K°/K°° and K = K°° (idempotent), K kills M ⊗ S and R ∩ K ≠ 0, yet M ≠ 0.

**Sources.**

- Yves André, *La conjecture du facteur direct*, Lemme 1.1.2 with proof, p. 76. Proves, for Noetherian R, finite M, faithfully flat S and an idempotent ideal K killing M ⊗ S, that elements of R in K are killed by 1 − r for some r annihilating M, by purity and Krull's intersection theorem.

## Corrections to the sources
