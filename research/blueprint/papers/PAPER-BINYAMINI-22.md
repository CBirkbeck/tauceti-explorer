## What the paper proves

Let M be an affine variety over a number field with a foliation generated by commuting rational vector fields ξ.

- **Theorem 1.** For V of complementary dimension, #(B² ∩ V) = poly(δ_ξ, δ_V, log R, log dist⁻¹(B, Σ_V)), where Σ_V is the locus of improper intersection with leaves. Corollary 2 handles any codimension.
- **Theorem 2.** B ∩ V is covered by poly(…) Weierstrass polydiscs of degree poly(…).
- **Theorem 3.** A polylogarithmic Pila–Wilkie theorem: the algebraic points of Φ(B² ∩ V) of degree ≤ g and height ≤ h lie in poly(…, g, h, log ε⁻¹) blocks coming from leaves ε-close to B.
- **Theorem 4.** It gives Wilkie's conjecture for ℝ_RE with poly(g, h) bounds.

The two main theorems are proved by simultaneous induction:

- **Theorem 1.** Inside a Weierstrass polydisc for the curve V(P₁,…,P_{n−1}), the zeros of P_n are counted through the analytic resultant and a Jensen-type bound. The needed lower bound comes from multiplicity operators, the Gabrielov–Khovanskii estimate and Brownawell's Diophantine Łojasiewicz inequality.
- **Theorem 2.** Weierstrass polydiscs are built from a volume bound (Lelong, Proposition 17), and the volume is bounded by the complex Crofton formula and Theorem 1 on linear subfoliations.

Polynomial reparametrisation keeps these subfoliations in general position (§4). Interpolation uses Wilkie's Thue–Siegel approach (Proposition 28).

The applications:

- **Theorem 6.** An effective Masser–Zannier theorem: simultaneous torsion on the square of the Legendre family has order poly(δ_C, [K:ℚ]), and so is computable in polynomial time.
- **Theorem 7.** André–Oort for ℂⁿ with poly_n(δ_V) bounds and a polynomial-time algorithm; the constant is ineffective through Siegel.
- **Theorem 8.** Galois-orbit lower bounds for torsion points on elliptic curves, by Schmidt's counting strategy.
- **Appendix A.** Polynomial growth estimates for inhomogeneous Fuchsian equations (Theorem 9).

## Source issues (`sourceIssues` E1–E8)

- **E8** (error, affects a stated result: Proposition 51 and Theorem 9).
  - **The problem.** The proof of Proposition 51 uses "L + 1 is also Fuchsian", which fails in general.
  - **Counterexample.** For L = ∂_t, which is Fuchsian with a regular singular point at ∞:
    - inf_φ ‖a₀(φ*L)‖ = 0, because φ(s) = κs gives a₀ = 1/κ;
    - Theorem 9 fails for b = (t − t₀)/(2r) and f = (t − t₀)²/(4r) on large discs.
  - **Repair.** Both results hold once L is normalized so that L + 1 is Fuchsian, for example when the finite zeros of a₀ have order ≤ n and deg a₀ ≥ n. The Legendre Picard–Fuchs operator, the only operator to which the paper applies Theorem 9, satisfies this. Theorems 1–8 are unaffected.
- **E1–E7** (misprints):
  - **E1:** in Lemma 15, 2^m should be 2^{2m} and c(n) should be c(m).
  - **E2:** in the general-position codimension argument of §4.4 and Proposition 22, the set must be restricted to points off Σ_V.
  - **E3:** in the proof of Lemma 40, δ_W should be δ_V, and "Corollary 39" should be "Proposition 39".
  - **E4:** in (108), poly(δ_C, R̂, h) should be poly(δ_C, log R̂, h). The argument needs log R̂ = poly(δ_C).
  - **E5:** "A := ℝ² ∩ Φ(…)" should be Mat₂ₓ₂(ℝ) ∩ Φ(…) in §8.6, and (ℂ² × ℝ²) ∩ Φ(…) in §10.3.
  - **E6:** in Theorem 8, "p ∈ A" should be "p ∈ E(K̄)", and poly_g should be poly.
  - **E7:** an index mismatch in Definition 49.

The Prop. 28 parameter choices, Lemma 26's exponent bookkeeping, Lemma 20, the Picard–Fuchs operator and the counting of §8.6 were rechecked. No correction notice is listed on the journal page, and Crossref has no correction relation.
