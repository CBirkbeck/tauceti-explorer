## What the paper proves

Bhatt–Lurie define syntomic complexes Z_p(i)(X) for every scheme X. Let Z/p^n(i)_X be their restriction to the small étale site. On X[1/p] it is μ_{p^n}^{⊗i}. On a regular F_p-scheme it is W_nΩ^i_log[−i]. On p-adic formal schemes it is the filtered Frobenius eigenspace of prismatic cohomology. The paper computes it on p-torsion-free schemes that are "absolutely smooth".

**F-smoothness (Definition 4.1).** A p-quasisyntomic ring A is F-smooth if two conditions hold:

- each divided Frobenius φ_i : N^iΔ_A → Δ̄_A{i} has fibre of p-complete Tor-amplitude ≥ i + 2;
- the Nygaard filtration on Δ̄_A{i} is complete.

This is a strong form of the Lη-isomorphism, read through the Beilinson t-structure (Remark 4.11). Examples:

- smooth algebras over perfectoid rings;
- over a perfectoid base, F-smoothness means p-completely flat L and Hodge-complete derived de Rham cohomology (Proposition 4.12, after Bouis);
- in characteristic p, F-smooth is exactly Cartier smooth (Proposition 4.14).

**Theorem 4.15.** Regular noetherian rings are F-smooth. Conversely, p-complete noetherian F-smooth rings are regular. The forward direction is an induction on dimension using Proposition 4.16: A is F-smooth if A/x and A[1/x] are. The converse uses a θ-criterion for complete intersections (Proposition 4.19), together with Avramov's and André's cotangent-complex criteria.

**Theorem 1.8.** Let X be p-torsion-free and F-smooth (for example regular and flat over Z), and i ≥ 0. Then Z/p^n(i)_X → τ^{≤i}Rj_*μ_{p^n}^{⊗i} is an isomorphism on cohomology sheaves below degree i. On H^i it is injective, with image the subsheaf generated by O_X^×-symbols. The proof has three steps.

1. **Étale comparison.** Theorem 5.1 recovers F_p(i)(X[1/p]) by inverting a class v_1 ∈ H^0(F_p(p−1)(Z)), for every qcqs derived scheme X.
2. **Coconnectivity.** On an F-smooth ring, multiplication by v_1 is highly coconnected (Proposition 5.2). The key facts are that v_1 lifts to θ̃ one Nygaard step higher (§2.2), and the Tor-amplitude bounds of Lemma 5.3 and Proposition 5.4.
3. **Top degree.** The top cohomology is identified through Bloch–Kato's description of p-adic nearby cycles (Propositions 5.5–5.7).

**Consequences.**

- **Theorem 5.8.** Syntomic complexes agree with Sato's p-adic étale Tate twists on regular semistable schemes over Dedekind rings.
- **Example 5.9.** The cofibre sequence Z/p^n(i)_X → τ^{≤i}Rj_*μ_{p^n}^{⊗i} → W_nΩ^{i−1}_{X_k,log}[−i] holds for X smooth over a mixed-characteristic DVR.
- **Corollary 4.20.** The Hodge–Tate stack of a p-complete regular local ring with F-finite R/p has cohomological dimension ≤ dim R + log_p[k:k^p]. This is Bhatt–Lurie's Conjecture 10.1 under F-finiteness.

The technical core is §3, which constructs:

- a Nygaard filtration on Hodge–Tate cohomology, and a twisted variant on Δ̄{i}/p;
- base change along relatively perfect maps;
- weight gradings;
- explicit computations for R[x] and R/x.

extraction and all seven routes, and made one correction. The full record is
[REV-PAPER-BHATT-MATHEW-23.md](../reviews/REV-PAPER-BHATT-MATHEW-23.md).

The arXiv v2 e-print hash reproduces byte for byte and the archive holds the recorded 2418-line
`syntomic-etale-final.tex`. The published Cambridge hash does not and cannot: two fresh downloads
gave `96e05343…` and `3bedeca4…` against the recorded `abbfd5b2…`, because Cambridge stamps each
download with the date and the requesting IP. **Correction:** a `sha256Note` on that artifact now
records this and names the arXiv e-print as the reproducible pin, as on `PAPER-HE-18`,
`PAPER-HE-21` and `PAPER-SHANKAR-SHANKAR-TANG-ETAL-22`.

102 items with all 74 missing ones routed exactly once; all six stage ids and all 27 planned ids
resolve; both Part II titles reproduce their parents' atlas titles exactly and both areas are the
galaxy id `padic`; the library item's `IsRegularLocalRing` and `IsRegularRing` are both in Mathlib's
`RingTheory/RegularLocalRing/Defs.lean`. The review notes one provenance gap it did not fill: no
`libraryPins` are recorded, so there is no commit against which that claim was checked. 87 of 90
locator checks land exactly and the other three are right as well.

All eight findings are **confirmed**, five of them by the paper contradicting itself — Proposition
3.12's closing display against its own statement and first paragraph (E2); Proposition 4.6's title
against its statement, with Proposition 4.7 supplying what Definition 4.1 and Proposition 4.9 cite it
for (E3); the weight `p − 1` against equation (1), Construction 2.2 and (24) (E1); `[BS22, Th. 4.1]`
against Construction 2.2's `[BS22, Th. 14.1]` (E4); and a sentence that breaks off (E8). The review
adds that **E4 and E5 are a swapped pair** one page apart: the number 14.1 that belongs to
Bhatt–Scholze's discreteness theorem is attached to `[BK86]`, whose paper has no section 14, and
`[Prisms]` is given 4.1.

`E6` is judged stronger than recorded. The printed `R[1/p]^× = π^Z ⊕ R^×` is not merely unproved but
**false** in the generality of the theorem, which is stated for `X` regular and semistable over the
characteristic-`p` points: for `X = Spec Z_p[x,y]/(xy − p)` at the origin, `R[1/p]^× = R^× × x^Z × y^Z`
with `π = p = xy`, and `x` has valuation vector `(1,0)` against `p`'s `(1,1)`. The recorded
correction states the right decomposition and is candid that the printed reduction to `i = 2` only
covers the one-branch case; the gap for `i ≥ 3` with several local equations among the `b_j` is real,
and the grading as a gap affecting the proof is right, since the theorem itself is not in doubt.
