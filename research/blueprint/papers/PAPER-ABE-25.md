## Result and scope

[Abe's published paper](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00222-025-01345-w.pdf), *Inventiones mathematicae* 241 (2025), 605–622, proves both parts of Serre's conjecture when the regular local ring A contains a finite field. The action assumptions are substantive: A is finite over its Noetherian invariant ring, every nonidentity fixed quotient has finite length, and the invariant-ring residue field is unchanged.

For σ≠1, define aG(σ)=−length(A/Iσ), where Iσ is generated by a−σ(a). Set aG(1) to make the sum of the values zero. The conclusions are an **actual** representation with this character and realization over Qℓ for every invertible ℓ. A virtual character with values in Qℓ is not enough. Characteristic zero and mixed characteristic are not covered by “contains a finite field.”

## Integral Swan exactness: the torsion terms are controlled

This continuation supplies the proof omitted after the inverse-limit definition on
p.616. It is conditional on **chosen projective Swan modules and compatible tower
isomorphisms**, whose existence remains `G-SWAN`.

Fix a finite projective Zℓ-algebra Λ, a compatible tower, and a finite Λ-module M.
Write `S_n(M)=sw(M/ℓ^nM)`. At a common finite quotient Δ, the functor is tensoring
with the finitely generated projective right Zℓ[Δ]-module P. Split P off a finite
free right module. The tensor functor is then a natural idempotent summand of a
finite direct sum of the input; lifting an element and applying the idempotent
proves surjectivity, and kernels/injections are preserved. This argument works
for the noncommutative group ring. The commutative flat-tensor library theorem
is not being applied to that group ring.

Tensor associativity and the chosen quotient isomorphisms give

`S_(n+1)(M)/ℓ^n S_(n+1)(M) ≅ S_n(M)`.

These are finite modules and finite sets. Choose generators at level 1 and lift
them compatibly. Nakayama makes their lifts generate every level. The kernels
of the resulting maps `(Λ/ℓ^n)^r→S_n` have surjective transitions: a lifted kernel
element has image in ℓ^n S_(n+1), which can be removed by an ℓ^n multiple of a
lifted generator combination. Passing to the limit gives a finite Λ-module S,
with `S/ℓ^nS≅S_n`. This is the specialized effectivity argument of
[Stacks 087W](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/087W). Its statement and proof
were read, along with the adjacent limit and exactness lemmas. It supplies both
finite generation and the finite-reduction comparison needed for traces.

For an exact sequence `0→M′→M→M″→0`, quotient reduction need not preserve the
injection. Its error is

`E_n=(M′ ∩ ℓ^nM)/ℓ^nM′`.

Choose c with ℓ^c annihilating the Zℓ-torsion of M″. If
`x=ℓ^(n+c)y` belongs to M′, then the image of y in M″ is torsion. Hence
ℓ^c y belongs to M′, and x belongs to ℓ^nM′. Consequently every transition
`E_(n+c)→E_n` is zero. Applying the fixed-tower exact Swan functor preserves
these zero maps.

Put A_n=S_n(M′), B_n=S_n(M), C_n=S_n(M″), D_n=ker(B_n→C_n),
and K_n=ker(A_n→B_n). There are two short exact sequences

`0→K_n→A_n→D_n→0`, `0→D_n→B_n→C_n→0`.

Every term is a finite set. For any compatible element on the right, its fibres
form an inverse system of nonempty finite sets, so the pinned theorem
`nonempty_sections_of_finite_inverse_system` supplies a compatible preimage.
Thus limits preserve surjectivity here; left exactness is coordinatewise.
The boundedly zero transitions give lim K_n=0. The two limit sequences therefore
prove exactness of `swΛ` without discarding the reduction kernels.

The test sequence ℓ^cZℓ⊂Zℓ has nonzero E_n whenever c>0, even though the
inverse-limit injection is valid. It prevents a false termwise-exact proof.
For a lattice over the integer ring O of a finite extension of Qℓ, apply the
result to multiplication by ℓ. The Swan output is finite and torsion-free,
hence free over O; a finite torsion quotient remains torsion. These are the
precise finiteness/freeness premises used by the rational trace and lattice
independence arguments.

## Fixed-length comparison: a proof of the E3 repair

Let B→B′ be the local ring map at the chosen fixed point after strict
henselization of the base. This is flat, local, and has the same residue field k.
Fixed ideals commute with base change because σ acts over the base. Take a
composition series of the finite-length B-module B/Iσ. Flat tensor preserves
its exact steps; each simple quotient becomes B′/m_BB′=k. The resulting series
has exactly the same length. This proves the numerical equality used on p.613.
The flat-local properties are those in
[Stacks 59.32.8](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/03QD); the pinned
`Module.Flat.lTensor_exact` and `rTensor_exact` were read for this commutative
application. The preceding spreading/strict-local suppliers in A20 remain
open. This argument retains every nilpotent in the fixed algebra and never
replaces it with the closed fibre.

## E6: the concluding Milnor sign depends on dimension

The first paragraph of §3.3 gives the identity Fourier trace as −μ_f in every
dimension. With the unshifted coefficients used in the paper, the corrected
formula for n=dim X is

`Tr(1; Fourier nearby stalk) = (−1)^n μ_f`.

Indeed, [Saito v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.03018v4), Theorem 4.9 on p.45,
equation(4.15), gives `−dimtot RΦ=(CCΛ,df)`. Page 46 and Lemma 4.11(1) give
`CCΛ=(−1)^n[zero-section]`. Combine this with Abe Proposition 2.5's already
present negative Fourier/total-dimension comparison. The cited pages and
Theorem 4.9’s proof were read, and p.46 was inspected as an image. Its recursive
characteristic-cycle infrastructure remains an import, not a newly closed proof.

For an explicit even-dimensional test, take k algebraically closed of odd
characteristic, f:A²→A¹ given by t=uv, and C2 acting by (u,v)↦(−u,−v).
Base change to the strict henselization at t=0. The total space is regular,
the map is flat and smooth off the origin, and the nonidentity fixed ideal
is (2u,2v)=(u,v). The critical ideal is (v,u), so μ_f=1. The corrected identity
trace is +1, whereas the printed formula gives −1. The nonidentity theorem
and the curve specialization are unaffected.

## Earlier source issues awaiting independent review

### E1 — missing ramification factor, §3.3 p.621

The printed equality disc(q)+disc(g)=disc(f) omits the pullback multiplicity. At algebraically closed residue points the different exponents satisfy

`d(f)=d(q)+e(q)d(g).`

In characteristic 5 take q:x↦u=x², g:u↦t=u³ and f:x↦t=x6. Then d(q)=1, d(g)=2, d(f)=5 and aG(1)=1. The printed sum is 3, not 5; μf−aG(1)=4, not 2. The corrected value is 2·2=4. The stated consequence when g is étale survives.

### E2 — derived sign/shift, §3.1 p.621

C is defined as the unshifted Rpr′*M. An H¹ realization contributes Sw[−1], hence −[Sw] in K0. Laumon's local transform is likewise concentrated in degree 1. The corrected virtual comparison is

`−[Ψ(C)]=[Sw]+[Λ[G]].`

For the trivial Galois group the Swan term is zero and the rank-one degree-one local transform has class −1, whereas the printed formula gives +1. The main Proposition 2.5 already has the minus sign. The full Katz comparison still requires the external concentration and extension inputs; this report does not claim those proofs complete.

### E3 — replacing a nonreduced fixed scheme by its closed fiber, p.613

In characteristic 2, let

`X=Spec k[t,z]/(z²+t²z−t),  σ(t)=t,  σ(z)=z+t².`

The map f to A¹t is finite flat of degree 2. X is smooth since ∂F/∂t=1; f is étale off t=0 since ∂F/∂z=t². The substitution is an involution preserving F. The fixed scheme has ring

`k[t,z]/(t²,z²−t) ≅ k[z]/z⁴,`

of length 4, while its closed fiber has ring k[z]/z² of length 2. Thus support at the closed point does not imply the printed fixed-scheme/closed-fiber isomorphism. The needed descent of length can instead use the Artinian local algebra's étale/strict-henselian comparison. This repairs the identified step; it does not furnish a counterexample to Theorem 1.6.

Abe's §1.3 setup omits the Noetherian condition explicitly present in the cited Lu–Zheng Theorem 2.16. Either retain it or give a broader argument. This is a citation-scope gap, not a demonstrated false theorem. The finite coefficient rings used in Abe's main proof satisfy it.
