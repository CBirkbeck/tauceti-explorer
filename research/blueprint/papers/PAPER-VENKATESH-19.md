## What the paper proves

The motivating phenomenon is that for a tempered cuspidal Hecke character `χ`, the same eigensystem appears in
`δ + 1` consecutive cohomological degrees of an arithmetic manifold `Y(K)`:
`dim H^{q+i}(Y(K), Q)_χ = C(δ, i) · dim H^q(Y(K), Q)_χ`, with `δ = rank G(R) − rank K_∞` and `2q + δ = dim Y(K)`.
The paper constructs extra endomorphisms that account for this and relates them to Galois cohomology.

* **The derived Hecke algebra.** `ℋ(G, U)_S := Ext^*_{S[G]}(S[G/U], S[G/U])`, the Ext being taken in smooth
  `G`-modules (Definition 2.2). Its higher part is almost killed by `q_v − 1`, so it is used at primes with
  `q_v ≡ 1 mod p^n`. It has an invariant-function model and a double-coset model, acts on derived invariants, and
  therefore acts on `H^*(Y(K), S)` with the `Ext^i`-part raising degree by `i`; concretely (Lemma 2.10) a
  generator acts as *pull back along a degeneracy map, cup with a class of the level subgroup, push forward*.
  Assembling over places and passing to the `p`-adic limit gives the global derived Hecke algebra `𝓗 ⊂ End
  H^*(Y(K), Z_p)`, whose degree-zero part is the usual Hecke algebra, and the *strict* variant using only places
  where the local algebra is graded commutative.
* **Derived Satake (Theorem 3.3).** For `S = Z/ℓ^r` with `ℓ^r | q_v − 1` and `ℓ ∤ |W|`, restriction identifies the
  derived Hecke algebra of `(G_v, K_v)` with the `W`-invariants of that of the maximal split torus; in particular
  it is graded commutative. This is what produces the "nice primes" the global theory runs on. Section 4 does the
  Iwahori-level analogue, with a Morita equivalence after étale localisation (Lemma 4.5).
* **The trivial representation (Theorem 5.2).** For all but finitely many `ℓ`, the Hecke-trivial part of
  `H^*(Y(K), Z_ℓ)` is cyclic over `𝓗`, generated by the trivial class, and the image of `𝓗` there is graded
  commutative and rationally equals the algebra generated by cup product with `H^*(Y(K), Q)_triv`. The proof needs
  Lemma 5.3: infinitely many places `v` with `q_v ≡ 1 mod ℓ^n` at which the pullback
  `H^*(G(F_v), Z/ℓ) → H^*(Y(K), Z/ℓ)_triv` is surjective, which rests on Quillen's computation of the cohomology
  of finite groups of Lie type and a Chebotarev argument.
* **Generation in minimal degree (Theorem 7.6).** Under Taylor–Wiles hypotheses (Sections 6.1–6.2), the
  cohomology `H^*(Y(1), Z_p)_m` is generated over the strict global derived Hecke algebra by its minimal degree
  `H^q(Y(1), Z_p)_m`. The proof patches complexes in the Calegari–Geraghty style, uses the straightening lemma
  7.5 to write `S_∞ → R_∞` as a quotient by `δ` variables, and reads off the exterior algebra by Koszul duality.
* **The reciprocity law (Theorem 8.5, Proposition 8.6).** There is an action of `V`, the `Z_p`-dual of the
  Bloch–Kato Selmer group `H^1_f(Z[1/Σ], Ad^*ρ(1))` — free of rank `δ` (Lemma 8.9) — on `H^*(Y(1), Z_p)_m` by
  degree `+1` endomorphisms, uniquely characterised by agreeing with the derived Hecke operators at every
  Taylor–Wiles prime; so the strict global derived Hecke algebra contains `⋀^*V` freely, and when
  `H^q(Y(1), Z_p)_m = Z_p` its image is exactly `⋀^*V`, giving
  `𝓗 ⊗ Q_p ≅ T ⊗ ⋀^* H^1_f(G_Q, Ad^*ρ(1))^*`.
* **The conjecture (Conjecture 8.8).** The motivic lattice — Scholl's integral motivic cohomology
  `H^1_mot((M_coad)_Z, Q(1))` of the coadjoint motive, conjecturally isomorphic to the Selmer group after
  `⊗ Q_p`, and of dimension `δ` by Beilinson — preserves the *rational* cohomology `H^*(Y(1), Q)_Π`. As the author
  writes, "the main point of this paper was to get to the point where we can make this conjecture".

  `PeriodsAndSpecialValues` (PS.3 regulators and leading terms, PS.6 Bloch–Kato assembly).

## The routes

### 1. Part II of *Arithmetic locally symmetric spaces and their cohomology* — 35 items

`ArithmeticLocallySymmetricSpacesPartII`, "…, Part II: the derived Hecke algebra and the reciprocity law", area
`automorphic`. It plans the global derived Hecke algebra and its action, the Hecke-trivial theorem, the
Taylor–Wiles package as used here with the patched complex and the straightening lemma, the Koszul computation,
the identification of the patching variables with Galois cohomology, the reciprocity law and the central
conjecture with its motivic ingredients.

The parent goes exactly this way and stops at the ordinary Hecke action — ALS.3 even asks for the Hecke
correspondences to be lifted to the derived category with coherence data. A graded extension of that action is
new layers in that direction, which PROTOCOL section 15 makes a Part II rather than a new roadmap. The brief names
every import (ALS.0–ALS.3; SR.0, SR.1, SR.4; AG2.3; R04.5; P7, P8, P9, R03.2, R03.5; MC.4; PS.3, PS.6) and flags
two inputs used without proof that deserve their own layers: the Borel–Wallach shape of tempered cohomology, and
Quillen's computation of the cohomology of finite groups of Lie type with the stable comparison.

### 2. Source for `SmoothRepresentationsOfLocalGroups` SR.0:derived-extension, SR.1, SR.4 — 14 items

Sections 2.1–2.5, 3, 4 and Appendix A are local representation theory: the derived Hecke algebra with its three
models and its action on derived invariants; the fact that the higher Ext-groups are almost killed by `q_v − 1`;
the derived Satake isomorphism; and the Iwahori-level structure with the Morita equivalence after étale
localisation. SR.0:derived-extension plans the derived extension of the smooth category, SR.1 Hecke algebras over
rings with their idempotents and module-category comparison, and SR.4 spherical representations and Satake — the
degree-zero parts of exactly these statements. The paper is a source for the graded versions, and the Part II then
imports them instead of developing local representation theory.

This is the one judgement call in the routing, and it is reversible: a reviewer who prefers to keep the derived
Satake with the rest of the paper can move these fourteen items into the Part II without changing anything else.
The reason recorded in the result file says so explicitly.

## Prerequisites the atlas does not cover

Eleven entries. The ones that matter most: **Prasanna–Venkatesh** (arXiv:1609.06370), the archimedean counterpart
of the conjecture; **Harris–Venkatesh** (arXiv:1810.00920), the coherent-cohomology analogue with the only
numerical verification; **Galatius–Venkatesh** (arXiv:1608.07236) on derived deformation rings, the companion
paper; **Schneider** for the same Ext-algebra in characteristic `v`; **Calegari–Geraghty** (arXiv:1207.4224) for
patching beyond the Taylor–Wiles method; **Scholze** (arXiv:1306.2070) for the Galois representations;
**Quillen** for the cohomology of finite groups of Lie type; **Borel–Wallach** for the shape of tempered
cohomology; and **Bloch–Kato** and **Scholl** for the `f`-cohomology and the integral motivic lattice that make
Conjecture 8.8 meaningful.

## Mistakes in the source

None recorded. The version read is the one revised after the referee report, and its own remarks already flag the
one point where the first version overreached: graded commutativity of the global derived Hecke algebra is *not*
claimed in general, which is why the theorems are stated for the strict global derived Hecke algebra and why
Proposition 8.6 is needed to obtain graded commutativity in the cases of interest.
