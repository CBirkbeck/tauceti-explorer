## Source and mathematical outcome

Gille–Parimala prove a local-global principle for twisted flags over F=K(X), where K is the fraction field of an arbitrary complete DVR T and X/K is a smooth projective geometrically integral curve. Let p be the residue-field characteristic exponent (1 in characteristic zero). The divisorial theorem assumes p is prime to the order of the automorphism group of the absolute root system of the adjoint group. Under this hypothesis, points over all divisorial completions imply an F-point. Only the divisorial valuations nontrivial on K are needed. The finite-patch and all-rank-one-valuations versions have no such characteristic restriction. Extension of the group to a reductive model also gives an unconditional discrete-valuations version. These are different quantified statements; none assumes that the residue field is finite or perfect, or that the group is rational.

## Proof chain and corrections

The proof first builds type-preserving form torsors and flag schemes, then ring-valued R-equivalence and elementary subgroups. Finite algebras can be nonreduced with inseparable residue fields. Nilpotent devissage compares their R-classes with those of their residue fields; for simply connected semisimple strictly isotropic groups these are elementary quotients. The geometric definition of strictly proper tests every absolute simple factor, so B×G in G×G is a useful negative test.

Complete saturated T-modules V,W inside the branch valuation ring supply a local analytic factorization. Conjugated unipotent or quasi-trivial-torus charts turn it into elementary and R-factorizations. The flag argument uses semilocal parabolic conjugacy and field intersection; its gluing equation is h1^(-1)x1=h2 x2 when x1=h1 h2 x2. The manuscript puts an incorrect inverse on h2. Restriction of scalars along an inseparable field extension remains essential: Res H may be nonreductive, and projective flag restriction needs its own representability proof beyond the existing affine restriction owner.

The P1 diamond gives finite-model patching, then all-special-fibre-points and rank-one consequences. Completed open rings must remain distinct from completed fraction fields. In equal characteristic,

`Frac(k[x][[t]])` is a proper subfield of `k(x)((t))`.

The errata's displayed series is not an outside element: sum(t^n/x^n)=t/(x−t). A correct witness is sum(t^n/x^(n!)). For a quotient a/b in k[x][[t]], remove the common t-order and write b0 nonzero, with m=ord_x(b0). The coefficient recurrence for c=a/b gives ord_x(c_n)≥−m(n+1) by induction: every numerator coefficient has nonnegative x-order and division by b0 costs at most m. Factorial negative orders violate this bound for every fixed m. This proof also explains why a linear-growth geometric series fails as a witness.

Further recorded corrections are the integral ball |v|≤1 in Lemma 3.2; the W-variable in Proposition 3.3; the square-zero ideal m^j/m^(j+1); the assignment of Laurent coefficient parts to V and W; and using the normalizer quotient N_Aut_I(G0)(T0)/T0 rather than the generally non-finite Aut_I(G0)/T0. The analytic proof uses strict small-remainder estimates, not just a single fixed Lipschitz remainder constant. The R-factorization density step needs no claim that a product of two subgroups is itself a subgroup.

Finally, finite structure reduction, SNC spreading and tame loop torsors convert divisorial points into F_P-points. The errata requires **A=the completed local ring at P** before henselian arguments. Compare the Grothendieck valuations of the original and completed local rings by their common complete valued field, uniformizer and residue field k(P)(u). If an SNC divisor has only one local component, it may be enlarged by a second parameter for the full-parameter loop theorem: the final target is the fraction field, so this enlargement is permitted.

## New purity proof and its precise scope

The original [Colliot-Thélène–Sansuc 1979 scan](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/download/pdf/PPN235181684_0244/LOG_0024.pdf) was acquired from GDZ (31 PDF pages including a cover; SHA-256 `5eb67a18278ce634385688693f7e8267b46e3965d5dbf9cb4bb8b418dc3576e3`). I read printed pp. 109–110, Lemmas 2.1–2.2 with proofs, and pp. 127–130, Proposition 6.11, Corollary 6.12, Theorem 6.13 with proofs and Remark 6.15. This is a targeted read, not a claim to have read the whole CTS paper.

CTS Theorem 6.13 assumes a **regular integral surface**. Its proof extends vector bundles, uses an affine linear quotient for a reductive group locally, and twists to preserve the torsor being extended. Items 129–138 make the ingredients explicit:

1. For a noetherian X, if U contains every point of local depth at most one, finite locally free sheaves satisfy E=j_*(E|U). In particular O_X=j_*O_U.
2. A vector bundle on U has coherent reflexive direct image. Coherence follows locally by extending a coherent dual presentation and embedding the direct image into a finite free sheaf.
3. A finite reflexive module over a regular noetherian local ring of dimension at most two is free. Use the existing module-reflexivity carrier; a dual presentation makes the module a second syzygy. The depth bound, regular-local finite global dimension and Auslander–Buchsbaum give projective dimension zero. Treat the zero module separately.
4. Thus vector bundles extend on a regular noetherian scheme of dimension at most two if U contains all points of codimension **at most** one. Hom bundles give full faithfulness.
5. Any affine X-scheme Y has Y(X)=Y(U) when O_X=j_*O_U: write Y as relative Spec and use the adjunction on algebra maps. CTS states its affine finite-type version; this argument proves the explicitly recorded generalization without a finite-type restriction.
6. Given P/U under G, extend its induced GL_n-torsor E_U to E/X. The associated quotient E/G is affine by descent, so its specified U-section extends. Pulling back E→E/G gives the required G-torsor with its specified restriction. Isom_G(P,Q) is affine and its sections extend uniquely; hence this construction is unique and glues.

Here GL_n/G must mean the **represented fppf sheaf quotient**. The quotient map is an epimorphism for that topology and satisfies the torsor identity. The argument does not need a separate flatness hypothesis on G and does not claim that this quotient map is flat. An arbitrary affine categorical quotient would not suffice. The flat finitely presented group used in GP26 Lemma 5.1 is safely within this corrected theorem.

There is a real boundary defect in the broader published formulation: take X=A²_R disjoint union Spec R, U=A²_R, and G=PGL_2. X is regular noetherian of dimension two, and U contains all codimension-one points. On the omitted component the split matrix algebra and Hamilton quaternion algebra define distinct torsors, yet their restrictions to U agree. The quaternion algebra is division by its positive norm; the matrix algebra has zero divisors. Thus restriction need not even be injective. Adding codimension-zero points to U, or retaining CTS's integral-surface hypothesis, repairs this. Items 89–90 say explicitly that they are corrected statements; item 138 records the counterexample. GP26's actual integral-model application is unaffected. G4's proof-inspection obligation is now discharged on this stated scope.

## New rank-one comparison proof

I read the entire ten-page [HHKP20 arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.08007v3), including §§1–3 and references. The downloaded [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.08007) has SHA-256 `5cc1325478af923b1a4774fa5a583da243cd33a01ec8318ba018f63794429fe8`. Its semiglobal definition does not assume F/K separable; the smooth-curve application in GP26 is narrower.

The family Ω_F is the set of **nontrivial valuations whose valuation rings contain T**, not all valuations of F. Ω_F^1 means rank one, including nondiscrete valuations and horizontal valuations trivial on K. Item 83 now states HHKP Theorem 2.5 with this exact scope; items 139–147 extract its proof.

At the generic point of each special-fibre component, the local-point hypothesis gives a completion point. HHK Proposition 5.8 spreads this to a nonempty open patch, whose field embeds in every corresponding F_P. Hence only finitely many closed points can be bad. Blow up the bad points and repeat. If this never stops, finite branching gives an infinite compatible chain of bad points. Their local rings have a directed union that is a valuation ring: Lipman **1969** Theorem 26.2 excludes infinitely many successive indeterminacy points for each fixed rational function, so eventually either f or f^(-1) belongs to the local ring. This is distinct from Lipman **1978** on resolution.

The resulting valuation has rank one or two. In rank two its rank-one coarsening has the same completion, and its henselization embeds into that of the finer valuation. Smoothness descends a completion point to the rank-one henselization via an étale chart and the henselian implicit function theorem. Henselization commutes with this filtered union of local rings, and its fraction field embeds in the union of the completed local fraction fields F_P_i. Finite presentation descends the point to one finite stage, contradicting badness.

For the henselization step use the **colimit** of residue fields. It is not necessary, and not generally justified, to identify every stage's residue field with the limit as a sentence in the printed proof does. Also, HHKP's Artin approximation argument in Proposition 2.3(b) is not needed for this Theorem 2.5 dependency; do not import it as a hidden assumption.

Combining this precise theorem with GP26's all-model-points theorem gives item 148: the rank-one T-valuation subfamily already suffices for flags, without a characteristic restriction. This is a derived strengthening, not a quotation of GP26 Corollary 4.8(1). The HHK spreading, Lipman 1969 and general implicit-function antecedents remain separately identified proof-interior obligations; reading HHKP does not certify that all its references have been extracted.
