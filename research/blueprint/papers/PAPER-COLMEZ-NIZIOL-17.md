## What the paper proves

Fontaine–Messing and Kato constructed period morphisms `α^FM_{r,n} : S_n(r)_X → i^*Rj_*Z/p^n(r)′_{X_tr}` from log-syntomic cohomology to p-adic nearby cycles. Kato, Kurihara and Tsuji proved they are isomorphisms for `i ≤ r ≤ p−1`, and Tsuji proved a version over `K̄`. This paper removes the restriction on the twist, at the price of universal constants.

> **Theorem 1.1 = Theorem 5.4.** For `X` with semistable reduction over `O_K` (or a base change of such) and `0 ≤ i ≤ r`, the kernel and cokernel of `α^FM_{r,n} : H^i(S_n(r)_X) → i^*R^ij_*Z/p^n(r)′_{X_tr}` are annihilated by `p^{Nr+c_p}` when `K` has enough roots of unity, `N` universal and `c_p` depending only on `p`; and by `p^N` with `N = N(K,p,r)` in general.
>
> **Corollary 1.10 = Corollary 5.26 (the semistable conjecture for formal schemes).** For `𝔛` a proper semistable formal scheme over `O_K` there is a natural `B_st`-linear Galois-equivariant `α : H^j(𝔛_{K̄,tr},Q_p) ⊗ B_st ≅ H^j_HK(𝔛) ⊗_F B_st` preserving `ϕ` and `N`, inducing a filtered isomorphism after `⊗B_dR`.

The proof of the first is not a direct comparison. The authors build a **second** local period map `α^Laz_r`, a version of an integral Lazard isomorphism between Lie-algebra and continuous group cohomology, prove directly that it is a quasi-isomorphism (Theorem 4.14), and then show it agrees with Fontaine–Messing's up to the same constants (Theorem 4.16). The chain, each step a `p^{Cr}`-quasi-isomorphism with an explicit `C`, is: the syntomic complex `Syn(R,r)` built from the PD envelope is moved onto a disk and then an annulus of convergence (`p^{6r}`, then `p^{2r}` after truncation), using the operator `ψ` left inverse to `ϕ` and the acyclicity of the `ψ = 0` eigencomplex; the crystalline Frobenius `ϕ_Kum` is exchanged for the cyclotomic `ϕ_cycl` of (φ,Γ)-module theory through a fat log-PD envelope and a filtered Poincaré lemma (`2^{2(d+1)}` — the base is 2, from the partial divided-power level); differential forms become a Koszul complex over `A^{[u,v]}_R`; multiplication by `t^r` kills the filtration and turns the derivations into the action of `Lie Γ_R` (`p^{40r}`, and **the one place where truncation at degree r is unavoidable**, since `t^r` is not a `p^N`-isomorphism in degrees `≥ r+1` for any `N`); analyticity passes from `Lie Γ_R` to `Γ_R`; (φ,Γ)-module technique walks the domain of convergence down to `A_R`; and Koszul formalism, almost étale descent with decompletion, and the relative Artin–Schreier sequence land on `RΓ_cont(G_R,Z_p(r))`. Globally (§5.1) this is glued by Abhyankar's lemma with Elkik approximation, a K(π,1)-lemma for the trivialised locus of an affinoid extending Scholze's to a non-trivial divisor at infinity, a rigid GAGA comparison, and a ramified descent that removes the roots-of-unity hypothesis.

The second theorem is where the method pays off. Combining the comparison with **Colmez's finite dimensional Banach Spaces** — whose Dimension `(a,b) ∈ N × Z` is additive on short exact sequences, and for which a map from a successive extension of `V_1`'s into a Dimension-`(0,d)` Space is zero — one shows that syntomic cohomology sits in a short exact sequence between a Frobenius eigenspace of Hyodo–Kato cohomology and a quotient of de Rham cohomology, and that the resulting filtered `(ϕ,N)`-module is weakly admissible with `V_st` equal to the étale cohomology. **No Poincaré duality is used**, and that is exactly what allows the passage from schemes to formal schemes, where duality is unavailable.

**Route 1 is new because the integral, logarithmic, constant-controlled theory has no home.** `PadicHodgeRegulators:D.2` builds a syntomic mapping fibre and the regulators in the *smooth unramified* setting and owns the Bloch–Kato exponential — so this paper's `S_n(r)_X`, its `α^FM_{r,n}` and its `α_{r,i}` (Corollaries 3.16 and 5.11, the exponential for a quasi-compact semistable formal scheme) are sources for it. `CohomologyComparisons:CP.4` owns the `B_st` comparison for proper flat semistable formal models, reached through `AI.6` and `CR.6`; this paper reproves it by a different route and extends it to formal schemes, so it is a source there too. And `PrismaticCohomology:PR.4` owns a *different* nearby-cycle statement — perfect base prism, smooth formal scheme, no constants — which must not be imported for this one. What has no owner is the machinery: the log-syntomic complexes and sheaves, §3's local computation with its changes of disk, annulus and Frobenius, §4.7's Fontaine–Messing map and its identification with the Lazard map, §5.1's globalisation, and §5.2's Banach-Space argument.

**Route 2 joins an id another extraction already proposed.** `PhiGammaModulesAndIwasawaCohomology` (PG.0–PG.7) is the cyclotomic (φ,Γ)-module engine over a p-adic field, with PG.7 covering the relative Robba category of Kedlaya–Pottharst–Xiao for affinoid families. This paper needs the *arithmetic-geometric* relative theory instead, and `PAPER-LIU-ZHU-17` has already proposed exactly that direction as `PhiGammaModulesPartIIGeometricTowers` — geometric toric towers, relative Robba rings at bounded radii, weak and local decompletion, `Γ_geom ⋊ Γ_cycl`. Reusing a proposed id is the convention of §15, so this extraction joins it and states what it adds: the **logarithmic and semistable** case with its **Kummer tower** alongside the cyclotomic one, the two Frobenii and the change between them, the integral `ψ` with its eigen-decomposition and the `ψ = 0` estimates, the fat log-PD envelopes of §2.6 with their structure theorem and filtered Poincaré lemma, and the Koszul calculus of §4.2–§4.5 with the integral Lazard isomorphism `Laz = (β,β^c)`.

**The relation to the Stein-space work is import, not overlap.** `PAPER-COLMEZ-DOSPINESCU-NIZIOL-20` proposed `SyntomicCohomologyAndSteinComparison` for the *overconvergent, rational* theory of Stein spaces, and cites this paper for the Fontaine–Messing identification; route 1 is its foundation.

## The 23 source issues

Crossref shows no correction relation for the DOI, the Springer page lists no corrigendum, the paper is not on arXiv, and the authors' later work on the same material carries no correction notice. Every finding below was checked by this session against the printed page — in rendered clips where the point is glyph-level — and the candidates that did not survive that check, or that could not be checked at their locator, were dropped rather than recorded.

**Reaches a stated result (1).** `E1`: **Theorem 1.1(ii) and Theorem 5.4(ii) are the same theorem and disagree about the constant.** §1 says `N = N(e,p,r)`, "which depends on e, r, but not on X or n"; §5 says `N = N(K,p,r)`, "which depends on K, p, r". The proof is §5's: it passes to `R_i = R(ζ_{p^i})` with `i ≥ c(K)+3`, where `c(K)` is the conductor of `K`, and descends by Lemma 5.9, whose constant involves the relative ramification index and the different. Remark 1.7 confirms the reading — the descent introduces "constants depending on K", and the authors write that it "seems likely" one could make them depend only on the different, which is an expectation. So the introduction's form overstates what is proved.

**Affects a proof (2).** `E16`: Proposition 4.9 is stated as a `p^8`-quasi-isomorphism, while the two lemmas its one-line proof invokes give `p^{p+7}`, and `p+7 > 8` for every prime. `E22`: Lemma 5.9's stated `p^{cre_1}` with `c` universal does not match the constants its proof produces (of the shape `rp·v_p(δ_{K_1/K_0}) + e_1`), and `K_0` is never defined in the paper — the notation is imported from Nekovář–Nizioł, while §2.2.1 defines an unrelated `δ_K`.

**Two constants in stated results are unusable as printed.** `E11`: Corollary 3.9 induces a "`p^{2c_e+2r}`-quasi-isomorphism", and `c_e` occurs exactly once in the whole paper — here — and is defined nowhere; the constant its proof produces is `p^{2r}`, since the two outer steps are quasi-isomorphisms with no constant and Lemma 3.6 gives `p^{2r}`. `E9`: Lemma 3.1's proof offers `N(r,e) = inf_k(−1−kr+p^{k−1}/e)`, which is negative, where Definition 1.13 needs a nonnegative exponent; the infimum is the valuation bound, so the exponent is its negative.

**The rest are misprints whose intended reading is clear** (`affects: nothing`), and they are worth having because a formaliser works from the corrected text: `ϕ^{cycl}` for `ϕ_Kum` in §2.2.7, before `ϕ_cycl` is defined (E3); an undefined `r_R` for `v_R` in Lemma 2.18, four times (E4); `ϖ_n = θ(ϕ^{−1}(π_K))`, whose right-hand side does not depend on `n` (E5); an exponent in Lemma 2.34's first display inconsistent with its own proof and with the second display, which is its `k`-fold iterate (E6); `π = π_1^{p−1} + pπ_1^{p−2} + ⋯ + p`, which has a constant term and so is `π/π_1`, not `π` (E7); `F^{r_1}R_1, F^{r_2}R_2` for `F^{r_1}S, F^{r_2}Λ` in §2.6.1, the symbols `R_1, R_2` appearing only in §3.6 (E8); a circular definition of `R^PD_{ϖ,N}` (E12); four slips in one proof of Lemma 3.2 — a multi-index in an exponent `p^jω_j` where `p^iω_j` is meant, `≤` for `⊂`, `⊂` for `∈`, and a spurious `p^{−s}` (E10); an index range `1 ≤ j ≤ d+1` where the rest of the paper uses `0 ≤ j ≤ d` (E13, harmless: the two span the same module); an exact sequence `0 → Z_p[[Γ_K]] --γ_0−1--> Z_p[[Γ_K]] → 0` missing its `Z_p`, whose presence is what the conclusion needs (E14); "Lemma 4.5" for "Proposition 4.5", twice (E15); `Syn(R,r)_n` defined with `E^PD_{R,n}` in place of `R^PD_{ϖ,n}`, which would make the period map a self-map (E17); `[8, 1.9.2]` for `[7, 1.9.2]`, attributing Beilinson's identification to Berger (E18); "section 2.1.2" twice, for §2.2.2 — the paper has no §2.1.2 (E19); a log-purity twist `(−1)` where `(−t)` is needed (E20); a localisation sequence whose first term is two degrees off (E21); `⊗_{Q_p} B_st` where `B_dR` is meant in Corollary 1.10's filtered display, correct in Corollary 5.26 (E2); and Remark 5.27's `[49]` — a 2008 K-theory paper — where `[52]`, the Fargues–Fontaine paper that is in the bibliography and cited nowhere, is the one that lifts these triangles to Banach Spaces (E23).

## Gaps

**Proposition 3.19 is stated without proof by the authors** — "We will state the following proposition without a proof" — and attributed to nobody, so a design job must plan the syntomic cohomology of `O^×_K` as an obligation; Remark 3.18 depends on it. **Three statements are expectations**: Remark 1.7's two (constants depending only on the different; a version for a general affinoid with constants measuring the defect of smoothness) and Remark 5.28's (that Scholze's de Rham conjecture follows from Corollary 5.26 via Hartl's local models), plus §2.2.6's aside that Breuil–Kisin or `(ϕ,τ)`-modules might replace `(ϕ,Γ)`-modules. **The constants are crude and inconsistently stated** (E1, E11, E16, E22), so a layer must fix one convention and prove the dependence it claims. **§5's bracketed squares are only commutative in the derived category** — §1.2.3 says the total-complex model is available "everywhere but in Section 5" — so a formalisation there needs genuine homotopy limits. **Corollary 5.21 appears to have lost a claim**: its proof speaks of a first, a second and a final claim, following Corollary 5.12, which has a mod-`p^n` part and a rational part. **The prismatic comparison is not addressed**: `PR.4` proves a different statement and the two must not be identified. **The roots-of-unity hypothesis is quantitative**, used both to have `ι_cycl` at all and, through `δ_R + 1 < e_0/(2p)`, to make the series defining `∇_j/τ_j` converge. And **truncation at degree `r` is unavoidable** in Lemma 4.4 for a structural reason, not merely to keep constants small.
