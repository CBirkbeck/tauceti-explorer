## What the paper proves

A map of qcqs schemes `Y → X` is a **v-cover** if every map `Spec(V) → X` from a valuation ring lifts to `Y` after
an extension `V → W` of valuation rings, and an **arc-cover** if the same holds with both valuation rings of rank
`≤ 1` (Definitions 1.1 and 1.2). Every v-cover is an arc-cover (Proposition 2.1); over a noetherian base the two
agree (Proposition 2.6, from Rydh); and in general they differ — for a rank 2 valuation ring `V` with height one
prime `p`, the map `V → V_p × V/p` is an arc-cover but not a v-cover (Example 1.3). The five strands of the paper:

* **A topological characterisation (Proposition 2.19).** Arc-covers are exactly the *universally spectrally
  submersive* maps: surjections such that, after any base change, a subset whose preimage is quasi-compact open is
  itself open. Unlike ordinary submersions, spectral submersions are stable under cofiltered limits (Lemma 2.17);
  Remark 2.18 gives a universal spectral submersion over a valuation ring with `Spec(V) = {0} ∪ {1/n}` which is not
  a quotient map, which is why the refinement is needed.
* **The criterion (Theorem 4.1).** For `C` compactly generated by cotruncated objects and `F` a *finitary* functor
  satisfying v-descent, three conditions are equivalent: `F` is an arc-sheaf; `F` is excisive, i.e. carries Milnor
  squares to pullback squares; `F` satisfies *aic-v-excision*, i.e. for every absolutely integrally closed
  valuation ring `V` and every prime `p ⊂ V`, `F(V) → F(V/p)`, `F(V_p) → F(κ(p))` is cartesian. The proof detects
  descent by ultraproducts (Lemma 3.25) and by absolutely integrally closed valuation rings (Proposition 3.30), and
  then runs an induction over the *intervals* `[p, q] ⊂ Spec(V)` of a valuation ring (Definition 4.6, Lemma 4.7).
  Sections 4.2–4.3 remove the finitariness hypothesis by showing that excision squares become pushouts in the
  arc-topos (Proposition 4.24, Corollary 4.25), which needs the coherence of that topos (Lemma 4.19) — and the
  arc-topology is not subcanonical (Warning 4.20), so sheafification is not optional.
* **The examples (Section 5).** Étale cohomology with torsion coefficients is an arc-sheaf (Theorem 5.4), and so is
  `X ↦ D^b_cons(X_ét, Λ)` for a finite ring `Λ`, hypercompletely (Theorem 5.13); Rydh's categories of étale objects
  are finitary arc-sheaves (Theorem 5.6); the Huber–Jörder h-differentials in characteristic zero are an arc-sheaf
  (Theorem 5.14); and perfect complexes on perfections of `F_p`-schemes satisfy arc-hyperdescent (Theorem 5.16),
  with arc-covers of perfect schemes being exactly the universally effective epimorphisms (Theorem 5.17).
* **The consequences (Section 6).** Every arc-sheaf is excisive (Theorem 1.12) and satisfies formal glueing
  (Theorem 1.15), because `Spec(B) ⊔ Spec(A/I) → Spec(A)` and `Spec(R̂_I) ⊔ (Spec(R) \ V(I)) → Spec(R)` are
  arc-covers (Lemma 2.7, Proposition 6.3) although neither is a v-cover in general (Remark 1.13, Example 6.8).
  Applied to étale cohomology this gives excision and formal glueing (Corollary 1.17), the Gabber–Huber affine
  analogue of proper base change when `R` is an algebra over a henselian local ring (Corollary 1.18(1)), and the
  Fujiwara–Gabber theorem `RΓ((Spec R \ V(I))_ét, F) ≅ RΓ((Spec R̂_I \ V(IR̂_I))_ét, F)` for a henselian pair
  (Theorem 6.11). An `arc_t`-variant adapted to rigid geometry (Definition 6.14) gives descent for the étale
  cohomology of rigid generic fibres (Corollary 6.17), GAGA in the proper case (Corollary 6.18), invariance under
  `arc_t`-equivalences such as completion and total integral closure (Proposition 6.21), and a Künneth formula
  (Proposition 6.22).
* **The application (Section 7).** For `K` complete and algebraically closed nonarchimedean and `A` topologically
  finite type of dimension `d`, `H^i(Spec(A)_ét, F) = 0` for `i > d + 1` for every torsion sheaf, and for `i > d`
  when `F` is `ℓ`-power torsion with `ℓ` prime to the residue characteristic (Theorem 7.3). The proof is an
  induction on dimension using Noether normalisation for affinoids, the analytic Mayer–Vietoris square
  (Proposition 7.6, itself an instance of `arc_t`-descent), the limit formula
  `colim_r RΓ(Spec R⟨f/π^r⟩, F) ≅ RΓ(Spec R/f, F)` (Proposition 7.7), and a tensor-power trick via the Künneth
  formula to improve `d + 1` to `d`. It extends Hansen's results and confirms his Conjecture 1.2 in characteristic
  zero.

## What the atlas already has

* **The v-topology in the atlas is a different one.** `DiamondsAndVStacks:D2` builds the big pro-étale site, the
  small pro-étale site and the **v-site of perfectoid spaces**, with the covering condition of Scholze's *Étale
  cohomology of diamonds*. That is not the v-topology on qcqs schemes of Definition 1.1, and Example 6.15 of this
  paper is the only place where the two are compared (the `arc_t`-topology on affinoid rigid spaces is *finer* than
  the diamond v-topology).
* **Classical sites and descent are planned.** `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.1` plans effective fpqc/fppf descent

  constructible coefficient categories; `EnhancedDerivedSheaves:E2` plans unbounded cohomological descent and
  Postnikov completion after Bhatt–Scholze's pro-étale paper. These are the inputs the paper uses, and three items
  of the extraction are marked `planned` against them (étale cohomology with its base-change theorems; fpqc descent
  and algebraic spaces; classical Artin–Grothendieck vanishing).

## The routes

### 1. New roadmap `ArcTopologyAndDescent` — 122 items

"The arc-topology and descent for étale cohomology", area `etale`. It plans the v- and arc-topologies on qcqs
schemes and their comparison; the valuation-theoretic toolkit (rank `≤ 1` and absolutely integrally closed
valuation rings, faithfully flat aic extensions, cardinality bounds, filtered-colimit presentations, the structure
of specialisations); the ∞-categorical descent formalism (finitary functors, universal `F`-descent, targets
compactly generated by cotruncated objects, detection by ultraproducts and by valuation rings); the interval
induction and the criterion; the arc-topos, its coherence and the pushout descriptions of excision and formal
glueing squares; the five families of examples; and the `arc_t`-variant with the rigid-analytic vanishing theorem.

**Why a new roadmap and not a Part II.** The nearest parents are `SchemeAndStackFoundations` — whose SF.1 and SF.2
own fpqc descent and the classical sites of schemes — and `DiamondsAndVStacks`, whose D2 owns the v-site of
perfectoid spaces. Neither goes in this direction: SF is a general foundations roadmap whose layers stop at the
pro-étale site and whose later layers are curves, deformation theory and intersection theory, and D2's v-topology
is a different site on a different category. What this paper adds is not further layers of either but a programme
of its own — a topology, a descent criterion, a list of sheaves for it, and four classical theorems reproved from
it — which imports from five existing roadmaps. PROTOCOL section 15 reserves Part II for new layers *in the
direction of* an existing roadmap; this is the `new` case of the maintainer's worked examples (Lawrence–Venkatesh:
"nothing in the atlas followed this proof").

The brief names the imports explicitly: SF.0 for schemes and limits of schemes, SF.1 for fpqc descent and algebraic
spaces, SF.2 for the classical sites and base-change theorems, EDC.0 for constructible coefficients and EDC.4 for
affine Artin vanishing, E2 for unbounded cohomological descent and hypercompleteness, D0 for spectral spaces and
profinite techniques, LD.0 for ultraproducts, K.5–K.6 for Milnor squares and K-theory localisation, the Tau Ceti
`AdicSpaces` roadmap (Layers 0 and 1) and `AdicEtaleGeometry` A0–A1 for Tate algebras and adic spectra, and
`GeometricSatakeAndFusion:GS0:Witt-geometry` for perfections in characteristic `p`.

### 2. Source for `DiamondsAndVStacks` D0 — 8 items

D0 extends the spectral-space API with constructible and pro-constructible subsets, spectral maps, "limits and
quotient criteria", and supplies "profinite decompositions, extremally disconnected covers and the Stone–Čech
argument where used". Submersions and spectral submersions, their stability under cofiltered limits, the
counterexample of Remark 2.18 — which is exactly the "not every quotient of a spectral space is spectral" trap D0
warns against — and the identification of sheaves on a profinite set with functors on its clopens, with stalks at
ultrafilters, are facts of that kind rather than facts about the arc-topology. The arc-topology roadmap imports
them.

### 3. Source for `LogicAndDefinabilityInNumberTheory` LD.0 — 6 items

LD.0 constructs ultraproducts and proves Łoś's transfer theorem. Section 3.2 of the paper is the ring-theoretic
half of that layer: the ultraproduct `∏_U A_t` as a filtered colimit, its identification with the coordinate ring
of the fibre of `Spec(∏_t A_t) → βT`, the transfer statement that an ultraproduct of absolutely integrally closed
valuation rings is again one, and the detection of equivalences and of truncatedness on ultraproducts.

### 4. Source for `GeneralAlgebraicKTheory` K.5 and K.6 — 3 items

K.5 plans relative K-theory of a pair and "excision under the hypotheses of the adopted theorem", with the explicit
warning that K-theory does *not* satisfy unrestricted excision; K.6 plans nonconnective K-theory and its
localisation sequences. Excision data and Milnor squares belong there, as does the formal glueing square for
nonconnective K-theory (Example 6.1), which is a localisation-sequence statement. The arc-topology roadmap imports
the definition and proves that every arc-sheaf is excisive — which is what makes the contrast with K-theory sharp.

## Mistakes in the source

Three findings, recorded under `sourceIssues`. The first two were found by auditing every hand-typed environment
name against the environment the `\ref` target actually names, over the whole source; those two are the only
mismatches in the paper.

* **E1** (misprint, Remark 3.31): "Proposition 3.29 and Lemma 3.27 imply…" — 3.29 is **Lemma** 3.29 (the
  cardinality bound for absolutely integrally closed valuation rings). Affects nothing.
* **E2** (misprint, proof of Corollary 6.18, last sentence): "this follows from formal glueing for étale cohomology
  (Proposition 6.4)" — 6.4 is **Theorem** 6.4. Affects nothing.
* **E3** (gap, Theorem 1.19 of the introduction together with Remark 7.4(2)). Theorem 1.19 asserts, for an
  arbitrary `ℓ`-power torsion sheaf `F` on a `d`-dimensional classical affinoid `A`, that `H^i(Spec(A)_ét, F) = 0`
  for `i > d` "if `A` is smooth **or** if `ℓ` is not the residue characteristic of `K`", and is labelled as
  Theorem 7.3. But Theorem 7.3 proves the vanishing above degree `d` only in the second case; the smooth case
  appears only in Remark 7.4(2), only for **constant** coefficients, and only as a sketch: "The algebraization
  method used to prove Lemma 7.8 below **can be adapted** to prove this statement when `A` is smooth (in the sense
  of rigid spaces) and `F` is constant, thanks to [Elkik, Theorem 7 and Remark 2 on page 587]." Remark 7.4 itself
  opens by saying the `p`-torsion case is what the authors "would expect", and lists the smooth constant case as
  one of two special cases "within reach". So the introduction states a result at a generality the body does not
  prove; the fix is either to restrict the smooth disjunct to constant coefficients and point at Remark 7.4(2), or
  to write the adaptation out. This affects a stated result, not the proofs of Theorems 4.1, 5.4, 6.11 or 7.3.

No erratum was found: arXiv 1807.04725 has versions v1–v4 with v4 the latest, the Project Euclid page for the
published version served no text to an automated request, and the second author's homepage (the link given in the
job) lists the paper without an erratum.

## A note on method

`pdftotext` was not available in this environment, so the paper was read in its arXiv LaTeX source. This is better
than extracted PDF text for faithfulness — no lost accents, hats or bars, and the exact hypotheses of every
statement — at the cost of page numbers, which is why the locators are statement numbers. The numbering was
computed from the source by walking `\section` and the shared theorem counter, and cross-checked against the
paper's own internal references (for instance Theorem 1.7 pointing at Theorem 4.1, and Theorem 1.8 at Theorems 5.4
and 5.13), which agree.
