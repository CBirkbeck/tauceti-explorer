## What the paper proves

Two theorems, one about a space and one about all Stein spaces.

> **Theorem 1.3.** For the Drinfeld symmetric space `H^d_K = P^d_K ∖ ⋃_{H ∈ H} H` and every `r ≥ 0`: `H^r_ét(H^d_C,Q_p(r)) ≅ Sp^cont_r(Q_p)*`; there is a strictly exact sequence of `G × G_K`-Fréchet spaces `0 → Ω^{r−1}(H^d_C)/ker d → H^r_proét(H^d_C,Q_p(r)) → Sp_r(Q_p)* → 0`; and étale cohomology is exactly the space of **`G`-bounded vectors** of pro-étale cohomology.

So the p-adic étale cohomology is the *same* dual of a Steinberg representation that Schneider–Stuhler found ℓ-adically, while the pro-étale cohomology is that dual extended by a huge space. This is remarkable because the Schneider–Stuhler method needs homotopy invariance for the open ball, which fails "rather dramatically" for p-adic étale cohomology — the p-adic étale cohomology of the unit ball is huge. What made the authors look anyway was Drinfeld's dimension-1 computation (Fact 1.1), and footnote 4 records that **Drinfeld's proof for `ℓ = p` is flawed**, as Grosse-Klönne and Berkovich pointed out to the authors; the same defect is reported in the companion paper, and it is in the errata register.

> **Theorem 1.8.** For `X` a semistable **Stein** weak formal scheme over `O_K`, a `G_K`-equivariant map of strictly exact sequences of Fréchet spaces from `0 → Ω^{r−1}(X_C)/ker d → H^r_proét(X_C,Q_p(r)) → (H^r_HK(X_k) ⊗̂_F B⁺_st)^{N=0,φ=p^r} → 0` to the de Rham sequence, with both vertical maps strict with closed images and `ker β̃ ≅ (H^r_HK(X_k) ⊗̂_F B⁺_st)^{N=0,φ=p^{r−1}}`.

Equivalently `H^r_proét` is the **pullback** of Hyodo–Kato cohomology and a piece of the de Rham complex: a *one-way* comparison theorem, with no converse recovering the Hyodo–Kato data. Remark 1.10 is the sharpest way to see what is new: for `X` proper the term `Ω^{r−1}/ker d` vanishes and an extra `B⁺_dR`-term appears — the classical semistable comparison theorem, which `CohomologyComparisons:CP.4` owns. This is the statement the two companion papers cite as "Theorem 1.8 of [17]" for the closedness of the image in their fundamental diagram.

The route is syntomic, and is the technical heart: the Fontaine–Messing period map identifies syntomic with (pro-)étale cohomology after truncation (Propositions 1.11, 3.45, 6.1); a Bloch–Kato style syntomic cohomology built from **overconvergent** differential forms is compared with it by Künneth maps that use the interpretation of period rings as crystalline cohomology of base rings (`Â_st,n ≅ RΓ_cr(O^×_{C,n}/r^PD_{ϖ,n})`), a rigidity of Frobenius eigenspaces, and a change of topology from crystalline to overconvergent that is available **because `X` is Stein**, so the components of its special fibre are proper and smooth (Proposition 3.48, Theorem 4.1). For the half-space the Hyodo–Kato term is computed by symbols, generalising Iovita–Spiess (Lemma 5.11), and the étale side is computed *integrally*: the standard model is pro-ordinary, its sheaves of differentials are acyclic with `d = 0` on global sections (Grosse-Klönne), so syntomic cohomology collapses to `H^0_ét(Ȳ,WΩ^r_log)`, which the mod p irreducibility of the Steinberg representation identifies with `Sp^cont_r(Z_p)*`. Everything is done in the quasi-abelian category of locally convex `Q_p`-vector spaces and in pro-discrete `O_K`-modules, so that every isomorphism is topological and every sequence strictly exact.

**Route 1 exists because the atlas plans Hyodo–Kato theory only for proper models.** `CR.6` builds the Hyodo–Kato complex with `φ`, `N` and `Nφ = pφN` for *proper* semistable models and says explicitly that `CP.4` owns its assembly with `B_st`; `CP.4` proves the `B_st` comparison for *proper flat* semistable models. Neither says anything about a non-proper space, and the whole point here is that for a Stein space the comparison takes a different shape. Nor is the machinery planned: dagger algebras and weak formal schemes, Stein dagger spaces and idealized log-schemes, Grosse-Klönne's overconvergent log-rigid cohomology with its compactified deformation spaces, the two syntomic theories and their comparison `ι_rig`, the convergent-cohomology detour, and §7's Chern classes and bounded Hyodo–Kato isomorphisms. `PadicHodgeRegulators:D.2` is a genuine source — it builds a syntomic mapping fibre with filtered de Rham complexes and Frobenius data, in the smooth unramified setting, for regulators — and this paper adds the semistable formal-scheme version over `O_C`, the sheafified `Q_p(r)`-variant, and the observation that the integral and rational versions **differ** when the scheme is not quasi-compact.

**Route 2 exists because the atlas has no Drinfeld symmetric space beyond dimension 1.** `ET.6a` constructs the Lubin–Tate and Drinfeld towers of `p`-divisible groups with their period morphisms and the ℓ-adic realisation (so the half-plane, as its base, is `planned` there), and `ProetaleCohomologyOfPAdicCurvesAndTowers` — proposed by the companion extraction — covers the half-plane and the tower in dimension 1. What has no home is: the generalized Steinberg representations `Sp_J` of `GL_{d+1}(K)` in their smooth, continuous and locally analytic forms with the Casselman/Grosse-Klönne irreducibility, the uniqueness of the `G`-stable lattice and the universal unitary completion; the Bruhat–Tits combinatorics of the standard semistable model; the Schneider–Stuhler computation through the Tits building; the Iovita–Spiess symbol description with its distributions and regulators; and the whole of §6 — ordinary log schemes with the Bloch–Kato/Illusie–Raynaud/Lorenzon criteria, pro-ordinarity, the logarithmic de Rham–Witt computation, the integral Hyodo–Kato isomorphism and the appendix's Artin–Schreier argument.

**Route 3 is a Part II, not a new roadmap.** `LocallyAnalyticDistributions` L0 builds Banach spaces of locally analytic functions, locally convex inductive limits, continuous duals and projective systems of Banach duals; §2 of this paper is the same direction carried much further — the quasi-abelian category `C_K`, strictness and strict exactness, Schneiders' t-structure with its hearts and the notion of *classical* cohomology, the completed tensor product with its exactness and its derived version, acyclic inductive systems, pro-discrete `O_K`-modules and the derived rationalisation functor. Every topological statement in this paper and in its two companions is proved there.

**Two items are `library`, and this is the pleasant surprise of the extraction.** Mathlib has the very notion of a strict map — `Topology.IsStrictMap` in `Mathlib/Topology/Maps/Strict/Basic.lean`, "open onto its image", with `LinearMap.isStrictMap_iff_isOpenQuotientMap_rangeRestrict` and `LinearMap.isStrictMap_iff_isHomeomorph_quotKerEquivRange` — and **Tau Ceti already proves the open mapping theorem and the closed-range criterion for strictness over a Tate ring**: `TauCeti.Huber.IsTateRing.isOpenMap`, `isStrictMap_of_isClosed_range` and `isQuotientMap` in `TauCeti/RingTheory/Huber/OpenMapping.lean`, for a surjective `A`-linear map continuous at 0 from a complete nonarchimedean module with countably generated uniformity. `K`-Fréchet spaces are complete with countably generated uniformity, so these are exactly the two statements §2.1.2 uses; only the LF version (countable inductive limits, which are not metrisable) is beyond them, and it is routed as missing.

## Source issues — 24, each verified in the text

Crossref shows no correction or update relation for the DOI, the Springer page lists no corrigendum, the paper is not on arXiv, and the authors' later papers carry no correction notice. The findings are all small — this is a very carefully written paper — but they are not all cosmetic:

- **E1 (§3.2.1, p. 19) — the map `ι : B̂⁺_st → B⁺_dR` is not defined as printed.** The chain reads `B⁺_cr/F^r ≅→ B⁺_cr,K/F^r ≅← B⁺_cr/F^r`, the same ring at both ends; the third term must be `B⁺_dR/F^r`. The next sentence then writes `B̂⁺_dR`, a ring that appears nowhere else. This `ι` is what defines `RΓ_syn(X_C,Q_p(r))`.
- **E2 (Example 3.34, p. 24) — a false identification as printed.** For `r = 0` the paper writes `(H^0_HK(X_0) ⊗̂_F B⁺_st)^{φ=p,N=0} ≅ B^{+,φ=1}_cr = Q_p`; the eigenspace must be `φ = p^0 = 1`, since `B^{+,φ=p}_cr` is infinite dimensional.
- **E3 ((3.37), p. 26)** — the exponent is printed `φ = p^i` in both rows of the diagram where `r` is fixed, and both horizontal maps are labelled `ι_HK ⊗ ι` although their target `H^r_dR(X_K) ⊗̂_K C` makes them `ι_HK ⊗ θ`.
- **E8 (Lemma 2.3(2), p. 10) — a missing hypothesis.** The proof interchanges `⊗̂_K` with an inductive limit citing §2.1.3, where only point (5) does that, under hypotheses the lemma does not assume (a *regular* system with *injective nuclear* transition maps); acyclicity alone does not give the interchange. In the applications the authors check the missing hypotheses separately, so the fix is to carry them in the statement.
- **E18 (Theorem 6.45(3), p. 58, and Remark 6.46, p. 59) — the domain of the regulators in the main theorem.** All four regulators are printed on measures on `H^{d+1}` instead of `H^{r+1}`; the regulators are defined on `(r+1)`-tuples of hyperplanes in §5.4.1, and the proof of this very statement uses `M(H^{r+1},Z_p) → Sp_r(Z_p)*`.
- **E20 (Lemma 7.3, p. 60) — the constant is not the one the proof produces.** The lemma asserts `p_0 ι_{ϖ,n} = p^{N_ι}` with `N_ι = N(d,e)` while the computation gives `p^M`, `M = M(d,e)`, and `N_ι` is used four lines earlier with a different meaning (Hyodo–Kato's constant for `K = F`). Since `ι_HK := p^{−N_ι} ι̃_HK`, it matters which constant is meant.
- The rest are single wrong indices, rings or cross-references, each checked against its context: `⊗̂_K` for `⊗̂_F` (p. 24), `D(C_K)` for `D(C_F)` (p. 17), `[74]` for Schneiders `[75]` (footnote 14), the index set `I` for `N` (p. 12), "components of `X`" for `X_0` (p. 32), `ι_HK⊗θ` for `ι_HK⊗ι` (Remark 4.13), the dual of `LC(X_J,Λ)` for the dual of `Sp_J(Λ)` (§5.2.2), `St^cont_J` for `Sp^cont_J` (p. 39), an undefined uniformiser `π` for `ϖ` (p. 40, twice), Orlik's paper attributed to part a) instead of b) (p. 40), `H^s_dR` and `Q_ℓ(s)` for `r` (p. 41), `H^s(BT_s,F)` for `H^r` (Lemma 6.8), "the Twist twist" for "the Tate twist" (p. 47), `r^PD_{ϖ,n}` for `r^PD_{p,n}` (p. 60), "Theorem 5.11" for "Lemma 5.11" (p. 53), the kernel of the Cartier operator (Appendix, p. 64), "Theorem 6.3" for "Proposition 6.3" and an index range `0 ≤ i ≤ j` that should start at 1 (p. 45), and three spelling slips.

## Recorded as gaps

The **integral statement is only announced**: Remark 1.7 says that for `r ≤ p−1` the period map is an isomorphism on the nose and that, with Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze and Česnavičius–Koshikawa, `H^r_ét(H^d_C,F_p(r)) ≅ Sp_r(F_p)*` holds for all twists — but that is the sequel (job #1322), as is the étale regulator of Remark 6.46. The **foundations of log-rigid cohomology are missing**, which is why §3.1 builds `RΓ_rig(Y/O^0_F)` by hand; a design job must plan the hand-made construction, not a general theory. **Convergent cohomology** is quoted from Ogus and Shiho and belongs to the Part II that `PAPER-KISIN-MADAPUSIPERA-SHIN-22` proposes under `CrystallineCohomology`; since that is not yet an atlas stage the items are routed here, to be imported once it exists. The **models of the examples are quoted, not constructed** — Fresnel–van der Put's theorem is used "with the proof easily modified" to weak formal schemes, and the higher-dimensional models require a blow-up along Saito's canonical ideal sheaf. **"Pro-ordinary" is never defined**, and Corollary 6.18 is deduced from a proposition that only covers the finite truncations, the case `s = ∞` needing Proposition 6.5 and an argument buried in the proof of Lemma 6.20(3). Several statements are **expectations**: that Lemma A.1 holds in much greater generality, that the weight-spectral-sequence route to pro-ordinarity works, and that `γ_HK` can be identified beyond the half-space. Finally, the **two syntomic theories genuinely differ**: `RΓ_syn(X,Z_p(r))_{Q_p} → RΓ_syn(X,Q_p(r))` is a quasi-isomorphism only for `X` of finite type, which is why the pro-étale theorem is stated rationally and the étale one integrally, and why §6 has to redo integrally what §§3–4 do rationally.
