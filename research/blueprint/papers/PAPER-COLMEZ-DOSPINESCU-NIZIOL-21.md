## What the paper proves

The integral refinement of the companion paper (`PAPER-COLMEZ-DOSPINESCU-NIZIOL-20`, job #1294), and the reason it needed a different method.

> **Theorem 1.1 = Theorem 5.1.** For the Drinfeld symmetric space `H^d_K` and every `i ≥ 0` there are `G × G_K`-equivariant topological isomorphisms `H^i_ét(H^d_C,Z_p(i)) ≅ Sp^i(Z_p)*` and `H^i_ét(H^d_C,F_p(i)) ≅ Sp^i(F_p)*`, compatible with the rational isomorphism of the companion paper. In particular the integral cohomology is `p`-torsion free, and both vanish above degree `d`.

The obstacle is stated plainly in the introduction. The integral comparison theorems — Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze as extended to the semistable case by Česnavičius–Koshikawa — deduce `p`-torsion freeness of the étale cohomology of the generic fibre from that of the de Rham cohomology of a **proper** semistable model, and the companion paper does prove that the integral de Rham cohomology of the standard model is torsion free; but those theorems are proved using properness, the standard model of `H^d_K` is not even quasi-compact, and the authors say it is not clear how to adapt them. The syntomic method of the companion paper is no help either: it "works well only up to some absolute constants", which is exactly why that paper stopped at the rational answer.

So the computation is moved one level down, to `A_inf`-cohomology:

> **Theorem 1.4 = Theorem 4.1.** For `𝔛 = X_{O_K} ⊗̂_{O_K} O_C` the base change of the standard semistable model, a topological `φ^{-1}`-equivariant isomorphism of `G × G_K`-modules `H^i_ét(𝔛,AΩ_𝔛{i}) ≅ A_inf ⊗̂_{Z_p} Sp^i(Z_p)*`.

and the étale answer is read off from it by a formula that **needs no quasi-compactness**: the nearby-cycle description of Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze's chapter 10 gives a twisted Artin–Schreier sequence `0 → H^{i−1}(AΩ{i})/(1−φ^{-1}) → H^i_ét(X,Z_p(i)) → H^i(AΩ{i})^{φ^{-1}=1} → 0`, and `A_inf^{φ^{-1}=1} = Z_p` with `A_inf{1}/(1−φ^{-1}) = 0` collapses it to `Sp^i(Z_p)*`. The mod `p` statement follows from `p`-torsion freeness and the mod `p` irreducibility of the Steinberg representation.

Theorem 4.1 itself is proved by a **regulator plus derived Nakayama**. §3 builds an `A_inf`-symbol map `r_inf : O(X)^{*,⊗i} → H^i_ét(𝔛,AΩ_𝔛{i})` out of two ingredients: the pro-étale Kummer sequence, which needs Scholze's `R^i lim Z/p^n = 0` because the pro-étale site is not replete, and Morrow's twisted Artin–Schreier quasi-isomorphism `Ẑ_p(1) ≅ [A_inf{1} —1−φ^{-1}→ A_inf{1}]`; Proposition 3.3 then proves the one compatibility the argument needs, `ι_HT ∘ r_inf = r_HT`, by reducing to a small framed algebra and using Fontaine's normalisation `θ̃(μx{1}) = (ζ_p − 1)x{1}` and the lax monoidality of `Lη`. §4 integrates those symbols against `Z_p`-valued measures on `H^{i+1}` — the Schneider–Stuhler/Iovita–Spiess description of the Steinberg dual as measures modulo the degenerate ones — and shows the result is an isomorphism by reducing modulo `ξ̃`: both sides are derived `ξ̃`-complete (Česnavičius–Koshikawa) and `ξ̃`-torsion free (proved by hand from the specialisation sequences), and modulo `ξ̃` the map becomes, through the Hodge–Tate specialisation and Grosse-Klönne's acyclicity of logarithmic differentials on the standard model, the Hodge–Tate regulator that the companion paper had already computed.

## Source issues — 14, each verified in the text

Crossref shows no correction or update relation for the DOI, the Euclid page lists no corrigendum, the paper is not on arXiv, and the authors' later papers on the same geometry carry no correction notice. Four of the fourteen are substantive:

- **E1 (§4.4, p. 22, twice) — `ι_HK` for `ι_HT`.** The diagram that carries the derived-Nakayama reduction labels its vertical map `ι_HK`, and the sentence after it says "the map `ι_HK` is a continuous isomorphism"; the map is the Hodge–Tate specialisation `ι_HT` of §2.3.2, Hyodo–Kato plays no role in this diagram, and the commutativity is justified on the next line "by Proposition 3.3", whose statement is about `ι_HT`. The companion paper does use an `ι_HK`, which is presumably where the label comes from.
- **E2 (§4.4, p. 23) — a lost twist.** The exact sequence extracted from the triangle `AΩ_𝔛{i} —ξ̃→ AΩ_𝔛{i} → AΩ_𝔛{i}/ξ̃` is printed with middle term `H^i_ét(𝔛,AΩ_𝔛/ξ̃)`; both outer terms carry `{i}`, and `α` was defined one page earlier as the map into `H^i_ét(𝔛,AΩ_𝔛{i}/ξ̃)`.
- **E3 (proof of Corollary 4.13, p. 21) — an object of the wrong degree.** The factorisation of `r_inf,n` is printed through `M_n := Hom(Π(n),Z_p(i))`; a degree-`i` regulator cannot factor through `H^1` for `i ≥ 2`. The analogous `M_n := Hom(Π(n),Z_p(1))` in the proof of Proposition 4.8 is correct because the degree there is 1, and the later use of `M_n` in this same proof ("both `A_inf ⊗̂_{Z_p} M_n` and `RΓ_ét(𝔘_n,AΩ{i})` are derived `(p,μ)`-adically complete") treats it as a complex.
- **E8 (proof of Lemma 2.10(b), p. 11) — an index `j` that the lemma never introduces.** The bottom row of the diagram is printed `A_inf/μ^j —1−ξ^iφ^{-1}→ A_inf/μ^j` and the proof then appeals to part (a); the row is the cone of multiplication by `μ^i`, so it must be `A_inf/μ^i`, and part (a) applies precisely because `i ≥ i` — for a general `j` it would need `i ≥ j` and would not give the claim.

The rest: `K^M_{i+1}(O(X)^*)` for `K^M_{i+1}(O(X))` and the range `0 ≤ j ≤ i` for `1 ≤ j ≤ i` in the same proof on p. 20 (E4, E5); `Sp^i(Z_p)*` for `Sp^{i−1}(Z_p)*` in the vanishing computation on p. 23, harmless because the factor is killed (E6); `a{1} = (1/μ)a(1) ∈ A_inf(1)` where the twist `A_inf{1}` is meant, in the very sentence that defines it (E7); the Stacks tag `091V` cited for two independent statements, the left adjoint and the generator-wise criterion (E9); `[5, Cor. 6.5]` on p. 5 against `[5, Lemma 6.5]` in footnote 9 for the same result (E10); an undefined `r_*` in the definition of the symbol maps, where the two constructed maps `r_proét` and `r_inf` are meant (E11); `γ` used in one paragraph both for the Artin–Schreier quasi-isomorphism and for the comparison of Theorem 2.9, which makes the definition of the symbol map ambiguous on a first reading (E12); **"The work of Schneider-Stuhler [10, Sec. 5.4.1]"**, which attaches the authors' own companion paper to Schneider–Stuhler, who are `[21]` (E13); and four spellings — "non zero-devisor", "we way assume", "continuty", "distibutions" (E14).

## Recorded as gaps

The **abstract duality is not a duality**: Remark 1.2(iii) pairs this computation with the compactly supported one of Colmez–Dospinescu–Hauseux–Nizioł and notes that no Poincaré duality is known for spaces like `H^d_C`, and that the unit ball shows there is no naive one — so a design job must plan the two computations separately and must not derive either from the other. **Properness is the obstacle in the sources**: `AI.5` and `AI.6` are stated for proper models and cannot be quoted here, which is the whole reason for the Part II. The **syntomic and `A_inf` routes are not interchangeable** — the former gives the rational answer and the integral de Rham torsion-freeness, the latter the integral étale answer. **Theorem 2.9 is quoted from a source that proves it only in the good-reduction case**; §2.4 extends the proof to an arbitrary flat formal model, which is why it is routed with the Part II and not as a planned item. And the subscript in `LC_c(H^{i+1},Z)` is **never explained** and cannot mean compact support (§4.1.1 notes that locally constant functions on these compact spaces automatically have it); it abbreviates harmonicity and vanishing on linearly dependent tuples, and a formalisation should take those two conditions as the definition.
