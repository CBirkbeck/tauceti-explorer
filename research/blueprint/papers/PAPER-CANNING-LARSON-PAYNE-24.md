## What the paper proves

Write `M̄_{g,n}` for the moduli stack of stable `n`-pointed genus `g` curves, `d_{g,n} = 3g − 3 + n` for its
dimension, `RH^*` for the tautological subrings of `H^*(M̄_{g,n})`, `L = H^2(P^1)` and `S_12 = H^{11}(M̄_{1,11})`,
`S_16 = W_{15}H^{15}(M_{1,15})` for the motives of the level-one cusp forms of weight 12 and 16.

* **The main theorem (Theorem 1.1).** For all `g` and `n`, `H^{13}(M̄_{g,n})^{ss} ≅ ⊕ L·S_12` and
  `H^{14}(M̄_{g,n})^{ss} ≅ ⊕ L^7`; and for `g ≥ 2`, `H^{15}(M̄_{g,n})^{ss} ≅ ⊕ L^2·S_12`. This confirms the
  predictions of Chenevier and Lannes in motivic weights 13, 14 and 15; previous work had settled all degrees
  `≤ 12`.
* **Semi-tautological extensions (Definition 1.2).** An STE is a collection of subrings of `H^*(M̄_{g,n})`
  containing `RH^*` and closed under the tautological operations that *raise* dimension: pullback along forgetting
  and permuting markings, pushforward along gluing. Dropping the other two operations is what makes the notion
  flexible; the STEs actually used here turn out to be closed under all of them (Proposition 2.4).
* **Finite generation (Theorem 1.3).** For each `k`, the STE generated by the finitely many groups
  `H^{k'}(M̄_{g',n'})` with `k' ≤ k`, `g' < (3/2)k' + 1`, `n' ≤ k'` and `4g' − 4 + n' ≥ k'` contains
  `H^k(M̄_{g,n})` for all `g` and `n`. Hence only finitely many simple Hodge structures or Galois representations
  occur in each degree (Corollary 1.4). The three inputs are Lemma 3.1(b) (`W_kH^k(M_{g,n}) = Φ^k_{g,n}` when
  `n ≥ k`), the virtual cohomological dimension `4g − 4 + n`, and Harer–Wahl stability.
* **Tautological results (Theorem 1.5).** `RH^*` contains `H^4(M̄_{g,n})` for all `g, n` — confirming a prediction
  of Arbarello and Cornalba from the 1990s, previously known only for `g ≥ 8` — as well as `H^6` for `g ≥ 10` and
  `H_k` for even `k ≤ 14`.
* **Nontrivial extensions (Theorems 1.6 and 1.7).** `H_{13}(M̄_{g,n})` lies in the STE generated by
  `H^{11}(M̄_{1,11})`, and `H_{15}(M̄_{g,n})` in the STE generated by `H^{11}(M̄_{1,11})` and `H^{15}(M̄_{1,15})`.
* **The new tool (Lemma 4.3).** A space `X` has the *Chow–Künneth generation Property* if
  `A_*(X) ⊗ A_*(Y) → A_*(X × Y)` is surjective for all `Y`. If `X` is an open substack of a smooth proper
  Deligne–Mumford stack and has the CKgP, then the cycle class map `⊕_i A_i(X) → ⊕_k W_kH^k(X)` is surjective; so
  `W_kH^k(X) = 0` for odd `k` and is pure Hodge–Tate for even `k`. This is the extension, to spaces that are not
  proper, of the statement that the CKgP forces the cycle class map to be an isomorphism, and it is what controls
  the motives in `H^k(M̄_{g,n})` for `k ≥ 13`, where — unlike for `k ≤ 12` — the required base cases are no longer
  pure Hodge–Tate.
* **The genus seven base cases (Theorem 1.10).** For `n ≤ 3`, `M_{7,n}` has the CKgP and `R^*(M_{7,n}) =
  A^*(M_{7,n})`. The proof stratifies by gonality: the hyperelliptic and trigonal loci come from Canning–Larson;
  the tetragonal locus is handled with Hurwitz stacks of degree 4 covers and their Casnati–Ekedahl bundles,
  stratified by splitting type; and the pentagonal locus needs a genuinely new construction — a marked version of
  Mukai's model realising a pentagonal genus 7 curve as a linear section `OG(5,10) ∩ P^6` of the orthogonal
  Grassmannian in its spinor embedding, which here becomes an open embedding of `M^◦_{7,n}` into a Grassmann bundle
  over `OG(5, V)^n` (Lemma 5.13), with all the relevant bundles identified tautologically (Lemmas 5.14–5.16).
* **The graphical calculus (Section 7).** `H^{15}(M̄_{2,12})` is shown to be spanned by boundary pushforwards by
  exhibiting 891 `ω`-decorated graphs with 55 relations among them, matching Bergström–Faber's count of
  `836 L^2 S_12`; Lemmas 7.4–7.7 give explicit bases of `H^{13}(M̄_{1,12})`, `H^{13}(M̄_{1,13})`,
  `H^{13}(M̄_{1,14})` and `H^{13}(M̄_{2,12})` by such graphs (11, 429, 6006 and 264 of them).
* **From Hodge structures to Galois representations (Section 8).** For `g ≥ 3` the pullback to the normalised
  boundary is injective (Lemma 8.2, which is where the genus 7 base cases are used), and an induction on stable
  graphs shows the `(15,0)` part of the next map is injective, proving Theorem 1.1 for Hodge structures. Lemma 8.3
  splits `H^{15}(M̄_{1,15}) = V ⊕ W` with `W ≅ S_16` and `V^{ss} ≅ 186263·L^2S_12`, using that `V` has Hodge type
  `(13,2) + (2,13)` while `W` has type `(15,0) + (0,15)`; since the relevant maps are the same maps of
  `Q`-vector spaces, the Hodge-theoretic vanishing forces the Galois-theoretic one.

no hit for "moduli of curves", "M_{g,n}", "tautological ring", "tautological class", "Chow ring", "cycle class

## The routes

### 1. New roadmap `MotivicStructuresInModuliOfCurves` — 84 items

"Tautological rings and motivic structures in the cohomology of moduli of stable curves", area `algebraicgeometry`.
It plans the moduli stacks and their boundary stratification, the tautological morphisms and rings with the ψ-, κ-
and Hodge classes, the two boundary exact sequences with the subspaces Φ and Ψ, semi-tautological extensions with
the filling criterion and finite generation, the decomposition of pure cohomology in terms of symplectic local
systems with the genus 1 and 2 classifications, the Chow–Künneth generation Property with its permanence properties
and Lemma 4.3, the genus 7 geometry (gonality strata, Casnati–Ekedahl, the marked Mukai model), and the graphical
calculus leading to the main theorems.

This is the `new` case of the maintainer's worked examples, not a Part II: there is no roadmap in the direction of
the cohomology of `M̄_{g,n}` to extend. The brief names every import — StableReduction Layers 1, 3 and 9; R09.1,
R09.4 and R09.5; SF.2 and SF.5; MC.0 and MC.2; HodgeStructures L0–L2; DWP.9; and R19.1 — and flags two inputs the
paper quotes that are large enough to deserve their own layers rather than a citation: the virtual cohomological
dimension of `M_{g,n}` with Harer–Wahl stability, and the Bergström–Faber–Payne vanishing of low-degree
Borel–Moore homology.

### 2. Source for `SchemeAndStackFoundations` SF.5 — 2 items

SF.5 owns Chow groups, Chern classes and intersection products. Two items are general facts of that layer rather
than facts about moduli of curves: the extension of Chow groups to algebraic stacks stratified by quotient stacks
(Edidin–Graham, Kresch) with excision and the exterior product `A_*(X) ⊗ A_*(Y) → A_*(X × Y)` — without which the
CKgP cannot even be stated — and the computation of the rational Chow ring of an orthogonal Grassmann bundle from
Schubert cells and the Kresch–Tamvakis Giambelli formula (Lemma 5.10), which needs rational coefficients because
the formula divides by 2.

### 3. Source for `MotivesAndAlgebraicCycles` MC.2 — 1 item

MC.2 constructs realisations and proves that cycle classes respect composition, cup products, pushforward, trace
and Tate twists. The cycle class map the paper needs is the one for a smooth but not necessarily proper variety or
Deligne–Mumford stack, landing in the lowest-weight part `W_{2i}H^{2i}`, with the consequence that algebraic
classes are of type `(p,p)` and Tate on the ℓ-adic side. That belongs to MC.2 in exactly that generality.

## Prerequisites the atlas does not cover

Seventeen entries are listed. The ones that matter most: **Chenevier–Lannes**, *Automorphic forms and even
unimodular lattices*, whose Theorem F is the prediction being confirmed; the authors' own earlier paper on
`H^{11}(M̄_{g,n})` and the weight 11 complex (arXiv:2209.03113), and **Canning–Larson** on the Chow rings of
`M_{7,8,9}` (arXiv:2104.05820) and on the CKgP for low-genus `M_{g,n}` (arXiv:2110.01059), which supply the
technique and most of the table of known cases; **Bergström–Faber–Payne** (arXiv:2206.07759) for the Borel–Moore
vanishing; **Getzler** for the pure cohomology of `M_{1,n}` and the multiplicity 186263; **Petersen** and
**Petersen–Tommasi(–Yin)** for genus 2 and for the Leray/Künneth analysis behind Lemma 3.1; **Mukai** for the
genus 7 model and **Casnati–Ekedahl** for degree 4 covers; **Arbarello–Cornalba** for degrees `≤ 3`; **Wahl** and
Harer for stability; **Edidin–Graham** and **Kresch** for Chow groups of stacks, **Totaro** for the weak
Chow–Künneth property, and **Kresch–Tamvakis** for the Giambelli formula; and **Bergström–Faber**'s point counts,
which give the dimension `836 L^2S_12` and which Theorem 1.9 makes unconditional for `M̄_{3,n}`, `n = 9, 10, 11`.

## Mistakes in the source

One misprint, recorded under `sourceIssues`. An audit of every hand-typed environment name against its `\ref`
target found no mismatched cross-references in this paper.

* **E1** (misprint, first sentence of the proof of Theorem 1.5(3) in Section 6.2): "We now show that
  `H_k(M̄_{g,n})` is tautological for even `k ≥ 14`" — the theorem states even `k ≤ 14`, and the proof itself uses
  `k ≤ 14` two sentences later when invoking the table of known CKgP cases. The proof is correct with `≤`. The
  published version at `doi:10.1017/fmp.2024.24` has the same sentence: checking the Cambridge Core text on
  22 September 2026 shows `k \geq 14` in the proof and `k \leq 14` in the statement, so the slip was not caught in
  production. It affects nothing.

**The new roadmap is justified on the checks, not merely permitted.** `MotivicStructuresInModuliOfCurves`

**Locators were checked mechanically.** The review rebuilt the paper's shared theorem counter from
its twelve `\newtheorem` declarations, all `[thm]`-counted by section, and found that **84 of the 85
statement references resolve to a statement of exactly the cited kind and number**; the one that does
not, item 49's `Lemma 10.5`, is labelled as Canning–Larson's own numbering in its locator.

**The single finding is confirmed verbatim**: the proof of Theorem 1.5(3) opens "for even `k ≥ 14`"
while the theorem states "for even `k ≤ 14`" and the proof's own next-but-one sentence uses

The review reports one provenance gap it did not fill: no `libraryPins` are recorded. It notes that
`PAPER-BHATT-MATHEW-23`, `PAPER-WOOD-19` and `PAPER-HACON-WITASZEK-23` have the same gap, so the
omission looks systematic and may deserve a queue-level fix.
