**Effect.** The Ax–Schanuel theorems are not claimed to fail. E5 shows the characterization Theorem 11.3 needs an extra first-order condition, which its intended use supplies. E1 and E4 are gaps in auxiliary steps. E6 is a gap in an alternative proof. E2 and E3 are misprints.

## E1. Corollary 3.2 is stated for closed sets but used for constructible ones (gap)

Corollary 3.2 assumes A "closed, constructible", which makes it Theorem 3.1 again. §11 applies it to images of G(C)-orbits in jet spaces:
- V′_{n,ℓ}, to get V_{n,ℓ};
- W′_{n,ℓ}, to get the foliation.

Neither set is shown to be closed. The needed version, for definable constructible sets, requires showing that their images under the uniformization are constructible analytic, which the one-line proof does not do.

## E2. B = K_C N⁻, not K_C N⁺ (misprint)

Lemma 7.1 says the stabilizer of id₁(o) in B is N⁻, so N⁻ is the unipotent radical of B. The proof's "Since B = K_C N⁺" should read B = K_C N⁻; N⁺ is the opposite group used in the rest of the proof.

## E3. The field in Corollary 9.3 (misprint)

The proof concludes once "the transcendence degree of C(z₁, …, z_n) is equal to dim G", but that field has transcendence degree n. The field meant is the one generated by the modular functions φ_j and their derivatives, and that is what the proof computes. "A C-basis of modular functions" should read "generators of the field of modular functions".

## E4. Stabilizers can shrink without losing dimension (gap)

The proof of Lemma 11.1 partitions jets by the first order at which the stabilizer stabilizes. It argues that each strict drop lowers the dimension, so that m = dim G works. But a proper closed subgroup of the same dimension, with fewer components, is a strict drop with no change of dimension. A uniform bound on such drops is needed for the lemma's m.

## E5. Theorem 11.3 needs a first-order condition (error)

The theorem characterizes the maps L(w, g, r) = (w, q∘g∘w, J_r(q∘g)∘id_r∘w) by conditions ending "and if v restricts to u". The proof uses this only to mean that the value of v equals u.

**The counterexample.** Under that reading the theorem is false. On a modular curve, near a point where q′ ≠ 0, take

  w(t) = 2t, u(t) = q(t), v(t) = J_r q(id_r(t)).

- **The hypotheses hold.** The jets of u lie in V, since u = q∘(z/2)∘w. v lies in V and moves along the identity leaf of W, and v has value q(t) = u(t).
- **The conclusion fails.** Theorem 11.2 forces g(z) = γ(z/2), and then J_r(q∘g) at 2t has first derivative q′(t)/2 ≠ q′(t).

In the proof, the leaf decomposition of v gives n(t) = translation by −t, not the identity.

**Correction.** Every L(w, g, r) satisfies du = v₁ dw. The theorem needs this condition, or the full compatibility of the jet v with the jets of u along w, and the last step of the proof must use it.

## E6. The inversion step in §12.4 (gap)

To show two fields coincide, the alternative proof of Theorem 9.1 in §12.4 asks for a map φ with (z, r, s, …)∘φ = id. It also asserts rank(z) = dim U.

Along a locus of non-degenerate jets, however, the base point z(t) may be constant while the formal coordinates r(t) vary. The equality instead follows by inverting the matrix r(t): ∂q/∂z = R r^{−1}, and so on. The final count only needs dim(z). Theorem 9.1 has its own proof in §10, so no result depends on §12.4.
