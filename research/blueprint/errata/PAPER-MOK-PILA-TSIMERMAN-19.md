# Mistakes in Mok–Pila–Tsimerman, *Ax-Schanuel for Shimura varieties*

Original errata worker: Claude Code, session `cc-fb70e5`, 22 September 2026. Independent review: Codex, session `codex-a71f92`, 23 September 2026, job REV-ERRATA-PAPER-MOK-PILA-TSIMERMAN-19.

The six inherited findings are confirmed **after the scope amendments below**. Three additional notation slips, E7–E9, are added. In particular, E1 is downgraded from a substantive gap to a wording slip; E6 affects the alternative proof even though the theorem has another proof.

## E1 — An unnecessary closedness hypothesis (misprint, amended)

Corollary 3.2, published p.951 / v3 p.6, retains “closed” although §11 uses the constructible version. Delete that word.

The earlier claim of a substantive missing descent theorem is too strong. By §2.1, q is an unramified covering. For a saturated constructible analytic set A, closure and boundary descend through that covering. The image is definable on a fundamental domain. Its closure is therefore closed analytic and definable, hence algebraic by Definable Chow; induction on the smaller-dimensional boundary shows that the image itself is constructible algebraic. The same argument applies to the jet and tangent coverings in §11. The review writes this out. No properness assertion for an arbitrary analytic image is needed.

## E2 — The wrong unipotent factor (misprint)

Lemma 7.1 proof, published p.959 / v3 p.12: write B=K_C N−, not K_C N+. The setup explicitly gives Lie(B)=n−⊕k_C and uses N+ for the opposite open cell.

## E3 — The wrong transcendence-degree field (misprint)

Corollary 9.3 proof, published pp.965–966: replace C(z₁,…,z_n) by the field generated by the modular functions and their partial derivatives up to order k. It is the latter field whose transcendence degree is dim G; the coordinate field has transcendence degree n.

The versions are not identical here. V3 p.18 instead writes C(φ₁,…,φ_N), still omitting the derivatives. Also interpret the finite “C-basis” as generators of the modular function field, consistently with the repeated statements.

## E4 — Strict jet-stabilizer drops need not lower dimension (gap)

Lemma 11.1 proof, published p.969 / v3 p.22, counts every strict stabilizer inclusion as a dimension decrease. This is false even for the compact dual of the Siegel domain.

In the symmetric-matrix chart of the Lagrangian Grassmannian for PSp₄(C), take the germ

X(t)=tI+t³diag(1,0)+t⁴[[0,1],[1,0]].

Its third-jet stabilizer has order two and its fourth-jet stabilizer is trivial; both have dimension zero. The review derives this from the fractional-linear symplectic action, with exact finite diagnostics as a check.

This refutes the printed dimension assertion, not the existential lemma or the possible value m=dim G. A valid finite-order determination and uniqueness argument is still needed; no complete replacement proof is supplied here.

## E5 — Zeroth-order agreement does not characterize a uniformized jet (error)

Theorem 11.3, published p.971 / v3 pp.23–24, is false if “v restricts to u” means equality of zeroth-order values, as used in its proof.

On a torsion-free modular curve take a small disk in the upper half-plane and set w(t)=2t, u(t)=q(t), v(t)=J_rq(id_r(t)), with r≥3. The pair (w,u) comes from z↦z/2, v follows the identity leaf, and v has zeroth-order value u. But any constant g giving u=q∘g∘w has g(2t)=γt locally for fixed γ∈Γ. Consequently J_r(q∘g) at 2t has first derivative q′(t)/2, not the q′(t) recorded by v. This uses only local covering uniqueness, not the disputed Lemma 11.1.

Add the first-order compatibility du=v₁dw, or full chain-rule compatibility. Locally on a fixed leaf, write v=J_r(q∘g₀)(id_r(s)); then du=v₁ds. Since v₁ is invertible onto the tangent space, the added condition forces s=w+c, and the constant translation can be absorbed into g₀. This supplies the local missing step; it is not a complete re-proof of the global characterization or of the differential Ax–Schanuel theorem.

## E6 — A formal jet is not the derivative of its moving base point (gap)

In §12.4, published pp.974–975 / v3 pp.26–27, the invertible formal matrix r does not imply that the base map z(t) has full rank or an inverse of the asserted kind. For independent parameters (a,b), b≠0, a modular-curve jet can have z=a and r=b, with higher input coordinates zero. Its locus has dimension two, while z has rank one and open image. This example does not depend on a constant projection into a weakly special point.

Recover the uniformizer derivatives by the triangular chain rule instead: Dq(z)=Rr⁻¹; at higher orders, subtract lower-order terms and invert the action of r on the relevant symmetric tensor power. This proves the field equality. Apply the differential theorem using rank(z), not dim U; the final generic-fiber dimension inequality supplies the remaining parameters. The theorem's direct §10 proof is not refuted, but the invalid steps do affect this alternative proof.

## E7 — The direction of functional dependence (new misprint)

In §12.2, published p.972 / v3 p.24, the rank test for “p₂ is a function of p₁” should be rank(p₁,p₂)=rank(p₁), not rank(p₂).

For p₁=(t,s), p₂=t, the projection is a function but the printed test fails. Reversing those tuples makes the printed test pass for something that is not a function of t. The intended notion is local analytic dependence at regular points, not necessarily rational dependence. Subsequent uses assume equal ranks, so their hypotheses are unchanged.

## E8 — Missing second-derivative superscripts (new misprint)

Both second-order chain-rule displays on published p.974 / v3 p.26 print ∂q_i/(∂z_p∂z_m). The numerators should be ∂²q_i. The page image confirms the missing superscripts. This notation slip is separate from the inverse-map gap E6.

## E9 — The weakly special exception belongs to the product (new misprint)

Theorem 12.2, published p.975 / the theorem numbered 12.5 in v3 p.27, has x∈X^ℓ but ends with an exception inside X. It should be inside X^ℓ. Applying the preceding theorem to G^ℓ and q^ℓ gives exactly the printed ℓ dim G term and the proper weakly special exception in the product, including relations such as the diagonal.
