## E1. Theorem 2.1 omits Euler factors (error; affects a stated result)

**What the paper says.** For an imaginary quadratic field K, a prime p and any nonzero ideal f, Theorem 2.1 asserts ξ(h²_Q) = ξ((h¹/z)_Q) as ideals of Z_p[[G_{p∞f}]] ⊗ Q. Here:
- h¹ is the inverse limit of the p-adically completed p-units;
- h² is the inverse limit of the p-parts of the class groups, over the tower K(p^∞f);
- z is Kato's module of elliptic units (§15.5 of Astérisque 295).

Footnote 3 adds that this formulation is "identical" with that of Johnson-Leung–Kings §7.2, from which the theorem is taken.

**Why it is wrong.** Kato's z is generated by the elliptic units of the single modulus p^n f. By the distribution relations (Johnson-Leung–Kings, Lemma 7.7), its projection to a character χ carries the Euler factors ∏(1 − χ(𝔩)Frob_𝔩^{−1}) over the primes 𝔩 | f with 𝔩 ∤ p. For that reason Johnson-Leung–Kings pair it with the Iwasawa cohomology group H² over O_K[1/pf]. BT's h² is the class-group limit, which Kato's (15.6.2) identifies at height one with H² over O_K[1/p]. By purity the two differ:

0 → H²(O[1/p]) → H²(O[1/pf]) → ⊕_{𝔩|f, 𝔩∤p} Z_p[[G/D_𝔩]] → 0,

with D_𝔩 the decomposition group of 𝔩. Frobenius at 𝔩 has infinite order in the Z_p²-quotient, so each D_𝔩 has Z_p-rank one. Each term is therefore torsion but not pseudo-null, and it contributes a nontrivial characteristic ideal. On the trivial-character component that ideal is generated by 1 − Frob_𝔩^{−1}, a non-unit even after inverting p. So the printed equality fails whenever f has a prime factor not above p.

This is the same phenomenon as for cyclotomic units of a fixed composite modulus, which differ from the primitive ones by exactly these Euler factors. Kato's statement of Rubin's theorem (Astérisque 295, Theorem 15.2(2)) has the same imprecision: Rubin's elliptic units include those of every modulus dividing f. Kato himself uses only the inequality, which stays true because the Euler factors only enlarge h¹/z.

**Correction.** ξ((h¹/z)_Q) = ξ(h²_Q) · ∏_{𝔩|f, 𝔩∤p} ξ(Z_p[[G/D_𝔩]]_Q). Equivalently, ξ((h¹/z)_Q) = ξ(H²_Q) with H² the Iwasawa cohomology over O_K[1/pf], which is what Johnson-Leung–Kings prove, for every p. The printed form holds when every prime factor of f lies above p. It also holds after localizing at height-one primes on components ramified at every such 𝔩, since there the extra terms vanish.

**Effect.** None on the paper's later results. In the proof of Theorem 2.6 (via Kato 15.13–15.17), f is the conductor of the Hecke character ψ, and the primes used lie on ψ-components ramified at every 𝔩 | f, where the Euler factors are units.

## E2. Kato §15 does not prove the equality attributed to it (gap; affects the proof)

**What the paper says.** The proof of Theorem 2.6 says: "In [12, §15] it is shown that the CM case of Kato's main conjecture [12, Conj. 12.10] in Λ⊗Q is a consequence of the equivariant main conjecture … up to tensoring with Q", with Rubin's input replaced by Theorem 2.1.

**Why it is incomplete.** Kato lists what §15 proves (p. 250): Theorems 12.4–12.6, 14.2 and 14.5 in the CM case, not Conjecture 12.10. His Proposition 15.17 is an inequality, length(H²)_𝔭 ≤ length(H¹/Z)_𝔭, deduced from Rubin's inequality. On p. 251 he says that Rubin has an equality in some cases "but we do not use it in this paper". No equality version appears in §15.

**Correction.** The equality does follow from Kato's tools with one more step.
1. Localize at the height-two prime q of the two-variable Iwasawa algebra over a one-variable prime r ∌ p. Let a generate the kernel to the one-variable ring (Kato's Lemma 15.13).
2. Apply Kato's Lemma 14.15 to both H²_q and H¹_q/A·z, not only to the first.
3. The Ker(a | H²) terms in (15.13.1) and (15.13.2) then cancel.
4. The remaining term Ker(a | H¹_q/A·z) vanishes. A is a regular local ring and hence a UFD, a is prime, H¹_q is torsion-free of rank one, and z generates it at aA (Kato 15.15).

The extraction now carries this step as its item `equality-descent`. The integrality of the elliptic-unit class at q, implicit in Kato's own proof, should be checked when it is formalized.

**Effect.** The proof of Theorem 2.6 is incomplete as written but repairable. Theorem 2.6 stands.

## E3. The Selmer display in the proof of Theorem 1.1 omits the Tate twist (misprint; affects nothing)

**What the paper says.** "Sel_{p∞}(E/K)^∨ ⊗ K ≃ H¹_f(Q, V_{Kλ}(f)) ⊕ H¹_f(Q, V_{Kλ}(f̄))."

**Why it is wrong.** BT take V_{F_λ}(f) from Kato (8.3), which is the cohomological realization. In weight two, V(f) ≅ V_pE^* ≅ V_pE(−1) (Kato (8.4.1), (14.10.2), (14.10.3); Kato uses T_pE(−1) on p. 274). The Selmer group of E corresponds to V(f)(1), not V(f). Theorem 3.1, which the proof then applies with k = 2, concerns V(f)(k/2) = V(f)(1). In addition, "⊗K" should be ⊗_{Z_p}K_λ, and the identification is up to duality.

**Correction.** The right-hand side is H¹_f(Q, V(f)(1)) ⊕ H¹_f(Q, V(f̄)(1)), up to duality.

**Effect.** None. Only the vanishing of both sides is used.

## E4. Theorems 2.3, 2.4 and 2.6 omit k ≥ 2 (misprint; affects nothing)

These theorems are stated for any newform f ∈ S_k(Γ₁(N)). The representation V_{F_λ}(f) is taken from Kato, whose construction uses cohomology with Sym^{k−2} coefficients and so requires k ≥ 2 (Kato's Chapter III fixes this). Weight-one CM forms are therefore not covered, and the hypothesis k ≥ 2 should be stated. Theorem 3.1 concerns even weight, so it is unaffected.

## E5. Equation (3.1) needs Kato's exact sequence twice (gap; affects nothing)

The paper derives H²(Z[1/p], T(k/2)) ⊗ Q = 0 from the vanishing of H¹_f(Q, V(f)(k/2)) "by the exact sequence [12, (14.9.3)]". A single application of (14.9.3) to T(k/2) involves the Selmer group of the Tate dual T(k/2)^*(1). By Kato's (14.10.1) that dual is a lattice in V(f*)(k/2), not in the representation of the hypothesis. The argument needs two steps:
1. Apply (14.9.3) first to the dual lattice. Its Selmer term is controlled by the hypothesis, so with (14.9.5) and Kato's Proposition 14.12 one gets H¹_f(Q, V(f*)(k/2)) = 0.
2. Apply (14.9.3) to T(k/2).

Kato uses the same device on p. 242. The conclusion is right.

## E6. The choice of γ uses a sign relation that is not mentioned (gap; affects nothing)

The proof of Theorem 3.1 picks γ with nonzero (−1)^{k/2−1}-component. It then concludes from Theorem 2.6 that the specialized class z of z_γ is a basis. Theorem 2.6 only gives that the module Z(f) generated by all z_γ fills H¹ at the prime q. Passing to a single z_γ uses Kato's relation z_{ι(γ)} = −σ_{−1} z_γ (Theorem 12.5(1), derived on p. 230). At q it makes the specialization of z_γ depend only on the (−1)^{k/2−1}-component of γ and kills the other component. The paper uses the relation without stating it. The conclusion is right.

## E7. Theorem 3.5 is not the statement of BKLOS Theorem 2.7 (gap; affects nothing)

BT state as "[1, Thm. 2.7]" that at least half of the quadratic twists have Z_3-corank of Sel_{3∞} equal to 0. The statement of Bhargava–Klagsbrun–Lemke Oliver–Shnidman Theorem 2.7 concerns Mordell–Weil rank. Mordell–Weil rank 0 does not give Selmer corank 0 without controlling Sha. The Selmer statement comes out of the proof (§9.2 and §11): for a maximal CM order, dim Sel₃ = 0 for at least half of the twists, which forces Sel_{3∞} = 0. Non-maximal orders follow because the corank is invariant under isogeny. So Theorem 3.5 is true, but it should cite the proof, not the theorem. The extraction had noted this.

## E8. Theorem 3.3 is a consequence of Smith's Theorem 1.2, not its statement (gap; affects nothing)

Smith's Theorem 1.2 (Part I, proved in Part II) orders all nonzero integers d with |d| ≤ H, of both signs. It gives the limiting proportions of 2∞-Selmer corank 0, 1 and at least 2 in the twist family. BT's Theorem 3.3 is a relative density-one statement for positive squarefree n ≡ 1, 2, 3 mod 8. Deducing it requires three steps:
1. pass to positive squarefree n;
2. restrict to those residue classes;
3. use the 2-parity theorem to exclude corank one there.

The paper mentions none of these. The curve y² = x³ − x satisfies Smith's hypothesis: it has full rational 2-torsion and no rational cyclic 4-isogeny. The extraction had noted this.

## E9. Footnote 2 holds only for positive n (misprint; affects nothing)

"ϵ(E^{(n)}) = +1 precisely when n ≡ 1, 2, 3 mod 8", where §1.0.2 defines E^{(n)} for every nonzero integer n. For n = −1 ≡ 7 mod 8, the twist −y² = x³ − x is isomorphic to E over Q (put x ↦ −x), so its root number is +1. The classification is right for positive squarefree n, which is the setting of Theorem 1.2. PARI confirms it for all squarefree 0 < n < 400.

## E13. Footnote 4 and the case K ⊂ Q(ζ_{p^∞}) (gap; affects nothing)

**What the paper says.** Footnote 4 says that, for (Λ⊗Q)-modules, the hypotheses of Kato's Lemma 15.13 hold.

**Why it is incomplete.** Working with Λ⊗Q secures only the lemma's condition that 2 and the order of Δ be invertible in the residue field. The lemma also assumes that K is not contained in Q(ζ_{p^∞}). That fails for:
- K = Q(i) or Q(√−2) with p = 2;
- K = Q(√−p) with p ≡ 3 mod 4.

These are exactly the cases the paper needs. Theorem 1.2 uses p = 2 for the congruent number curve, with K = Q(i). Remark 3.6(i) uses p = 3 with K = Q(ζ_3).

**Why nothing is lost.** Kato's 15.14 gives the modified lemma, with G_∞ replaced by Gal(Q(ζ_{p^∞})/K), and his proofs of 15.15 and 15.17 go through with it. The equality step of E2 uses only the lemma's conclusions. The proof body cites all of Kato §15, so Theorem 2.6 stands, and only the footnote is wrong.

## E14. "(1.1) holds" in Theorem 1.1 (misprint; affects nothing)

**What the paper says.** Theorem 1.1 ends: "In particular, if E descends to Q, then (1.1) holds."

**Why it is wrong.** (1.1) is displayed for every r, as "the rank r p-converse". The theorem proves only r = 0, as the title says.

**Correction.** Read "(1.1) holds for r = 0".

## E15. The target of ψ (misprint; affects nothing)

**What the paper says.** The proof of Theorem 1.1 takes "ψ : A_K^×/K^× → K^×".

**Why it is wrong.** A character of the idele class group with infinity type (−1, 0) is z ↦ z^{−1} on C^×, so it does not take values in K^×.

**Correction.** Read ψ : A_K^×/K^× → C^×. Its values on ideals prime to the conductor lie in K, as in Kato 15.7.
