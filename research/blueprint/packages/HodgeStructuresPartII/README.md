# Roadmap: Hodge structures, Part II: variations, period maps and non-abelian Hodge theory

This roadmap extends the linear algebra of pure, mixed and polarized Hodge structures to families. It builds the operator library behind Higgs bundles and parameter connections, the complex non-abelian Hodge correspondence, variations and their logarithmic extensions, general period manifolds, rigidity and integrality, degeneration, definable period maps and real Noether–Lefschetz interfaces. The common thread is compatibility: a connection, its graded Higgs field, its monodromy, its period map and its arithmetic realizations must refer to the same coefficient object and the same conventions.

The development has nine layers. H.0 is an algebraic library usable in any characteristic. H.1 is projective complex non-abelian Hodge theory. H.2 supplies variation and monodromy interfaces, and H.3 supplies represented period geometry and derivatives. H.4 proves the parabolic estimates used in deformation arguments; H.5 treats rigid objects and their arithmetic consequences. H.6 supplies degeneration estimates, which H.7 uses to prove definability and algebraicity of individual Hodge loci. H.8 exports real variation interfaces to applications on surfaces.

Suggested homes are `TauCeti/Geometry/Hodge/ParameterConnection/`, `TauCeti/Geometry/Hodge/Variation/`, `TauCeti/Geometry/Hodge/PeriodGeometry/`, `TauCeti/Geometry/Hodge/Degeneration/` and `TauCeti/Geometry/NonabelianHodge/`. [Suggested.lean](Suggested.lean) gives suggested signatures. The mathematical contracts here determine the generality of the development.

## Scope and ownership

The starting point is [Hodge structures](../../../../content/tau-ceti/HodgeStructures/README.md), `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/HodgeStructures`. Consume its pure structures, conjugations, polarizations, rational substructures, mixed filtrations and period-domain points. The new work starts at operators on sheaves and geometric families. Fibrewise opposedness, the rational Deligne bigrading, strictness and the integral symmetry group are imported. Coefficient extensions and geometric realizations need their stated comparison maps; a rational theorem is not silently a theorem about an arbitrary complex mixed structure.

Every reference to a layer below means its stated mathematical interface, including the coefficient, descent and comparison hypotheses. In particular, the general operator definitions require genuine sheaf tensor products and exterior differentials. An affine computation alone does not provide that interface.

## Conventions

All rings in the algebraic operator layer are commutative. On a ringed site write O for the ring sheaf, Ω¹ for relative differentials and Ωⁿ for their exterior powers. Tensor products are sheaf tensor products when the coefficients are sheaves. Locally free rank is locally constant; a frame is local data, not a field of a geometric object. Formulae on elementary sections are checked after restriction to trivializing opens and then descended.

A parameter connection D has Leibniz rule D(ae)=aD(e)+λe⊗da. Its global parameter is relatively constant, dλ=0. Its exterior extension on E⊗Ωⁿ is D(e⊗ω)=D(e)∧ω+λe⊗dω. The affine ambient theory also admits varying λ, but then curvature has the displayed scalar defect and need not be O-linear. Zero parameter gives a Higgs field and unit parameter gives an ordinary connection. Trace zero, nilpotence and a lattice are extra conditions.

For a twisted Higgs field retain the invertible coefficient line T: its one-form coefficients are Q=Ω¹⊗T. Ordered iteration uses Q tensor powers, never exterior powers. The zeroth iterate is the tensor-unit identification; every successor puts the new coefficient in slot zero. The associated contracted product acts with its rightmost factor first. Integrability and ordered nilpotence are different predicates.

Filtrations F are decreasing and integer indexed; weight filtrations W are increasing. A finite Griffiths filtration consists of locally split subbundles with finite locally free graded pieces, Fᵖ=E for sufficiently negative p and Fᵖ=0 for sufficiently positive p. Its Rees module is generated by t⁻ᵖFᵖ, with parameter operator t∇. Tate twisting by m changes weight n to n−2m and filtration Fᵖ to Fᵖ⁺ᵐ. A Tate frame does not remove the character of the twisting line.

The projective complex moduli layer fixes X, a base point x, an ample polarization and positive rank r. Its comparison component has all rational Chern classes zero and a prescribed finite-monodromy determinant. Fixing the determinant connection is stronger than fixing the underlying line. Determinant rigidification changes automorphism groups: unrigidified stable objects have scalar inertia C×, while a preserved determinant identification leaves μᵣ. Stable fixed-rank, fixed-determinant objects are not a tensor subcategory.

A complex polarized variation may have no real structure or integral lattice. Rational, real and integral coefficient data are added explicitly. Canonical extensions choose a residue strip; tensor products must respect that normalization. For d+A dq/q and a positive loop, monodromy is exp(−2πiA). Write L=log T, qᵢ=exp(2πizᵢ) and Φ(z)=exp(ΣzᵢLᵢ)Ψ(q); the normalized residue is −Lᵢ/(2πi). A centered multigrading σ gives exponent σ for squared norms and σ/2 for norms.

The full period domain and a Mumford–Tate orbit domain are distinct carriers. Arithmetic estimates fix the canonical maximal compact K attached to the marked Hodge point. A splitting torus from Schmid's theorem needs a comparison with the Cartan-stable reduction convention. Algebraicity is proved for each special pullback. The exceptional Hodge locus is a countable union of algebraic subvarieties; that union need not itself be definable.

In the real weight-two layer separate geometric complex-linear action σ_C, coefficient conjugation κ and their conjugate-linear product. Geometric action exchanges Hodge indices, while σ_Cκ preserves them. Z(1) has Galois sign −1, so its fixed part identifies with the untwisted anti-invariant part. The derivative of a moving filtration is the connection symbol; the obstruction derivative of a fixed flat class has the opposite sign. Variation lattices use integral cohomology modulo torsion; ordinary integral topological statements retain torsion.

## Layer 0: Higgs fields and parameter connections

The algebraic layer develops full operator interfaces before invoking moduli or an analytic correspondence. There are three useful levels: arbitrary affine modules with supplied differential calculus; finite free charts with explicit matrices; and finite locally free sheaves with intrinsic forms. The comparisons between these levels must be constructed. A coordinate formula is not a definition of universal differentials, and a module tensor product is not a definition of a sheaf tensor product.

The common source convention for ordinary extension and curvature is the Stacks Project, §60.15, tag [07J5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07J5), including Lemma 60.15.1. The parameter convention comes from Esnault–Groechenig [EG20], §4.2, pp.131–132; its 44-page author manuscript [EGa] uses §4.2, pp.23–24. The arbitrary-ring formulae and categorical coherence below are algebraic deductions from those conventions and Mathlib's universal properties. They are not generality attributed to a printed correspondence theorem.

### Intrinsic operators, their curvature and specializations

Take a commutative ringed Grothendieck site with a relative exterior differential calculus: Ω⁰=O, Ωⁿ=∧ⁿΩ¹, restriction-compatible alternating wedge, d²=0 and graded Leibniz. Let λ be a global central section with dλ=0. For finite locally free E, define `Preconnection` by the additive sheaf map and parameter Leibniz rule above. Its API must provide construction from that rule, the additive operator, evaluation, extensionality, restriction and compatibility with maps of the differential site. Prove D(be)=bD(e) when db=0. Integrability is a separate condition.

Construct its additive exterior extension Dₙ by a balanced local formula. Moving a scalar between e and ω produces cancelling differential terms, and the extension agrees on overlaps. Prove uniqueness from local elementary tensors and D₀=D through E⊗O≅E. For u of degree n, the right graded Leibniz rule is D(u∧ω)=D(u)∧ω+(−1)ⁿλu∧dω. Construct κ_D=D₁∘D:E→E⊗Ω² and prove O-linearity using dλ=0. The full square formula is Dₙ₊₁Dₙ(e⊗ω)=κ_D(e)∧ω; thus curvature zero is equivalent to square zero in every degree. Define `LambdaBundle` as E together with D and the equation κ_D=0, with constructor, operator projection, restriction and extensionality for fixed E. The operations and their laws are theorems about this object.

A horizontal map u:E→F is O-linear with D_Fu=(u⊗1)D_E. Give identity, composition, addition, zero, extensionality and the category of integrable parameter bundles. The unit is O with λd. Construct same-parameter tensors by D(e⊗f)=D_E(e)⊗f+e⊗D_F(f), using the actual tensor reassociations. The balanced pair has scalar correction λ(e⊗f)⊗da, and the native associator, symmetry and unitors are horizontal. Prove κ_tensor=κ_E⊗1+1⊗κ_F. Finite locally free duality is characterized by

λd(φ(e))=⟨D∨φ,e⟩+⟨φ,De⟩.

Prove uniqueness, horizontal evaluation and canonical biduality, and the sign κ_D∨=−transpose(κ_D). Finite locally free biduality reflects as well as preserves flatness. Pullback along a ringed differential morphism uses the supplied one-form comparison and its differential/wedge compatibility, with D_Y(b⊗e)=λ_Y e⊗d_Yb+b·df(D_Xe). Prove horizontal functoriality, identity, composition, tensor and dual comparisons, preservation of curvature and unique descent along horizontal transition maps satisfying their cocycle. Pullback needs no flatness premise for finite locally free E. Coordinate comparison identifies the intrinsic operator with λd+A only when the chart supplies a genuine differential basis and its commuting dual derivations. If λ is a unit, dividing D by λ gives the ordinary connection with curvature λ⁻²κ_D and inverse functor ∇↦λ∇. At λ=0 identify the object with an integrable Higgs field; at λ=1 identify it with CR.1's ordinary carrier.

### Matrix charts, gauge and determinant

For a k-algebra R, take commuting k-derivations δᵢ indexed by Fin d and λ∈R with δᵢλ=0. `Frame` records those equations. Construct its zero frame, its polynomial partial-derivative frame and its coefficient-one specialization; extensionality depends only on the derivations. For a finite decidable index V, a coordinate preconnection is the matrix family Aᵢ∈Mat_V(R), acting by Dᵢs=λδᵢs+Aᵢs. Provide zero matrices, projection, operator additivity and its scalar rule. Its curvature is

Cᵢⱼ=λ(δᵢAⱼ−δⱼAᵢ)+AᵢAⱼ−AⱼAᵢ.

Prove [Dᵢ,Dⱼ]s=Cᵢⱼs, Cᵢᵢ=0 and Cⱼᵢ=−Cᵢⱼ. Flatness is Cᵢⱼ=0 for every pair; it is equivalent to commutation of the operators, and every one-direction model is flat. Zero parameter curvature is exactly the matrix commutator. `JointNilpotent` requires a positive N such that every word of length N in the coefficient matrices vanishes; provide monotonicity, the zero-field bound 1, the bound-one criterion that all matrices vanish, and preservation of the exact bound under duality.

Fix the component convention s′=Gs for a matrix unit G. Then Aᵢ′=GAᵢG⁻¹−λδᵢ(G)G⁻¹ and D′(Gs)=GD(s). Prove composition, identity and curvature conjugation C′ᵢⱼ=GCᵢⱼG⁻¹; gauge therefore preserves and reflects flatness. Gauge of the zero model has coefficient −λδᵢ(G)G⁻¹. The coordinate tensor has Aᵢ⊗I+I⊗Bᵢ; the dual has −Aᵢᵗ and curvature −Cᵢⱼᵗ. Give elementary-tensor evaluation, curvature, dual involution and zero/one/rank-one comparisons. The same λ appears once in the tensor Leibniz rule. Invertible rescaling replaces Aᵢ by λ⁻¹Aᵢ, the operator by λ⁻¹Dᵢ and curvature by λ⁻²Cᵢⱼ, agreeing with the intrinsic construction.

The determinant line has coefficient tr Aᵢ. Prove its curvature is tr Cᵢⱼ, hence flatness follows from that of E. Duality negates the determinant coefficient; tensoring ranks r,s gives s·tr Aᵢ+r·tr Bᵢ. Derive δ(det G) as the sum of determinants with one row differentiated, derive the trace identity for infinitesimal row action, and use the ColemanPowerSeries L1 matrix-unit identity to prove

tr Aᵢ′=tr Aᵢ−λ(det G)⁻¹δᵢ(det G).

Thus determinant gauge transport agrees with gauge by the native one-by-one unit induced by det G, and determinant curvature is gauge invariant. For a family of sections stored as the rows of S, alternating evaluation gives Σ_r det(updateRow S r (Dᵢ(S_r)))=λδᵢ(det S)+tr(Aᵢ)det S. The connection's matrix action on this family is S Aᵢᵗ. The auxiliary row-action identity uses A S and states Σ_r det(updateRow S r ((A S)_r))=tr(A)det S; transposing gives the required section formula. Neither identity requires S invertible or the connection flat. This is the bridge from matrices to exterior determinant descent. Its API includes trace evaluation, gauge, tensor, dual and the alternating intertwining map.

### Tensor fields, exterior cancellation and integral shuffle

For arbitrary E,F,Q define the tensor field θ⊗1+1⊗ψ on E⊗F. The elementary-tensor formula and coefficient-postcomposition law determine it; provide horizontal tensor maps, tensor associator, symmetry and both unitors. Contraction yields A_α⊗1+1⊗B_α. Actions on separate factors commute regardless of integrability on either factor. Consequently a repeated contraction has its integral binomial expansion. More generally, any ordered word expands over all choices of which factor acts at each position, retaining the order within each selected factor. ### Analytic comparison and harmonic metrics

## Layer 2: Variations, extensions and monodromy

This layer extends D3's common variation interface. It keeps the pure, real, rational, integral and complex conventions separate and constructs maps to the parent fibre Hodge structures. The logarithmic connection and coefficient cohomology constructions consume CR.1 and ComplexComparison C5; they do not rebuild their differential or derived engines.

### Pure and mixed coefficients

For a complex manifold S, a complex polarized variation of weight n consists of a finite-rank complex local system, its flat smooth bundle (V,D), a finite decomposition V=⊕_(p+q=n)V^(p,q), and a flat nondegenerate Hermitian form ψ. The summands are orthogonal and (−1)^pψ is positive on V^(p,n−p). The connection has type shifts (1,0) preserving p or lowering it by one, and (0,1) preserving p or raising it by one. Its D^(0,1) holomorphic structure makes Fᵃ=⊕_(p≥a)V^(p,n−p) holomorphic, with Griffiths transversality. Provide projections, fibre structure, Hodge filtration, flat form, restriction, morphisms, tensor and dual comparisons. Source: [LL22], Definitions 4.1.1–4.1.2, pp.27–28.

Realification takes L⊕conjugate L with real points (v,conjugate v). Put conjugate L^(q,p) in the second factor's (p,q) component and choose the bilinear polarization with the fixed weight sign. L is a complex Hodge summand of this polarizable real variation. A real form on L itself is extra data. Tests include an irrational unitary line, a conjugate pair and an actual real line; a lattice must never be inferred. Sources: [Del87], §1.11, p.8; [Timm87], Theorem 7.1 proof, pp.169–170.

A mixed K-variation, K=Q or R, has a finite-rank local system, bounded increasing W by local subsystems, and bounded decreasing F by holomorphic subbundles. Fibres are mixed K-Hodge structures and ∇Fᵖ⊆Fᵖ⁻¹⊗Ω¹. Require locally free graded holomorphic bundles. Morphisms are flat K-linear maps preserving both filtrations. Rational fibres compare with native `MixedHodgeStructure` by taking V_Z=V_Q as a Z-module; that abstract integral presentation supplies no finite free lattice. Real mixed structures and complex mixed structures with two opposed filtrations require their own coefficient extensions.

Construct Gr^W_k as a quotient local system with its induced connection and filtration (Fᵖ∩W_k+W_(k−1))/W_(k−1). Its fibre agrees with native `gradedHodgeStructure`, and graded maps are functorial. A chosen graded polarization consists of flat nondegenerate Q_k on these pure quotients, parity (−1)^k, target K(−k), orthogonality Fᵖ against F^(k+1−p), and positivity i^(2p−k)Q_k(v,conjugate v)>0. It need not polarize the whole mixed extension or be integral/unimodular. Provide fibre, graded, restriction, tensor, dual, Hom and evaluation APIs with the corresponding weight shifts. Sources: [SZ85], Definitions 3.4–3.5, p.508. Tests include a nonsplit two-weight extension, a constant rational mixed structure and a polarization on graded pieces that does not extend to a nondegenerate form on the total space.

For GLᵣ it is equivalent to a local system of finite projective O_K-modules whose complex extension is the given system; after finite extension one can express it using matrices over algebraic integers for finitely generated Γ. Give conjugacy invariance, coefficient extension, projective-lattice comparison, projectivization and recovery from an integral projectivization with finite determinant. S-integrality is weaker. `StronglyIntegral` means complex conjugacy into GL_n(Z), or a rank-n preserved Z-lattice spanning C^n. Restriction of scalars takes an integral representation to the strongly integral sum over embeddings, with rank multiplied by [K:Q]. Sources: [LL24], Definition 8.3.1, pp.39–40; [EG20], Remark 6.2, p.146 and Proposition 8.2 proof, p.153.

For the GLᵣ representation API, `IsIntegralRepresentation.iff_projectiveLattice` characterizes integrality by a number field K⊂C and a stable submodule Λ⊂C^r over O_K. Require Λ finite and projective, and require the inclusion to exhibit C^r as C⊗_(O_K)Λ using native `IsBaseChange`; mere stability of a smaller module does not suffice. No basis over O_K, irreducibility or finite generation of Γ is imposed. An integral matrix realization gives a free lattice. Conversely, a projective lattice becomes free after a finite field extension principalizing its Steinitz class, yielding an integral matrix conjugate. This is the representation component of the projective-local-system criterion; passage to local systems uses monodromy classification and scalar-extension compatibility. Prerequisites: Mathlib's ring of integers, finite projective modules, number-field ideal-class finiteness and scalar extension, and H.1's native representation adapter. Tests include the trivial representation, impossibility for rank-one monodromy 1/2, failure of the zero lattice to span positive rank, and a stable lattice after conjugating the unipotent generator [[1,1/3],[0,1]] by diag(3,1). Sources: [EG18], §1, pp.1–2; [LL24], Definition 8.3.1, pp.39–40.

A strongly integral unitary representation has finite image by compactness intersecting the discrete lattice group. An O_K-integral representation unitary at every embedding also has finite image. One embedding is insufficient: a unit-circle root α of x⁴−x³−x²−x+1 that is not a root of unity defines an infinite integral unitary surface-group character. Another embedding sends α to a real root of modulus greater than one. Its determinant has infinite order and it is not rigid in the unrestricted rank-one moduli. Sources: [EG20], Example 6.3, p.146; [LL22v2], Lemma 7.2.1 and proof, pp.51–52.

For finitely generated Γ and an O_(K,Σ)-valued representation, local integral conjugacy at every place of Σ gives, after a finite extension, a globally integral conjugate. Outside Σ it is already integral. The GL route needs local lattice gluing, Dedekind projective modules and principalization; the reductive route needs a simply connected lift and strong approximation. These are arithmetic supplier contracts. Sources: [EG18], §1, pp.1–2; [KP20], Proposition 3.1, p.5.

Esnault–Groechenig integrality applies to an irreducible cohomologically rigid system on smooth quasiprojective X with finite determinant and quasi-unipotent boundary monodromy. Klevdal–Patrikis gives the analogous G(O_L) conjugate for split reductive integral G, G-irreducibility, G-cohomological rigidity, quasi-unipotent boundary and finite abelianized image; its connected monodromy is semisimple. Their inputs include finite-field Langlands, purity, GL/group-valued companions, tame specialization, local acyclicity, compatible-system local monodromy and étale–Betti comparison. Curve Langlands at GS.6 alone does not supply companions on higher-dimensional varieties; the group-valued-companion and tame-specialization extensions are required. Sources: [EG18], Theorem 1.1, p.1 and proof §§2–3; [KP20], Theorem 1.2, p.2; [LL24], Lemma 8.3.3 proof, p.40.

The target need not be Hermitian symmetric or algebraic, and R_an alone is not the general conclusion. Sources: [BKT20], Theorem 1.3, p.920 and §§4.1–4.2, pp.928–929.

For a compact arithmetic Hodge target the map is R_an-definable. At a neat level, unipotent local monodromy must be trivial because a cocompact arithmetic group has no nontrivial unipotents. H.6 then extends the map into D across the SNC boundary, and finite compact analytic charts give restricted-analytic graphs. Source: [BKT20], Remark 1.4(1), p.921. This requires the finite-monodromy extension into D, not only extension into the compact dual.

### Special images and individual algebraic pullbacks

`specialHodgeImage` is the set-theoretic image of a rational pure Hodge-data morphism with compatible arithmetic levels and components. The identity image is special. Hodge compatibility makes the group map compatible with the canonical compacts and Cartan involutions, so the image is R_alg-definable. To prove it closed analytic, first factor out the possibly noncompact kernel, then use a proper arithmetic immersion of the rational image datum and the proper holomorphic image theorem. Definability alone does not imply closedness or analyticity; the original morphism need not be proper. These general arithmetic and analytic-image extensions belong to AA.3 and C4. Sources: [BKT20], §5, pp.933–934; [BKT23], Corollary 1.3, p.3; [PS09], §6.1, Theorem 6.1, draft p.16.

For a flat rational tensor t on a simply connected chart, `tensorHodgeLocus(t)` tests type (0,0) in its actual finite tensor/dual/Tate construction. In weight zero and for real rational t this is the zero locus of its holomorphic quotient section in T_C/F⁰. In nonzero weight the locus is the whole chart only for zero t and otherwise empty. An untwisted (p,p) tensor becomes type (0,0) only after the stated Tate twist. Give tensor/marking transport, restriction and the local analytic zero-locus API. Sources: [K17], §2.4, Lemma 2.5, p.8; [BKT20], §1.4, pp.921–922.

`exceptionalHodgeLocus` fixes the generic Mumford–Tate datum and indexes all rational vectors in all allowed finite tensor constructions and Tate twists, excluding the generic type-(0,0) tensors, including zero. Monodromy transforms both the tensor and the generic subspace, so the projected locus is marking independent. It equals the union of pullbacks of strict rational special images for this generic datum, each enforcing a nongeneric tensor. Generic identity images are excluded from this union. Rational tensor vectors and component/level choices give a countable index, not a definable parameter family. Sources: [K17], §§2.6,3.4, pp.9,12–13; [BKT20], §5, p.934.

For each special image Y, global definability and independent closed analyticity make Φ⁻¹(Y) a closed definable complex analytic subset of S. LD.6's definable analytic Chow theorem makes its reduced subset algebraic, with finitely many irreducible components. A strict image meeting the period image has proper pullback. Enumerating strict rational images and these finite decompositions proves the exceptional locus is a countable union of proper closed irreducible algebraic subvarieties. The whole union need not be closed, algebraic or definable, and no analytic nilpotent ideal is automatically algebraized. Sources: [BKT20], Theorems 1.6,4.13 and §5, pp.922,933–934; [PS09], Theorem 4.4 and Corollary 4.5, draft p.12.

Tests distinguish a generic tensor from a nongeneric one, zero and nonzero weights, an explicit Tate shift, identity versus strict special images, monodromy transitions and a countable union that is not definable. The canonical finite real-algebraic domain atlas, tensor/subdatum classification, kernel/image Hodge factorization and proper-image interfaces are required H.3 extensions. Native sets, subrings and first-order `Set.Definable` predicates do not by themselves define o-minimality, R_an,exp or the algebraic/analytic complex-point comparison.


### Examples: hypotheses on the receiving data

The rough-function, reducedness, finite-containment and algebraicity conclusions above apply to their constructed geometric inputs. They do not extend to an arbitrary set of functions, matrix family, image or subset. The following native checks make these boundaries explicit; they accompany the definition checks above.

**Checks.**

- An arbitrary real-valued function is not a member of the empty set of functions. Thus a receiving set alone cannot stand for the restricted-analytic rough-monomial class, even when the vector whose norm is evaluated is nonzero.
- For the zero one-by-one real matrix, the product of diagonal entries and its determinant are both zero, so the strict determinant bound would read 0<0 for every C. This differs from the rank-zero convention det=1 and tests the positive-definite metric hypothesis.
- For the one-by-one matrix with entry −1 and every C>0, the row bound would read 1≤−C, which fails. An arbitrary matrix family cannot replace the real positive Hodge metric.
- An empty indexing type gives an empty finite union of proposed Siegel sets; it cannot contain the image of a point. Finiteness of the chosen subfamily is a conclusion about the specified same-K arithmetic family, not about arbitrary receiving subsets.
- The underlying `specialHodgeImage` of the inclusion (0,1)→R is exactly (0,1), which is not closed. Closed analyticity therefore uses the pure Hodge morphism, its arithmetic factorization and proper-image theorem, rather than the set-theoretic image operation alone.
- On a one-point base every proper subset is empty, so any union of proper subsets is empty. The whole base cannot satisfy the exceptional-locus conclusion with arbitrary input locus; removing generic tensors and using the genuine tensor/subdatum comparison are essential.

### Real action and transported classes

Extend the common finite-rank effective rational weight-two variation over a complex base with antiholomorphic involution c by a flat rational involution σ_b:H_b→H_c(b), with σ_c(b)σ_b=id. Its complex-linear extension exchanges (p,2−p) with (2−p,p). Coefficient conjugation κ also exchanges types, so τ=σ_Cκ is conjugate-linear and preserves them. Give action projection, involution, transport, type exchange and combined-type preservation, restriction to an invariant flat chart and extensionality. On a pure tensor the geometric map sends z⊗v to z⊗σ(v), whereas the combined map sends it to conjugate(z)⊗σ(v); these are separate operations. This is extra action data on the common variation, not another VHS carrier. Sources: [B18], (1.1), p.1050 and (1.3), p.1052.

The coefficient sign of R(1) identifies V(1)^G with ker(σ+id)⊆V. Prove fixed iff σv=−v under the Tate generator. The choices 2πi in B18 and i in B19 give the same sign equation. At a real basepoint define J_b=H_R,b^(1,1)(1)^G. Use a different symbol, ℐ, for the untwisted real (1,1) bundle on the complex base: its fibre is H_R,b^(1,1), before taking a Tate twist or fixed points. A smooth projective real surface family provides the effective weight-two variation and compatible action through its actual Hodge/de Rham/Betti comparisons, H¹(Ω¹)≃H^(1,1) and H²(O)≃H^(0,2). Remove torsion only when forming the VHS lattice. Source: [B18], §1.4, pp.1051–1052.

On a simply connected flat chart Δ and real marked λ, define `transportedHodgeLocus(λ)` by transported λ∈H^(1,1), equivalently λ∈F¹ because λ is real. The real locus intersects B(R), with twisted invariance when required. Give membership, constant-family example, flat-frame transport, overlap equality when e transforms both Hodge data and λ, and cocycle compatibility. On a nontrivial monodromy loop transform the class too; no globally preferred branch is supplied. Sources: [B18], Proposition 1.1, pp.1050–1051; [B19], Theorem 1.5, pp.70–71.

At a zero of the obstruction section given by a fixed flat class in H_C/F¹, its derivative is −θ(v)(λ) in H^(0,2). H.3's derivative of the moving filtration has the positive sign. Keep these as separate APIs with a graph-chart test distinguishing them. The positive moving-filtration formula is [G68], Proposition 1.20 and Theorem 1.23, pp.814–815. ### Geometric contraction and normal boundaries

For a smooth projective complex surface family define φ_λ(v)=KS(v)∪λ in H²(O), with KS first and contraction T_X⊗Ω¹_X→O. Its construction uses the actual deformation and coherent cup maps. Under the natural geometric Hodge comparisons, θ(v)(λ)=φ_λ(v). Supplied arbitrary linear maps model the local contraction algebra only, not this geometric equality. Sources: [G68], II.1(a), (1.4)–(1.20), pp.810–815; [B18], (1.4), p.1052; [B19], (5.2), p.86.

For smooth projective C⊂T⊂X of dimensions 1,2,3, let λ be the Atiyah/Chern class of O_T(C) in H¹(Ω¹_T). The normal tangent sequence gives ρ:H⁰(N_T/X)→H¹(T_T). Then

ρ(n)∪λ=δ_div(δ_norm(n|C))∈H²(O_T),

where δ_norm comes from 0→N_C/T→N_C/X→N_T/X|C→0 and δ_div from 0→O_T→O_T(C)→i_*N_C/T→0, where i:C↪T and N_C/T≃O_T(C)|_C. The last term is a sheaf on T by pushforward, not a sheaf on C silently inserted into the sequence on T. For an embedded family with characteristic map k:T_bB→H⁰(N_T/X), φ_λ=δ_divδ_norm res_C k. State restriction, boundary, naturality and sign APIs through the coherent sequence maps, plus the comparison of the Betti divisor class with its coherent Chern class. In particular δ_norm lands in H¹(C,N_C/T), not a differently twisted group. Sources: [B18], Proposition 2.1, pp.1053–1054; [B19], Proposition 5.4, (5.7)–(5.9), p.88.

If H¹(T,N_T/X(−C))=0, H¹(C,N_C/X)=0 and H²(T,O_T(C))=0, the resulting map from H⁰(N_T/X) is onto H²(O_T). A family conclusion additionally needs k onto or the composite restriction onto H⁰(C,N_T/X|C). Source: [B18], Corollary 2.2, p.1054. Generic coherent deformation, boundary/cup naturality and Atiyah signs require SF.2,SF.4,SF.5 interfaces; coherent analytic cohomology alone does not supply the cohomological Hodge decomposition. Test a zero characteristic map despite the three vanishings and a family supplying surjective restriction.

### Green submersion and real cones

A `PositiveOpenCone` in finite-dimensional real V is a nonempty open set stable under every positive scalar. Convexity and additivity are not required; zero is allowed. Openness is relative to the actual fixed or kernel subspace. Give constructor, positive-scalar action and invariance, image under a linear equivalence and relative topology APIs. Source: [B18], Proposition 1.1, pp.1050–1051; [B19], Theorem 1.5, pp.70–71.

For an effective real weight-two variation, the smooth untwisted bundle ℐ of real (1,1) classes has evaluation by flat transport into a fixed fibre. For λ∈ℐ_b, if θ_λ:T_bB→H_b^(0,2) is complex surjective, evaluation is a real submersion at (b,λ). It holds for a chosen rational subvariation with its own target; ambient surjectivity is a different condition. Sources: [V06], Lemma 2 and Proposition 1 proof, author pp.4–5; [B18], Proposition 1.1 proof, p.1051.

An equivariant surjective real-linear map between involutive spaces restricts surjectively to fixed spaces, by averaging; changing both actions by the Tate sign preserves this. Combine this with the general finite-involution fixed-manifold theorem, whose tangent is the fixed tangent space. For a smooth algebraic base B over R, b∈B(R) and λ∈J_b=H_R,b^(1,1)(1)^G with θ_λ onto, any G-stable connected trivializing Δ with connected contractible Δ(R) has a positive open cone Ω⊆H_R,b(1)^G whose classes become twisted-invariant (1,1) classes at real points of Δ. Such Δ exist arbitrarily small; Ω depends on Δ. Its openness is in the twisted fixed space. Source: [B18], Proposition 1.1 and proof, pp.1050–1051.

Define `GoodRealLocus` by existence of λ∈J_b=H_R,b^(1,1)(1)^G with surjective actual Griffiths symbol T_bB(C)→H_b^(0,2). The witness is in the twisted fixed subspace; existence of an arbitrary real (1,1) class is a different predicate. With real-analytic local frames and locally constant dimensions, this locus is open and on each connected component of B(R) is either empty or dense. The complement is closed real analytic. For a geometric family use the contraction equality above. One good point gives density only in its own component. Sources: [B18], Propositions 1.2–1.3 and Remark 1.5, pp.1051–1052. Tests include a fixed zero target, an empty good component, two disconnected real components, an anti-invariant class and a map equivariant only after both Tate signs.

### Constant and vanishing subvariations

Let C be a constant rational Hodge subsystem with fixed filtration and a flat symmetric pairing Q nondegenerate on C, of total Hodge type (2,2). Define K=C^⊥. Prove H=C⊕K as flat Hodge variations, with flat rational Hodge projector, and preservation of K under a real action preserving C,Q. The full surface intersection form here is not automatically a polarization without Lefschetz sign corrections. Sources: [B19], Proposition 6.6 proof, p.94.

The symbol vanishes on C and takes K^(1,1) into K^(0,2). For λ=λ_C+λ_K, θ_λ=θ_(λ_K). An image contained in K^(0,2) with ambient codimension ≤dim C^(0,2) must be onto K^(0,2); the opposite bound follows from containment. Thus an ambient twisted-invariant λ with contraction cokernel of that dimension gives the real open-cone theorem on K, using the projected λ_K. A real curve class need not itself lie in K. Give orthogonal projector, decomposition, real compatibility, symbol/image containment and restricted-codomain rank APIs. Source: [B19], Proposition 6.6 proof, p.94, with its vanishing-component correction. Tests use a nonzero constant (0,2) part and an ambient λ with nonzero constant component.

A full finite real lattice Λ generated by a basis meets every positive open cone in every coset a+nΛ, n>0. Prove this in the same real span by scaling an interior ball until lattice rounding lands in it. It applies to full finite-index sublattices, not to lower-rank lattices in larger spaces. No rationality of Ω or a is required. Sources: [B19], Proposition 1.2 proof, p.71 and Proposition 6.6 proof, p.94. Tests include a shifted coset, zero-dimensional V and a lower-rank counterexample to the missing span hypothesis.

### Voisin's complex pushforward-kernel theorem

Take smooth projective X of dimension three and p:X→S to a smooth projective surface, with smooth rational generic fibre. Choose ample H with H²·K_X<0, replacing it by a high power for very ampleness and the positive-twist vanishings. For all large m and general smooth T∈|mH|, there is λ∈ker[p_*:H¹(T,Ω¹_T)→H¹(S,Ω¹_S)] for which φ_λ:H⁰(N_T/X)→ker[p_*:H²(T,O_T)→H²(S,O_S)] is onto. Keep the negative canonical intersection and all degree thresholds. Source: [V06], Proposition 9, author p.21, with Lemmas 6–10, Propositions 3–7 and Appendix, author pp.8–18,22–23.

The kernel is a weight-two subvariation in the universal divisor family. Green's criterion yields a nonempty Zariski open of smooth parameters such that every connected contractible neighbourhood has a positive open cone in ker[p_*:H²(T,R)→H²(S,R)] whose classes become (1,1) on complex nearby fibres. This theorem does not itself give real nearby points. Sources: [V06], Proposition 8 proof and Proposition 9, author pp.20–21; [B19], Theorem 1.5 and discussion, pp.70–71.

For a connected smooth projective complex surface S, X=P¹×S and H=pr₁*O(1)⊗pr_S*A, where A is very ample and A²>K_S·A, deduce that kernel-cone conclusion for all sufficiently large m. The degree threshold is geometric and is unrelated to a Brauer period. Source: [B19], Theorem 1.5, p.70. General supplier closure requires the symmetric determinantal node count, incidence Koszul/Bott vanishings, uniform position, low-codimension multiplication and Jacobian/residue comparisons in Voisin's proof, including the pushforward identification with H²(S,O_S). These reusable statements are prerequisites rather than application-specific vanishing assumptions.

### Ordinary integral topology and divisor export

Andreotti–Frankel gives a CW model of real dimension ≤d for a smooth complex affine variety of dimension d, using a proper Morse function with bounded index. In particular a smooth affine surface has Hⁱ(U(C),Z)=0 for i≥3, with all torsion retained. Apply this separately to a smooth finite étale cover, which is also affine of the same dimension. Do not infer sign-coefficient or equivariant vanishing from the ordinary coefficient statement. Sources: [M63], Theorem 3.5, p.20 and Theorem 7.2, pp.39–41; [B19], Proposition 3.2 proof, p.78.

For a smooth ample Cartier surface i:T↪X in a smooth projective complex threefold, ordinary integral Gysin gives H³(T,Z)≃H⁵(X,Z) and a surjection H²(T,Z)→H⁴(X,Z), including torsion. Use complex orientations, integral Poincaré duality and weak Lefschetz; no H² injectivity is claimed. Sources: [M63], Corollary 7.3, p.41; [B19], Lemmas 1.3–1.4, p.70. Global Morse handle/CW, smooth analytification orientation and the inclusion/Gysin dictionary are topology supplier extensions, beyond local Morse index or existing handle constructions.

An integral class whose realification is in a complex Green/Voisin cone becomes (1,1) nearby, and MC.7's integral Lefschetz (1,1) theorem gives a line bundle with that Chern class. In the real setting the cone supplies a twisted-invariant real Hodge class at a real point. To get a real line bundle, the consumer must supply an actual equivariant integral lift in H²_G(T(C),Z(1)) and use the real integral divisor theorem. Invariant ordinary cohomology, rational classes and untwisted invariance are weaker inputs. Transport of the lift and the finite-index lattice image belong to the consumer/topology interfaces. Sources: [B19], Proposition 1.2 and Proposition 6.6 proofs, pp.71,94–95; [B18], Remark 1.4, p.1052; [BW20], Proposition 2.8, arXiv numbering. Use the six named definitions of this layer as reusable interfaces, with the following basic API and examples. The maps are taken from the geometric suppliers above; a coordinate fixture checks an algebraic law without supplying the geometric comparison theorem.
