Two calculations underlying these qualifications were reconstructed directly.

For **/30**, the bad locus embeds in the image of a common-point factor times two root-section factors of degrees `d+ρ−N∓−1`. Apply Riemann–Roch to the factor with `N±=min(N+,N−)`: its fibre has dimension `d−N±−g`, provided `d ≥ 2g+N±`. The nonzero-section factor on the other side has dimension `d+ρ−N∓−1`. Adding the common-point dimension gives at most `2d+ρ−N−g=m−1`. The theorem's printed bound implies this requirement. For the borderline genus-one unramified and genus-zero minimally ramified cases, either `N=0` and `d≥2g`, or `max(N+,N−)≥1`; no extra `2g+N` gate is needed. The smallness bound follows from the same Hurwitz constraints. Theorem 5.20's octahedron proof does not invoke Lemma 5.19.

For **/34**, let `L=O_X(x)` and take the generic inclusion and local generator `ϖ⁻¹`. The map `τ_x ∘ τ_η⁻¹` in A.5 is then multiplication by `ϖ`, which maps `L` to the positive uniformizer class, contrary to the inverse printed in the statement. One can choose the inverse equivalence instead, but must also change every divisor and line-bundle consumer. For **/38**, at the trivial upstairs line the homotopy kernel of `1−σ` contains arbitrary trivialization scalars. On a geometric base, scalar automorphisms cannot remove them; choose a scalar with square different from one to see the missing descent/norm constraint. The unramified quadratic line also witnesses the kernel of pullback on Picard classes, so ordinary injectivity cannot be inserted into the repair.
