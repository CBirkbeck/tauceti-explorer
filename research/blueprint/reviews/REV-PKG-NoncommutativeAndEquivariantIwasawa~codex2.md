## Findings preventing acceptance

These are missing steps in the dependency/proof plan, not claims that the published theorems are false. General targets remain in the package. The following sections now distinguish a correctly located statement from an available proof of the statement.

1. **Filtered homological transfer, §0.5.** Schneider–Teitelbaum Proposition 1.1 and Lemma 1.4, pp.4–6, supply the noetherian/strict-generator ingredients. Venjakob 2002 Definition 3.19, Lemma 3.20 and Theorem 3.21, pp.16–17, supply the stated closure condition and the Auslander transfer statement. They do not replace the cited Björk proofs or state the separate numerical global-dimension bound with just the hypotheses asserted here. The exact strict filtered-resolution theorem, its hypotheses and its proof/source remain obligations already acknowledged in the reconciled text. Noetherianity alone must not be used for a homological conclusion.
2. **Nonuniform global dimension, §0.11.** The proposed equality of projective dimensions under finite-free restriction does not follow merely from the Frobenius comparison of Ext into the ring. Such Ext groups can vanish in positive degrees even for a module of infinite projective dimension. The new negative control is the trivial module over the self-injective ring `F_p[C_p]`: it has infinite projective dimension, restricts to a vector space of projective dimension zero, and has vanishing positive Ext into the ring. This example is outside the conclusion's no-p-torsion hypothesis; it refutes the proposed intermediate argument. Brumer's no-p-torsion resolution theorem still needs a proof/source with the pseudocompact-to-abstract and integral/residual coefficient comparisons. Venjakob §1.1, p.5, and CFKSV p.174 locate the equality but do not prove that missing step. The independent uniform theorem remains an import. This also qualifies the bounded resolutions used in §0.15 and the module version of §2.9.
3. **Determinant presentation, §2.7.** The generalized determinant and localized K₁ now have explicit owner targets and real numerical supplier references. However, an identification on π₀ of a K-theory fibre does not prove that the determinant-trivialization presentation has precisely relations (0)–(3). The denominator/stabilization construction of an inverse must be independent of its choices and respect the elementary matrix, homotopy and exact-sequence relations. Those comparisons remain missing. I repaired the independently provable Ore-perfect localization and relative-triple route in §§2.8–2.9, so this is no longer hidden in a circular argument.
4. **Finite Whitehead inputs and continuity, §3.11.** Kakde's cited pages identify Wall/Oliver's finite-group results and the p-adic division-algebra reduced-norm kernel theorem; they do not reproduce their proofs. Elementary induction alone does not calculate those kernels. The targets `sk1_finite`, `sk1_semisimple_padic`, `dressWall_induction`, `sk1_vanish_lElementaryQuotient` and `k1_torsion_wall` require their actual proofs or an accessible replacement with the same coefficients and groups. For `k1_continuity`, generation by units at each finite level is insufficient: compatible classes must lift modulo commutators, with the inverse-limit kernel identified. I corrected the vanishing family: the l-ℚ_p-elementary family of §6.5, l≠p, has finite quotients `C_(p^n) × (C ⋊ P)`, with C cyclic of p-power order and P of order prime to p. Its p-Sylow need not be cyclic. There is no claim of SK₁ vanishing for arbitrary p-groups or the p-elementary family of §6.6. Independently, the arbitrary-finite-group torsion formula used in §6.5 is false: at p=5, ℤ₅[C₂]≅ℤ₅×ℤ₅, so the actual K₁ torsion has order 16, while μ₄×C₂ has order 8 and SK₁=1. I restricted the Wall formula to finite p-groups, used μ(ℤ_p) so that p=2 retains the scalar −1, preserved the general l-elementary target, and exposed the additional coefficient-block logarithm-kernel detection it needs. ClassicalArithmeticCompletion §7.73 explicitly has finite-p-group scope and cannot supply this step. The same overbroad sentence occurs in Kakde v3 p.26; agreement with that source would not make the package claim true. E42 records this against the preprint actually read. The version-of-record status and any existing correction have not been established, so this is not labelled a new error in the published article.
5. **Arithmetic perfection with p-torsion, §4.5.** Naming `iwasawaComplex_perfect_withPTorsion` does not close it. ArithmeticGaloisDuality D7.3 gives its perfect-amplitude criterion over a complete noetherian local **commutative** coefficient ring. It does not supply the noncommutative induced-coefficient theorem for Λ(G), its identification with the dual complex here, or the asserted derived quotient comparison. These must be proved with compact coefficients, finite-generation hypotheses and the relevant Shapiro maps. The finite-global-dimension shortcut is unavailable when G has p-torsion. The general p-torsion target is retained and explicitly marked as a proof gap.
6. **Local determinant and height comparisons, §§7.15–7.17.** The Weil–Deligne local constant does not itself construct the de Rham determinant-line isomorphism or the finite-cohomology duality trivialization of §7.15. Burns–Venjakob §6.1, pp.28–29, restates the Fukaya–Kato input rather than proving it. I named these missing comparison targets. Likewise §7.16's natural Bloch–Kato finite-cohomology comparison cannot be inferred from abstract splitting of vector-space complexes. Finally §7.17's Bockstein composite defines a pairing, but equality with Nekovář's independently defined height, ramified local descent, and compatibility with duality still require proofs. The relevant BV pp.30–32 identify the statements and original references, not the full missing proofs. The elementary DVR-block semisimplicity/sign calculation is valid conditional on these comparisons. The conjectures remain propositions, and §7.18 retains its motivic Selmer and regulator/period hypotheses; this review does not promote a conjectural comparison to a theorem.

These findings are sufficient to withhold acceptance. The report does not certify that all other primary arithmetic proof leaves have been developed: it records a target-level review, source checks and the specific outstanding closure obligations.

## Corrections made in the package

- Qualified the CompletedCohomologyPartII F.2 contract and its use in §0.15; removed the invalid nonuniform finite-projective-dimension argument and added the self-injective negative control.
- Distinguished the commutative determinant-line functor from an equivalence of the full virtual-object Picard groupoid with graded lines. General K₀/K₁ information must not be discarded.
- Corrected §2.7's two-term characteristic class orientation: for the indicated degrees the class is `h⁻¹`, so the ratio representing a cleared fraction has the opposite order.
- Rebuilt §§2.8–2.9 using the generic left-denominator universal localization, right-module flatness, stable flatness and Neeman–Ranicki Theorems 0.5/0.7. Idempotent completion and the possible failure of K₀-surjectivity are explicit. The construction order is §2.9, §2.8, then §2.7, with only the final characteristic-map comparison depending on §2.7.
- Deleted the invalid use of left scalar multiplication by a noncentral denominator on an arbitrary left projective module. The map on a free left module is right multiplication; arbitrary summands use the fibre comparison. Added the matrix witness `s=diag(T,1)`, `r=e₁₂`, where `sr=T e₁₂` and `rs=e₁₂`.
- Distinguished localization at S from localization at S*: in the one-variable case these give Λ_(p) and the fraction field respectively.
- Corrected the finite SK₁ vanishing family and exposed the finite Whitehead and compatible-unit lifting obligations. Removed the false arbitrary-finite-group torsion formula, added the p=2 scalar-root control, and replaced §6.5’s use of it by the explicit `lElementary_logKernelDetection` obligation, with the p=5, C₂ counterexample.
- Corrected U_β to the double coset of `diag(1,β)`, with upper-triangular representatives giving `c(μ,U_β f)=c(μβ,f)`, including μ=0. For β=p at level p over ℚ, this coset and that of `diag(p,1)` have different images modulo p; the level preserves the first coordinate line.
- Replaced the unit-cusp comparison by conjugation with the adelic diagonal unit D_γ. Since γ is a unit at every finite place, it normalizes the b- and c-entry level lattices, including the different and fractional-ideal factors, and commutes with the Hecke representative. Hence it preserves `K t_β K`; conjugation permutes its finite cosets and gives U-commutation with the same counting normalization. Deligne–Ribet §§5.7–5.8, pp.262–263, transport this statement to the cusp expansions in every polarization component. No congruence γ≡1 modulo the varying conductor is needed. §§6.24–6.25, E34 and E41 now use this route. A nonunit γ fails the lattice-normalization test.
- Corrected Ritter–Weiss 2011 §6, Lemma k to pp.24–25; the former pp.25–27 locator extended into the appendix and references.
- Exposed the local epsilon, finite-cohomology, height and descent proof gaps instead of asserting that all Fukaya–Kato inputs have accessible proofs here.
- Removed two proved examples about a library ideal from Suggested.lean. The final maintainer rule permits examples only for the package's own kept definitions. Their mathematical negative controls remain in the README, whose stale claim about those Lean examples was removed.
