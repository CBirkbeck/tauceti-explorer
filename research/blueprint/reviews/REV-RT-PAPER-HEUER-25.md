## Repairs that need qualification

**/1: comparison ownership.** CP.3 uses the primitive comparison and rational local higher-direct-image calculation; P8 requires CP.3. Coordinate the singular finiteness input /22 and existing Scholze13/Zavyalov sources.

**/2 and /27: Picard torsors.** The Enriques example confirms that Corollary 2.19's group must be `Pic^0[p]`, not all of `H^1_et(mu_p)`. Dolgachev's public account establishes the characteristic-zero Picard calculation; Kummer and proper GAGA give the analytic example. The proposed proof that an open analytic subgroup must be closed is invalid. For `P=Pic`, `G=P^0`, `N=P/G` and the étale-sheaf cokernel `Q` of multiplication by p on G, the locally constant Kummer boundary vanishes on G. The snake sequence gives `N[p] → Q` surjective. Lemma 2.21 makes `G → Q` constant, hence zero, proving Q=0. Fargues then supplies étaleness and finiteness with the finite kernel. For the whole Picard group, specify a torsor over the image; its image is clopen here because of the locally constant Kummer boundary. The elliptic degree calculation rules out a torsor over all components. /27 also needs the §2.7/Theorem 2.4 locator and mu_p correction.

**/12: descent coordination.** Kedlaya–Liu II 3.5.8 and Berkeley 17.1.8 supply the common perfectoid descent core. Existing extractions route or classify it inconsistently. RF2's divisor application and VB1's curve application are downstream of D2; importing them into D2 would reverse ownership. Coordinate one early generic supplier, then reuse it in these applications. Do not declare the full generic theorem planned solely from an application contract. The rigid-space transfer in /5 remains separate. The Kedlaya–Liu15/373 note/route conflict also requires coordination.

**/14 and /15: logarithms.** The topological p-torsion subgroup need not itself be p-divisible with finite kernel when there are infinitely many components. The direct sum of copies of Q_p/Z_p verifies the distinction, and HWZ v2 corrects its proposition. State the identity-component assertion and logarithm sequence; retain the stronger assertion only with its component hypothesis. /15 is a real citation/hypothesis gap: HWZ Theorem 6.12 assumes local p-divisibility, whereas the printed Heuer statement only assumes surjectivity on K-points. The union of radius-one discs centred at K distinguishes these hypotheses, but **is not a counterexample to the K-point splitting conclusion**: it has the identity splitting. Record the missing justification for the broader attribution, extract the version proved by HWZ and retain the stronger hypotheses already available in both actual applications.

Add planned extraction items that reuse them, coordinated with /13. These general complete-field contracts avoid an unnecessary widening of the arithmetic P8 node. An extension to arbitrary coherent B must explicitly include coherent descent/local presentations, rather than be inferred from finite-projective acyclicity.

**/30, /38 and /43: conventions and versions.** /30 needs a log-normalized identification so that diagram (7) has the claimed compatibility. The source's existence statement does not establish a false proposition or a literal misprinted formula; avoid manufacturing that stronger sourceIssue. /38 mixes citation conventions: the journal's [21], [24], [20] are correct for rank-one Heuer, HWZ and Moduli spaces, while v3 has [18], [23], [21].
