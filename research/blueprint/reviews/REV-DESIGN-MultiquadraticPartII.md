The suggested kernel and character-descent signatures permit either quadratic signature; their hypotheses are sufficient and the real use is a specialization. The genus-cardinality signature also needs no positivity assumption: the imported narrow two-rank theorem is valid for either signature. These stronger signatures agree with the packet’s real specializations.

For genus cardinalities, the quotient is the existing additive elementary-two quotient. Its comparison with the quotient by squares makes Mathlib’s group fibre equivalence applicable; it is not a claim that the quotient map from a multiplicative group is itself a multiplicative homomorphism into an additive carrier. The rank and ramification declarations supply the exponent. Surjectivity and finiteness supply nonempty finite fibres, and cancellation by a positive power of two gives equality of their cardinalities. No effective class enumeration or class-number computation is assumed.

The two arithmetic targets are genuine adapters. In a positive fundamental discriminant, every odd prime divisor has valuation one. The valuation criterion therefore excludes any prime congruent to three modulo four. The dyadic exponent is unrestricted by that criterion. With signed prime discriminants, the odd factors are positive exactly when their primes are one modulo four; if all odd factors are positive, positivity of D forces the sole possible even factor to be 8. This proves the required dyadic implication without assuming it.

The obstruction target bundles direct consequences at the permitted target granularity. A residue-three divisor makes J nonsquare; one is square, so J is nontrivial. The kernel is then exactly a pair of distinct classes, giving h⁺=2h and failure of injectivity, hence the imported norm-minus-one unit obstruction. The converse is absent. The arithmetic case D=136=8·17 checks the weaker square condition without asserting J=1.
