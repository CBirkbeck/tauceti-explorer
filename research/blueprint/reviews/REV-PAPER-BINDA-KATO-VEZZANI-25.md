## Independent source-finding verdicts

The correction search and precise public-source URLs are recorded in the result and extraction report. E13 and E19 are confirmed in the **published Appendix HTML** as well as arXiv v2. E14 is an already corrected external AGV result. The remaining main-body confirmations are arXiv-v2-only, with journal persistence unverified. E10 is rejected rather than registered as a verified error under an unproved meaning of “variety.”

- **E1 — confirmed.** Rendered p. 6. For P=0,Q=N the printed condition holds trivially by q′=q,p=0, but the cokernel is N, not a group. The required condition is q+q′=phi(p).
- **E2 — confirmed.** Read and rendered pp. 7–8. A nontrivial group is integral, but sending both a nonidentity unit and its inverse to zero while sending their product to one is not multiplicative. Sharpness fixes the prelog map; gamma must be nonunit for compatibility with the standard log base. Relevant N and positive-value-monoid applications survive.
- **E3 — confirmed.** Rendered p. 8 and independently read AGV v3 Definitions 2.1.15–17 p. 30. The reduced unit-torus cofiber is T, and the conventional Tate twist is T[-1]. Stabilization agrees, but subsequent named twist formulas need this correction.
- **E4 — confirmed.** Read Remark 2.17 p. 9: its fibre product is X×_S X′, so the second tensor factor must be Q_S(X′).
- **E5 — confirmed.** Theorem 2.20 proof p. 11 cites Ogu90 with book-style locators I.2.2.5 and IV.3.1.11, while its bibliography identifies Ogu90 as the 1990 convergent-topos article and separately lists the 2018 logarithmic-geometry book. Confirm the bibliographic mismatch only. Ogu18 is a candidate replacement; the exact corollaries were not acquired, so G3 remains open.
- **E6 — confirmed.** Read Remark 2.8 p. 7 in its allowed equal-characteristic scope: in characteristic p, O_X[1/p] is the zero ring, whereas inverting the pseudo-uniformizer gives the generic fibre. The intended replacement by pi is clear.
- **E7 — confirmed.** Rendered p. 22. For X a point and k=1, the torus motive has both degree-zero unit and reduced Tate summands. The two displayed equalities omit the unit and, in higher rank, the intermediate binomial summands. Restoring them preserves membership in the good-reduction-generated subcategory.
- **E8 — confirmed.** Read Definition 3.1 p. 15 and Definition 5.1 p. 27. On Q_p with phi=0,N=id the relation holds and N is not nilpotent. Beilinson §1.15 p. 21 explicitly assumes finite dimension and invertible phi. The intended cohomological isocrystals have that narrower scope, but the literal definition needs repair.
- **E9 — confirmed.** Rendered Remark 5.8 p. 29. X0X1 in O(2,0) on P1×P1 cuts out two disjoint smooth P1 fibres, a positive-dimensional scheme-theoretic hypersurface. Independently read SGA2 XII Corollary 3.5, printed p. 120: its line bundle is ample. Confirm the unrestricted connectedness assertion is false; the impact on Theorem 5.6 depends on the integral-variety convention and no counterexample to weight–monodromy is claimed.
- **E10 — rejected.** The proposed connected reducible curve counterexample is valid if variety means an arbitrary finite-type scheme, but it does not satisfy the common integral-variety convention. The base in Remark 3.21 is algebraically closed. No explicit source convention admitting reducible varieties was established, so reject this as a confirmed source error. Retain geometric irreducibility in the extracted general trace contract and keep the interpretation question in G2.
- **E11 — confirmed.** Read the two proofs on pp. 23–24 and Proposition 3.8 on pp. 16–17: Proposition 3.8 has no numbered part (2). Confirm the broken locator only; reconstructing the intended arithmetic comparisons remains G7, without guessing a replacement.
- **E12 — confirmed.** Read the introductory Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 3.13 p. 18. The latter explicitly uses RΓ_HK^(e), with N/e. The introduction identifies its result with that corollary, so treat the omitted normalization as notation to restore, not a new failure of the comparison theorem.
- **E13 — confirmed.** Rendered p. 29 and read the published Appendix A.1 HTML. Both assert uniqueness over arbitrary perfect k. BGV v2 Remarks 4.17–18 p. 32 identify the augmentation torsor with k× tensor_Z Q; for the perfect closure of F_p(t), the class of t is nonzero. The chosen projection remains valid, and uniqueness holds when k is algebraic over F_p.
- **E14 — confirmed.** Independently read all three pages of the published 2025 AGV erratum, including Example 2.1.4 bis. The omitted refinement and hypercover hypotheses are real. The erratum states all uses in AGV remain valid; this is an already corrected external input, not proof that BKV’s applications fail.
- **E15 — confirmed.** Under the literal unrestricted endomorphism-module definition, for k=F_p the category is modules over R=Q_p<F,N>/(NF-pFN). The free rank-one R-module in degree zero is compact in D(Mod R), but infinite-dimensional over Q_p (R surjects onto Q_p[F]). Thus the stated compactness description cannot hold literally. Beilinson §1.15 instead defines finite-dimensional invertible-Frobenius modules and their bounded derived category. This confirms a missing category convention/comparison, not a failure of the geometric theorem. Confirmed only in arXiv 2207.00369v2; main journal text not collated.
- **E16 — confirmed.** The rendered formula places the equal-characteristic variety over C_p even though RΓ_HK-flat was defined immediately above on varieties over C_p-flat. A characteristic-p variety cannot acquire this characteristic-zero field extension. Confirmed only in arXiv 2207.00369v2; main journal text not collated.
- **E17 — confirmed.** The upper middle object and the right-hand rigid categories are over K-flat; the left vertical operation takes lax fixed points of Frobenius and does not change the field. In mixed characteristic O_K has no absolute characteristic-p Frobenius used in this diagram. Confirmed only in arXiv 2207.00369v2; main journal text not collated.
- **E18 — confirmed.** Frobenius sends dlog(T) to p*dlog(T), while the conventional Tate twist (1) has Frobenius p^(-1). The negative twist agrees with the reduced motive T=1(1)[1] after contravariant realization. This sign is separate from Definition 2.11’s missing shift. Confirmed only in arXiv 2207.00369v2; main journal text not collated.
- **E19 — confirmed.** The preceding displayed map has source H^q(X^(-p))(1) and target H^(q-2)(X^(-p-2)); moving the source twist across gives target (-1). Equivalently N phi=p phi N makes N:D→D(-1) Frobenius-equivariant. The rank-two phi=diag(1,p), N(e2)=e1 example distinguishes the signs. BGV v2 Theorem 1.7(2) also has (-1). Confirmed in arXiv v2 and the published Appendix HTML.
