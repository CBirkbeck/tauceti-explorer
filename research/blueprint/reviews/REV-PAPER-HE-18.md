## The thirteen findings

All are printed as quoted. The four substantial ones hold up:

- **E1 (error, a stated result).** Page 9 argues "Since the action of θ on V is of finite order, each
  θ-orbit on Ω is a finite set. Therefore (a) Each θ-twisted conjugacy class of W̃ intersects only
  finitely many W_a cosets." But the image in `Ω = W̃/W_a` of the θ-twisted class of `w` is not a
  θ-orbit: `Ω` is abelian and `[xwθ(x)^{-1}] = [w] + (1 − θ)[x]`, so the image is the coset
  `[w] + (1 − θ)Ω`, finite exactly when `(1 − θ)Ω` is finite. Finiteness of θ-orbits does not give
  that — take `Ω = Z²` with θ the swap, where every orbit has at most two elements while `(1 − θ)Ω`
  is infinite. The hypothesis the correction adds is the right one.
- **E3 and E6 (the coefficient ring).** Theorem 20 concludes that a restriction "is generated by
  `N_ν[I : I_n]` elements", while the proof bounds the generators of `H̄_R(G, I_n; ν)` and passes to
  the `R`-dual; a dual of a module with `N` generators embeds in `R^N` but need not be finitely
  generated over a general commutative `Z[1/p]`-algebra. The proof of Theorem 23 likewise argues
  through "finite dimensional" spaces and detects relations by the dual. Both are standard over a
  field, which is the hypothesis the corrections name.
- **E5 (gap).** "Note that `Ĭ_n ∩ gĬ_ng^{-1}` is a pro-p subgroup of `Ĭ_n`. Thus any element in
  `(Ĭ_n/(Ĭ_n ∩ gĬ_ng^{-1}))^σ` can be lifted to an element in `Ĭ_n^σ = I_n`." Pro-p-ness gives no
  σ-descent; the lifting is Lang's theorem for a connected (pro-)unipotent group over the residue
  field.

The rest are misprints, each verified on the page: `⊕` for `∏` in the dual of the Newton
decomposition (E2); the wrong translate in the `(P,Q,x)`-graph (E4); `W_a` where the elements
constructed lie in `W̃` (E7); `X_*(Z)` for the Iwahori–Weyl lattice when `G` is not quasi-split (E8);
the untwisted length condition `sws < w` where the twisted `swθ(s) < w` is meant (E9); `g_1` repeated
for `g_2` in the contracted-product action (E10); `θ^{nl}` for `θ^{n_0l}` (E11); the stray second
argument in `µ_G(K, µ)` (E12); and `µ_G` for `µ_{G×G}` on a subset of `G × G` (E13).
