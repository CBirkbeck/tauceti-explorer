## R2 — Ordinary connections still have the wrong supplier scope

**Blocking; unchanged mathematical gap.** H.0 accepts a commutative ringed Grothendieck site with supplied restriction-compatible calculus: Ω⁰=O, Ωⁿ=∧ⁿΩ¹, alternating wedge, d²=0 and graded Leibniz. The one-form module need not be generated by scalar differentials. At λ=1, the comparison must identify actual additive operators, horizontal maps, exterior extensions, curvature and restrictions on that same calculus.

Checked both `CR.1/integrable-connection` in the CR.0 packet and the current CrystallineCohomology package, §CR.1.3, **Modules with integrable connection and their de Rham complexes**. The ring clause assumes a surjection Ω¹_(B/A)→Ω¹ with descending exterior derivative. The sheaf clause is on the small crystalline site, with a PD base and the stated p-nilpotence hypotheses. The tensor, dual and base-change API there stays within those scopes.

A one-point example separates the interfaces. Let O=Q, Ω¹=Qω, Ω²=0, scalar differential zero and E=Q. Then D(q)=qω satisfies ordinary Leibniz and is flat, while D(1)=ω≠0. But Ω¹_(Q/Q)=0, so the required Kähler surjection cannot exist. Pinned `KaehlerDifferential.subsingleton_of_surjective` and the package's proved no-surjection example certify that algebraic obstruction.

[Stacks Remark 60.6.8, tag 07I0](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07I0) uses the quotient calculus; [§60.15, Lemma 60.15.1, tag 07J5](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/07J5) supplies the crystalline connection of a crystal. Read both statements and proofs. Neither supplies the general-site export.

**Required owner correction:** export the additive sheaf connection for the supplied calculus, balanced exterior extension, O-linear curvature for constant parameter, horizontal arrows, restriction/gluing and differential-site pullback. Then reconcile H.0's request and its identity-on-operators unit fibre. Retain PD and quasi-nilpotence hypotheses on crystal comparisons. Narrowing the consumer to universal-generated forms would discard its separating example. These edits belong to the supplier, outside this issue's permitted files.

## R3 — Ordinary finite Rees sheaves are still not exported

**Blocking; unchanged mathematical gap.** H.0 requires a bounded decreasing locally split subbundle filtration of a finite locally free sheaf E, with finite locally free grades. The owner must supply the lattice Σ_p FᵖE t⁻ᵖ inside E[t,t⁻¹], independently of a chosen splitting, with restriction/descent, filtered-map, quotient and tensor laws. Its ordinary maps are:

- Rees/(t) ≃ ⊕_p grᵖE, sending e t⁻ᵖ to its graded class;
- Rees/(t−1) ≃ E, sending e t⁻ᵖ to e;
- Rees[t⁻¹] ≃ E[t,t⁻¹], induced by the lattice inclusion.

Checked the DD.1 packet and the current DerivedDeRhamCohomology package, §§1.12–1.15. They provide enhanced coherent filtered diagrams, derived cofibres, a graded derived Rees equivalence, inversion and completeness. They do not state this ordinary finite sheaf lattice, its fibre at one or the required descent/quotient coherence. Their Day tensor is also an enhanced construction; its existence alone does not supply the ordinary comparison maps.

Read Bhatt's [Prismatic F-gauges notes](https://www.math.ias.edu/~bhatt/teaching/mat549f22/lectures.pdf), §2.2.1, Proposition 2.2.6, printed p.16, and Remark 2.2.8, printed p.17. The proposition is the derived equivalence; the remark identifies its bounded finite-projective specialization. Constructing the ordinary sheaf maps and proving descent from that local specialization remains work. Current Tau Ceti `reesAlgebra.grade` and `mem_grade_iff` describe ideal-power monomials in R[X], not this filtered module sheaf.

**Required owner correction:** supply the lattice and the three maps above, including restriction, tensor and quotient naturality and comparison with the enhanced interface. H.0 then transports t∇ with relative dt=0: the zero fibre is the graded Higgs field, the one fibre the ordinary connection, and inversion permits division by t. Preserve the separate unbounded period-lattice/Tate contract. A local split basis and successful Laurent-polynomial tests do not establish these sheaf maps. Supplier packet edits are outside this review's scope.
