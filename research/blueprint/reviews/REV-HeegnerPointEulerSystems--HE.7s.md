## R1: norm-family signatures need revision

Howard Lemmas 2.3.2–2.3.3 and the weaker-torsion/class-number adaptation in CGLS Theorem 4.1.1 were independently read. Their arithmetic existence and compatibility statements are sound as recorded in the packet. The suggested algebraic interface fails to retain data and hypotheses that the existing additive-group and additive-map types already express.

**Projection obstruction.** The construction `universalNormFamily` accepts an arbitrary family of projections π, an endomorphism Φ and a family of initial points. Its level-zero API asserts πₙ(Qₙ)=Φ(Pₙ) without a lift-existence premise. Take both groups to be ℤ, every πₙ to be zero, Φ the identity and Pₙ=1. The API then asserts 0=1. The same declaration is promoted as `universal-norm-level-zero`, so its node cannot be verified independently of the construction.

**Auxiliary norm obstruction.** The construction takes neither the auxiliary trace maps nor their Hecke scalars. The trace API quantifies over arbitrary such maps and scalars afterward. With π and Φ the identity and Pₙ=1, the level-zero identity forces every Qₙ=1. Taking an auxiliary trace to be the identity and its scalar to be zero again gives 1=0. A single family cannot satisfy every trace system that can be supplied after its construction. The auxiliary example also asserts trace zero without supplying a permitted-prime condition, a zero-scalar premise or the required trace compatibility; its comment does not constrain the binders.

**Transition obstruction.** The corestriction API takes arbitrary projections and transitions without their commutative-square compatibility. The separate theorem `stabilized_corestriction` accepts arbitrary y and corestriction maps without tying y to `stabilizedPoint` or supplying the arithmetic norm recurrence. Constant y=1 and zero corestriction give 0=1. The `universalNormFamily_choice` signature proves the elementary zero-projection fact for a difference, but does not express the homogeneous auxiliary norm relations promised by its packet API.

Protocol §13 allows an arithmetic condition that cannot yet be stated to be omitted honestly. This review does not reject numerical theorem prototypes merely because the actual fields, Selmer groups, characteristic ideals or main-conjecture hypotheses are unavailable in the baseline. R1 concerns compatibility and lift data **already expressible in the supplied algebraic types**. The omission prevents these API signatures and the auxiliary test from representing the construction in the packet. The comments and new gap now identify this limitation explicitly; comments alone do not repair it.

A revision must design coherent supplied inverse-system data, include the actual family of transition and auxiliary maps before making the choice, and obtain lift existence from the compactness/norm argument. It must connect the stabilized trace theorem to the constructed family and express the homogeneous choice relations. Do not repair this by inserting the desired conclusions into opaque Prop-valued fields. This is a coordinated interface design, so the review leaves the three affected nodes unverifiable rather than inventing a replacement construction.
