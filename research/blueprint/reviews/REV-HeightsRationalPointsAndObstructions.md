## Mathematical corrections

RP.0 keeps the native quotient of real functions by uniformly bounded functions. Conrad Definition 8.1, pp.27–29, requires more than Mathlib's admissible-absolute-value class: the place classes are infinite, inequivalent and nontrivial, with excellent discrete valuation rings in the nonarchimedean case. Relative logarithmic heights retain `totalWeight`; absolute heights divide it out. The imaginary-part degree argument uses the Galois-stable ordered differences of conjugates divided by `2i`, not an assertion that the imaginary part lies in the original number field. The canonical-height torsion kernel uses one bounded-degree Northcott set and does not depend on Mordell–Weil. The unhalved polarization is twice Tau Ceti's elliptic pairing. For a constant family with Segre height `h_A+h_S`, the canonical-height error includes `−h_S`, and the duplication error includes `−3h_S`. DGH Appendix A pp.36–40 and Remark 3.2 p.14 require normal-base generic-trivial bundle descent and arbitrary-dimensional compactification/resolution; a curve-only supplier does not prove them.

The local-functions target now requires continuous metrics on proper analytifications and admissible placewise families; bare continuity on algebraic points does not justify uniform bounded differences. The product formula kills principal logarithms, not arbitrary constants. Müller–Stoll Remark 3.1 and Proposition 3.2 pp.3–4 check the elliptic normalization: converting their local function gives the `(O)`-normalized duplication term `(1/4) log|Δ|`. This elliptic check does not verify the general Néron-function construction.

RP.1 distinguishes fppf Kummer theory from the étale case and applies Lang's theorem to the connected special fibre of the abelian scheme, rather than to its finite torsion subgroup. The torsor for an isogeny `A→B` is that map itself. Conrad §7 pp.23–27 proves Lang–Néron before the later height-kernel result; the former cannot use Theorem 9.15 circularly. Hom/Albanese and étale-H¹ finiteness remain exact requests. Original `K/k`-trace zero suffices for the rational-point kernel over K; assertions on all algebraic points require geometric trace zero, including positive characteristic Manin–Mumford. A quadratic isotrivial twist explains the difference. A two-covering class in H¹(J[2]) differs from its image in H¹(J): a rational Pic¹ point kills the latter, whereas zero of W[2] requires an odd unordered factorization. Irreducibility alone is insufficient (`x⁶−2` has quadratic-conjugate cubic factors). BGW Theorem 5 supplies the empty Selmer-set example; its Theorems 10–11 address Brauer–Manin failures. Integral two-isogeny examples retain projective points at infinity.

RP.2 corrects the topology of general adelic points following Conrad §4 pp.9–10: use the integral-model direct limit. A global open immersion need not induce an adelic embedding; the idele-to-adele inclusion is a continuous injection whose topology is finer. The quaternion `(t,−1)` example belongs on Gm over ℝ, not on ℙ¹, where it is ramified at 0 and infinity. Local constancy is for a fixed Brauer class; it does not make evaluation into a discrete Hom group continuous for arbitrary infinite Br(X). Finite support is made uniform on an integral-model neighborhood. Azumaya versus cohomological Brauer comparison retains Gabber's ample-line-bundle hypothesis. Zero-cycle rational equivalence requires Brauer reciprocity on proper curves. The product-norm matrix includes both the per-class sum over places and the per-place sum over classes (Skorobogatov–Sofos §5.2 pp.722–724). Complete-intersection vanishing needs the Picard/H²(μ_n) Lefschetz comparison. Compact local evaluation fibres give finite partitions; a certified algorithm requires additional terminating computation contracts. The corresponding planet is now “Brauer–Manin test”.

RP.3 consistently twists by the inverse class, `[Z^τ]=[Z]−τ`. Harpaz–Wittenberg's parameter is `σ=−τ`, giving `ε_τ=ε₀+PT(τ)`; the corrected locators are (2.4) p.9, Lemma 2.4 p.10 and the unnumbered functional pp.10–11. The Selmer set of a trivial torsor agrees with Sha only when adelic points exist. The undefined elementary-obstruction shortcut is replaced with the explicit type obstruction `δ(id)` and its requested exact sequence. A rational Picard-scheme point is distinguished from a descended line bundle. Universal torsors, étale–Brauer/descent comparison, and the Cassels–Tate/Brauer comparison retain their deep proof gaps. Genus-one curves with a marked Jacobian are classified by H¹(E); forgetting the marking requires an Aut(E) quotient. The two quartic examples are assigned to the correct elliptic curves. Product torsor examples use `Aⁿ×Gmⁿ→Aⁿ`.

RP.4 corrects finite-S monotonicity for integral points, characteristic zero for de Franchis on all maps, and the Kodaira–Parshin branch/genus calculation. Szpiro's full-branch construction (§2.3 pp.86–87) has genus `g₁=1+2^(2g−1)(4g−3)`, hence 41 at g=2; it is not Milne's separate two-point variant. Halving the branch divisor uses Jac(C₁), with safe extension-degree bound `2^(2g₁)`. The upward Faltings and Lawrence–Venkatesh dependencies are removed: RP.4 imports the independent Vojta–Bombieri Mordell assembly from RP.5, whose proof no longer imports RP.4. Shafarevich in the Parshin route remains a conditional input. Corvaja–Zannier's Siegel proof needs the number-field S-adic systems corollary of DT.2, not just a rational-only theorem. Function-field Mordell retains a separate primary-proof gap.

RP.5 preserves scheme-theoretic stabilizers and characteristic zero where smoothness and scheme fibres are used. Quotients by positive-dimensional abelian subvarieties need a scheme quotient, not merely a finite-subgroup theorem or an algebraic-space slogan (Conrad Theorem 3.7 p.6). The controlled-complement bound in GGK Lemma 7.4 pp.43–44 requires X to generate A. Full finite-rank Mordell–Lang implies finiteness off the Ueno locus; that isolated finiteness assertion is not declared an equivalent decomposition theorem. Bombieri §10 Theorems 1–2 pp.637–639 gives an effective high-point count with a curve-dependent norm-ratio constant; it does not give an effective bound for all heights. The product/Roth lemma stays a deep proof gap. Mumford's inequality yields doubling in cones of angle cosine greater than 3/4, followed by Northcott for small points. Translation-coset height tests require infinite order in A/B, not merely in A. Equidistribution requires a generic sequence with no infinite subsequence in a proper closed subset, a field of definition keeping Galois orbits in X, and admissible semipositive metrics; a constant torsion sequence fails. The vanishing-form example excludes a form zero on all of A.

RP.6 separates geometric Abel–Jacobi point and tangent separation, degree-one Picard classes and actual line bundles, and the choice of a finite extension when no base class exists. Mordell–Lang is applied to `ι(C)(K̄)∩Γ`. Genus-one Brauer–Manin sufficiency retains its finite-Sha hypothesis. Vojta's rational-point conjecture is Conjecture 14.6(b), p.160; the bounded-degree algebraic-point/discriminant variant is not silently substituted. Poonen Conjecture 9.2.27 p.267 supports the nonemptiness formulation for rationally connected varieties; the density formulation is separately located in HW 2024 Conjecture 1.1 p.787.
