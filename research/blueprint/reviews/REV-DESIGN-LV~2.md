## Source-issue verdicts

| Entry | Independent reason |
| --- | --- |
| `E2` | The standing S excludes every place above p. Good reduction makes p-adic cohomology crystalline there, but does not make it unramified. For example H²_et(P¹,Q_p)=Q_p(−1) is ramified at p although P¹ has good reduction. The finiteness argument allows any fixed finite ramification set: enlargement to T is harmless. |
| `E3` | Confirmed on LV p. 12: the local norm-power identity is on units. For K=ℚ, η=χ_cycl⁻¹ of weight 2, local reciprocity sends a uniformizer to an element on which the cyclotomic character is trivial, whereas its norm squared is p²; a finite-order correction cannot absorb p². |
| `E7` | Friendliness ensures K_v/Q_p is unramified, but does not imply p>2. The quoted estimate for flat sections is ∣z∣<p^(−1/(p−1)). At p=2, points with ∣z∣=1/2 in a residue disk are on its boundary, so that estimate does not justify §6’s assertion that the analytic domain contains the entire residue disk. Subdivision into disks modulo 4 avoids this issue. The original claim that other primes above p must be good for crystallinity at v was incorrect: v∉S already supplies good reduction at v. |
| `E8` | size_v counts the elements lying in Frobenius orbits of size < 8 (Definition 5.2), that is, the elements killed by T^i − 1 for some i ⩽ 7; i = 8 is not needed. It is also not available: the next step reads 'For every odd prime factor r of q − 1 we know that q_v^i is not congruent to 1 modulo r', and that is precisely condition (iii) imposed on v two pages earlier, that the class of q_v in (Z/r)^* has order at least 8. An order exactly 8 is allowed, and then q_v^8 ≡ 1 mod r, so (q_v^8 − 1) need not be prime to r and the conclusion 2⟨m_1, m_2⟩ = 0 fails for i = 8. With i ⩽ 7 every step is correct. |
| `E10` | Both W=0 and W=V are φ-stable and satisfy the printed inequality for every Lagrangian F. Consequently the literal bad locus is the entire parameter space. The printed lemma also allows A=∅, so read literally it is vacuous rather than false. Lemma 6.4 and the preceding sublemma use nonzero proper W. |
| `E11` | y' is a point of Y', not of Y, so 'Y − y'' is not defined. The preceding sentence says the cover Z_{y'} → Y is ramified exactly at π(y'), and part (i) attaches to y' a conjugacy class of surjections π_1^geom(Y − y, *) ↠ G with y = π(y'); so the group meant is the one of the punctured curve Y − π(y'). |
| `E12` | The fibration is the second map of (7.5), (Y² − Δ, y) → (Y, y_0), (y, y') ↦ y', whose fibre over y_0 is Y − {y_0}. Its long exact sequence reads π_2(Y) → π_1(Y − {y_0}) → π_1(Y² − Δ) → π_1(Y) → π_0, so injectivity on the left is governed by π_2 of the base Y; π_2 of the fibre enters the sequence one step earlier and is irrelevant to it. The conclusion is correct because Y is a closed surface of genus ⩾ 2, hence aspherical, so π_2(Y) = 0 — and in any case only the middle exactness, which gives the normality of Γ in Γ̃^geom that the argument goes on to use, is needed. |
| `E13` | A simple curve bounding a disk away from the branch point is permitted by the paper’s definition. Its q inverse images bound disks, so all q homology classes are zero, contradicting the claimed independence and primitive rank q−1. For a nonseparating curve one can choose the one-face CW decomposition used in the proof; the lifted edge classes are independent and transfer supplies the single relation after primitive projection. The required distinguishing curves can be chosen nonseparating, including curves with q-cycle monodromy; they are not all liftable curves in the paper’s special sense. |
| `E14` | The lemma is deduced from Lemma 8.2, which needs e nonseparating (finding E13), and it fails without that hypothesis. If e bounds a disk in Y − {y} then Mon(D_e^M) = Id and the rank is 0, while Cov(e) = 1; if e is nonseparating with Cov(e) a q-cycle then k = 1 and the rank is again 0. Two different conjugacy classes give the same rank, so the rank does not determine the class. With e nonseparating the three possible cycle types in Aff(q) — (1^q), (q) and (1, r, …, r) — have k = q, 1 and 1 + (q − 1)/r cycles respectively, all distinct, and the lemma holds. The applications, in the proof of Lemma 8.9, are to nonseparating curves. |
| `E22` | For i=j, the two projected operators are identical and cannot have fixed spaces of different dimensions. The printed hypothesis is therefore impossible when N≥1, making the implication vacuous and unusable in Lemma 8.7. Pairwise distinct factors are intended. |
| `E23` | The respective closures are subschemes of projective spaces over the opposite fields from those paired with them in the sentence. The proof constructs the common ideal over K and gives the correctly matched base changes. |
| `E24` | For a liftable e the multiplier of Cov(e) generates F_q^×. A dual curve meeting e once has its abelian multiplier changed by this generator under D_e. Conjugation in Aff(q) preserves multipliers, so D_e does not stabilize the cover. The preceding lifting formula instead applies to the (q−1)-st power. If u is the primitive projection of [e+], then the projection of [e−] is −u and the lift acts by x↦x+q⟨u,x⟩u, up to the intersection-sign convention. |
| `E25` | Confirmed on LV p. 33: transported forms must be scalar multiples of the common symplectic form, requiring φ to be a semilinear similitude. An arbitrary invertible linear map need not preserve the Lagrangian condition, e.g. a nonuniform diagonal scaling in a symplectic four-space. No determinant or slope inference replaces this hypothesis. |
| `E26` | V_v has rank 2d over E_{0,v}; its first Hodge step and its Lagrangians have rank d. The letter g denotes the genus of the base curve and need not equal d: the Kodaira–Parshin formula gives d=(q−1)(g−1/2), for example d=3 when g=2,q=3. |
| `E29` | Iterating b_n=a_{n+1}+αb_{n+1} m times gives α^m. For m=1, g=x and f=x(x−α), the printed formula gives b₀=1−α instead of 0. The corrected remainder still tends to zero, so the theorem and limiting proof survive. |
| `E30` | Reverse the orientation of a nonseparating curve: the twist is unchanged and its nonzero primitive homology class is negated. Equality of the rank-one symplectic operators conversely identifies the primitive line up to sign. This sentence is not needed for the surjectivity theorem. |
