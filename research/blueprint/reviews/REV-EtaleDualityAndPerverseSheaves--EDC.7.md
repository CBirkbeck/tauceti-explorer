## Corrections and the real gaps

The general fixed-scheme/normal-cone comparison in Varshavsky Lemma 5.1 is false. Over an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from 2, let g(u,v)=(−u,−v) on X=𝔸² and Z=V(uv). Then N_ZX=Spec k[u,v,t]/(uv) and g(t)=t. Its fixed scheme is 𝔸¹, while X^g=Z^g=Spec k and N_(Z^g)(X^g)=Spec k. Both D=µ₂ and Z are reduced. The proof treats exact vector-space coinvariants as the algebra quotient defining a fixed scheme. This contradicts the version of record too (Compositio 159 (2023), §5, pp.995–997), not merely the preprint.

The reduced-point cone implication at a singular isolated point also fails. On X=Spec k[x,y,z]/(x³+yz), g=(x,−y,−z), the fixed scheme is Spec k[x]/(x³), supported at the origin. The tangent cone at the reduced origin is Spec k[x,y,z]/(yz), with fixed line Spec k[x]. This is a tame involution in a torus. These computations refute the cone statements, not the local-term equality.

The finite-order theorem can be repaired without those statements. Take the full fixed subscheme Z=Fix(g). On a g-invariant affine neighbourhood, the fixed ideal I is generated by the nontrivial eigenspaces of A. Tameness supplies the finite spectral projectors. Since A/I has trivial action, I/I² has no trivial-character part. The associated graded algebra is generated in degree one, so its fixed-scheme quotient kills all positive-degree generators and retains exactly A/I. Thus Fix(N_Zg)=Z. For scalar-twisted cone maps only reciprocals of nontrivial m-th roots can be bad; there are finitely many. The new full-fixed-subscheme lemma proves the no-almost condition. Theorem 4.10 then identifies local terms with those on Z. An isolated nonreduced fixed component has the étale site of its reduced point, giving its stalk trace. This repairs HKW Proposition 5.6.2’s finite-type input (v4 pp.61–62); perfection and trace transport remain the Part II task.

For a general semisimple element, the smooth isolated-point application is valid using the requested smooth fixed-locus tangent theorem. The arbitrary singular application is still unverified: separatedness alone was not used to invent a torus-invariant affine neighbourhood on a nonnormal scheme. Node EDC.8/finite-order-local-terms records exactly this remaining scope.
