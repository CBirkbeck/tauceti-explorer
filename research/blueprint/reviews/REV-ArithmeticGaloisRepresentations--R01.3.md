## Corrections

1. **Schur-index multiplicities.** The proof of Brumer–Kramer Theorem 5.5 on p. 242 has `dim W_j=m_j(p−1)p^i`, with `m_j` the constituent’s Schur index. The original sketch dropped that factor. The corrected sketch sums the `m_j` into the carry coefficients, applies the constituent bound over the fixed field of wild inertia and cancels the tame index. The theorem’s final bound is unchanged.
2. **Source locators and scope.** Serre 1961 Proposition 3 is the quotient theorem and Proposition 4 the tower formula (pp. 127–128). The digit function is defined in Brumer–Kramer §1, p. 227; it is not equation (5.4). Serre–Tate Theorem 2 states integral independent traces; the endomorphism comparison gives characteristic polynomials, and Corollary 2 supplies the potential-good criterion (pp. 496–498). The reciprocity comparison now distinguishes Serre’s proalgebraic construction from ordinary local-field reciprocity. Removed the unsuccessful query suffix from the public Serre–Tate URL; its PDF hash is unchanged.
3. **Direct dependencies and API justification.** The Artin definition now names its integrality import. The residual API names coefficient extension, semisimplification and averaging, and explains why inertia invariants give an inequality while the wild term remains equal. This applies to finite-image residual representations without assuming an integral characteristic-zero lift. The existing geometric gap now includes the semistable monodromy and Euler-characteristic inputs needed by its API.
4. **Tests and Lean agreement.** The tame unipotent test specifies the action on F² and derives the fixed-space dimension instead of assuming it. The break-invariant theorem now includes the right-limit formula. Residual product tests explicitly treat excluded-place numbers as unused formal exponent data.
5. **Ownership and auxiliary primes.** The WD conductor comparison consumes the invariant-space identity already supplied by R01.2. The elliptic bound uses ℓ=3 at p=2 and ℓ=2 at p=3, with their exact finite-field root conditions; the p≥5 case uses Swan vanishing. The current-library note now names the proved finite upper quotient theorem and wild-inertia declaration, keeping them as existing supplier imports.
