## Mathematical corrections

1. **Analytic invariance and perfect schemes (D7).** Count reduced geometric fibres, allowing nilpotents in the finite fibre, and identify integral valuation subrings by integral closure for finite maps. Remove the claim that topology alone determines an analytic diamond. Require a nonempty source in the minimal-point lemma (SW20 Lemma 18.3.2, p. 166). Closed schematic olivine subsets are cut out by open ideals, which need not be prime (GLE Proposition 2.36, p. 22); the whole spectrum of `F_p × F_p` is the countertest. Quotient ambient rings by the ideal generated by topologically nilpotent elements, with the integral closure of the image plus ring. Handle arbitrary closed-immersion ideals without assuming finite generation. Remove the forward cover-comparison dependency and add the construction as a direct input to that comparison. Distinguish open valuation-comparison loci from rational domains.
2. **Reduction and cutoff functors (D8).** Increasing the cutoff cardinal uses inverse-image extension, with restriction its right adjoint in the other direction; rename the planned API to `SchVSheaf.extend_fullyFaithful` (GLE Proposition 3.9, pp. 23–24). A nonperfect affine scheme is not a site object, but its restricted functor is a sheaf represented by its perfection. Repair the quotient plus ring in the non-analytic locus (GLE Propositions 1.19 and 3.18, pp. 9,25). Use a totally disconnected cover and descent before applying the parent's local closed-subobject classification.
3. **Formal neighbourhoods and étale lifting (D9).** Restore the underlined continuous-map sheaves in the formal-neighbourhood pullback (GLE §1.1, p. 7, Definition 4.18, p. 31). Membership constrains the specialization image of the whole perfectoid test space, not one point; its underlying set can be strictly smaller than the specialization inverse image. The factorization API is for maps of prekimberlites. Ordinary étale lifts are not unique: `R⁺` and `R⁺ × R⁺[1/ϖ]` have the same reduction. Use completed formally étale lifts and request the nonnoetherian henselian/nil-ideal input from SF.0 (GLE Lemma 4.25, p. 33). For formal-scheme prekimberlites, obtain formal separatedness from the monomorphic counit/diagonal criterion; do not assume every formal scheme is formally p-adic.
4. **Recovery (D10).** Formal schemes locally formally of finite type can have non-adic structure maps, as `Spf O_E[[q]]` with its `(π,q)`-adic topology shows. State continuity of morphisms explicitly. In triple fullness, prove that ideal-of-definition generators map into the radical of an ideal of definition using specialization compatibility and closed-point density; finite generation then proves continuity. The existing R1 analytification theorem is over a field, so request relative analytification over `Spa(O,O)` rather than silently changing its base. Request the pseudorigid Jacobson and rational-localization inputs from R0; correct the locator for Lourenço Proposition 4.6 to p. 9. Use formal GAGA for morphisms between already algebraic proper schemes, avoiding unnecessary algebraization assumptions. Replace the inaccurate arbitrary-root-closure description of absolute weak normality by the equations in AGLR Definition 2.12, p. 12. The affine-line check uses the explicit nonalgebraic entire endomorphism `Σ π^{n²}T^n`; no claim about analytic automorphisms is needed.
5. **Facets and components (D11).** Defining facets are over characteristic-p `C` itself; smooth spaces over an untilt acquire such facets by the separate tilting argument (GLE Definition 4.50 and Proposition 4.51, p. 37). Add separatedness to the formal-scheme richness test and exclude the zero ring in the non-flatness test. The connected-components proof now states the closed-specialization condition, invokes the rich quotient theorem to descend saturated clopens, and uses local connectedness of a locally noetherian space to pass from clopens to actual components. The formal-scheme application satisfies the extra condition because its reduction has the same underlying space as a locally noetherian scheme. Its disc example now accounts for finite unramified coefficient extensions at non-rational closed points.
6. **Normality through tubes (D11).** Restrict the criterion to algebraically closed residue field and prove it by finite normalization and weak normality directly. A singleton closed fibre need not have a purely inseparable residue extension over a merely perfect field; the printed generality of AGLR Proposition 2.39, pp. 18–19, is false. The counterexample and proof boundary are detailed below.

## Two boundaries that matter downstream

For the normality criterion, take any nontrivial finite unramified extension `L/Q_p`, put `B = O_L` and `A = Z_p + pB`. This is a finite free, complete local `Z_p`-algebra and a domain, with generic field `L`; its normalization is `B`, strictly larger than `A`. Its conductor `pB` is radical in both rings, with residues `F_p` and `k_L`. Any reduced finite birational universal-homeomorphism modification embeds into `B`. Its residue subextension must be purely inseparable over `F_p`, hence trivial inside the separable field `k_L`; the intermediate ring is therefore contained in `A`. This verifies weak normality. The maximal-ideal and p-adic topologies agree, so the single closed-point tube is `Spa(L,O_L)`, which is connected. After an unramified base extension it splits. This proves E9 without assuming that a topological singleton implies a residue-field isomorphism.

Over algebraically closed residue field, normalization has a single point over every closed point by the decomposition of a completed finite algebra into local factors and nonemptiness of their tubes. The residue fields are then the base field. The finite-map Jacobson criterion gives universal injectivity on the special fibre, hence a universal homeomorphism, and weak normality forces equality with the normalization. A version using geometrically connected tubes over a perfect residue field is a follow-up question, not a proved target here.

For Gleason Lemma 4.55, Definition 4.52 only imposes local noetherianity of the underlying reduction topology. This differs from being a locally noetherian scheme. For example, take the perfect Hahn valued field with coefficient field `F_p` and divisible value group the lexicographically ordered direct sum of countably many copies of `Q`, and its valuation ring `V`. Its primes form `0 < P_1 < P_2 < ⋯ < m`. The open subscheme `Spec(V) \ {m}` has finite-chain open neighbourhoods, hence locally noetherian topology, but no closed points. This refutes the topological inference in the printed argument, not the kimberlite theorem itself: no rich-kimberlite counterexample is asserted. The corrected theorem assumes closed specializations. This agrees with the pre-existing hypothesis `hclosed` in the suggested Lean topological core. Perfectly finite-type reductions and the formal-scheme application satisfy it. The remaining generality is E10 and a precise gap.
