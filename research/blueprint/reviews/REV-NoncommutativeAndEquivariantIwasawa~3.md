## Revision-3 mathematical checks

The claimed removal of the old augmentation/radical leaf is justified by the actual proof outlines, rather than by a new citation:

- For a finite p-group Δ, choose a central element z of order p. The central ideal generated by z−1 has p-th power zero in characteristic p. Induction through Δ/〈z〉 gives `I_Δ^|Δ|=0`. Normality then gives the same bound for the ideal generated by Δ−1 in the larger completed group algebra. The integral counterexample is essential: z−1 is not thereby nilpotent over Z_p.
- For the two-sided prime radical, the finite sum of nilpotent ideals is nilpotent: in a long enough word, enough factors lie in one of the two-sided ideals. The ascending chain condition makes the sum of all such ideals finite. The quotient has no nonzero nilpotent ideal; a sequence with its next term in `a_k R a_k` gives the prime-ideal separation argument. This concerns nilpotent ideals, not the set of nilpotent elements. The matrix example detects that distinction.
- The central localization is injective because the original algebra is free over the central power-series domain. Nilpotent-radical contraction and extension therefore agree. The localized algebra is finite-dimensional, so a regular element is a unit. These facts give the regular-modulo-radical characterization used in the Ore proof.

The finite logarithm imports also have the required scope. ClassicalArithmeticCompletion §§7.46 and 7.62 provide the conjugacy-class logarithm and its integral commutator image; §7.73 identifies its determinant torsion kernel. For the scalar calculation in NE.6, the unramified Frobenius F is an automorphism and F−p is invertible on the finite-free Z_p-module O, which gives the scalar image and roots-of-unity kernel with the selected normalization. This does not prove the abelian image from Oliver's Theorem 6.6, identify every K₁-prime group with a determinant image, or prove semisimple SK₁ vanishing. Those obligations remain.

The added Ritter–Weiss discussion preserves the quantifiers. For a fixed smoothing element g, the smoothed Möbius sum satisfies the stated cocycle identity; multiplication by the invariant transfer preserves the trace ideal. Admissible g therefore form a subgroup, but that does not give a nontrivial element common to all conductor levels. In arXiv:1001.2091v3, Proposition 4, pp.5–6, needs one g for all test functions, whereas Proposition 5 and the special-cusp calculation, pp.6–11, choose conductor data for a given function. The indicated cyclotomic norms can tend to 1. The finite-conductor calculation and the published uniform theorem remain distinct, with obligation 19 retaining the missing proof audit. This review does not infer that the published theorem is false.

The remaining target specifications were checked with their hypotheses and uses. In particular, the full subgroup Möbius congruences retain multiplicities, completed-to-canonical descent has an actual comparison argument rather than an inverse completion map, and the shifted determinant formulas retain their boundaries, orders and cyclotomic normalization.
