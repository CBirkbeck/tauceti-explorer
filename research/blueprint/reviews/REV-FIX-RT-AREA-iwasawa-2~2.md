**/1.** [Morita, 1975](https://repository.dl.itc.u-tokyo.ac.jp/record/39763/files/jfs220209.pdf),
§1, Lemma 1 and Theorem 1, printed pp.255–256, supports extension of
the signed finite product to continuous unit-valued Gamma. The recurrence
factor is −x at units and −1 at nonunits. The packet's finite congruence
excludes the dyadic exponent-two case, and its precision buffer avoids
that case in the continuous construction. Fresh probes at p=2,3,5,7,
exponents 1 through 4 and starting arguments 0 through 15 pass all 240
allowed periods; 128 recurrence checks also pass. At p=2 the values at
1 and 5 reduce to 3 and 1 modulo four, detecting the false unrestricted
period-four statement. These computations establish no analytic theorem.

[Gross–Koblitz, 1979](https://www.sas.rochester.edu/mth/sites/doug-ravenel/otherpapers/gross_koblitz.pdf),
§1, equations (1.2), (1.5), (1.6) and Theorem 1.7, printed pp.570–571,
uses a negative Gauss sum and the root with pi^(p−1)=−p and
pi congruent to zeta−1 modulo (zeta−1)^2. The original theorem assumes
odd p and a nonzero character exponent. The packet keeps this source
range and treats the trivial-character endpoint separately; its negative
Gauss sum there is +1. The Robert/Dwork route retains its coefficient and
splitting inputs. Gamma continuity alone provides neither those inputs
nor global analyticity on the closed unit disc.

**/2.** [Zhao, 2022](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/1DF77ECEC0EE657089F2E26C0F8AA351/S0013091522000177a.pdf/sum_expressions_for_kubotaleopoldt_padic_lfunctions.pdf),
§4, Theorem 4.1 and equations (4.1)–(4.6), printed pp.470–473,
supports the L3-2 derivative contract. For primitive odd chi of conductor
N>1 prime to p, the derivative belongs to the even chi-omega branch. Its
Gamma-log sum uses chi(a), and the additional term is
−(1−chi(p)) L(chi,0) log_p(N). The additional term vanishes when
chi(p)=1. Zhao's §1.2, p.461, specifies the dyadic omega of conductor
four. Existing convergence and arithmetic nonvanishing obligations
remain separate.

**/3.** [Ertl–Niziol v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.01705v2),
§§2.1–2.2, pp.4–8, Proposition 2.1 and Theorems 2.2–2.3, distinguishes

undivided comparison with bounded torsion error and modified Tate lattice.
