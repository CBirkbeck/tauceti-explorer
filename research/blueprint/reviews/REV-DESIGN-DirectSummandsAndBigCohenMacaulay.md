## Mathematical and interface corrections

1. **Arbitrary modules and completion.** Corrected the direction of the balanced-completion discussion and its quotient example. The completion result is stated by André §4.1 p.85 and BMPSTWW §2.2 p.12, but neither the finite-module depth theorem in R03.3 nor the existence of Koszul complexes proves the arbitrary-module result. G3 now names both this proof and the Ext-colimit/Čech comparison. The Lean equivalence with Mathlib's regular-sequence convention now assumes `IsNoetherianRing R`; finite generation of the maximal ideal is needed to compare the maximal-ideal and parameter-ideal nonvanishing conditions.
2. **Flatness and obstruction arguments.** In the Tor induction choose a maximal regular sequence in P of length h=ht(P), independently of the Tor index i. The complete-intersection quotient has P as an associated prime, supplying the embedding of A/P that the exact sequence needs. E18 records this correction in addition to the source's module-name errors. A finite free split algebra has projective quotient, without a claim that the quotient is free. Degreewise Artin–Rees, the bounded p-torsion in H², and finite free models now explain the complex comparisons actually used in DS.0 and DS.3. The annular limit argument uses finite filtrations of each perfect dual and the relevant almost lim¹; it does not assume an unstated uniform theorem for arbitrary complexes.
3. **Cohen presentation and characteristic cases.** Membership of the defining equation in (p,X)² does not make its constant coefficient vanish. If c is that coefficient, normalise using the unit 1−c/p and replace f₀ by (f₀−c)/(1−c/p). This preserves the coefficient map and the ideal generated by p−f. For a ring with p-torsion, choose a maximal-dimensional component first and branch on its characteristic; nonnilpotence of p does not force the chosen domain quotient to have mixed characteristic. Choose a component again after residue extension. The characteristic-p absolute-integral-closure proof applies Huneke–Lyubeznik Theorem 2.1 at every finite intermediate extension, not just at the original ring.
4. **Pointed partial modifications.** An arbitrary module has no coefficient 1 for a bare polynomial variable. The construction now carries (M,e), with relation F=u−Σx_j(eX_j), scalar-polynomial multiples of F, and maps satisfying α(e)=1. Evaluation then kills F and preserves the distinguished element. This makes the denominator lemma well typed; its exponent remains ND+D+N. The API adds induced pointed maps and specifies identity, composition and determination by monomial generators. Its unit-c check states the hypothesised colon equality rather than claiming full regularity of a parameter sequence.
5. **A false fixed-relation comparison is isolated.** For R=k⟦x⟧, T=R[t]/(xt), and the relation xt=0 with no formal variables, every degree-bounded partial quotient is T/Rt. It retains t². The full algebra modification T/Tt kills t². Thus Hochster 2002 §4.1 p.8's fixed-relation colimit assertion does not identify the module colimit with the full algebra quotient. The corrected API states M[X]/F·R[X], adds this counterexample as a test, and records the required finite-bad-witness comparison as G4. This finding does not refute Hochster Theorem 4.2 or the known existence theorems. The regular-algebra example has an evaluation retraction; no free-complement claim survives. For instance R=k⟦x,y⟧ with relation x·0=0 and degree bound one gives R ⊕ (R/(x))X, whose complement is not free.
6. **Pure maps and relative residue extension.** Completion now tests all finite modules over B̂, by reducing modulo powers of the maximal ideal and applying Krull intersection; checking only modules descending from B would not prove purity. Separately extending the two residue fields does not construct a compatible faithfully flat comparison square. R2 precisely requests the common completed Cohen extension, its faithful flatness, and preservation of purity and injectivity. Both completion/descent and weak functoriality cite it directly. R1 still requests the ramified, several-discriminant Kummer-flatness input that RA.5 presently lists only as a hypothesis note.
7. **Derived splitting and arbitrary proper bases.** A finite affine Čech cover gives the cohomological bound for a proper scheme over a Noetherian affine base; no finite Krull-dimension hypothesis is silently imposed. Current StableReduction Layer 2 already owns general proper coherence. In the perfectoid proper argument, Spf(A) for an arbitrary perfectoid A need not be an admissible finite-type formal model, so the AdicSpacesPartII R2 Raynaud/specialisation imports were removed. The scheme blow-up domination supplied by SF.4 and rational charts with bounded ratios give the factorisation in Bhatt's Proposition 6.2, footnote 8, p.11. Global sections need integrality here, without importing Noetherian finiteness over A. EnhancedDerivedSheaves supplies ringed-topos pushforward.
8. **Characteristic-p and characteristic-zero derived steps.** The cohomology-killing proof uses finite Frobenius-stable modules, monic equations, gluing by Bhatt 2012 Proposition 3.1, and the finite-amplitude iteration of Lemma 2.2. A finite elliptic multiplication-by-p map replaces an affine test that confused ordinary and local cohomology. The killed map lifts RΓ(X) to H⁰(Y) through the truncation triangle; its unit is preserved, so the finite-algebra splinter retraction gives the derived splitting. Kovács' Theorem 1 assumes a rational source; Theorem 2 treats projective complex varieties with rational singularities on both sides. Those exact hypotheses replace the broader attribution. G2 now specifies the resolution, vanishing, projective reduction and approximation bridges still required.
9. **Locators and attribution.** Corrected Matlis references to Hochster's lecture notes Theorem 5.1 p.19 and Theorem 5.4 p.21; Huneke–Lyubeznik Theorem 2.1 starts on p.2; Heitmann–Ma Theorem 1.6 spans pp.3–4. The historical Frobenius reference is Hochster 1973 Theorem 2 pp.31–32, whereas p.30 contains the inverse-limit/coset argument used elsewhere. André labels the direct summand statement Conjecture 0.1.1 and proves it in the paper. Removed the two unread HH95 node citations; the read secondary existence statements and the self-contained descent argument remain, with G1 honestly open. HH95 remains a historical bibliography entry explicitly marked unread.
