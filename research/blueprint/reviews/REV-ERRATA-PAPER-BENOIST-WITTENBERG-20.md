## E1: confirm the counterexample and specify the repair

Take the smooth projective completion E of y²=x³−x over R. Its affine cubic has no repeated root (discriminant 4, or gcd with its derivative equal to 1). At infinity the sole point is [0:1:0], where the derivative in Z of Y²Z−X³+XZ² equals 1. Thus the curve is smooth, proper, geometrically integral and has genus 1. It has a real point. Coherent H²(E,O_E) vanishes by dimension, while H⁰_G(E(C),Z)=Z is generated by the equivariant class of E. Hence the real integral Hodge conjecture for 1-cycles holds.

A reduced geometrically irreducible closed curve contained in E must equal E, so the even-genus condition fails. On the other hand, evaluation at the real point splits Br(R)→Br(E). In the low-degree Hochschild–Serre sequence

0 → Pic(E) → Pic(E_C)^G → Br(R) → Br(E),

the obstruction map is therefore zero, and Pic(E)≅Pic(E_C)^G. This remains an isomorphism on 2-primary torsion. The claimed equivalence is false under the printed dimension-one hypothesis. A small symbolic check verified the discriminant, gcd and projective derivatives; the cohomological argument does not rest on this computation.

For part (ii), **dim(X)≥2 or X(R)=∅** is a sufficient correction. In the real-point case, Pic descent is always an isomorphism and Proposition 3.9 supplies the even-genus curve in dimension at least two. Its even-genus construction uses a blow-up at a real point and adjunction; the dimension hypothesis is explicit. In the no-real-point case, the surjectivity criterion in Theorem 3.3 and the cycle interpretation in Theorem 3.6 give the assertion without Proposition 3.9. In dimension one, Lemma 3.7 directly identifies the Picard-surjectivity condition with even genus. Its proof uses the Brauer obstruction sequence and Riemann–Roch: for even genus every torsion invariant line bundle descends; for odd genus an invariant non-descending degree-zero class produces a non-descending class of order 2 by subtracting a norm after division by 2.

The JSON clarifies that this restriction concerns the equivalence in **part (ii)**. It does not force a stronger hypothesis in part (i), nor does the counterexample affect the stated surface and higher-dimensional applications. In the remaining case of a real curve with real points, the Picard condition is automatic and the even-genus condition is exactly the parity of the curve itself. Verdict: **confirmed, error / a stated result**.

## E2: confirm the excluded zeroth power

At k=0, choose X=Spec R. The class is 1 in H⁰_G(pt,Z)=Z, not torsion. It is algebraic and satisfies Definition 1.19: only its degree-zero component appears and Sq⁰ is the identity. Yet X(R) is nonempty, disproving the stated equivalence in that case.

For positive even k the definition in §1.1.2 makes ω^{2k} a 2-torsion class. If X(R) is nonempty, §1.2.5 identifies its degree-zero restriction component with 1. Definition 1.19 demands α₀=Sq^{−k}(α_k)=0, since −k<0, so the condition fails. If X(R)=∅ the condition is vacuous. This independently checks both directions of the proposed repair **k>0 even**. Retain **misprint / affects nothing** for the intended positive-degree argument, while explicitly recording the false literal endpoint assertions.
