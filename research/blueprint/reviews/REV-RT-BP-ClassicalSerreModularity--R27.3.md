## /1 — stable lattice in the order-three type: confirmed

The generic dihedral induction's split residual API does not realize a nonzero residual Steinberg inertia extension. The prescribed-type lifting theorem needs a compatible stable integral lattice, not only a characteristic-zero type and Frobenius trace. DP's compatibility definition and its Lemma 2.3 were checked directly, including their displayed matrices.

I independently verified the lattice calculation over Z[z]/(z²+z+1). Put π=z−1. For the standard basis, D0=diag(z,z²) and F0 is the swap matrix. For the lattice generated by e1+e2 and πe2, D1=(z,0;z,z²) and F1=(1,π;0,−1). The change-of-basis matrix P=(1,0;1,π) satisfies D0P=PD1 and F0P=PF1. Both satisfy D³=F²=1 and FDF⁻¹=D², with Frobenius trace zero.

Modulo π, D0 is identity and D1=(1,0;1,1). Their inertia invariant spaces have dimensions two and one, so these reductions cannot be identified. This is a local discriminator, not a counterexample to the global modularity theorem.

**Correct repair:** tie `dihedralType_residual` to its standard lattice, then require an adapted stable lattice for the specified full residual inertia module in `orderThreeType_reduction`. Retain unramified twists and distinguish zero/nonzero extension classes. R08.6/export-away-from-p plans the level-two local construction with the Frobenius relation; R24.3/required-lift-types(4) and KW I Theorem 5.1(4), at p=3,q=2, provide the scoped global interface. ## /2 — dyadic conductor propagation: confirmed

The node's equality with the original prime-to-p conductor is false in the p=2, weight-four branch. The original prime-to-2 conductor has exponent zero. The first weight-two lift has unramified semisimple WD part and nonzero rank-one monodromy; its exponent is 2−dim ker N=1. The theorem allows this when r≥1 and explicitly excludes it when r=0.

Let A be the first system's dyadic exponent. For odd p, A equals the original exponent and is ≤r by minimality at 2. For p=2,k=2, A=0; for p=2,k=4, A=1≤r. Reduction at p′ gives an exponent at most A. The second lift is minimal at 2 relative to that intermediate residual representation. Final reduction at s can decrease the exponent again. Thus the required conclusion is a chain of inequalities ending at ≤r, not equality with the original conductor.

**Correct repair:** record this case split and each comparison through R01.3's monodromy/reduction-conductor interface and the existing compatible-system imports. Add the boundary case p=2,k=4,r=1 and permit conductor drop. Theorem 3.4's conclusion survives unchanged. Exact arithmetic checks verified nonzero nilpotent rank-one monodromy and 528 permitted exponent chains for r=0 through 8.
