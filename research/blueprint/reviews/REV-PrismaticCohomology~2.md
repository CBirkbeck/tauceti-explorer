## Mathematical checks of the additional targets

**PR.0/general-prismatic-envelope.** The proof constructs a solution set in ordinary delta-rings rather than assuming animated completion is discrete. For a fixed target, close the image under delta, unique division by the regular orientation, and the inverse telescope operations for p and d. A cardinal closed under countable powers controls this closure through the required transfinite stages. The resulting small subring remains derived complete and d-torsion-free; the prism relation comes from the base. Products of the small targets and the same closure give existence, while preservation of the uniquely determined operations gives uniqueness. No boundedness or complete-flatness conclusion is inferred. Bhatt's Lecture V has a bounded-prism convention on p.1, so Lemma 5.1 on p.6 is context for this extension, not its full proof. Koshikawa Proposition 3.1, p.13, states the orientable existence interface.

**PR.1/laurent-relative-frobenius-cosimplicial-lemma.** A pointed choice of residues in G/pG gives the free relative-Frobenius basis, including negative exponents. Its compatibility with cofaces is sufficient for the unnormalized cochain calculation; compatibility with codegeneracies is neither true nor needed. Transporting to the split augmented Čech construction on k[G/pG] contracts the added factor after arbitrary degreewise tensoring with N. For infinite rank each group element has finite support; one need not descend an arbitrary coefficient module to a finite polynomial ring. The four checks exercise precisely these points. This extends the proof pattern of BS22 Lemma 5.4, pp.48–49. Koshikawa Appendix B, pp.61–62, retains the separate log Cartier-type and grading arguments of the consumer.

**PR.2/quasiregular-quotient-initial-prism.** KY Lemma 4.18 and its proof, pp.39–40, cover a bounded base prism that need not be perfect. The auxiliary root-variable presentation has the requisite regularity after extension; it does not assert regularity of the original kernel. Hodge–Tate discreteness and the natural idempotent are as in BS22 Lemma 7.7, Example 7.9 and Proposition 7.10, pp.57–60. The resulting initiality is relative to the fixed base, including unbounded targets; it is not absolute independence of an arbitrary base. The reduction's complete faithful flatness supplies boundedness separately from the general-envelope construction.

**The four PR.5 targets.** The pointwise category is an enhanced limit with coherent derived scalar change. The universe convention controls presentability; it is not a limit of triangulated homotopy categories. Pointwise dualizability is justified by HA Proposition 4.6.1.11 and Remark 4.6.1.12, p.620; the perfect-module identification uses Proposition 7.2.4.4, pp.1263–1264. SAG Corollary D.6.3.3, p.2177, supplies unbounded faithfully flat module descent, with ordinary tensor powers valid because of flatness. Space-valued sheafification is an explicit D0 extension request. Flat affine pushforward is pointwise restriction of scalars and satisfies the asserted base change and projection formula. The Z→F_p Tor example correctly excludes the discarded arbitrary ordinary affine assertion. BL22 Corollaries 3.2.9–3.2.10, p.42, distinguish affine prism maps from flat transversal ones.

For **formal-affine-complete-qcoh**, I checked the regular-ideal proof on unbounded complexes. The rectangular quotients by powers of a finite regular sequence are perfect with a uniform finite Tor-amplitude bound. Their transition maps kill the cohomology of the coefficient fibres uniformly. A bounded number of ghost compositions then kills the coefficient map itself before tensoring with an arbitrary complex; this order is essential. Perfectness permits the required limit/tensor interchange. Reconstruction and the complete unit follow from the Koszul tower. For a noncomplete base R the unit is Λ_J R; R=Z, J=(p) distinguishes it from R. Crystalline and transversal prism charts lie in this range. SAG's connective theorem, Theorem 8.3.4.4, pp.667–668, is not used as an automatic unbounded ordinary-quotient theorem. The general bounded nonregular case remains the stated gap. BL22 §3.3, pp.43–45; BS15 Lemmas 3.5.4–3.5.7, pp.25–26; and BS22 §1.2 footnote 7, p.12, are the relevant completion/descent interfaces.

**The four PR.6 targets.** The automorphism construction uses the imposed classical and derived completeness, ind-étale reductions, completeness of the principal quotients and regularity of tX_s. The inverse and delta-compatibility clauses need their stated hypotheses. The cofinal-power inclusions for (p,[p]_q), (p,t), and (p,ξ) are valid, including p=2, and are not ideal equalities. Derived comparison retains its own hypotheses. The merely p-complete localization example detects failure to lift the q-scaling.

Exact division gives D-linear commuting q-derivatives. The finite Koszul differential has the required wedge signs, the extra-direction condition gives chain functoriality, and reduction requires the first-order automorphism modulo t². The toric change of basis and the [p]_q X_s^(p−1) Frobenius factor agree with BS22 Constructions 16.19–16.20, pp.114–115. Scholze's §3, pp.6–8, supports the completed framing and twisted Leibniz conventions; an ordinary commutative DGA structure is not inferred.

For completed forms, decay makes each coefficient sum finite modulo I^n, so the differential extends to the inverse limit and squares to zero. The common-Y counterexample shows that individual continuity and completeness do not imply decay. For framed algebras over flat Z_p[[t]]-bases, the associated graded polynomial ring gives strictness, completeness of the necessary quotients and the uniform colon estimate. In the finite-coordinate argument the descended data include both the étale presentation and the representative of the particular element. Uniqueness of étale lifting then fixes that element in all other directions. This proves decay on P for arbitrary coordinate sets; it does not prove decay on its q-PD envelope or a general monoid chart. The generic Koszul interface remains with AI.1 and continuous forms with DD.2.

## R6 row 5: exactification without bounded targets

The full argument is recorded as `consumerProofAdjustment` in the route and beside the reader's consumer. It is a small proof repair, not another general completion theory.

Invert S=1+pB. Frobenius preserves S, so the delta-log structure extends. The resulting B_loc has p in its Jacobson radical: for x=b/s, the numerator s+pb again belongs to S. Every target prism already inverts S, as does its quotient, so this localization and the corresponding localized quotient preserve the original mapping problem.

Now exactify the monoid by M′=(h^gp)^(-1)(N), and form E=B_loc⊗_(Z_(p)[M_B])Z_(p)[M′]. Koshikawa Proposition 2.16, p.11, applies under the Jacobson-radical hypothesis. Construction 2.17, p.12, uses classical p-completeness only to ensure the denominators 1+p·δ_log(m) are units, which is already ensured here. Apply the general prismatic envelope to E and its quotient kernel and carry the integral monoid M′ along. Successive localization, exactification and envelope universal properties give unique lifts to all target prisms. Koshikawa's footnote 8, pp.13–14, concerns nonorientable descent for the regular-envelope theorem, not a bounded-target restriction on Proposition 3.6, p.15.

The accepted PR.8 packet still contains its older bounded-target proof and acceptance sentence. Applying row 5 must replace those together with the prerequisite. A prerequisite-only edit would leave the inconsistency in place.
