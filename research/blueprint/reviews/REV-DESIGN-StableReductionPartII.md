For E3, work over C and take three elliptic curves E_L, E_M, E_R with different j-invariants. Join them in a chain, choosing distinct attachment points on E_M, and put the sole marking at a third smooth point of E_M. Every component is stable. The target has genus three. Cutting either end node gives an unmarked genus-one tail and a marked genus-two curve, with the gluing point serving as an additional marking on each factor. Thus both cuts define points of the same source M̄_(1,1) × M̄_(2,2), with H empty and K={1}. The first factors have different j-invariants and are not isomorphic, while both glued targets are the same pointed chain. A closed immersion is a monomorphism, so the two distinct geometric preimages contradict Corollary 3.9(b)'s asserted closed immersion even though g₁≠g₂. This does not refute the restricted normalization description planned elsewhere.

For E11, take A=Z, M=Z/2 and N=Z. The free presentation by multiplication by 2 has first syzygy 2Z, a coherent projective module. Every map from that syzygy to N is zero in the stable category modulo maps through coherent projectives. The same free resolution computes Ext¹_Z(Z/2,Z)=Z/2, which is nonzero. This refutes the unrestricted stable-Hom identification as printed in v3; it does not refute the restricted approximation statements used later. Both the underline on Hom and the source's definition of the stable category were inspected.

For E4, a constant smooth family of genus at least two over A¹ has constant moduli image. Its finite-cover components still have dimension one, while their images in the chosen moduli cover have dimension zero. Closing those images cannot compactify the base. For E5, attach a rational self-nodal component to an elliptic component at one smooth point. The rational normalization has three node flags and dualizing degree one; the elliptic component has dualizing degree one. This stable genus-two curve exhibits the missing self-node convention.
