## What the revision supplies

The new signatures use genuine Mathlib carriers and retain their substantive
hypotheses:

- `shortEquationFamily.height_count` uses the integral coefficient lattice,
  its canonical map into the real tensor product, and a real vector-space
  norm. It asserts finiteness and equality of the two unweighted counts.
  The three additional examples distinguish nonpositive bounds, one equation
  and two distinct equations representing isomorphic curves.
- The five `_functionFieldDegree` declarations state the extension degrees
  of ℚ(x) over the subfields generated by the displayed rational functions:
  4, 6, 3, 10 and 6. These are distinct from integer degree and do not
  identify the proper modular curves or their j-morphisms.
- `B_mordell_weil` gives a point whose integer multiples biject with the
  actual rational point group. It expresses rank one, trivial torsion and
  saturation. Its additional example rejects doubling as a saturated
  generator.
- `quadratic_level_fifteen_torsion` uses the canonical point-group
  base-change maps. It retains the quadratic degree assumption, distinguishes
  the two E15 exceptions from the single Es35 exception, and counts eight
  new Gaussian torsion points separately from eight rational points. It
  does not assert rank zero over every quadratic field.
- `small_imaginary_quadratic_level_fifteen_finite` supplies the arithmetic
  finiteness input for d ∈ {1,2,3,5}; it is not the modularity endpoint.
  `sqrt_minus_ten_infinite_level_fifteen` states infinite order of the
  explicit point (−1,6√−10), a nonrational y-coordinate and infinitude of
  the E15 point group. Its geometric X₀(15) comparison still needs
  `level_fifteen_models`.

These changes resolve the earlier missing norm-count signature and give three
of the forty-five named theorem/comparison targets point-group signatures.
They leave the central modularity predicate and geometric comparisons absent.

mathematical type. Rerun the comment-stripped inventory and `lean-check` once

## Sources, baseline and verification

The six public PDFs and five scripts were fetched independently on 2026-10-10;
all eleven SHA-256 values match the accepted plan. In particular, checked:

- Caraiani–Newton, [arXiv:2301.10509v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.10509v3),
  Definition 2.1.27, p.25, Theorem 5.2 and Remark 5.2.3, pp.73–74, and
  the applications on pp.87–102. The README retains the inverse-cyclotomic
  dual normalization, existential generic witness, monodromy, independent
  seed, residual exceptions and finite-X₀(15) hypothesis. The genericity
  definition excludes eigenvalue ratios q and q⁻¹; it need not exclude
  repeated eigenvalues when neither ratio occurs.
- Allen–Khare–Thorne, [arXiv:1910.12986v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12986v2),
  Theorems 7.1 and 8.1, pp.67, 71–72, and Propositions/Corollaries
  9.12–9.15, pp.79–81. The dyadic dihedral and odd-prime ordinary seeds
  remain distinct, with their local Tate and p=5 projective-field conditions.
- Zywina, [§1.1, pp.1–2, Proposition 5.2, p.6, and §5.4, p.9](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~zywina/papers/MaximalGalois.pdf).
  The ordering counts integral coefficient pairs by a fixed norm, including
  distinct equations for isomorphic curves. It is not an isomorphism-class
  density claim.
- Bruin–Flynn, [§2, pp.2–4](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/flynn/arts/art24.pdf),
  and Box, [Proposition 3.1, pp.8–9](https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/sci/maths/people/staff/box/quadratic_points_4.pdf).
  Rational divisors remain distinct from rational Jacobian points. The
  package uses only its stated weak bielliptic pullback inclusion, rather
  than accepting the stronger full-group assertion from that proposition.

  [commit e6e2e9014f55f91e1d27a23e225a5f474d2e1d81](https://github.com/jjmnewton/modularity-iqf/tree/e6e2e9014f55f91e1d27a23e225a5f474d2e1d81).
