## Corrections to the mathematics

1. **Growth cutoff.** PMIA §4.7 uses `growthTransition(n,n₀)=ω_n/ω_(n₀−1)`, whereas the arithmetic relation uses `ν_(n,m)=ω_n/ω_m`. Thus set `n₀=m+1`, cutoff parameter `c=m`, and conclude `d(H_m)≤m`. The old `m−1` bound was false. The reader now tests `Λ/(T)` and `Λ/(ξ₁)` as well as the separate one-prime case, whose cutoff remains −1. RJW Appendix A, Lemma A.9, Proposition A.10 and Corollaries A.12–A.13, pp. 203–207, supply the arithmetic and elementary-module arguments.
2. **Dependency direction.** Removed the global-local dictionary's dependencies on the finite ray sequence and principal closures: it is an input to their refinement. Removed the adapter's dependence on the superseded defect definition. `inheritedTargets.newNodes` is now expressly a list of relevant refinements, not a list of prerequisite edges. In particular, the tower-invariant definition cannot be made an input of the growth theorem it imports. The parent Ferrero–Washington proof replaces its old Bernoulli/digit prerequisites; the obsolete Bernoulli implication can then follow from Ferrero–Washington because its antecedent is impossible. The explicit edge deletions prevent a reverse cycle.
3. **Dual action.** With this node's action convention, the covariant dual of the Tate tensor has action multiplied by `u`, hence twist **+1** and series `f(u⁻¹(1+T)−1)`. Kolster's label −1 must be translated through the actual action. A rank-one test distinguishes `ua` from `u⁻¹a`, including the latter's spurious infinite-coinvariant case.
4. **Coleman coordinates and hypothesis.** RJW Definition 5.18 and Remark 5.21, pp. 147–148, use exponent `1−s`. I.3's analytic series uses exponent `s`; the comparison is the integral involution `a(T)=u/(1+T)−1`. For the principal component, I.3's factor becomes `−T`, as required for the augmentation ideal. RJW Theorem 12.23, pp. 188–189, supplies the local quotient unconditionally; the further arithmetic identification with `X∞⁺` uses Vandiver, as in Theorem 13.11, pp. 192–195. Tests distinguish the values at 0 and 1, the two pole positions, and Vandiver from regularity at 37.
5. **Greither's ray module.** Greither §4, pp. 492–493, defines `A′(F)` as the full ray-class limit. It retains the free cyclotomic quotient and must not be redefined as torsion to force an isomorphism. The exact sequence is `0→X∞_Γ→A′(F)→Γ→0`; real Leopoldt identifies the kernel with the torsion. The nontrivial tame projector kills Γ, giving precisely the comparison used in Theorem 4.11. The example `F=ℚ`, odd p, separates the full ray limit `ℤ_p` from the zero kernel. Sharifi Theorem 1.3.16, pp. 19–20, Proposition 3.2.19, p. 83, and Theorem 3.3.3's proof, p. 84, supply the surrounding arithmetic comparisons.
6. **Content normalization and Sinnott.** The packet now distinguishes uniformizer content `μ_π` from Sinnott's `v_p(p)=1` normalization: divide by the coefficient ramification index. At 2 the content of 2 is that index, not always 1; zero content is ∞. In the independence proof, substitution at exponent zero is not a Laurent-field endomorphism. Use nonzero substitutions, continuity and character independence. The rational-measure adapter now supplies the smoothing-quotient integrality argument, inversion of the analytic variable, and the content-zero smoothing factor. For dyadic interpolation, first prove equality on `2ℤ₂`, then use the analytic identity principle; continuity on the even integers alone is insufficient. These are Sinnott §3, pp. 277–279, and Proposition 4.1 with proof, pp. 280–282.
7. **Principal branch.** An arbitrary simple-zero factor does not preserve content. The prescribed integral factor is `P(S)=(1+S−u)ζ_p(s)`, with `S=u^s−1`, and it is halved at 2. Its value at `S=u−1` is `u log_p(u)(1−p⁻¹)`, a unit at odd p and twice a unit at 2. The exact all-prime residue comes from `DirichletPadicLFunctions:L3/rjw2-principal-residue`; RJW Theorem 7.1, p. 154, is only the odd-prime statement.
8. **Relative and full dyadic μ.** Finite relative orders do not by themselves justify restricting a full-module pseudo-isomorphism to dyadic norm-minus parts. The revised first step proves relative μ=0. For `A_n=Cl(k_n){2}` and `B_n=Cl(k_n⁺){2}`, norm-surjectivity, `Nj=2`, and the CM unit bound `#ker j≤2` give `r₂(B_n)≤1+v₂(h_n⁻)` and `r₂(A_n)≤1+2v₂(h_n⁻)`. Relative μ=0 makes these ranks O(n), so their ratio to `2^n` tends to zero and excludes every vertical elementary summand. This is the weaker inequality actually sufficient at 2: Washington 1975 Lemma 4, p. 184; Ferrero 1978 Lemmas 1.5–1.7 and Theorem 1.8, pp. 11–12. Full μ=0 first makes the full compact modules finite over `ℤ₂`; the inverse norm-minus submodule and direct norm-minus dual quotient inherit this property. No dyadic exact projector or minus pseudo-isomorphism is assumed.
9. **Conductor descent and Washington.** A general abelian E need not lie in the first-kind field K itself: it lies in some `K_a`, and `K∞=E∞K_a`. Norm descent applies after this finite shift. Washington 1975 Lemma 1, p. 179, supplies the reduction; Theorem 2, p. 183, supplies the dyadic relative prime-to-p theorem. Lemmas 4–6 and Propositions 1–2, pp. 184–186, turn relative boundedness into full boundedness and eventual constancy. Together with Washington 1978's proof, pp. 87–97, these close the unread-source gap. At the distinct class-group prime ℓ, bounded rank forces eventual stability through the growing degree of faithful cyclic p-power representations over `𝔽_ℓ`; a rank bound alone does not bound orders without this argument.
