**Mathematical corrections and the remaining closure tests.** The following arguments explain the repairs and provide discriminating cases for revision.

The symmetric-space action of the full G(F_infinity) is not proper when the positive split centre A_infinity is noncompact and fixes every point. The proper homogeneous action is by G(F_infinity)/A_infinity. Even a discrete subgroup with trivial intersection with A_infinity need not project discretely: in GL2(R), take the cyclic group generated by e times a rotation R_alpha with alpha/pi irrational. Scalar growth makes the group discrete; no nonidentity power is a positive scalar. Every power fixes i in the symmetric space, giving infinite stabilizer. Arithmetic projected discreteness/finite central kernel, or a noncircular neat reduction argument followed by finite index, must be supplied. Merely saying an arithmetic subgroup is discrete at infinity does not finish nodes 6-7. Lean's arbitrary Datum is weaker still: GF=Z, Gf=1 and X a point satisfy the declared compact-open hypotheses but make the asserted properly discontinuous action false.

Standard level tests have several independent distinctions. At c=1, Gamma_p/Gamma_1 has order the p-primary part of q-1; p=3,q=7 gives 3, whereas p=3,q=5 gives equality. The projective Gamma_1 preimage requires diagonal entries to agree. On SL2, their common value a satisfies a^2=1 modulo the level. Odd prime powers give ±1, but modulo 8, a=3 gives an extra solution. PGL1 is trivial, so the Taylor-Wiles quotient k_v^× formula starts at n=2. The original API named Gamma_1/Gamma_p only in the description of gamma0; separate constructor/data items now give each its own name. The existing Lean gamma1 condition fixes only the lower-right entry and must be replaced by the projective one.

For coefficients on a slice g_i, the quotient action sends (x,g,v) to (gamma x,gamma g k,rho_GF(gamma)rho_K(k)^(-1)v). Keeping g_i fixed forces k=g_i^(-1)gamma^(-1)g_i. Thus the fibre representation is rho_GF(gamma)rho_K(g_i^(-1)gamma g_i), with commuting factors. Inverting only the second factor does not make a homomorphism when K's image is noncommutative. Identifying deck transformations with based loops may introduce an opposite convention, but it must be fixed for the whole representation. Rank-one sign tests cannot detect this error. At non-neat level, invertible stabilizer orders permit exact invariants, but do not turn an arbitrary invariant pushforward into a local system on the coarse quotient.

Group-ring perfectness requires freeness for the full group G(F) times K, not just its subgroup at K-prime. A bounded cell model free over the full group ring restricts to the subgroup and gives a finite-projective quotient-group complex after taking Hom with finite projective coefficients. A merely free K-prime model can yield permutation modules with stabilizers at K and fail quotient-group projectivity. The simplest finite-group test is the point with trivial C_p action over F_p: its underlying coefficient complex is perfect, but the trivial F_p[C_p] module has infinite projective dimension, and derived invariants have unbounded cohomology. Nodes 16,54,62 now preserve this distinction. Invertibility of the full quotient order is sufficient for averaging; it is not equivalent to invertibility of the orders of actual stabilizers.

Boundary geometry must distinguish the universal P-space P(F)\(e(P) times P(A_f)) from its induction, which has G(A_f) in place of P(A_f). Inducing the latter again double-counted the adelic direction. At level K the induced stratum decomposes over P(A_f)\G(A_f)/K. Each g transports K to a P-level L_g and has its own Levi base. The nilmanifold fibration is built on those pieces at good decomposed level, using the arithmetic group extension; no arbitrary splitting of that extension is available. Nodes 39,41,42 and the filtration formulas still need the corresponding propagation. Corner neighborhoods always contain the open G-face, so they cannot have the original empty intersection alternative. Precise analytic charts remain a genuine missing input.

The compact-support exact couple for a finite stratification and the ordinary closed-cover Mayer-Vietoris construction are different constructions. The packet must state exact pages, signs, connecting maps and differential bidegrees for each. NT's proof of Lemma 4.4 uses compact-support induction and uses duality to get the needed H_c vanishing from ordinary vanishing, at the inverse Hecke ideal. The current ordinary-cohomology shortcut does not justify nodes 43,47,48. An early finite-level duality dependency is needed, and the stage-level order must avoid a cycle. The checker verifies the local node graph, not this missing proof or every atlas stage cycle.

For the upper Borel of GL2(Q_p), with S(f)(t)=integral_N f(tn)dn and vol(N(Z_p))=1, the corrected image is p[diag(p,1)]+[diag(1,p)]. Indeed at t=diag(p,1), the condition on the upper-right entry permits n in p^(-1)Z_p, of measure p; at t=diag(1,p), it permits n in Z_p, of measure 1. Delta_B(diag(p,1))=p^(-1), so multiplying by Delta_B^(1/2) yields sqrt(p) times the symmetric sum. Keeping the former coefficient order with this same delta formula was inconsistent. SR.2/SR.4 and RG2.4 remain the owners of induction, normalized Satake and positive/Iwahori inputs.

Characteristic-zero Nomizu/van Est is retained without any integral replacement. M acts algebraically on Lie cochains; on a fixed lattice quotient, only its lattice normalizer acts geometrically. General commensurators compare transported lattices and Hecke maps. Kostant's summands are irreducible Levi modules; for nonabelian Levi they are not all one-dimensional characters, even at lambda=0. The GL_N Harder-Raghuram argument does not by itself supply every reductive E2 splitting. NT Lemma 4.5 explicitly uses discrete cohomology of its compact unipotent subgroup; continuous profinite cohomology requires a comparison theorem, not density. The incorrect generic continuous Hochschild-Serre prerequisite for node 42 was replaced by the discrete bundle/cover supplier, with the arithmetic bridge still a gap.

The derived Hecke image is not simply its action on cohomology. Over a DVR O, C=k[0] direct-sum k[1] has a nonzero off-diagonal Ext^1_O(k,k) endomorphism which is zero on cohomology and squares to zero. An O[epsilon]/epsilon^2 action using it tests the generic derived-image input. The old single complex [O --varpi--> O] is just one shifted copy of k and does not have that ghost. GL1 examples must also preserve archimedean unit tori: only the stated Q level is a point. For a finite component group acting regularly the image is O[C], not O^C. A bare map T→End_D(C) supplies no chosen strict cochain action; when a compatible D(T) lift is given, derived tensor localization is valid. This resolves the old contradiction between node 27 and node 44's no-tensor test.

Character twisting is an operation on a character of G(A_f) trivial on G(F), not on a quotient group by a generally nonnormal subgroup. ACC's twisting theorem uses an invertible global section of a rank-one sheaf, tensors it with V and then applies RΓ; one cannot replace higher arithmetic cohomology of the universal adelic space by H0. A changed eigenvalue of a fixed k-valued eigensystem detects change, but over a larger residue field changed systems may be Frobenius conjugate with the same maximal ideal. The constant-functions GL2 eigensystem has T1=1+q and T2=1, so its polynomial is (X-1)(X-q). The previous T2=q made the determinant q^2. The Frobenius convention must determine cyclotomic versus inverse cyclotomic character.

Removing A_infinity also removes its Lie algebra directions. The de Rham and automorphic pair is (m_G,K_infinity), with m_G=g/a_G and a cancelling coefficient central character, not unrestricted (g,K_infinity). For GL1/Q the arithmetic symmetric space is a point; the old full one-dimensional g complex gives a spurious degree-one class. This correction propagates through nodes 56-58 and the AS.5 request. The representation pi-prime in Scholze's real-place argument must be twisted by the chosen quadratic character; the character is not an innocuous change of notation unless it is absorbed into the representation.

The strict cuspidal-versus-interior test needs replacement. Classical Saito-Kurokawa lifts are cuspidal CAP forms, as [Ralf Schmidt's introduction and lifting theorem](https://www.math.uni-sb.de/ag/schulze/rschmidt/papers/skfunc.pdf) explicitly show; they do not furnish the claimed residual noncuspidal class. General Saito-Kurokawa packets have more cases, so this observation does not assert every member is cuspidal. No proof of a specific noncuspidal interior contribution at the proposed level/weight was supplied. The test now says it is unresolved instead of certifying a false example. The unitary degree theorem separately needs an exact Galois attachment/constituent supplier for its unitary factors; the GL_m-only AG2.4 stage cannot stand for that theorem.

Tower tests must distinguish open Y(7) from its compactification X(7). The latter has genus 3 and 24 cusps; the open curve has H1 dimension 2*3+24-1=29. Whole-adelic GL2 principal-level covers have GL2(F_p) deck quotient, while SL2(F_p) is the component-preserving group. The lowest-degree argument needs a p-group quotient, not an arbitrary full product k_v^×. These tests validate finite descent only. The accepted ALS.6 scope also requires completed inverse-limit, chain and boundary exports from CC.2/CC.4/CC.7. They were absent. ALS.6 is now partial, and because the packet has fewer than 300 nodes it is partial too. This corrects coverage without planning the CC owner's objects again.
