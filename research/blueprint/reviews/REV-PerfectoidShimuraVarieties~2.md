For the fan test take γ=diag(2,1,1,2) in GSp₄(ℚ₂), multiplier two, with upper block A=diag(2,1). At the standard cusp its action is Q↦AQAᵀ/2. The standard perfect cone generated by e₁²,e₂²,(e₁−e₂)² maps to a cone containing both axis rays and (2e₁−e₂)². The only genus-two perfect maximal cones containing both axes have third ray (e₁±e₂)²; their matrices satisfy |Q₁₂|≤min(Q₁₁,Q₂₂). The transformed ray has matrix [[4,−2],[−2,1]] and violates that inequality. Thus the original fan is not compatible with this Hecke map; use a common refinement. The added Lean example checks precisely the rank-one matrix and inequality, not an unavailable analytic fan construction.

For the induction correction use (y,h)∼(y·s,s⁻¹h) and a continuous σ:S\H→H, obtained by inversion from the pinned H/S section. The product coordinate is [(y,h)]↦(y·(hσ(Sh)⁻¹),Sh), with inverse (y,q)↦[(y,σ(q))]. The H-action is transported through these formulas. Finite preimages use Y×^{S∩H_n}H_n and (S∩H_n)\H_n; sections are locally constant modulo each finite quotient on a clopen partition. This is the extra density argument needed by the tilde-limit claim, not a bare set-theoretic product identification.
