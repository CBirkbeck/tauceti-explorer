## Corrections that affect the mathematics

At I.1 the cyclotomic tower argument now separates algebra from topology, uses local cyclotomic inertia at every prime above p, and states the condition under which projections of inverse limits are surjective. The splitting argument for an abelian tower uses its torsion-free rank-one Z_p quotient. Height-one tests apply to suitable reflexive or invertible objects, not arbitrary integral ideals. Counterexamples and working characterisation APIs were added where needed; the construction APIs can be used without unfolding the definitions.

At I.2–I.4 the unaugmented global–local unit sequence is restricted to odd p or totally imaginary fields. The real dyadic extension needs sign factors, wide/narrow conventions and a separate comparison of norm-compatible global units with their closure. Including real places in S does not make ordinary dyadic cohomological dimension at most two. The rank bound uses the p-split module, not arbitrary enlarged S. In the unit-cohomology formula the contribution of a finite 2-group B is determined by B/2B, equivalently B[2], rather than its full order; B = Z/4 separates the assertions (Kolster 1989, proof of Theorem 1, printed pp. 249–250).

The character comparison in twisted descent now records its prime-to-p and weight range. Adding one prime gives the Euler factor with κ(Frob_v) = ⟨Nv⟩, retaining the Teichmüller factor; it is not legitimate to replace this directly by Nv (Kolster 2009, Lecture 1, pp. 5–11). Finite-layer sign groups and tower sign modules are also separated: a Λ/(2) summand is visible at the height-one prime (2). A quotient of several such summands gives no equality of ranks or real-place factor.

At I.5 the weak distinguished-polynomial conjecture is kept separate from its stronger μ assertion; the Q equivalence needs Ferrero–Washington and the stated nonexceptional character range. In the Ribet argument the coefficient rings are explicit: a faithful finite Hecke module gives vanishing Fitting ideal over the Hecke ring, its Eisenstein quotient has vanishing Fitting ideal over that quotient, and only then does one deduce divisibility over Λ. An untyped containment in the Eisenstein ideal was removed (Sharifi, AWS notes, §4.3, pp. 45–52). The abelian generalisation requires the character field to be abelian over Q. Rognes–Weibel Appendix A.2, pp. 47–49, supplies a dyadic going-up argument; it does not establish the proposed arbitrary-prime interface. The totally real Wiles proof and codimension-zero congruence supplier remain open.

At I.6 all DKSW integrality assumptions are retained. Anti-invariance of the full Stickelberger element needs a depletion set containing infinity with at least two places; otherwise the trivial character can survive. The minus quotient is separated from the eigenspace by the free module Z with trivial conjugation: the quotient is Z/2 and the negative eigenspace is zero. Minus Selmer exactness and disappearance of the real-place term are restricted to coefficients with 2 invertible. The generalised DKSW Ritter–Weiss presentation uses its modified module V′, not the original V^θ. A square presentation alone does not make its determinant regular. Exact dyadic class-number powers require the norm-kernel/cokernel computation, not an away-from-two splitting (DKSW Brumer–Stark–Zeta, conditions A1–A5 and Theorems 1.2, 2.3, Lemma 2.13, pp. 4–8, 16–17).

At I.7 the odd-prime and dyadic proof routes are now separate. In the latter, d = [F:Q], the minimal depletion and smoothing sets follow DKSW, and the relevant coefficient algebras can be nonreduced. There is no general embedding of the Hecke algebra into a product of integer rings. The cocycle construction includes the finite matrix lattice and its induced adic topology, the local generators and quotient, the surjection from the generalised Ritter–Weiss module, and the **weighted** local Fitting bound before Hecke cancellation (DKSW Brumer–Stark–Zeta, §§2.5, 3.1–3.3, Theorems 2.15, 3.5, 3.14, pp. 17–30; DKSW Ribet, Theorem 2.1 and §2.1, pp. 8–10).

The source arithmetic remains true, but the checked supplier interfaces do not cover this whole route. Current IHG.6 `local_ribet` assumes residual coincidence; a dyadic character with nontrivial odd-order part need not satisfy it. The global distinct-character theorem has no matching weighted local conclusion. DKSW Ribet Remark 2.2, p. 8, explicitly permits the local theorem without residual coincidence, so this is a missing exact supplier contract, not an accusation that the mathematical extension is false. Likewise R19.2’s ordinary Hilbert representation node assumes p odd and even [F:Q], while I.7 requires p = 2 and arbitrary degree. The read Λ-adic Hilbert and q-expansion contracts do not supply the full cusp-correction and level/coefficient range required by DK Theorems 8.8, 8.9 and 8.15, pp. 54–59. Three precise requests were added to the existing owners.

The finite Fitting theorem remains, but the claimed coherent norm-compatible free matrices and regular determinant were removed: split primes can produce zero determinants. I.9 needs an exact intrinsic field-change/Fitting-continuity interface instead (DK Appendix B, Lemmas B.1–B.7, Theorem B.8, pp. 89–97; Kurihara §4.2, pp. 24–27). The Brumer–Stark annihilator consequence is not equivalent to construction of the anti-unit. The degree-one case is separated from the divisibility-by-two argument. Rubin’s fixed depletion set is distinguished from the additional split places; using the latter on the right-hand Stickelberger element made that expression zero (DK §3.3, pp. 21–27).

At I.10 the real-place map, its rank and finite cokernel are explicit outstanding targets. Kolster’s discrete direct-limit formula uses Γ-invariants, not coinvariants (Kolster 1989, Theorem 1 and Lemma 2, printed pp. 249–250). For Q_2/Z_2 with γ acting by 3, invariants have order 2 while coinvariants vanish. The compact derived comparison now states H² = X(−2)_Γ and H³ = Z_2(−2)_Γ, with the roots-of-unity denominator retained. The full comparison is conditional on the unresolved real-place dictionary.

## Source issues and red-team findings

Both source issues have independent confirmed verdicts scoped to the exact editions read. E1 confirms the existing Kolster 2009, Lecture 1, p. 6, dyadic counterexample: for F = Q(√−2) the cyclotomic image is torsion-free although F(i)/F has degree two. Use the actual torsion subgroup and the whole-group Teichmüller projection. E2 records a sign inconsistency between DKSW Ribet v2 Theorem 2.1, p. 8, and its module construction in §2.1, p. 9. Changing the local generators’ signs reconciles the formulas; the existence and Fitting conclusions are unaffected. Both entries explain the checks in our own words and record the correction searches; “new” means no correction was found in those searches.

All three assigned red-team findings were checked. RT-AREA-langlands-1/8 is respected by removing the IG.1 → I.3 dependency and using the Hilbert ordinary supplier through APL L3/T5. RT-AREA-langlands-1/27 gives IHG.1 the proposed generic congruence ownership without pretending its requested codimension-zero API already exists. RT-AREA-ktheory-2/26 gives I.2 Leopoldt’s map and defect, while L4, P.6 and APL L0 import it for their comparisons. Ownership proposals and upstream observations remain notes for the maintainer; no other roadmap was edited.
