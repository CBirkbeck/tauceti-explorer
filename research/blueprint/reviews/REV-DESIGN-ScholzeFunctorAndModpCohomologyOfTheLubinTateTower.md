## Supplier closure and existing work

All direct node suppliers were read. The supplier families are the equivariant and diamond sites, perfectoid/almost geometry, the tower and duality, analytic cohomology, smooth/mod-p and p-adic representations, completed cohomology, reductive buildings, patching/deformation theory, quaternionic/Hilbert automorphic forms, global Galois realisation and modularity. The reader enumerates every direct reference and its consumers; requests are used for the four stage-only mathematical inputs that have no exact node.

- `AdicSpacesPartII:R0`: Temkin’s criterion for good germs, in adic language: for a separated quasicompact analytic adic space V over Spa(C, O_C) (C complete algebraically closed) and a maximal point x whose closure in V is the Riemann–Zariski space of K(x) over C, x has an affinoid open neighbourhood containing the closure of x (Temkin, Math. Ann. 318 (2000), Theorem 3.1 with Remark 2.6, as used by Scholze, On the p-adic cohomology of the Lubin–Tate tower, proof of Corollary 3.6).
- `ClassicalAdicEtaleCohomology:H0`: (i) For a rigid-analytic space X of dimension d over a complete non-archimedean field, sheaf cohomology on the underlying topological space |X| (the analytic topology) vanishes above degree d (de Jong–van der Put, Doc. Math. 1 (1996), Proposition 2.5.8); used with X = ℙ^{n−1}_C and ℙ¹_C. (ii) For a rigid space X over a finite extension K of ℚ_p, quasicompact or Stein, the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence Hⁱ(Gal_K, H^j_ét(X_{ℂ_p}, Λ)) ⇒ H^{i+j}_ét(X, Λ) for torsion Λ (de Jong–van der Put, Corollary 3.7.5).
- `PerfectoidShimuraVarieties:S1`: For the GL₂ modular curve at infinite level, Serre–Tate/Rapoport–Zink uniformisation embeds the height-zero Lubin–Tate tower over a supersingular point into the perfectoid modular curve, compatibly with the Hodge–Tate map and the GL₂(ℤ_p)-action. Use the compact-level action on this height-zero component; GL₂(ℚ_p) moves heights and is not asserted to preserve the component. Together with the existing Γ₀-infinite-level perfectoid node this is the input to Ludwig Theorem 3.11 and Corollary 3.13, pp.20–22.
- `LocalGaloisDeformationRings:R08.6`: Existence of potentially Barsotti–Tate lifts: for p > 2, K/ℚ_p finite and every continuous ρ̄ : Gal_K → GL₂(𝔽̄_p) there is a potentially Barsotti–Tate lift (Snowden, §7.8, for ρ̄ irreducible; Barnet-Lamb–Gee–Geraghty, Lemma 6.1.6, for ρ̄ reducible), as used by Gee–Kisin Theorem A.2.
- `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ClassFieldTheory#layer-9-the-local-weil-group`: Import ClassFieldTheory Layer 9: the locally compact Weil group W_F with its inertia topology, dense inclusion in Gal_F, finite-extension functoriality and inertia exact sequence, and localWeilArtinEquiv : F^× ≃ₜ W_F^ab for F/ℚ_p finite. Invert the arithmetic reciprocity normalisation to define Art_F : W_F^ab ≃ₜ F^× with geometric Frobenius mapped to a uniformiser. This is not just the profinite absolute Artin map of Layer 7. For finite coefficient modules, use the inertia sequence to extend finite actions and finite-valued continuous 1-cocycles to finite Galois quotients.
- `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/ClassFieldTheory#layer-5-local-coefficients-the-brauer-group-the-local-invariant-and-duality`: Import ClassFieldTheory Layer 5: the cohomological local norm-residue (Hilbert) pairing and its norm-kernel/reciprocity compatibility for Moore’s computation; finite_H for H⁰,H¹,H² of finite smooth discrete local Galois modules. With the Weil inertia topology from Layer 9 this supplies the degree-0/1 Weil finiteness used in ascent, and the finite outer terms in the geometric-multiplicity Hochschild–Serre argument.
- `tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/Chebotarev#layer-10-dirichlet-density-chebotarev`: Imported, no new content: the Chebotarev density theorem for a finite Galois extension of a number field, giving infinitely many places w₁ with prescribed Frobenius conjugacy class (DPS Lemma 8.2).

## Source findings

- **E1 — confirmed** (CDN23, proof of Corollaire 3.13, p. 37, and proof of Théorème 4.22, p. 46 (published version; the same text in arXiv:2204.11214v2)). In the published paper cited, the finiteness for a p-adic reductive group is Lemma 6.6; Lemma 6.3 there is the auxiliary statement for a group with an open normal subgroup N of finitely generated cohomology and finite or finitely generated abelian quotient. The numbering ‘6.3’ is that of arXiv:2106.04473v1, where the reductive-group lemma is Lemma 6.3. Reason: The journal Lemma 6.3 is the open-normal-subgroup auxiliary result; reductive-group finiteness is Lemma 6.6. In the inspected arXiv v1, the latter theorem is indeed Lemma 6.3. The proof uses the right theorem after correcting the version-dependent number. Reach: nothing. Prior status: new.
- **E2 — confirmed** (CDN23, proof of Théorème 3.2, (3.7)–(3.8), p. 35). That lemma’s printed proof only covers coefficients killed by an integer prime to p (PAPER-SCHOLZE-17/E69). For quasicompact U_s and profinite S the needed identity holds for all torsion coefficients by continuity of étale cohomology along U_s × S = lim_m U_s × S_m over the finite quotients S_m of S (Étale cohomology of diamonds, Proposition 14.9); SZ.2/cartan-leray-spectral-sequence uses this argument. Reason: Lemma 23.6 of the diamonds paper passes through Rh_! and the projection formula, which are set up there only for prime-to-p torsion; CDN apply it to ℤ/p^k-modules. Reach: the proof. Prior status: The gap in the diamonds paper is PAPER-SCHOLZE-17/E69 (confirmed by REV-PAPER-SCHOLZE-17); its consequence for CDN23 is new..
- **E3 — confirmed** (CDN23, (4.15) and the computation following (4.16), p. 44 (published version; the same in arXiv:2204.11214v2)). The central element p = diag(p, p) acts on π₀ through det(diag(p, p)) = p², so π₀(M^p_{Dr,∞}) ≅ ℚ_p^×/p^{2ℤ}, H⁰_ét(M^p_{Dr,∞}, k_L) ≅ C⁰(ℚ_p^×/p^{2ℤ}, k_L), and C⁰(ℚ_p^×, k_L) = Ind_1^{p^ℤ} C⁰(ℤ_p^× ⊔ pℤ_p^×, k_L). The conclusions (admissibility of H⁰ for Ǧ_n and the vanishing of H¹(p^ℤ, −)) are unchanged. Reason: At level zero M_{0,F̆} = ℤ × Ω_{Dr} with g ∈ G shifting the height by v(det g) (§0.2.1, p. 4), so diag(p, p) shifts it by 2 and M^p_0 has two copies of Ω, not one; the infinite-level description must agree. Reach: nothing. Prior status: new.
- **E4 — confirmed** (SCH18, Proposition 4.7, arXiv v1 p.20 and published p.833; repeated in CDN23 Théorème 3.1(4), p.33; compare DPS23 Lemmas 7.3–7.4, pp.30–31). Under the DPS23 convention used in this roadmap, S⁰(π) ≅ π^{SLₙ(F)} carries (w,d) through Nrd(d)Art_F(w)⁻¹, so S⁰(δ∘det) has character δ⁻¹ ⊠ δ. State the right-G/left-D conversion explicitly when importing Strauch’s geometric component labels. Reason: For n=1, F=ℚ₅ and δ the mod-5 unit character with δ(5)=1, DPS Lemma 7.3 forces d=2 to act by δ(2)=2, while an inverse norm gives 3. DPS Lemma 7.4 also computes S⁰(δ)=δ⁻¹ ⊠ δ directly from components. Thus the inverse norm cannot coexist with those central and twist conventions. Reach: a stated result. Prior status: new as a normalisation conflict with the DPS23 convention.
- **E5 — confirmed** (CDN23, Corollaire 2.12, first display of its proof, p.30 (published version)). Put a continuous dual on the right as well, or remove the left dual while making the initial Hom identification. The next display then dualises finite-group invariants to coinvariants. Reason: Before dualising, Hom_GalF(ρ̄,H) = Hom_H(ρ̄,H^{GalF′}) because ρ̄ is trivial on GalF′. The next display identifies this compact Hom with the dual of the smooth coinvariant module, so the first display cannot identify its dual with the undualised Hom. Reach: nothing. Prior status: new; a local typographical omission, with the intended argument fixed by the following display.
- **E6 — confirmed** (PAS22, Lemma 5.1, formula(26), p.17 of arXiv:1804.07567v3; correction in DPS23 footnote 15, p.40). Use Hom_O(λ,O) as that tensor factor, then tensor with the torsion-valued function module. This is the lattice dual, before forming the O-torsion module. Reason: For λ=O, the printed torsion dual L/O would give (L/O)⊗_O(L/O)=0, whereas Hom_O(O,L/O)=L/O is nonzero. DPS23 footnote 15 explicitly supplies the lattice-dual correction. Reach: nothing. Prior status: Dospinescu–Paškūnas–Schraen, footnote 15, p.40 explicitly corrects Paškūnas formula(26).
