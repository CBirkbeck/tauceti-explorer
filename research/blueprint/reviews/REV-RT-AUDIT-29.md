# REV-RT-AUDIT-29

Independent verification of the red-team result `RT-AUDIT-29` on the accepted, review-enriched
library audit **AUDIT-29** — the batch `K2SymbolsBrauer`, `K3BlochGroups`,
`KTheoryFiniteLocalFields`, `KTheoryLowDegrees`.

**The red team reports no findings**, so there is nothing to confirm or reject and
`RT-AUDIT-29.review.json` carries an empty `findings` array.

A clean result is a claim like any other. Its `checked` list is unusually falsifiable — it names
counts, pins, file totals and specific resolved false positives — so I recomputed the numbers
independently rather than reading them, and pressed on the one claim that would overturn the whole
audit if it were wrong. **Everything I could test came out exactly right.**

## The attack that would have mattered

The audit's load-bearing assertion is negative: that nothing K-theoretic in this batch exists, and
that Tau Ceti's algebraic K-theory stops at K₀. A single counterexample in either pinned tree would
turn 33 "not built" verdicts into a defect. I searched both trees for it.

- **`Steinberg` in Mathlib: zero occurrences**, as the audit states in terms.
- **`Milnor` in Mathlib: two occurrences**, an exotic 7-sphere in `Wanted/` and Milnor–Moore in
  `Bialgebra/Primitive.lean`. No Milnor K-theory.
- **`Whitehead` in Mathlib: three occurrences**, all topological — CW complexes, the Whitehead
  manifold, the model-category Whitehead theorem. No Whitehead group, no K₁.
- **No Hilbert symbol and no tame symbol in either tree.**
- **Mathlib's only `GrothendieckGroup` is `MonoidLocalization/GrothendieckGroup.lean`**, the group
  completion of a commutative monoid — substrate, not K₀ of a ring.
- **Tau Ceti's K-theory is exactly `CategoryTheory/GrothendieckGroup/*`** (abelian, exact, split,
  triangulated, monoidal, graded, resolutions, Euler characteristics) plus a representation-theoretic
  Grothendieck group. Nothing in degree one or above. "Stops at K₀" is precise.

My own best lead survived longest and then dissolved. Tau Ceti has
`Algebra/Lie/UniversalEnveloping/Kostant/RootSubgroup/Steinberg.lean`, whose header defines
`E(A) = ⟨xᵢ(t)⟩`, an elementary group generated by root subgroups over an arbitrary ring — which
looks very much like the Steinberg presentation the audit calls absent. Reading it: it constructs
`steinberg = γ ∘ Frob_q`, a Steinberg **endomorphism** of an elementary Chevalley group, which is
precisely the first of the three categories the audit's own note names. Its `E(A)` is `E(Φ, A)` for
fixed Kostant data, not the stable `E(A) = colim E_n(A)`, and there are no stabilisation maps. The
audit's characterisation holds.

I also checked the two false positives the report says it resolved, and both are real traps it
avoided correctly. Tau Ceti's three `Weil reciprocity` hits are docstring prose in
`FunctionField/Divisor/Eval.lean`, `Divisor/DisjointSupport.lean` and `Place/Residue.lean` saying
what the divisor-evaluation apparatus is *for* — "is what Weil reciprocity `f(div g) = g(div f)`
compares. This file defines it" — with no reciprocity theorem anywhere; the audit lists that
apparatus as substrate, which is the right classification. The `Dieudonné` hits are
Cartan–Dieudonné and Dieudonné–Manin, not the Dieudonné determinant.

One adversarial check spot-verified: `TauCeti.commutatorElement_transvectionUnit`, cited for the
Steinberg relations, sits under `variable [CommRing A]` at Transvection.lean:126, so the
commutative-versus-general-ring distinction the report draws against stable K₁ is a real hypothesis
gap and not a rhetorical one.

## Judgement

The clean result is earned, and earned on the hardest part. A no-findings red team on a 199-target
audit is worth nothing unless its census, its citations and its negative searches are real; here all
three are. Every number reproduces exactly, all 306 citations resolve to the right file and line at
the pins, the coverage records agree precisely where the claim says they do, and the negative
searches that carry 33 "not built" verdicts hold up against an independent sweep of both trees,
including against the one lead that looked for a while like it might break them.
