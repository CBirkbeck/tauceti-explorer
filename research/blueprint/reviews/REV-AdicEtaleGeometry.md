## Findings

### REV-AdicEtaleGeometry/F1 — high: inverse substitution is missing in the transported equations

**Where:** `AdicEtaleGeometry:A3/perturbation-of-generators`, statement (c) and its proof step.

The node defines the algebra automorphism theta by theta(X) = X + epsilon(X), and pi' = pi composed with theta. Part (b) correctly gives ker(pi') = theta^{-1}(ker(pi)). Part (c), however, replaces equations F_i for ker(pi) by their forward substitutions theta(F_i), rather than their inverse substitutions theta^{-1}(F_i). The displayed forward chain rule reinforces this mismatch.

**Counterexample satisfying the stated smallness hypotheses.** Take A = Q_3, A_0 = Z_3, varpi = 3, one variable, pi evaluation at 0, F = X and epsilon = 3. Then theta(f)(X) = f(X + 3), while pi'(f) = f(3). The correct kernel is (X - 3). The proposed generator theta(F) = X + 3 has pi'-image 6, not 0. Thus it is not even an element of the kernel it is asserted to generate.

**Required correction.** Put F'_i = theta^{-1}(F_i). Then

- ker(pi') is the closure of the ideal generated by the F'_i;
- with the same coordinate convention, the determinant identity uses the inverse substitution: J(F') = theta^{-1}(J(F)) times det(D(theta^{-1}X));
- alternatively keep the forward chain rule as a separate identity, without identifying its equations with ker(pi').

For the ideal assertion, apply the homeomorphism theta^{-1} to the closed ideal generated by F. This proves the correction directly, without a new geometric theorem. Audit every use of this node in presentation transport and finite-stage approximation for the same direction.

**Acceptance test:** evaluation at 0 followed by X -> X + 3 must transport X = 0 to X - 3 = 0, and must reject X + 3 = 0. This is a blueprint error, not a newly discovered error in Huber or Scholze.

### REV-AdicEtaleGeometry/F2 — low: the dilation non-example gives a false witness

**Where:** the acceptance paragraph of `A3/perturbation-of-generators`.

The substitution X -> varpi X is indeed not surjective on Q_3<T>, but its image does not miss X: X is the image of varpi^{-1}X. The printed witness is wrong because varpi is a unit in the Tate algebra.

**Replacement witness.** The restricted series g(X) = sum_{n >= 0} varpi^n X^n belongs to Q_3<X>. A preimage under f(X) -> f(varpi X) would have every coefficient equal to 1. These coefficients do not tend to 0, so that preimage is not restricted. Retain the non-surjectivity test with g, and add the check that every polynomial, in particular X, does have a preimage.

### REV-AdicEtaleGeometry/F3 — medium: the fixed-Jacobian iteration is not quadratically convergent

**Where:** `AdicEtaleGeometry:A3/restricted-power-series-newton`, second proof step.

The iteration there keeps M fixed, where M J(t_0) = varpi^k. It nevertheless claims quadratic growth of residual valuations. This is not the Newton iteration with a freshly inverted Jacobian, and the rate claim is false.

**Exact example.** Work over Q_3 with varpi = 3, k = 0, F(X) = X^2 - 4, t_0 = 1 and M = 1/2. The hypotheses hold: F(t_0) = -3 and M J(t_0) = 1. Write

x_{n+1} = x_n - (x_n^2 - 4)/2,  e_n = x_n + 2.

Then e_{n+1} = e_n(3 - e_n/2), and v_3(e_n) = n + 1. Since x_n - 2 is a 3-adic unit, v_3(F(x_n)) = n + 1 as well. The growth is linear, not quadratic. Exact rational arithmetic was run for n = 0,...,8 and returned residual valuations 1,...,9; the recurrence proves the general assertion.

**Required correction.** Either retain the fixed M and prove the sufficient bounds

F(t_n) in varpi^{2k+1+n} C_0^N, and t_{n+1} - t_n in varpi^{k+1+n} C_0^N,

or use M_n = varpi^k J(t_n)^{-1} and prove the stronger Newton bounds with this changing matrix. For the fixed-M version, Taylor expansion and J(t_n) - J(t_0) in varpi^{k+1} give the extra factor varpi in the residual at each step. Linear growth suffices for convergence and for the stated existence/uniqueness conclusion. This finding does not refute the existence/uniqueness theorem.

**Acceptance test:** distinguish the two iterations on X^2 - 4 over Q_3. Do not claim a quadratic estimate for the frozen matrix.

### REV-AdicEtaleGeometry/F4 — medium: E7 relies on a false statement about the closed analytic disc

**Where:** `sourceIssues`, record `AdicEtaleGeometry/E7`, its reason.

The record asserts that the closed unit disc over an algebraically closed complete field of characteristic zero has no nontrivial finite étale covers. This is false over C_p. It must not be used as evidence for a new source error.

**Counterexample.** Consider A = C_p<T> and B = C_p<U>, with T mapping to U^p - U. The algebra B is A[U]/(U^p - U - T), finite free of rank p with the usual monic basis; the convergent presentation is obtained by eliminating the bounded coordinate T. Its derivative p U^{p-1} - 1 is a unit, since p U^{p-1} has Gauss norm less than 1. Therefore Spa(B) -> Spa(A) is finite étale. B is an integral domain and the degree is p > 1, so the cover is connected and nontrivial.

There is also a direct section test. A bounded section would reduce to a polynomial h in the residue-field polynomial ring satisfying h^p - h = T. The degree is either 0 or divisible by p, never 1. The use of characteristic zero for C_p does not remove wild phenomena in residue characteristic p.

**Required treatment of E7.** Reject the finding as presently justified and replace its false reason. This does not prove that the intended interpretation of the corrected pro-étale coverings is wrong. That interpretation requires its own correct argument; an étale factor and the distinction between X_et and X_fet should be examined separately. In particular, do not turn this review into a claim that Scholze's intended site has been disproved.

A suitable proposed source-issue review is `verdict: rejected`, with the reason above and `by: REV-AdicEtaleGeometry`. This child review is not yet inserted into the packet.

### REV-AdicEtaleGeometry/F5 — medium: the conormal proof invokes an underived base-change API without its hypotheses

**Where:** `AdicEtaleGeometry:A3/conormal-description`, final proof step; compare `A3/pseudocoherent-module`, API item `Module.IsPseudoCoherent.baseChange_of_isPseudoCoherent_algebra`.

The consumer infers pseudocoherence of I/I^2 = I tensor_R (R/I) from pseudocoherence of I. The cited API explicitly distinguishes restriction of scalars from underived tensor product, and requires higher Tor vanishing for the latter inference. The consumer provides no such vanishing. Stacks tag 064Z is a restriction-of-scalars equivalence for an already specified complex; it does not justify discarding higher derived tensor terms.

The missing hypothesis cannot simply be inserted as a consequence of a regular embedding. For R = C_p<T_1,T_2>, I = (T_1,T_2) and S = R/I, the exact resolution

0 -> R -> R^2 -> I -> 0

has first map r -> (-T_2 r, T_1 r). After tensoring with S this map is zero, so Tor_1^R(S,I) = S, not 0. This is an ordinary smooth two-dimensional ball with its closed origin, within the geometric kind of example under discussion. Its conormal module is nevertheless free of rank 2: the example disproves the claimed applicability of that API, not the conormal theorem.

**Required correction.** Establish conormal freeness from the local regular-sequence presentation and explicitly glue the resulting module/sheaf comparison, or supply the precise additional pseudocoherence lemma that this presentation gives. Do not assert blanket Tor independence of I and R/I. Check that the global argument identifies the algebraic module I/I^2 with the locally constructed conormal sheaf before using the affinoid equivalence.

## Focused source-issue checks: E19 and E20

### E19: the literal incomplete-Tate-ring obstruction is supported

ECD arXiv v4, Lemma 15.3, printed p. 90 was read and checked on the rendered page. Its wording of the second assertion does not add completeness of the initial Tate algebra. The algebraic example Q_p[T] with its p-adic topology is materially different from the analytic disc in F4.

Here is a proof route supporting the record's completeness objection. A connected finite étale cover of the algebraic affine line over an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field is trivial: compactify, and apply Riemann-Hurwitz, with ramification possible only above infinity. If its degree is d and there are r points above infinity, the formula gives 2g - 2 = -d - r; hence d = r = 1. Descent then identifies finite étale Q_p[T]-algebras with finite étale Q_p-algebras tensored with Q_p[T]. Thus a nonempty finite étale torsor tower has colimit B[T] with B ind-finite-étale over Q_p.

A prime quotient of B embeds into C_p, giving a bounded map B[T] -> C_p<T>, and hence a map from its uniform completion R to C_p<T>. Were R perfectoid, the image of T would be a p-th power modulo a topologically nilpotent pseudouniformizer. Reduction of the target's power-bounded subring gives the residue-field polynomial ring, where T is not a p-th power. This rules out perfectoidness of R.

The remedy is to complete the base first, or to state the henselian hypothesis actually supplying the finite étale comparison. It is not a counterexample to the completed-base construction of the diamond. This focused check supports E19 against the literal version read; it is not an audit of all A4 tower or descent nodes.

### E20: retain the known Berkeley correction

On the same rendered ECD page the Frobenius step uses P(X) = X^p - varpi^p X - f. Merely assuming varpi^p divides p does not make P étale: with varpi^p = p and f = 1 - p, one has P(1) = P'(1) = 0.

Requiring p/varpi^p to be topologically nilpotent makes P' a unit at the integral root, by the geometric-series argument. Berkeley Lectures, Lemma 10.1.6 and its proof on printed p. 75 explicitly include the stronger choice. That passage was read in parsed text; its screenshot was unavailable. The existing `known` attribution to Berkeley should be retained. This is a confirmed, already-recorded correction, not a claim of novelty.
