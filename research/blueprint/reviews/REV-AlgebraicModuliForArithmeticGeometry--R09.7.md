## Corrections

1. **Certificate diagram versus initial diagram.** The `samuelCertificate_hilbert` API incorrectly said conditions (1)–(3) identify the actual initial diagram. Bierstone–Milman Lemma 7.5, printed p. 263, identifies the Hilbert–Samuel function with the complement count of the chosen division diagram. These assertions differ. For I=(x+y) in Q[[x,y]], supported division can use the diagram generated by (1,0), with remainders in Q[[y]]. The graded-lex initial exponent of x+y is (0,1). Both diagrams have the same complement counts. This example satisfies conditions (1)–(3); it need not satisfy the essential-variable condition (4). The packet now makes that distinction, its source match says chosen division-diagram vertices, and the suggested file adds `certificate_division_not_initialDiagram`. The existing Lean Hilbert signature already states the correct complement-count conclusion and was retained.
2. **Pointed exceptional tests and continuations.** The equivalence definition now names the unique point over a on the strict transform of the chosen H₁, as in (4.4)(iii), p. 242. Its transformation API preserves the continuation policy after the first test. In the restricted s* case, that policy retains the exceptional-block history and fixed old boundary label from Definition 4.10, pp. 243–244. The numerical Lean interface already demands that its extension map take remaining tests into the original allowed set; its comment now explains the geometric history condition still to be supplied.
3. **Samuel centre containment.** The transform statement now says C is contained in the Samuel stratum, equivalently I_C contains its ideal. This removes an ambiguous use of “centre containing equations”; Theorems 7.20–7.21, pp. 268–269, use this direction of ideal inclusion.
4. **Explicit Jacobson dependency.** Removed the duplicate SF.0 prerequisite on `global-centres` and replaced the second occurrence by the actual `SchemeAndStackFoundations:SF.0/jacobson-finite-type` node. Its statement supplies Jacobson finite-type schemes over a field and finite closed-point residue extensions. General coherent localization/gluing remains the other SF.0 prerequisite and an explicit request.
5. **Current Cartier API reuse.** Current Tau Ceti already defines `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.IdealSheafData.IsEffectiveCartier` for arbitrary schemes in `TauCeti/AlgebraicGeometry/EffectiveCartierDivisor/Basic.lean`. Its definition, neighbourhood refinement, affine-comap and affine-open-immersion characterizations, unit ideal and flat-comap statements were read. The SF.3 request now imports these interfaces and asks only for the remaining unrestricted additive-divisor/ideal-product bridge and regular-equation pullback compatibility. A new upstream note records current commit a91d3aafa8cd3e6bc33dfde0d7677ed0f1625039. The module is absent at the pinned Tau Ceti commit, so it is not presented as a pinned baseline citation. The suggested `CartierIdeal` is explicitly a pinned-baseline adapter to replace during packaging. Current `Scheme.CartierDivisor.IsEffective` and its additive API require `IsIntegral`, so they cannot silently restrict the separation target.

## Sources and proof closure

The full published [Bierstone–Milman 1997 article](https://mahalex.net/teaching/seminars/gabber/Bierstone-Milman%20Canonical%20desingularization%20in%20characteristic%20zero.pdf), Inventiones mathematicae 128, pp. 207–302, was checked against the nodes' printed-page locators and their invoked proof routes. Its downloaded SHA-256 matches the packet. The reviewed mathematical dependencies are the local coordinate and order calculus, three test policies, maximal contact and weighted coefficients, guarded residual presentations, formal division, all five Samuel-certificate conditions, finite-degree diagram stabilization, determinantal jet comparison, common regular semicoherence, history-sensitive recursion, maximum-centre gluing, termination and the resolution/cleanup outputs. The source's finite-type characteristic-zero algebraic setting is retained; numerical geometric invariants are on closed points.

The fixed-dimension descending Hilbert–Samuel input was checked in [Bierstone–Milman 1989, Uniformization of analytic spaces](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/255647609_Uniformization_of_Analytic_Spaces), Theorem 5.2.1 and proof, pp. 820–821. The proof stabilizes finite vertex prefixes on subsequences and uses finite generation of diagrams; it does not assert stabilization of arbitrary functions or arbitrary rational invariant words.

[Boxer–Pilloni, Higher Hida theory for Siegel modular forms](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~pilloni/higherhidaSiegel.pdf), author PDF dated 5 November 2025, §4.1.10, Proposition-construction 4.1.11 and proof, p. 42, and its use in 4.1.13, pp. 42–43, supplies the Cartier adapter. The PDF hash matches the packet. On a regular-pivot blowup chart, the chart ring is the subring R[I/x_j] of R[1/x_j]. Original nonzerodivisors remain regular in the localization and its subring. The factorization x_i=e a_i therefore makes e and a_i regular. This justifies the effective residual ideals on arbitrary schemes without assuming the blowup is flat. Their sum is the unit ideal because on each of the two charts one residual equation is a unit.

The two source mistakes were checked in the published PDF images as well as the surrounding calculations:

- **E1901**, Example 2.1, p. 226: the parenthesized year-one monomial must use x₂ instead of x₃. The x₁-chart substitution and subsequent displayed calculation establish the intended variable.
- **E1902**, completion of Theorem 9.6's proof, p. 282: the derivative generator bound must be strict, as in Definition 7.9, p. 265. For t² marked 2, the order-two derivative adds a unit, destroying the intended stratum. Strictly lower derivatives generate (t).

These are independent confirmations of the recorded local printing errors, not claims of a discovered published erratum. No restricted source was needed, and no source passages were added to the repository.

The proof graph is closed at its stated target granularity subject to the five named owner interfaces. In particular, finite-jet rank comparison avoids using Hilbert–Samuel semicoherence circularly in the Samuel transform argument; the finite-degree theorem supplies one common algebraic neighbourhood; the residual monomial guard and birth-prefix blocks remain essential; and termination uses both stabilized realized words and decreasing monomial mass. Cleanup uses the stronger centres-in-Σ* argument of Theorem 12.4 and Lemmas 12.7–12.8, pp. 293–295, rather than substituting regular-locus preservation. Theorem 13.2, pp. 297–298, supplies local-isomorphism compatibility with empty steps omitted, not arbitrary smooth-morphism functoriality.

The compactification target is explicitly a deduction from resolution of a chosen projective closure and principalization of its complement. Projectivity and the projective closure are requested separately; properness does not supply projectivity. The analytic polydisc target is likewise a deduction from SNC coordinates and analytic inversion after the small carrier bridge is supplied. Neither is falsely attributed as a quoted theorem of BM1997.
