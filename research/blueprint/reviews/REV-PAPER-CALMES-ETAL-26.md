## Routing: one new roadmap, one Part II, six source routes

The two constructive routes are the ones that need checking, and both hold.

**The new roadmap** `HermitianKTheoryOfPoincareCategories` takes 104 items — Poincaré ∞-categories,
the family of Poincaré structures interpolating between quadratic and symmetric, surgery and the
algebraic Thom construction, genuine versus non-genuine L-theory. I searched every layer title and
description in `data/atlas.json`: "Hermitian K" and "Grothendieck–Witt" occur in **exactly one**
layer, `GeometryOfNumbersAndQuadraticArithmetic:GN.6`, and that layer builds Grothendieck–Witt
groups from exact categories with duality while "retaining invertibility-of-two and regularity
restrictions" — an outcome, not this machine. The only other "L-theory" in the atlas is in
`LogicAndDefinabilityInNumberTheory:LD.0`, where it means a first-order theory.

What makes the route convincing is that the extraction does **not** ignore GN.6: route 8 sends 22
items to GN.6 and GN.2. The paper is split between what that layer already owns and what nothing
owns, which is the treatment section 15 asks for.

**The Part II** `QuadraticArithmeticAtDyadicPrimes` extends
`GeometryOfNumbersAndQuadraticArithmetic` with the theory at the primes over 2. The title
reproduces the parent's atlas title exactly, `algebraicnt` is a galaxy id, and the brief is 260

and mentions the dyadic case only as an acceptance criterion, and GN.6 keeps 2 invertible, while

worked example over the Eisenstein integers.

## The five recorded mistakes

All five are printed exactly as quoted.

- **E2 (misprint, reaching a stated result).** Proposition 1.3.1 on p. 27 is stated for a general
  stable ∞-category: "Let C be a stable ∞-category with a bounded t-structure … Let Q be an
  r-symmetric Poincaré structure on C with duality D: C → C^op …", and its conclusion is "the
  canonical map L^{a,b}_n(C, Q) → L_n(C, Q) is an isomorphism". But parts (i) and (ii) both say
  "Poincaré object in **P_n(D^p(R), Q^{[−n]})**", for a ring R the hypotheses never introduce. The
  statement as printed is about a different category from its own hypotheses and conclusion, so the
  reach is right.
- **E5 (gap, the proof).** "The discriminant of K is (3), and as (2) does not divide (3), we deduce
  that (2) is a prime ideal in R [Neu99, Corollary III.2.12]" — quoted verbatim from p. 46. Not
  dividing the discriminant gives unramifiedness, and an unramified rational prime in a quadratic
  field is either inert or split; the extraction's counterexample, Q(√−7), settles that the
  inference does not follow. The conclusion is nonetheless true for the Eisenstein integers, which
  is why recording this as a gap rather than an error is the right call.
- **E1** (p. 18): "…is also injective (hence bijective) when **n ≤ 2n − 3**", where the bound is on
  m; **E3** (p. 23): "Using **Remark** 1.1.6", where 1.1.6 is printed as "1.1.6. **Lemma**" and is
  cited correctly as a Lemma five times elsewhere; **E4** (p. 47): the kernel of ·⟨1,3⟩ "is
  additively generated by 2⟨1⟩ and **⟨1,−2⟩**", where ⟨1,3⟩ is the norm form of Q_2(√−3) and the
  generator should be ⟨1,−3⟩.
