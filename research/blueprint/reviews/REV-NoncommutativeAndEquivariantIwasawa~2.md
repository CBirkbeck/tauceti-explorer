### Multiplicative logarithm

The odd-prime, unramified coefficient-domain restrictions remain in force. For the dimension-one reduction, write G=H⋊Γ and choose central Z=Γ^(p^e). Norms give the kernel tuple `(ζ,g) ↦ (ζ^[G:U_P] ver(g))_P`; a diagonal scalar embedding would be wrong. The additive comparison fixes the last map as ω_A composed with δ. Its abelian top component is untruncated, so central group elements are retained.

The finite abelian integral-logarithm kernel is μ(O) times the finite abelian group. Passing compatibly to the completed abelian group gives μ(O)×A; the quotient of the unit group by that subgroup is torsion-free. This does not say that all kernel group units are torsion. Downward use of the cyclic relations kills the nontrivial α terms, while the trivial component satisfies x_1^p=φ(x_1). The noncyclic equation must keep its central term:

`x_P^(p²|P|) = φ(x_1)^p`.

Thus a power lands in the abelian logarithm kernel, and torsion-freeness of the quotient puts x_P in μ(O)×A_P. The index-p trace argument compares the Teichmüller scalars first and then differences supported on at most two group elements. At odd p, an invariant support of that size has no nontrivial p-orbit; coefficients of invariant basis elements in a trace are p-divisible. Finite quotients then identify the group elements, and chains of norm relations recover the scalar exponents. This is the required kernel argument, rather than a claim that completed group units have finite order.

For the cokernel, put m=|G/Z|, A_C=log α_C(x_C) and U=Σ_C |C|φ(A_C), with A_1=p log x_1. The additive inverse and top-component identity give

`Σ_C |C|A_C = pm log x_top`, and `U = pm φ(log x_top)`.

Consequently the abelian component of δℒ(x) is the ordinary integral logarithm of x_top, which ω_A kills. Conversely, the additive isomorphism and the abelian integral-logarithm image description lift every element in the kernel of ω. The separately recorded finite Oliver/Wall supplier remains needed for that image description; it is not newly proved here.

The replacement Schneider–Venjakob source itself drops the central factor in Lemma 4.9, pp.28–29 (E37). For G=C_p×Γ, Z=Γ^p, the tuple θ(γ) lies in the logarithm kernel and has noncyclic top component γ of infinite order. This refutes the printed conclusion that this component equals 1. The revised proof already keeps the factor and does not rely on that conclusion. Its source-match note now makes this distinction explicit.

### Möbius congruences and canonical descent

The revision correctly abandons Kakde v3, Proposition 98 (12), p.69, as its analytic input. In G=C_(p²)×Γ with Z=Γ^p, the subgroup P=pC_(p²)×0 has p cyclic overgroups whose p-th powers equal P. Their Frobenius zeta terms all reduce to ζ_P modulo p by the index-p congruences. The unweighted sum in (12) therefore reduces to zero, whereas ζ_P is a unit. The identity-ideal fixed pair in the proposed cancellation proof has multiplicity 1−p. This verifies E33 within its stated preprint scope; it is not an allegation about an unread version of record.

The replacement full subgroup sum uses the Möbius function on the subgroup lattice. At fixed conductor, restriction changes the Eisenstein weight by [F:K], so the inputs of weight [L:F]k all reach common weight [L:K]k; U_[F:K] gives the correct trace-index condition. Descending ideals away from the ramified set and grouping by stabilizers leads to the sum Σ_(V≤P) μ(V)r^[P:V]. Its p-divisibility is the finite-group Möbius input. The prime-to-p normalization by powers of 2 is retained consistently.

For the constant term, the chosen idèle and its inverse lie in the prescribed level congruence class. The resulting special cusp has the needed trivial lower-right level factor, so the restriction/U calculation has the stated constant term. An arbitrary-cusp formula does not follow from the standard-cusp formula. RW, Congruences II, p.3 and Lemma 10, pp.10–11, supplies exactly this repair (E34). The finite-conductor calculation checks out.

**One quantifier is still a proof-audit leaf.** RW's theorem on p.2 asserts a single smoothing element working for every even locally constant function. Proposition 4, pp.5–6, needs that fixed element; the explicit special-cusp construction depends on a conductor. This review has not proved the passage to a common element. Gap 20 states that obligation precisely. The plan imports the source theorem's stated contract and does not present conductor-dependent choices or compactness as a proof of uniformity. This qualifies target-level acceptance and prevents a claim of gap-free closure.

The completed-to-canonical comparison is now an actual construction. Start with an RW completed K₁ preimage b and a canonical localized characteristic lift f with boundary −[C]. Abelian main conjectures and base-change comparisons show that ζ_P/θ_P(f) is an integral unit, as is its inverse. Its completed tuple is θ(b/f_B), hence satisfies the completed image relations. Injectivity modulo every p^n and contraction of the trace ideals bring those relations back to the integral tuple. For the central coefficient ring, denominators stay regular modulo p^n; the trace action has an equivariant finite coset basis and orbit-stabilizer p-powers. This proves the required contraction, including p times the trace ideal. The θ image theorem then lifts the tuple to an integral class z, and zf is the desired canonical-localized class. There is no assumed inverse of a completion map.

The patching criterion needed one small correction here: **component localization injections are hypotheses of the entire criterion**. They let integral component classes be treated as subgroups of their localized groups and make the integral preimages unique. The revised argument uses those injections for the intersection statement and its converse. The applications already carry this hypothesis, so no claimed arithmetic scope changes.

### Determinants and leading terms

BV's relative pair is `[C,a]` with a:1→d(C), boundary −[C], and order Σ_i(−1)^(i+1)rank H^i(C)^Γ. In the elementary complex `[R --T→R]` in degrees −1,0, the characteristic term has order −1; shifting reverses the order and determinant line. This elementary test detects the sign convention before arithmetic comparisons enter.

The global FK map has target d(RΓ_c)^−1. With D=RΓ_c[1], it becomes a:1→d(D) by the determinant shift identification. The given map is transported, not monoidally inverted. Its pair has boundary +[RΓ_c] and order the negative of the unshifted order. For the rank-one Tate comparison, B²=−ℓ/c_γ and the inverse-line Bockstein trivialization is −c_γ/ℓ. Composing the supplied ζ=L*ℓ with this and the leading sign gives c_γL*. Thus the normalization is c_γ^−1, and in rank m it is c_γ^(−m). Replacing γ by γ^a multiplies the raw term by a^m and c_γ by a, leaving this normalized value unchanged. These calculations confirm E36 for the specified repair of the ill-typed source formulas, not for an unspecified alternative convention.

For the cyclotomic Selmer complex SC, semisimplicity and a nondegenerate height of rank h give T-adic blocks in degrees 1,2. Their ordinary torsion cohomology is in degree 2, so r(SC)=−h; for SC[1] the order is h. Its determinant is the inverse Selmer determinant and the Bockstein trivialization contributes the height determinant rather than its reciprocal. The leading sign (−1)^h is retained. The critical interpolation formula keeps both archimedean and p-adic periods/regulators, Γ-factor and local Euler-factor ratio, under the full named global/local zeta, semisimplicity and regulator hypotheses. The published Theorem 6.5's (D2) must be retained unless its precise replacement comparison has been supplied. This is a conditional theorem target, not unconditional zeta existence.
