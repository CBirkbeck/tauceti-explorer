## Verdicts

Reading the source rather than a rendered PDF settled several of these outright, because the neighbouring
text names the right object.

- **E3 (Lemma 4.7).** The definition of outer exponent on p. 13 is the smallest `e | d` such that the powering
  action of `(Z/d)^×` on conjugacy classes factors through `(Z/e)^×`. For `p = 2` that action factors through
  the trivial group `(Z/2)^×`, so the outer exponent is 1, not 2 — and the proof's opening step, "as the
  abelianization of `U¹` has exponent `p`, the outer exponent is divisible by `p`", fails for the same reason.
  The lemma is false at `p = 2`, so `a stated result` is right; and the use downstream is unaffected, since
  `e` enters only through the requirement that `L` contain the `e`-th roots of unity, vacuous for
  `e ∈ {1, 2}`.
- **E4.** "the exponent of `U¹` is divisible by 9" must be "divides 9": the inference that follows needs
  `(Z/9)^×` to be the acting group, and the kernel of `(Z/9)^× → (Z/3)^×` is `{1,4,7}`, generated by 4, which
  is exactly why showing `M⁴ ~ M` suffices.
- **E6, E7, E8, E11, E14, E15.** Each is settled by the surrounding text. The lift in E6 is of a homomorphism
  to `U/Z`, and the obstruction group named in the same sentence is the one for lifting to `U`; the map in E7
  is named `ρ_{u,v}` correctly between its two wrong occurrences; the vertical map under E8's arrow lands in
  `H¹(k, P̂^{r,s})`, the `W` side; `P_4` in E11 is defined one sentence earlier as spanned by the `e_{i,j}`
  with `j ≤ 4`, whose indices run over `0,…,4`, so the matrices are `5 × 5`; in E14 the paper's own
  conventions — `U^m` generated by the `e_{i,j}` with `j − i > m`, and `e_{i,j}` corresponding to
  `Hom_R(N_j, N_i)` — force the eigenspaces to be `Hom_R(N_{i+m+1}, N_i)`; and in E15 the two families
  overlap at `Hom_R(N_n, N_0)`, so the expression is not a direct sum.
- **E9 (archimedean places).** The gap is real and the source pins it exactly: p. 19 fixes "by a *local
  field*, we mean a complete discretely valued field with finite residue field", so Proposition 5.3 is
  available only at the finite places, while the reduction quantifies over "any place `v` of `k`". The
  archimedean cases are easy in the way the correction says, but they do need saying.
- **E10 (Lemma 5.5).** `Hom(C,A)` with the discrete topology is a discrete `Γ`-module only when the
  stabilizers are open, which finite generation of `C` guarantees and which can fail otherwise, so the class
  `[γ] ∈ H¹(Γ, Hom(C,A))` need not be defined as stated. The only application has `C = P^{r,s}/Z` finite, so
  the added hypothesis costs nothing.
- **E12 (Proposition 7.3).** A defining system has arbitrary cocycle representatives in its adjacent entries
  `a_{i,i+1}`, whereas a lift of the *fixed* `α : Γ_k → A(W)` has those entries pinned, so the asserted
  bijection needs the restriction the correction states. The existence statements used later are unaffected.
- **E13.** Theorem 7.4 opens "Fix `n ≥ 3` and let `R = F_p`" and then speaks of classes in `H¹(k, M_{i,i+1})`
  without introducing `k`; §7 opens with generalities over `Γ_k` for an unspecified field, so `k` is not fixed
  earlier, and the proof runs through the number-field results of §5.
- **E1, E2, E5, E16.** Verbatim, and each correction is the evident one.

## What this does to the main results

I agree with the errata. The Massey vanishing theorem for number fields is not in doubt. Three findings reach
a stated result — Lemma 4.7 at `p = 2` (E3), Lemma 5.5 without a finiteness hypothesis (E10) and
Proposition 7.3's bijection (E12) — and in each case the corrected statement is the one the applications use.
One reaches a proof (E9), with an easy repair. The other twelve are misprints.
