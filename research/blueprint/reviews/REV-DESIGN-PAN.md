## Strictness: the blocking source error

Pan II v1 Lemma 3.4.3, pp. 28–29, proposes an equivalence between strictness of a continuous Banach map `i:A→B` and strictness of `F=(i,wi):A⊕A→B`, where `w` is a continuous involution. For Banach spaces over `K=Q_p`, a continuous linear map is strict exactly when its image is closed: the forward implication follows from completeness of the Banach quotient, and the reverse from open mapping onto the closed image.

Put `E=c₀(N≥1,K)` with the supremum norm, and define `T:E→E` by `(Tx)_n=p^n x_n`. This map is continuous and injective. Its image contains all finite-support sequences and is dense. It is not closed: `y=(p^n)` belongs to E and is the limit of its finite truncations in `T(E)`, but its unique preimage would be `(1,1,…)`, which is not in E.

1. **F strict does not imply i strict.** Set `A=B=E⊕E`, `i(x,y)=(x,Ty)` and `w(u,v)=(v,u)`. The image of i is `E⊕T(E)`, so i is not strict. However `F((x,0),(y,0))=(x,y)` gives a continuous right inverse to F. Thus F is surjective and strict.
2. **i strict does not imply F strict.** Set `A=E`, `B=E⊕E`, `i(x)=(x,Tx)` and `w(u,v)=(u,−v)`. The image of i is a closed graph, and projection onto the first coordinate is a continuous inverse. But `F(x,y)=(x+y,T(x−y))` has image `E⊕T(E)`, since 2 is invertible in Q_p, including when p=2. Hence F is not strict.

The printed proof treats composition of strict maps as strict without the needed extra conditions. Both counterexamples use injective i and continuous involutions, so adding injectivity does not fix the criterion.

The replacement must prove closed range of the actual geometric restriction map (Lemma 3.4.2), the sum of Čech images and its weight-χ version (§3.4.4), and strictness compatible with the truncated period filtrations (Corollaries 6.2.10–6.2.11). Preservation of closed embeddings by analytic vectors does not prove closedness of a sum, or that a weight-isotypic summand is a topological direct factor. The packet deletes the false API lemma, marks LA.3 and LA.11 partial, and adds the conditional Banach quotient universal property. Downstream continuity, Hodge–Tate quotient and Fontaine-cohomology assertions remain conditional.
