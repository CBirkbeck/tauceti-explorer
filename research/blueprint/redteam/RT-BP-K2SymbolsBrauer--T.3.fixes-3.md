The three base-change controls are substantive:

1. **Ramified self-base change:** t=s² over ℚ and F′=E. There are two generic factors, each with relative index 1; the residue tensor is ℚ of length 1 and r=2. The sum is 2, not 1.
2. **Residue larger than the compositum:** s²=t, u²=2t. Over F′=ℚ((u)) the single compositum is ℚ(√2)((u)); l_i has degree 2 over L_j=ℚ although both original residues are ℚ. Both relative indices are 1, r=2, and the sum is 2. Replacing l_i by L_j fails.
3. **Nonreduced residue tensor:** k=F₂(a), F=k((t)), E=F(α), α²+tα+a=0. The derivative is t≠0 and reduction X²+a is irreducible over k. R[α] is free on 1,α with maximal ideal (t), hence a DVR; thus e=1,f=2 and residue l=k(√a). Self-base-change has separable generic tensor E×E, but l⊗k l=l[ε]/ε² has length 2. Here e=r=e_i=e′_i=1 and two degree-one residue factors give 2=1·2. Reducing away the nilpotent multiplicity fails.

The three actual Lean matrix controls compute determinants −t, a and u² for multiplication on these proposed quadratic bases. They prove the matrix identities only; they do not claim a formalized Laurent-series extension or general norm theorem.
