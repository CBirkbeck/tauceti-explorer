## 1. Theorem 8.5 still promises a false unrestricted exceptional list

**High; error.** Location: item `extraneous-exceptional`, route 2
`SmoothRepresentationsPartIIUnitarySpherical`, and the corresponding reader
endpoint. Route 3 must also retain an explicit dependency gate where it uses
this list in the conditional nongeneric conclusions.

The item says that, under geometricity and connected center, no extraneous
point occurs for G2 or E6. Route 2 asks the designer to finish Theorem 8.5.
Unlike `generic-local-temperedness` and `nonsplit-temperedness`, this endpoint
has not been restricted to real Satake parameters. The adjacent E11 caveat
in the route concerns the earlier local temperedness tests; it does not
correct this item or the claimed exhaustive list.

The source itself asserts, in Remark 8.7 (p.30),
“there are no extraneous points for G2 or E6”. However, the G2 parameter
already supplied in E11 is geometric in the full Definition 2.5 sense and is
an extraneous point of the zero orbit. The additional argument is as follows.

### A reproducible witness

Take split G2 over a local function field with residue cardinality q > 1.
Its center is trivial, hence connected, and its dual is again G2. Use the
short simple root α1 and long simple root α2 of the dual root system; these
are the coroot coordinates used in E11. Define

\[
  (a\alpha_1+b\alpha_2)(s_c)=(-1)^a,
  \qquad \langle a\alpha_1+b\alpha_2,\nu\rangle=b/2,
  \qquad s=s_cq^\nu.
\]

Thus ν is E11's ω2/2. The positive-root values are:

| Root coordinates (a,b) | Value at s |
| --- | --- |
| (1,0) | −1 |
| (0,1) | q^(1/2) |
| (1,1) | −q^(1/2) |
| (2,1) | q^(1/2) |
| (3,1) | −q^(1/2) |
| (3,2) | −q |

Negative-root values are their inverses. In particular no root value is q
or q^−1. The compact centralizer has roots ±α2 and ±(2α1+α2), of type
A1 × A1. The real-part stabilizer has order two, generated by the reflection
in α1, which does not fix s_c; consequently W_s is trivial.

These calculations were independently checked over the integers by generating
the Weyl group from its action on root coordinates:

\[
 r_1(a,b)=(-a+3b,b),\qquad r_2(a,b)=(a,a-b).
\]

The enumeration gives |W| = 12, |W_(s_c)| = 4, |W_ν| = 2 and |W_s| = 1;
each of the 12 matrices preserves the twelve-root set. To reproduce the
stabilizer computation, test preservation of a modulo 2 and of b on the two
simple roots. This is a finite arithmetic check, not Lean formalisation.

Kato's Theorem 2.2 (p.935) gives irreducibility when the c-function numerator
has no zero at the parameter or its inverse and the stabilizer is generated
by the denominator-root reflections. In this equal-parameter split case the
conditions are precisely the absence of root values q^±1 and the indicated
stabilizer condition. At s the stabilizer is trivial. Along s_c q^(tν),
0 < t ≤ 1, it remains trivial and the same root test excludes reducibility.
At t = 0, the stabilizer is the A1 × A1 Weyl group generated by the roots
whose value is 1, so Kato's second condition also holds there.

The longest Weyl element is −1. It fixes the involution s_c and reverses
tν, so this is a hermitian family. At t = 0 it is unitarily induced.
The irreducible deformation argument used in Lemma 6.7 (p.18) therefore
preserves positivity up to t = 1. Thus L(s) = X(s) is unitary and generic.
This verifies the representation-theoretic part of E11 independently at its
irreducibility input; it does not assume the false Corollary 6.32.

### Why it satisfies the stronger arithmetic hypothesis

For G2 the weight lattice equals the root lattice. Every torus weight in
every algebraic representation is aα1+bα2 with a,b integers. Its eigenvalue
at s is therefore (−1)^a q^(b/2). This is an algebraic number all of whose
complex conjugates have absolute value q^(b/2), hence a Weil q-number of
integer weight b. The unramified parameter with Frobenius s satisfies
Definition 2.5 (p.7). This is stronger than merely checking the real
half-lattice condition. One can realize the parameter over Q̄_ℓ for ℓ
different from the residue characteristic; these eigenvalues are ℓ-adic
units, as required for its unramified continuous realization.

### Why its orbit is zero and its real part is extraneous

The q-eigenspace of Ad(s) is zero: the table and its inverses exclude q,
and the Cartan subalgebra has eigenvalue 1. In the definition (8.1) on p.27,
the open orbit in that eigenspace is consequently {0}. Hence s lies in
Sph(G2,0), and its unitarity puts it in CS(G2,0).

For the zero nilpotent orbit the central point is h/2 = 0. But the real part
of s is ν ≠ 0. It is therefore extraneous by Definition 8.4 (p.28). This
contradicts the extraction's unrestricted no-G2 assertion, with both of its
advertised hypotheses satisfied.

The source proof of Theorem 8.5 (p.29) applies the real generic classification
of Theorem 6.14 to the relevant centralizer. For the zero nilpotent orbit
that centralizer is G2 itself, and this is exactly where forgetting the
noncentral compact part repeats the E11 problem. Theorem 8.3 does not repair
it: its CS(G,0)_0 is a set of real parts of possibly non-real parameters.

### Actionable correction

Propagate E11 to Theorem 8.5 and Remark 8.7. Replace the unrestricted
`extraneous-exceptional` endpoint and update the route and reader together.
Retain the certified real-parameter classification with an explicit real or
central-compact hypothesis; leave the general compact-part classification
as a separate unresolved target until the centralizer-of-s_c reduction and
all arithmetic restrictions have actually been established. Do not merely
add G2 to an otherwise asserted complete list without doing that analysis.

Audit `extraneous-Sp`, `extraneous-Spin` and `E8-extra-region` at the same
interface rather than assuming the present witness settles those cases.
Propagate the gate to uses of Theorem 8.5 in the global route, preserving the
already recorded real-part-versus-stratum gap. Extend E11's affected locators
or add a linked source issue scoped to v1. The witness above should become a
regression test: full geometricity and connected center do not imply the
current exceptional list. No failure of a global automorphic theorem follows
from this local witness alone.

## 2. Theorem 4.2 has no arithmetic supplier in its assigned local route

**Medium; missing.** Location: `galois-discrete-series`, assigned to route 2;
route 2's import contract; and route 3's arithmetic prefix.

The route owning Theorem 4.2 imports ordinary smooth representation theory,
the unitary dual, nilpotent orbits and semisimple local parameters. None of
its named imports supplies the globalization and arithmetic coefficient
argument needed for this item. This is visible without treating the source's
unproved sketch as a proof: its first step on p.11 obtains a number-field
model by globalizing the discrete series using Beuzart-Plessis's appendix
to GHS and then using the cusp coefficient theorem. The later steps require
geometric local parameters, embedding-independent Weil absolute values,
compatibility with induction and Casselman's criterion (pp.11–12).

The item's own note correctly names the GHS appendix and the existing E10
gap. That attribution is useful source evidence, but does not give route 2
an import edge, a shared prerequisite contract or a place to construct the
missing arithmetic interface. The source requires an independently justified
discrete-series theorem; the general geometricity assertion in the sketch
cannot be used as a substitute while E10 remains open.

The already planned coefficient theory has a precise owner:
`FunctionFieldArithmetic:FA.6`. Geometricity, `ghs-discrete-series-purity`,
and the corresponding Galois-temperedness work are assigned to route 3,
`GlobalShtukasPartIIRamanujanArthur`, whose brief explicitly consumes the
unitary-spherical route. Thus repairing the omission by making the entire
local extension consume the entire global extension would introduce a
two-way roadmap dependency. This is a defect in the proposed supplier plan,
not a claim that a corresponding cycle already exists among live stages.

The neighboring GHS extraction is a lead, not a settled escape hatch.
`PAPER-GAN-HARRIS-SAWIN-ETAL-24` has a **revise** verdict and its
`GlobalShtukasPartIISupercuspidalParameters` candidate is held for revision.
Its current brief expressly leaves globalization suppliers to be closed.
The Ciubotaru–Harris purity item's own note also acknowledges that owner is
not yet accepted. Importing the whole proposed GHS roadmap as though it were
an available theorem would obscure these obligations.

### Actionable correction

Move `galois-discrete-series` to the early arithmetic portion of route 3,
before its local-to-global applications. That portion already owns the
closely related `galois-temperedness` and geometricity work. Import FA.6 for
coefficient models, GS.5/GS.7 for parameters and compatibility, and SR.2/SR.3
for induction and Casselman's inequalities. Name a source-gated GHS
globalization/discrete-series-purity supplier with its group and central
character hypotheses. Coordinate that shared supplier with the pending GHS

Alternatively keep Theorem 4.2 in the local extension only after specifying

dependency on either Part II's final Ramanujan/Arthur conclusions. In either
arrangement record the stage order and use the independently established
