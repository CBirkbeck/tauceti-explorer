## Finding /1: allow repeated CM fields

`model-ring` now uses a finite indexed collection, permitting repeated field
isomorphism classes. `model-comparison-conjecture` indexes by pairwise
non-isogenous simple abelian factors, retaining their individual ranks and
central idempotents. The GN.2 source route and finite-field design brief use
the same convention.

For the regression, over F_5 the curves y²=x³+x and y²=x³+4x have respectively
4 and 8 points, traces 2 and −2 and distinct Frobenius polynomials. They are
ordinary and non-isogenous, while both Frobenius fields are Q(i). The
F_5-defined automorphism (x,y)↦(−x,2y) places i in each endomorphism order,
which is therefore Z[i]. Their product uses Z[i]×Z[i], with two central
idempotents. M_2(Z[i]) would have the wrong center and multiplication.

## Finding /2: distinguish Lee's theorem from its proof support

New **E22** is scoped to [Lee arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.04420v3),
§2.1 p.3 and §3.2 p.6 (11). It records the repeated-root counterexample and
its use in Corollary 3.3 p.8 and Theorem 3.4 p.9. E7, E21,
`main-unpolarized-source` and T2 of the design brief now retain the corrected
`2^(34g²) p^((69/4)g²(1+o(1)))` route. The optional `45/4` enhancement
requires a multiplicity-sensitive replacement or a separate repeated-factor
argument before adoption on this evidence.

The curve E/F_2: y²+y=x³ has three points and polynomial X²+2. In E^g the
roots ±i√2 each occur g times, so the 2g² ordered unequal-root pairs have
product of absolute values `2^(3g²)`. At g=16, (11) would bound `2^768` by
`32^32 · 2^496 = 2^656`. The asymptotic normalized exponents are also
different, 3 versus 2. A full Vandermonde bound, whose product is zero when
roots repeat, cannot justify deleting the zero factors. Lemma 3.1 itself is
not disproved.

This is a false intermediate estimate and a resulting proof-support gap,
**not a counterexample to the final 45/4 counting theorem**. The
[published IMRN abstract](https://academic.oup.com/imrn/article-abstract/2022/6/4290/5865446)
states that theorem. Article and candidate PDF endpoints returned HTTP 403;
the published proof was not obtained. The defect is not attributed to unread
journal pages.

## Finding /3: state the uniformity hypothesis

`model-count-source` and `model-concentration-source` now require every L_i
to be generated by a p-Weil number for the **same fixed p**. The GN.3 and
ST.5 source routes and finite-field brief carry that restriction. It belongs
to these applications; the general model-ring definition still accepts
arbitrary CM fields.

LT v1 p.27 (40) uses the Weil generator to control the discriminant. Without
that restriction the O_p(g²) constant and one g_0 depending only on p are
not justified. At p=2, quadratic Weil generators have integral traces
t∈{0,±1,±2}, and discriminants t²−8∈{−8,−7,−4}. The general definition
admits Q(√−163), whereas this restricted quadratic family does not. The
existing mass formula, residue-ratio and supplier obligations remain imports;
the fix does not claim to have proved Gan–Yu or the mass formula.

## Finding /4: register both quaternionic corrections

New **E23** records the finite maximal compact: after splitting D_0 at finite
K_0-places, use ∏_v GL_(2d)(O_(K_0,v)). Over Q_ℓ this is restriction of
scalars from K_0⊗Q Q_ℓ, of dimension 8d², rather than GL_(4d), of dimension
16d². An embedding of representations does not provide the printed group
isomorphism.

New **E24** records the narrow class group in the d≥2 reduced-norm
comparison. Global norms over the totally definite quaternion algebra are
positive at both real places. For K_0=Q(√3), the Minkowski bound √12/2<2
gives ordinary class number one, but x²−3y²=−1 is impossible modulo 3,
giving narrow class number two. The exact class sets differ. The bounded
narrow/ordinary ratio leaves the coarse fixed-p exponential estimate intact.
The existing corrected `nonabelian-class-comparison` statement and separate
d=1 quaternion class set are retained. Both entries are scoped to
[LT v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.02212v1) p.14; the Duke journal PDF endpoint
returned non-PDF HTML, so no journal comparison is claimed.

## Finding /5: narrow E17's proof criticism

E17 and `elliptic-pgroups-large` now state precisely that the displayed proof
works for p≥5. The Hasse intervals put the first group order strictly between
1 and p² and the second strictly between p and p³, forcing the p-powers
p and p². Thus a=b=1; the identity b=a²−2p supplies exactly the printed
contradiction. The original unqualified Hasse inference has a small-prime
gap. At p=3, the possible p-group orders over F_3 are 1 and 3, giving a=3
or 1 and orders 7 or 15 over F_9; neither is a 3-power.

The existing all-odd-p factorization proof remains, as does the genuine
p=2 example y²+xy=x³+x²+1 with two points over F_2 and eight over F_4.
The current reader is synchronized; the historical checkpoint section is
preserved byte-for-byte, including its attributed earlier repairs.
