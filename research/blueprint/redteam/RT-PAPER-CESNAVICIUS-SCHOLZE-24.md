## Method

**The source.** arXiv 1912.10932v3, the final version (<https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.10932v3>), was re-downloaded on
2026-10-01 with its LaTeX source. Its SHA-256 is `2f9d3ee8…5cd6ed4`, equal to the extraction's. The published Annals
text is paywalled and was not read, so misprint findings are scoped to v3.

**The passes.** Six parallel passes were run by this session.
- Five read all 97 pages against the extraction: §§1–2, §§3–4, §§5.1–5.3, §§5.4–5.7, and §§6–7 with the references.
- The sixth checked the seven routes, the two new roadmaps, all planned and library statuses and a sample of missing
  ones, against the atlas, the packets, the accepted restructures, `make_queue.py`, other papers' accepted routes and
  the pinned libraries.
- Bhatt–Mathew's arc-topology paper (arXiv 1807.04725v4) was read for the formal-glueing finding.

**What held up.** Both new roadmaps are justified; ArcTopologyAndDescent joins the accepted Bhatt–Mathew roadmap. Every
missing item is routed exactly once. The main theorems of §§6–7 match the paper's hypotheses and index bounds, and the
recorded mistakes are rightly confirmed.

**Merging.** I merged findings reported by more than one pass:
- item 028 (formal glueing), item 063's status, and Raynaud's embedding theorem;
- Ferrand–Raynaud patching, and route 1's reason on absolute purity;
- the library statuses of items 001 and 007, and the Henselization of pairs and animated rings.

**What I re-verified myself, in the TeX or the atlas:**
- item 028's square with A[1/f] for arbitrary f ∈ I, against Bhatt–Mathew Theorem 6.4 (the punctured spectrum);
- Corollary 5.6.13's hypotheses "R is I-Henselian, R′ is IR′-Henselian", which item 115 drops;
- the "p-power order" hypothesis of Theorem 5.3.5 and Corollary 5.3.7, which route 2's brief drops;
- PAPER-ZHU-17 route 16 and PAPER-VANHOFTEN-24 route 8, which send Gabber–Lau Dieudonné theory to the FiniteFlatGroups
  Part II.

**Severities I changed.** The single-item duplicates of the Cohen structure theorem (004) and parafactoriality (133) are
medium, not high. The full list of what was checked is in the result's `checked` field.

## The high findings

### /1 — error

**Where.** PAPER-CESNAVICIUS-SCHOLZE-24/028; PAPER-CESNAVICIUS-SCHOLZE-24/028 (statement and note; locator includes
proof of Lemma 5.4.2, p. 65)

**Claim.** The statement of 'Arc descent gives formal glueing' is false as written and is not the cited theorem. It
forms the square with F(A[1/f]) and F(A'[1/f]) for an arbitrary f ∈ I, whereas formal glueing uses Spec(A) ∖ V(I) and
Spec(A') ∖ V(IA'). It omits that I is finitely generated and that A/I^n ≅ A'/I^nA' for every n. Its hypothesis
'(I-complete) arc descent' is not Bhatt–Mathew's: their Theorem 6.4 is for arc-sheaves on qcqs schemes, finitary and
valued in an ∞-category compactly generated by cotruncated objects, and Corollary 6.7 covers all arc-sheaves. The paper
applies it only to étale cohomology, which is a plain arc sheaf by Theorem 2.2.5. Counterexample to the item: take k
algebraically closed, ℓ ≠ char k, F = RΓ_ét(−, ℤ/ℓ) (an arc sheaf), A = k[x], I = (x), A' = k[[x]] (so A/I^n ≅ A'/I^n)
and f = x(x−1) ∈ I. Then A'[1/f] = k((x)), and the square is Cartesian iff RΓ_{V(f)}(A) → RΓ_{V(f)}(A') is an
isomorphism. But H^2_{V(f)}(𝔸^1, ℤ/ℓ) = H^1(𝔸^1 ∖ {0,1}, ℤ/ℓ) = (ℤ/ℓ)^2 (Kummer classes of x and x−1), while H^2 with
support in the closed point of k[[x]] is H^1(k((x)), ℤ/ℓ) = ℤ/ℓ. The restriction kills the class of x−1, an ℓ-th power
in k[[x]]. Also: The item states formal glueing with the square F(A), F(A'), F(A[1/f]), F(A'[1/f]) 'for f ∈ I' when
A/I^n ≅ A'/I^n. That is false and is not what the cited theorem says. Take f = 0 ∈ I: the square then asserts F(A) ≅
F(A'). With A = Z → A' = Z_p, I = (p) (so A/I^n ≅ A'/I^n) and F = RΓ_ét(−, Z/2), which satisfies arc descent (item 026),
this would give H^1_ét(Spec Z, Z/2) ≅ H^1_ét(Spec Z_p, Z/2). The left side is 0 (Minkowski) and the right side is
H^1(F_p, Z/2) = Z/2. It fails for nonzero f as well. With A = C[x, y, 1/(y−1)], I = (x, y), A' = C[[x, y]] and f = x,
the square would force RΓ_{V(x)}(A, Z/ℓ) ≅ RΓ_{V(x)}(A', Z/ℓ). By purity H^3 of these is H^1 of V(x) with Z/ℓ(−1)
coefficients, and V(x) is A^1 ∖ {1} on one side and Spec C[[y]] on the other. Bhatt–Mathew's theorem uses the punctured
spectra Spec(A) ∖ V(I), which equal Spec(A[1/f]) only for I = (f). It is also stated for arc sheaves, not for
'I-complete arc descent'. Every use in this paper is along a principal ideal: a in Lemma 5.4.2, p in Theorem 5.5.1, ϖ in
Theorem 2.2.7. The item also duplicates the Bhatt–Mathew extraction's items without saying so; compare item 029, whose
note does.

**Evidence.** Paper (arXiv v3), proof of Theorem 2.2.7, p. 21: 'which this time uses formal glueing for étale cohomology
in place of Beauville–Laszlo glueing to pass to completions, so, concretely, it uses Theorem 2.2.5 and [BM21, Theorem
6.4]'; proof of Lemma 5.4.2, p. 65: 'since arc descent implies formal gluing squares [BM21, Theorem 6.4]'. Bhatt–Mathew,
The arc-topology, arXiv 1807.04725v4, Definition 1.14: 'A formal glueing datum is given by a pair (R → S, I) where R → S
is a map of commutative rings and I ⊂ R is a finitely generated ideal such that R/I^n ≃ S/I^nS for all n ≥ 0 … square
Spec(S) ∖ V(IS) → Spec(S), Spec(R) ∖ V(I) → Spec(R)'. Theorem 6.4: 'Let 𝒞 be an ∞-category that is compactly generated
by cotruncated objects. Let ℱ: Sch_{qcqs,R}^op → 𝒞 be a finitary arc-sheaf. Then ℱ satisfies formal glueing'. The same
theorem is already PAPER-BHATT-MATHEW-21/22 and /123, both with the correct square. Also: Paper, proof of Lemma 5.4.2,
p. 65: 'Consequently, since arc descent implies formal gluing squares [BM21, Theorem 6.4], the first and the last
squares are Cartesian.' Bhatt–Mathew, arXiv 1807.04725v4, Theorem 6.4: 'Let C be an ∞-category that is compactly
generated by cotruncated objects. Let F: Sch_{qcqs,R}^op → C be a finitary arc-sheaf. Then F satisfies formal glueing,
i.e., if (A → B, I) is a formal glueing datum … then the natural square F(Spec(A)) → F(Spec(B)), F(Spec(A) ∖ V(I)) →
F(Spec(B) ∖ V(IB)) is cartesian.' Definition 1.14 there: 'I ⊂ R is a finitely generated ideal such that R/I^n ≃ S/I^nS
for all n ≥ 0.' The atlas's Bhatt–Mathew extraction records this as PAPER-BHATT-MATHEW-21/21, /22, /24 and /123.

**Fix.** Replace the statement with Bhatt–Mathew's. Let I ⊂ A be finitely generated and A → A' a map with A/I^n ≅
A'/I^nA' for all n ≥ 0. Then every arc-sheaf F carries the square formed by Spec(A') ∖ V(IA') → Spec(A') over Spec(A) ∖
V(I) → Spec(A) to a Cartesian square. (BM Theorem 6.4 needs F finitary, valued in an ∞-category compactly generated by
cotruncated objects; BM Corollary 6.7 covers all arc-sheaves valued in a complete ∞-category.) When I = (f), the opens
are A[1/f] and A'[1/f]. Drop '(I-complete)'. Note on the item that it is PAPER-BHATT-MATHEW-21/22 and /123, as item 029
already does for its Bhatt–Mathew counterpart. Also: Restate item 028 as follows. For an arc sheaf F on qcqs schemes
(Bhatt–Mathew Theorem 6.4 for finitary F; Theorem 1.15 and Corollary 6.7 in general) and a map A → A' with I ⊂ A
finitely generated and A/I^n ≅ A'/I^nA' for all n, the square F(Spec A) → F(Spec A'), F(Spec A ∖ V(I)) → F(Spec A' ∖
V(IA')) is Cartesian. For I = (f) the punctured spectra are Spec A[1/f] and Spec A'[1/f]. Drop '(I-complete)'. Add a
note naming PAPER-BHATT-MATHEW-21/22, /24 and /123 as the same result so that one home is chosen.

### /2 — error

**Where.** routes[1].brief (route 2, SchemeAndStackFoundationsFlatCohomologyPartII), final theorems Theorem 5.3.5 and
Corollary 5.3.7

**Claim.** The brief, which is the design job's specification and must state the final theorems exactly as the paper
does, drops the hypothesis 'G of p-power order' from Theorem 5.3.5 and Corollary 5.3.7. It frames the whole Part II as
'flat cohomology with commutative, finite, locally free coefficients', so it asserts Corollary 5.3.7 for every such G.
That statement is false. Theorem 5.3.5 without the hypothesis happens to stay true: the prime-to-p part is étale over a
p-Henselian ring and Gabber's affine proper base change handles it. But that is not the paper's statement, and the
paper's proof (p^k kills e^*(L_{G/R}); e^*(L_{G/R})[1/p] = 0) uses the hypothesis.

**Evidence.** Paper, Theorem 5.3.5, p. 59: 'For a prime p, a ring R, a commutative, finite, locally free R-group G of
p-power order, and an animated R-algebra A such that π0(A) is p-Henselian, RΓ(A,G) ≅ R lim_n RΓ(A/^L p^n, G)'. Corollary
5.3.7, p. 61: 'let G be a commutative, finite, locally free R-group of p-power order. For each animated R-algebra A such
that π0(A) is p-Henselian, H^i(A,G) ≅ 0 for i ≥ 3 (resp. … for i ≥ 2 if (π0(A)/(p))^red is perfect)'. The brief reads:
'Theorem 5.3.5 (p-adic continuity RΓ(A, G) ≅ R lim RΓ(A/^L p^n, G) for p-Henselian π_0(A)), Corollary 5.3.7 (H^{≥3} = 0,
and H^{≥2} = 0 when (π_0 A/p)^red is perfect)'. The items themselves (096, 097) keep the hypothesis. Counterexample to
the brief's Corollary 5.3.7: take p = 3, R = ℤ_3, G = ℤ/2 (finite étale, so finite locally free and smooth), and A the
3-adic completion of ℤ_3[x^{±1}, y^{±1}, z^{±1}]. A is 3-adically complete, hence 3-Henselian. By Grothendieck's
comparison and Gabber's affine proper base change, H^3_fppf(A, ℤ/2) = H^3_ét(A, ℤ/2) ≅ H^3_ét(𝔽_3[x^{±1}, y^{±1},
z^{±1}], ℤ/2). This group contains the cup product of the Kummer classes of x, y, z (μ_2 = ℤ/2 in characteristic 3). The
cup product is nonzero because its pullback to 𝔽̄_3 generates H^3(𝔾_m^3, ℤ/2) ≅ Λ^3((ℤ/2)^3) by Künneth. The same
construction with the perfection of 𝔽_3[x^{±1}, y^{±1}] gives H^2 ≠ 0 with (A/p)^red perfect.

**Fix.** In the route-2 brief, state Theorem 5.3.5 and Corollary 5.3.7 with 'a commutative, finite, locally free R-group
G of p-power order', and keep 'a prime p, a ring R, an animated R-algebra A with π0(A) p-Henselian'. Copy the wording of
items 096 and 097. While editing, check the other parentheticals in the brief against their items: Theorem 4.1.8 there
also omits 'locally of p-power order' and 'killed by p^n' (item 061). Also qualify the summary's 'p-adic … continuity …
with finite locally free coefficients' to p-power order.

### /3 — error

**Where.** PAPER-CESNAVICIUS-SCHOLZE-24/115 (statement; the Corollary 5.6.12 and 5.6.13 clauses)

**Claim.** Item 115 merges Corollaries 5.6.10, 5.6.12 and 5.6.13 and Example 5.6.11 into one statement and loses
hypotheses on the way. Its last clause reads 'for Noetherian R → R' with R/I^n ≅ R'/I^nR', H^i(U, G) ≅ H^i(U_{R'}, G)
for i ≥ 2 and opens U ⊇ Spec(R) ∖ V(I)'. The Henselian hypotheses of Corollary 5.6.13 (R I-Henselian, R' IR'-Henselian)
are attached only to the preceding Corollary 5.6.12 clause. As recorded, the 5.6.13 clause is false. Counterexample: R =
Q[t]_(t), I = (t), R' = Q[[t]], U = Spec(R), G = μ_2, i = 2. The quaternion class (1+t, −1) lies in Br(R)[2], because 2,
1+t and −1 are units of R. It is nonzero: its residue at the place t = −1 of Q(t) is the class of −1 in Q^×/Q^×2, and
Br(R) ↪ Br(Q(t)). It dies in Br(Q[[t]]) because 1+t is a square there. Since Pic(R') = 0, H^2(R', μ_2) ↪ Br(R'), so a
lift of the class to H^2(R, μ_2) maps to 0, and H^2(R, μ_2) → H^2(R', μ_2) is not injective. The Corollary 5.6.12 clause
also drops 'finitely generated' for I, which the proof needs because it goes through Theorem 5.4.4.

**Evidence.** Corollary 5.6.13, p. 77: 'Let R → R′ be a map of Noetherian rings, let G be a commutative, finite, locally
free R-group, and let I ⊂ R be an ideal such that R is I-Henselian, R′ is IR′-Henselian, and R/I^n ≅ R′/I^nR′ for n >
0.' Corollary 5.6.12, p. 77: 'let I ⊂ π0(A) be a finitely generated ideal such that π0(A) is I-Henselian, π0(A′) is
I(π0(A′))-Henselian, and π0(A)/I ≅ (π0(A)/I) ⊗^L_A A′'. Its proof starts 'The excision of Theorem 5.4.4 …', and Theorem
5.4.4 (p. 67) needs 'each finitely generated ideal I ⊂ π0(A)'.

**Fix.** Split item 115 into three items, each with all hypotheses of the paper. (a) Corollary 5.6.10, with Example
5.6.11 as examples. (b) Corollary 5.6.12: I ⊂ π_0(A) finitely generated, π_0(A) I-Henselian, π_0(A') Iπ_0(A')-Henselian,
π_0(A)/I ≅ (π_0(A)/I) ⊗^L_A A'; then H^i(U_A, G) ≅ H^i(U_{A'}, G) for i ≥ 2. (c) Corollary 5.6.13: R → R' Noetherian, R
I-Henselian, R' IR'-Henselian, R/I^n ≅ R'/I^nR' for all n > 0, and Spec(R) ∖ V(I) ⊂ U ⊂ Spec(R); then H^i(U, G) ≅
H^i(U_{R'}, G) for i ≥ 2. Add all three to route 2 in place of 115.
