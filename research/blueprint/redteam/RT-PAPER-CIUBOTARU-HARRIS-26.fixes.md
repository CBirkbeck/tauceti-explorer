### Fully geometric zero-orbit regression

Use split G2 with short root α₁, long root α₂, q>1, and

`(aα₁+bα₂)(s)=(−1)^a q^(b/2)`, so `s=s_c q^ν`, `ν=½ω₂`.

The positive roots have coordinates `(1,0),(0,1),(1,1),(2,1),(3,1),(3,2)` and values `−1,√q,−√q,√q,−√q,−q`. Negative-root values are their inverses. None equals q or q⁻¹. Along `s_c q^(tν)`, 0<t≤1, roots with positive compact sign have absolute real exponent at most 1/2; roots with |b|=2 have negative sign. This excludes numerator zeros of Kato's equal-parameter c-function on the entire interval.

There is also a stabilizer condition in Kato's Theorem 2.2; avoiding q⁺⁻¹ alone would not suffice. The exact Weyl computation uses root-coordinate reflections `r₁(a,b)=(−a+3b,b)` and `r₂(a,b)=(a,a−b)`. It generates 12 elements. The compact stabilizer has order 4 and is generated by reflections in the denominator roots `±α₂, ±(2α₁+α₂)`; the real stabilizer has order 2, and their joint stabilizer is trivial. Thus at t=0 Kato's root-generated-stabilizer equality holds, and at t>0 both denominator subgroup and full stabilizer are trivial. The induced modules are irreducible throughout. Longest Weyl element −1 fixes the order-two compact factor and reverses tν, giving a hermitian family. At t=0 unitary induction supplies positivity; the irreducible hermitian deformation argument of CH Lemma 6.7 preserves it.

G2's Cartan determinant is 1, so every weight of every algebraic representation is an integral root combination. Its eigenvalues are `±q^(b/2)`, algebraic numbers whose conjugates have absolute value `q^(b/2)`: Weil numbers of integer weight b. For ℓ≠p these are ℓ-adic units and give continuous unramified parameters. This establishes full Definition 2.5 geometricity, beyond a half-integral real-part test.
