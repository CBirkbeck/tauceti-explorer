## Summary

The PDE roadmap (tauceti:TauCetiRoadmap/PDE, 32 stages in six lanes) is well aimed, and AUDIT-40's library citations for it hold at the pinned commits. The red team found 39 findings, 6 of them high. Four statements the roadmap would build are false as written. Poincaré–Wirtinger is claimed for any bounded or finite-measure Ω (two disjoint balls refute it). Interior H² regularity (E-20) is stated without the C¹ coefficient hypothesis it needs. The end-to-end acceptance test says the zero-boundary Dirichlet solution on a ball equals the Newtonian potential, but it is the Green's-function solution. The W^{1,p}(Ω) embeddings are stated without boundary regularity. B-10's Mihlin–Hörmander route cannot consume Mathlib's FourierMultiplier, which is the zero operator for the non-smooth symbols of the Riesz transforms. Two layers are out of date or duplicated. De Giorgi–Nash–Moser (E-23) already has a sorry-free Lean formalization (Armstrong–Kempe, arXiv 2604.05984), which Tau Ceti partly vendors. Hille–Yosida (F-26) is built by the OneParameterSemigroups roadmap. AUDIT-40 is accepted but not yet merged. It over-grades Lane D as built: nonzero boundary data, the adjoint Fredholm parts and the eigenvalue sequence are missing. It also grades the Newtonian potential as exact when only the kernel exists. Boundary infrastructure (Lipschitz domains, surface measure, Gauss–Green), boundary regularity, VMO, Campanato spaces and Bochner-space time derivatives are unplanned. The atlas records no stage edge for the roadmap.

### 1. error, high: `PDE#milestone-a-5`

**Claim.** The roadmap's hypotheses for Poincaré–Wirtinger are false. The document says that ‖u‖ ≤ C‖∇u‖ holds 'modulo constants (Poincaré–Wirtinger, zero mean), for Ω bounded (or of finite measure, or bounded in one direction)', and A-5 asks for Poincaré–Wirtinger 'with explicit constant dependence' with no domain hypothesis at all. Poincaré–Wirtinger needs Ω connected and a regularity hypothesis on Ω, for example a bounded connected Lipschitz domain. Counterexample: in ℝ², take Ω = B((−1,0),1) ∪ B((1,0),1), which is bounded and open, and u = 1 on the first ball and −1 on the second. Every test function on Ω is the sum of test functions on the two balls, so the weak gradient of u is 0. Also ∫_Ω u = 0 and ‖u‖_p = (2π)^{1/p} > 0, so no C works. Explicit constants are known only for special geometries such as convex domains (d/π for p = 2); the Lipschitz-domain proof by compactness gives none. AUDIT-40 repeats the unhypothesised target.

**Evidence.** Stage A-5: 'Poincaré on W^{1,p}_0(Ω) (bounded Ω) and Poincaré–Wirtinger (zero mean), with explicit constant dependence.' README lines 94-97: 'holds on W^{1,p}_0(Ω) (zero trace) or modulo constants (Poincaré–Wirtinger, zero mean), for Ω bounded (or of finite measure, or bounded in one direction). State that side condition explicitly; it is the load-bearing hypothesis'. AUDIT-40 A-5 target: 'Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality (zero mean) with explicit constant dependence'. Wikipedia 'Poincaré inequality' (raw wikitext line 14, read 2026-09-24): 'Assume that 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ and that Ω is a bounded connected open subset of the n-dimensional Euclidean space ℝⁿ with a Lipschitz boundary'; line 52: 'if Ω is a bounded, convex, Lipschitz domain with diameter d, then the Poincaré constant is at most d/2 for p = 1, d/π for p = 2'. Tau Ceti f790474 TauCeti/Analysis/Sobolev/Poincare/Potential.lean:253-257 proves Gilbarg–Trudinger Lemma 7.16 for C¹ u on a bounded convex open Ω (enorm_sub_setAverage_le_of_convex), and line 34 says Poincaré–Wirtinger follows by integrating it, which is the convex route.

**Fix.** In AUDIT-40 (PDE, milestone-a-5), replace the Poincaré–Wirtinger target with two targets. ### 2. error, medium: `PDE#milestone-a-4 and PDE#milestone-a-6`
