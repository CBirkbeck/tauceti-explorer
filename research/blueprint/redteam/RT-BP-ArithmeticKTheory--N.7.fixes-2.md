## The two generation proofs (the review's remaining objection)

Five nodes are added to N.8. All are at target level, with statements,
hypotheses, proof steps citing their sources, prerequisites and acceptance
cases; the definition carries ten API items and five unit tests.

1. **`N.8/tate-norm-filtration`** (definition). The places of a number field
   ordered by norm, the S_m-units U_m (Mathlib's `Set.unit`), the filtration
   K₂^{S_m}(F) by their symbols, the graded tame symbol, the maps α and β and
   Tate's group U₁. The proof steps show that the filtration exhausts K₂(F),
   that α is onto with ∂ ∘ α = β (T.3's convention ∂_v{u, t} = ū), and that α
   kills U₁ (the Steinberg identity {1 + πx, −πx} = 1). Source: Browkin (2000),
   §2; Zhang–Xu (2016), §2.
2. **`N.8/tate-criterion`** (theorem). Tate's Proposition 1 (three conditions
   on subsets W, C, G of U make the graded symbol bijective), Tate's Lemma 1
   (a ≡ b mod v and |N(a − b)| < (N v)² give a/b ∈ U₁) and the descent to
   step 0. Browkin and Zhang–Xu state these without proof, citing Tate's
   appendix to Bass–Tate (Lecture Notes in Mathematics 342), which was not
   obtained. The packet writes out the proofs: reduction to injectivity of β
   on U/U₁; injectivity on the image of C by words in G; C̄ is a subgroup
   containing ⟨Ḡ⟩ and W̄. Browkin's Remark 1 gives the first half of the
   second step.
3. **`N.8/gaussian-tame-kernel-vanishes`** (theorem). K₂(ℤ[i]) = 0. At a place
   of norm at least 5: C = G = {c ≠ 0 : 2N(c) ≤ N v} and W = {i, π₁, …, π_{m−1}};
   the three conditions follow from Tate's Lemma 1 in the form |a| + |b| < N v,
   with the inequalities √(N/2) + 1 < N, N/2 + √(N/2) < N and
   (1 + 1/√2)√N < N. At the place 1 + i: W = {i}, C = G = {1}, because
   i = 1 + (1 + i)·i ∈ U₁. Step 0 is generated by {i, i} = {i, −1} = {i, i}², so
   it is trivial. The source attributes the result to Tate (K-book III.5.2.2,
   and Browkin §1 for the shape of his argument). The choice of sets and the
   inequalities apply the criterion and are written out in full.
4. **`N.8/tame-kernel-of-q-zeta-five`** (theorem). Zhang and Xu's theorem
   K₂(ℤ[ζ₅]) = 0 (Math. Comp. 85 (2016)), proved by Tate's method. The proof
   steps follow their Lemmas 3.1–3.2 and Theorems 3.3–3.7: the sets W_m, C_m
   and G_m; condition (1) for norms ≥ 90 by estimate and below 90 by machine
   check; condition (2) for norms > 364 by estimate and ≤ 364 by the data of
   Tables 1–2; reduction to the six unit symbols; no element of order 2; and
   {ζ, ξ} = {ζ³, 1 − ζ³}² = 1. The paper takes the 2-rank zero from Browkin's
   formula. The packet derives it from Tate's Theorem (6.2) (K₂O_S/2 = 0 for
   S = {(2)} ∪ ∞) and T.5's relative sequence, whose cokernel 𝔽₁₆^× has odd
   order. Skalba's generalised Thue theorem, which Zhang–Xu use to build G_m,
   was not obtained and is a recorded gap.
5. **`N.8/real-quadratic-upper-generation`** (theorem). {−1, −1} and {−1, ε}
   generate K₂(𝓞_{ℚ(√5)}). The proof has two parts:
   - **Killed by 2.** Restriction to E = ℚ(ζ₅) lands in K₂(𝓞_E) = 0, and
     transfer after restriction is multiplication by 2 (K2SymbolsBrauer
     T.4/restriction-transfer-degree).
   - **Two-torsion.** By Tate's Theorem (6.1), an element of order dividing 2
     is {−1, b}. Unramifiedness at the odd primes makes v_𝔭(b) even. Class
     number one (Minkowski: |d| = 5 < 16) and the units ±εⁿ then give
     b ∈ ±εⁿ·2^k·c², and {−1, 2} = 1.

   Tate's Theorem (6.2) gives a cross-check: K₂(𝓞_F)/2 ≅ (ℤ/2)². The
   deduction for the real subfield is not in Zhang–Xu; it is written for this
   packet from the cited nodes.

Neither proof uses a zeta value, the Birch–Tate formula or a Lichtenbaum-type
statement. Zhang–Xu prove unconditionally what Browkin had conjectured under
Lichtenbaum's conjecture. So SpecialValuesBirchTate B.3 can import the ℚ(√5)
certificate as the independent input of its check 1/30 = 4/120, which is what
RT-AREA-ktheory-1/11 requires.
