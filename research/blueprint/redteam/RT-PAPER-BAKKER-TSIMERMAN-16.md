## Findings

### RT-PAPER-BAKKER-TSIMERMAN-16/1 — Separate the two uniformization surjections (high)

**Location:** `research/blueprint/papers/PAPER-BAKKER-TSIMERMAN-16.result.json, item PAPER-BAKKER-TSIMERMAN-16/118 (compare item /29)`.

Item 118 conflates two different surjections. It types gamma_p as Gamma(2,3,p) -> PSL_2(F_p), but assigns it the kernel of the map PSL_2(Z) -> Gamma(2,3,p). The claimed containment of that kernel in Gamma(p) is consequently in the wrong ambient group. This contradicts item 29 and gives the height and cusp arguments the wrong map.

**Evidence.** https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v184-n3-p02-p.pdf#page=9, printed pp.717-718, section 3: the displayed definition is "gamma_p := i_{p*}" (transcribed), from pi_1(Y(1)) to pi_1(X(1)_p). The following paragraph identifies these groups with PSL_2(Z) and Gamma(2,3,p). Printed p.718 separately defines Xi(p) as the image of Gamma(p) and G^0(p)=Gamma(2,3,p)/Xi(p). Algebraic check: T^p is already the identity in Gamma(2,3,p), so its normal closure there is trivial, whereas Xi(p), the compact genus-at-least-two surface group for p>5, is not trivial. These statements were checked in the PDF, including the displayed arrows, not inferred from OCR.

**Required correction.** Keep gamma_p: PSL_2(Z) -> Gamma(2,3,p), with kernel K_p the normal closure of T^p in PSL_2(Z), equivalently the subgroup generated by the parabolic/unipotent elements of the projective principal congruence subgroup. Introduce a separately named q_p: Gamma(2,3,p) -> PSL_2(F_p), with kernel Xi(p)=gamma_p(Gamma(p)). State reduction modulo p = q_p composed with gamma_p and q_p^{-1}(1)=Xi(p). Keep K_p contained in Gamma(p) in PSL_2(Z). Align item 118 with item 29 and use gamma_p, not q_p, on integral matrices in items 38-40.

### RT-PAPER-BAKKER-TSIMERMAN-16/2 — Restore the common-stabilizer hypothesis (high)

**Location:** `research/blueprint/papers/PAPER-BAKKER-TSIMERMAN-16.result.json, item PAPER-BAKKER-TSIMERMAN-16/113`.

Being above the same elliptic j-value does not imply that two level points have the same nontrivial stabilizer. Item 113 omits the hypothesis (x,y) in CM and therefore incorrectly concludes that every g with x=g.y normalizes Stab(x). Without that hypothesis, its Heegner/anti-Heegner classification is not even applicable.

**Evidence.** https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v184-n3-p02-p.pdf#page=18, printed p.726: the normalizer inference explicitly uses "by the CM condition". Section 2.4, printed p.716, defines that condition using a common nonidentity stabilizer; it is stronger than equality of the images in X(1). Counterexample for every prime p>3: let x be the order-two elliptic point with projective stabilizer generated by s=[[0,1],[-1,0]], let g=[[1,1],[0,1]], and let y=g^{-1}.x. Then x=g.y and x,y have the same image j=1728. But g s g^{-1}=[[-1,2],[-1,1]] is not a scalar multiple of s modulo p, so g does not normalize <s>. The arithmetic was independently checked at p=7,11,13,17; the nonzero diagonal entries give the general proof.

**Required correction.** Begin item 113 with (x,y) in CM, or explicitly require a common nonidentity stabilizer. Explain that the order-two/order-three stabilizers then coincide. Only after that hypothesis infer g in their normalizer and apply the lift-centralizer index-two criterion for CM+ versus CM-. Retain the distinction between the projective normalizer and the subgroup commuting with a chosen GL_2 lift; add the translated-j=1728 example as a negative test.

### RT-PAPER-BAKKER-TSIMERMAN-16/3 — Require a positive height parameter (high)

**Location:** `research/blueprint/papers/PAPER-BAKKER-TSIMERMAN-16.result.json, items PAPER-BAKKER-TSIMERMAN-16/39 and /40; hypothesis notes on /38-/40`.

The range delta < 1/2 in the cusp height statements is missing a lower bound. As standalone extracted statements, items 39 and 40 permit a fixed negative delta and are false. The review deliberately removed the positive-small-parameter condition to match the printed shorthand. Item 13 explains the positive small-parameter convention elsewhere, but the items and their review notes do not restrict these particular quantifiers to that convention.

**Evidence.** https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v184-n3-p02-p.pdf#page=14, printed p.722, Lemma 11: "take delta < 1/2" (transcribed). Its inequalities are non-strict, so the identity displacement is allowed. Set delta=-1/4. In (a) choose c=c_0 and z=i y_p; in (b) choose gamma=1. The hypotheses hold for every hyperbolic X(p), since the displacement is zero. The conclusion requires an integral determinant-one matrix with h(M)=O(p^{-1}). Every such matrix has h(M)>=1; hence h(M)/p^{-1}>=p is unbounded through primes. No constant depending only on the fixed delta can make this true. This is an independent counterexample to the extracted quantifier range, not a claim that the positive-delta estimates fail.

**Required correction.** State a positive parameter explicitly, for example 0<delta<1/2, in items 39-40 and consistently in the height-estimate conventions/notes (item 38 may use the same range). Keep p>5 prime and the meaning of the asymptotic constants explicit. Explain that the positivity makes the source shorthand mathematically usable rather than claiming the printed upper bound alone is sufficient. A fixed negative-delta identity-displacement case must be rejected by the API.
