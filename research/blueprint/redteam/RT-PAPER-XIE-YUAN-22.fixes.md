## Mathematical checks and refinements

**Néron-model coordinates.** The correct map is A×A′→B, (x,t)↦(x,t−x), whose inverse is (x,y)↦(x,x+y). Hence h is exactly the second projection. The ambient extension of this map need not be a closed immersion. The closure of Y in the product is proper and dominates the target generically, hence surjects. Prime-to-p torsion is smooth over the curve, so the smoothness/flatness hypotheses needed by Proposition 2.1 are restored. Projectivity and the height comparison are now named suppliers.

The E16 explanation retains the verifier's **trivial-trace** counterexample route: a generically ordinary abelian surface on a projective moduli curve meeting the p-rank-one locus; after a finite cover, two distinct generic μ_p subgroups specialize to the same subgroup. The generic diagonal into the two quotients is embedded, but its special kernel has length p and the image closure has a multiple fibre. The familiar constant α_p family demonstrates the same kernel mechanism but by itself would not satisfy the paper's trivial-trace assumption. The result distinguishes those roles and does not claim a Lean construction of either example.

**Dependent fields.** In E18 put d_i=trdeg(k_i/k), e=trdeg(k₁k₂/k), δ=d₁+d₂−e. Take a k₁-copy τK independent from K over k₁. Writing k₁=acl(k(c)), k₂=acl(k(b)), one has trdeg(k(b,τb,c)/k)=2e−d₁. With a=trdeg(c/k(b,τb)), the defect of k₂,τk₂ is δ′=2δ−d₁+a≤δ. If equality holds, the two loci of c have the same base extension and descend by Lemma 3.4 to k, contradicting their dimension d₁−δ<d₁. Thus δ′<δ and induction terminates. Lemma 3.3 returns the descended isogeny to k₁. The proof only uses algebraically closed fields and finite transcendence degree, so it applies in characteristic p.

**Height normalization.** Gubler 2007 Theorem 3.4(e) identifies model metrics with intersection heights; Theorem 3.6 and Gubler 2003 Theorem 11.18 identify their Tate limits. The factor (dim X+1)deg_L(X) is applied last. For a finite map of degree r onto its image, both the mixed height and geometric degree acquire r. On a finite curve cover of degree d, the numerator acquires d and the generic geometric degree does not, so the relative height acquires d. For a point defined over K′(x), the identity [K′(x):K]=[K′(x):K′][K′:K] gives the same result. It does not contradict the fixed-M_K-field convention. Constant extension preserves the degree; the fundamental inequality/converse is needed when descending density of small points.

**Avoiding a torsion-density cycle.** The verifier's quotient proof is valid with divisibility and abelian quotients/Poincaré reducibility. Those suppliers are later than A3. Instead let H be the reduced closure of the prime-to-p torsion, C=H⁰ and N=|π₀(H)|. Pick n>1 prime to pN. The component group has no n-torsion, so A[n](k)⊆C(k). A3 gives n^(2dim A)≤n^(2dim C), forcing C=A. Here C is a smooth connected proper group over the perfect algebraically closed field, hence an abelian subvariety. This proof uses no quotient supplier. The direct route keeps A3 before A6 and RP.0/RP.5.
