# RT-PAPER-GAN-SAVIN-23-B

Codex — `codex-rtOQ9t` · 2026-10-01 · Refs #4232

Three high-severity errors: the extraction generalises the G₂ packet component-group formula without removing the dual centre; Main Theorem (vi) incorrectly sends every generic discrete series to a discrete PGSp₆ lift; Proposition 11.6 replaces Fell closure of the set of summands by a subsequence of the original list. The last two are inherited from the published paper. Each has a concrete counterexample and a scoped repair.

The findings concern the accepted extraction. They do not assert that the local Langlands correspondence for G₂ is false. In particular, the correction to Proposition 11.6 preserves the isolation corollary used for globalization.

## Evidence and corrections

### 1. The fixed split group needs the dual-centre condition — high

**Location:** research/blueprint/papers/PAPER-GAN-SAVIN-23-B.result.json; items[id=PAPER-GAN-SAVIN-23-B/1].statement; routes[roadmap=ExceptionalThetaCorrespondencesForG2].brief, clause (b).

Item 1 extends the G₂ packet formula to every split connected reductive group using the unquotiented component group S_φ = π₀(Z_{G∨}(φ)). Its assertion that a packet on the fixed split group is parametrised by all Irr(S_φ) is false when the dual centre contributes components. This is an extraction error, not a claim made for arbitrary G on the cited source page.

Gan–Savin, published Forum Math. Pi 11 (2023), e28, §1 p. 2, only asks for a refined parametrisation of the fibres for general split G. The formula using all Irr(π₀(Z_{G₂}(φ))) in Main Theorem (vii), p. 4, is specifically for G₂, whose dual has trivial centre. The paper itself uses Irr(S_φ/Z(Spin₇)) for packets on split PGSp₆ in §7(c), p. 22. Counterexample to the extracted general formula: take G = PGL₃ over Q₅ and φ(w,g) = Sym²(g) in SL₃(C), trivial on W_F. Schur’s lemma gives Z_{SL₃}(φ) = μ₃, so the extracted Irr(S_φ) has three elements. But this is the Steinberg parameter, and the packet on split PGL₃ contains only St_{PGL₃}: inflation identifies its representations with GL₃ representations of trivial central character, and the GL₃ LLC is a bijection. The other two characters of μ₃ select the two nonsplit degree-three inner forms, not additional representations of split PGL₃. The source’s §3.5, pp. 14–15, explicitly distinguishes these three forms through H¹(F,PGL₃), Kottwitz and j ∈ Z/3Z. This counterexample does not use the self-dual G₂ parametrisation gap already recorded as E1. Published source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/2479D805B0F248C91A33671F3A03752E/S2050508623000276a.pdf/local_langlands_conjecture_for_g2.pdf (read 2026-10-01).

**Correction:** Keep the raw component group S_φ = π₀(Z_{G∨}(φ)) for maps between centralisers, but distinguish it from the packet enhancement group. For a fixed split group state the usual trivial-dual-centre-character condition, equivalently use Irr(π₀(Z_{G∨}(φ)/Z(G∨))) for its packet. If several inner forms are included, specify the Kottwitz character condition for the chosen form. Preserve the unquotiented formula for G₂ and the explicit centre quotient for PGSp₆; do not silently change raw S_φ in Lemma 2.4. Correct item 1, the shared-definition clause of the exceptional-theta route and the human report. Record this as an extraction overgeneralisation, rather than a new published-source erratum.

### 2. The theta diagram has an incorrect discrete-series codomain — high

**Location:** research/blueprint/papers/PAPER-GAN-SAVIN-23-B.result.json; items[id=PAPER-GAN-SAVIN-23-B/9].statement; item PAPER-GAN-SAVIN-23-B/64 and routes[roadmap=LocalLanglandsCorrespondenceForG2].

Item 9 falsely asserts that θ(π) is a generic discrete series of PGSp₆ for every generic discrete series π of G₂. A generic discrete series in Irr^♣ has nonzero tempered, nondiscrete PGSp₆ lift. The incorrect intermediate codomain is already printed in Main Theorem (vi); E1–E15 do not record it.

The published Main Theorem (vi), p. 4, displays θ: Irr_{gen,ds}(G₂) → Irr_{gen,ds}(PGSp₆); the page image confirms both ds subscripts. Yet Proposition 2.3, p. 8, gives exactly one discrete-series lift among PD×, PGL₃ ⋊ Z/2Z and PGSp₆. Take π = π_gen[1] = θ_B(St_{PGL₃}^+). The companion Gan–Savin arXiv:2102.00372v1, Proposition 3.1(ii) p. 9 and Theorem 8.5 with proof pp. 27–28 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.00372v1), identifies this as a nonzero generic discrete series, with discrete-series lift back to St_{PGL₃}^+. It is therefore in Irr^♣_ds, and the published sequel’s Proposition 2.3 rules out a discrete-series PGSp₆ lift. Explicitly, companion §10.3 pp. 32–33 and Theorem 15.2(iii) pp. 52–53 give θ(π) = I₃(St_{PGL₃})_gen, a tempered constituent of normalized Siegel induction from the proper Levi GL₃. The sequel’s Proposition 5.2, pp. 20–21, correctly assumes separately that σ = θ(π) is a PGSp₆ discrete series; that extra assumption cannot be inferred for all π in the diagram. Its Corollary 5.3 does not remove this mismatch. Source of the false diagram: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/2479D805B0F248C91A33671F3A03752E/S2050508623000276a.pdf/local_langlands_conjecture_for_g2.pdf#page=4 (read 2026-10-01).

**Correction:** Remove the false assertion θ(π) ∈ Irr_{gen,ds}(PGSp₆) from item 9 and replace the intermediate codomain by generic tempered PGSp₆ representations. Record a published-source issue for the diagram. State explicitly that Proposition 5.2 proves the spin compatibility on Irr^♦_{gen,ds}(G₂), where the lift is discrete. For the remaining Irr^♣_{gen,ds} case, retain the full spin-compatibility identity as a target requiring its own argument through the explicit PGL₃/Siegel lifts and classical theta/triality, rather than citing Proposition 5.2 alone. Annotate item 64 and the LLC route/report with this scope distinction; alternatively the proved corollary can first be stated on the diamond locus. Do not discard the known nondiscrete lifts or change Proposition 2.3.

### 3. Weak containment gives closure, not a subsequence of the original enumeration — high

**Location:** research/blueprint/papers/PAPER-GAN-SAVIN-23-B.result.json; items[id=PAPER-GAN-SAVIN-23-B/117].statement; routes[roadmap=SmoothRepresentationsPartIIUnitaryDual].brief.

Proposition 11.6 is extracted as convergence of a subsequence of the original indexed list of summands. Weak containment in their direct sum does not imply that: it allows a representation which occurs only once. The published statement and the Part II endpoint therefore ask for a false theorem.

The published Proposition 11.6, §11.2 p. 33, concludes “a limit of a subsequence of π_n” (page image checked). Counterexample within its stated simple split p-adic setting: set G = G₂(Q₅), σ = St_G, π₁ = σ and π_n = 1_G for n ≥ 2. The representation σ embeds as the first Hilbert direct summand, so every normalized coefficient of σ is already one of the coefficients of ⊕̂_n π_n. Thus σ is weakly contained by Definition 11.5. Every ordinary subsequence with strictly increasing indices is eventually constantly 1_G. It cannot converge to σ: Proposition 11.3, pp. 32–33, says St_G is isolated in the unitary dual when rank G ≥ 2. One can also use a nontrivial Steinberg K-type and Lemma 11.4. The proof of 11.6 produces approximation from the set of coefficients of the summands; it never makes their original indices strictly increasing. The correct set-closure formulation is also stated in Bekka–de la Harpe–Valette, author PDF https://perso.univ-rennes1.fr/bachir.bekka/KazhdanTotal.pdf, Exercise F.6.4, printed p. 444 / PDF p. 450: weak containment of direct sums corresponds to inclusion of the sets of irreducibles in Fell closure. This is consistent with Proposition F.2.2, printed p. 428, which tests convergence against every subnet. Main source: https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/2479D805B0F248C91A33671F3A03752E/S2050508623000276a.pdf/local_langlands_conjecture_for_g2.pdf#page=33 (both sources read 2026-10-01).

**Correction:** Replace item 117 and the Part II endpoint by: if irreducible σ is weakly contained in ⊕̂_i π_i, then [σ] lies in the Fell closure of {[π_i]}. If a sequential version is desired in this setting, say it is a limit of a sequence chosen from that set with repetitions allowed, and justify the required countability; do not call it a subsequence of the original list. Another valid sufficient hypothesis for a subsequence version is weak containment in every tail, with the appropriate countability argument. Add a published-source issue and update the report. Keep Corollary 11.7 (item 118): isolation applied to the corrected closure statement still forces σ ≅ π_i for some i, so the globalization applications need not claim any new failure.
