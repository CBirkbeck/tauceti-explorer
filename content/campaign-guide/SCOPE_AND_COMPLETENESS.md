# Scope and completeness standard

The aim is a connected, reusable account of modern number theory, from elementary arithmetic through analytic, algebraic, geometric, computational and probabilistic methods to current arithmetic geometry and Langlands theory. The coverage register includes every detailed MSC2020 number-theory code and additional arithmetic interfaces in algebraic geometry, K-theory, topology, logic and analysis.

There are three different completion tests:

1. **Coverage and ownership:** every registered subject and stage has a named roadmap owner, an explicit construction target, a source route and an acceptance requirement. Every encoded dependency resolves to a stage or to a registered external construction contract.
2. **Proof-plan closure:** every target has been decomposed against its full source proof; each leaf has an actual statement, verified prerequisites and compatible producer/consumer signatures. No missing theorem is hidden in an assumed field, a broad subject label or a future-paper citation.
3. **Formal completion:** all those leaves and their assembled endpoints are proved, build on the target pin, pass axiom checks and instantiate in their consumers.

This revision expands and repairs the first layer and provides the queue and checks for the second. It does not claim the second or third is finished. The review supplied with the archive explicitly said the complete theorem-by-theorem, PDF-backed audit was unfinished; the reconstructed assignments were pending requests, not recovered approvals. Their residual source/proof checks remain visible.

“No gaps” is enforced operationally: a discovered prerequisite becomes an owned stage, a source issue becomes a source task, a normalization mismatch becomes a conversion task, and an open conjecture remains an explicit frontier target. None can silently disappear from an endpoint. A finite subject classification is an audit scaffold, not a mathematical proof that every present or future theorem has been listed.

## Boundaries

- Global **number fields and function fields** are both included. Equal and mixed characteristic are distinguished in local theory.
- Integral, torsion, rational, real/complex and p-adic theories have separate comparison obligations.
- Classical analytic number theory, additive/metric/probabilistic methods, transcendence, rational points, dynamics, arithmetic statistics and certified computation are first-class strands.
- Schemes, stacks, cohomology, motives, K-theory, Iwasawa, automorphy and Langlands share foundations through named owners.
- Topological, logical and quantum connections are included to the extent they supply or express arithmetic mathematics; this is not a claim to cover all physics or all algebraic geometry.
- The `xx` families and `99` miscellaneous classes have admission owners. An unforeseen topic is assigned there and expanded into specific construction stages before anyone claims it is covered in proof detail.
