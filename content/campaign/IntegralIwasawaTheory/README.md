# Arithmetic Iwasawa theory and the main conjecture

Shared arithmetic towers, cyclotomic units and integral determinant main conjectures.

## Canonical ownership and interfaces

- Early finite cyclotomic arithmetic and tower control are available before ColemanPowerSeries's quotient theorem; the later exact sequence consumes that quotient. General Λ algebra and determinant formalism are imported from PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras.
- The abelian cyclotomic Euler-system proof belongs to EulerSystemsCyclotomicMainConjecture. This owner adds Wiles's totally real congruence proof and the modern integral Brumer–Stark/Kurihara determinant route. Deligne–Ribet existence is imported from AutomorphicPadicLFunctions.

**Campaign dependencies:** [PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras](../PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras/README.md), [DirichletPadicLFunctions](../DirichletPadicLFunctions/README.md), [ColemanPowerSeries](../ColemanPowerSeries/README.md), [SelmerIwasawaCohomology](../SelmerIwasawaCohomology/README.md), [EulerSystemsCyclotomicMainConjecture](../EulerSystemsCyclotomicMainConjecture/README.md), [AutomorphicPadicLFunctions](../AutomorphicPadicLFunctions/README.md).

The source-label dictionary and audit resolves H/K/Z/U/T/V/S/M/L/N/R/P/D/I/B/E/ER/HB and R01–R16 references below. The shared conventions are part of this specification. Suggested.lean contains non-exhaustive prototypes; the complete targets are here.

**Prefix:** I. **Dependencies:** T, M, N, R.5; ClassFieldTheory and the completed-group-algebra portion of ProfiniteProPGroups. **References:** Mazur–Wiles (1984); Wiles (1990); Rognes–Weibel, Appendix A by Kolster; Dasgupta–Kakde; Dasgupta–Kakde–Silliman–Wang (DKSW), *The Brumer–Stark conjecture over Z*; Kurihara (2025), *On class groups and Iwasawa modules of CM-fields*, especially §4.

This is an arithmetic prerequisite, not a corollary of a power-series-ring library. Canonical ownership is fixed: PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras owns analytic/Λ algebra, DirichletPadicLFunctions owns Kubota–Leopoldt, AutomorphicPadicLFunctions owns Deligne–Ribet, and this area owns arithmetic towers and the stated arithmetic proof routes. No unverified or merely proposed main-conjecture endpoint is counted as an implemented dependency.

## I.1 — Cyclotomic towers and coefficient rings

Construct the cyclotomic Z_p-extension of a number field, its finite layers, Galois actions, norm maps, decomposition and inertia groups, and ramification outside p. Identify the procyclic quotient with Z_p after choosing a generator, and prove generator-change formulas. Reuse the completed group algebra, but prove its arithmetic-module actions.

For Λ=Z_p[[Γ]], import from PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras the characteristic ideals of finitely generated torsion modules by height-one lengths, Fitting ideals of finitely presented modules, pseudo-isomorphisms, elementary modules and the μ/λ invariants. Prove the precise finite-level control and change-of-generator statements. The kernel of passage to Γ/Γ^{p^n} is generated by `(1+T)^{p^n}−1`, not by `T^{p^n}` or `T^n`.

For finite abelian extensions of a totally real base, also construct Z_p[[G∞]], the total quotient ring and invertible determinant modules. If p divides the finite group order, integral character idempotents need not exist. Characterwise calculations after scalar extension do not prove an integral group-ring identity without an additional descent argument.

## I.2 — Arithmetic inverse limits

Define inverse limits of p-primary class groups, S-class groups, units, S-units and the appropriate maximal abelian pro-p Galois groups, with the transition maps specified individually. Establish the comparison of class-group and Galois descriptions by class field theory. Prove finite generation and the exact sequences involving local units, global units and ramification modules.

Distinguish the unramified class-group module from the module classifying extensions unramified outside S. They are not interchangeable in a main conjecture. Account for roots of unity, finite submodules, decomposition groups and the trivial character. Weak Leopoldt in the cyclotomic tower is a theorem to prove here with its standard hypotheses; do not assume Leopoldt's conjecture for the original number field.

## I.3 — p-adic zeta functions and interpolation

Import measures, distributions and Amice/Mellin transforms from PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras and LocallyAnalyticDistributions. For abelian extensions of Q, import the Kubota–Leopoldt pseudomeasure from DirichletPadicLFunctions L1–L2 and prove its comparison with the Bernoulli-distribution/Stickelberger normalization used here, including the Euler factor at p, Teichmüller twists and the trivial-character pole. No second p-adic zeta carrier is constructed.

For a general totally real base, import the Deligne–Ribet pseudomeasure from AutomorphicPadicLFunctions L3 and prove its dictionary with the equivariant Stickelberger normalization. This includes Hilbert modular Eisenstein series, their algebraic Fourier coefficients, q-expansion principles, the relevant congruences and the distribution/bounded-denominator theorem. These general Hilbert modular constructions must be imported from a verified supplier or implemented as the Iwasawa proof's explicit prerequisite; weight-two quaternionic forms alone do not supply them.

The interpolation theorem fixes the exact complex L-value, omitted Euler factors and cyclotomic-character power. A p-adic analytic function specified only by the desired interpolation values is insufficient until its existence, uniqueness and pole behaviour have been proved.

## I.4 — Specialisation and Euler characteristics

Apply PadicMeasuresIwasawaAlgebras L5's derived specialization/determinant formalism and ArithmeticGaloisDuality's actual continuous cochains, restriction, corestriction, twisting and duality. Use SelmerIwasawaCohomology L3 for Iwasawa complexes. Prove the arithmetic finite-layer base-change and Euler-characteristic comparisons here, including higher Tor terms; do not construct a second cochain or compact-support theory.

Compute the Euler characteristic at an arithmetic character only after showing the relevant cohomology is finite and the specialisation is defined. Treat a zero or pole through a leading-term theorem, not division by a potentially zero value. Record separately local factors at S, the augmentation factor for the trivial character, and real-place corrections at p=2.

## I.5 — Classical main conjectures at odd primes

Prove the abelian-Q main conjecture of Mazur–Wiles, with the chosen algebraic module and p-adic L-function conventions. Prove Wiles's totally real main conjecture at odd primes. The proof programme includes Hecke algebras at the needed levels and weights, Eisenstein ideals, congruence modules, Galois representations and stable lattices, construction of the required class-group extensions, and both divisibilities of characteristic ideals.

Package the comparison between the Hecke-theoretic extension and I.2's module, rather than using an abstract extension with the expected order. Separate the ordinary, residual and character hypotheses at intermediate steps and prove the induction/change-of-field argument that removes them in the final cited theorem.

The endpoint consumed by B is an equality of finite arithmetic Euler characteristics and the p-adic valuation of ζ_F(−1), not merely an unnamed assertion “the main conjecture holds”.

## I.6 — Integral Brumer–Stark prerequisites

Construct equivariant Artin L-functions for the abelian CM extensions used in the modern proof, their S- and T-modifications, integral Stickelberger elements, ray-class modules, roots-of-unity corrections, and the archimedean minus construction with the precise convention of DKSW. In characteristic two, do not identify a minus quotient, a minus eigensubmodule and an integral idempotent summand without proof.

Build the Ritter–Weiss modules and their presentations, the comparison to class/ray-class groups, and their Fitting-ideal functoriality. This is also where the finitely generated module and finite-presentation hypotheses required by Kurihara's finite-level theorems are proved.

## I.7 — The integral Brumer–Stark theorem

Formalise the proof of the integral Brumer–Stark theorem and the stronger module-theoretic statement needed in Kurihara's Theorem 2.12. The odd-prime result of Dasgupta–Kakde and the integral DKSW refinement have distinct proof obligations. Include the necessary Hilbert modular congruences, integral Galois representations, the generalised Ribet lemma for residually indistinguishable characters, the Fitting-ideal comparison, and the passage from modified to unmodified data.

The residually indistinguishable case is essential at p=2; it is not an application of a theorem assuming distinct residual characters. Import the general representation/lattice/extension-class/annihilator theorem from IntegralHeckeAndGaloisDeterminants IHG.6, including this residual coincidence case; that owner proves the separate generalized Ribet lemma with its local-factor Fitting bound. Here prove every hypothesis for the actual Hilbert/Eisenstein Galois representation and compare the resulting arithmetic class and Fitting ideal. The shared theorem is not an unexplained black box or a second generic construction here.

Prove the finite-level equivariant Tamagawa-number/Brumer–Stark comparison actually used by Kurihara. A statement about annihilation of a class group is generally weaker than the Fitting or determinant identity needed downstream.

## I.8 — The compactly supported Iwasawa complex

Let k be totally real, F/k finite abelian and totally real, and F∞/F cyclotomic. Allow F=k. For S containing the archimedean places, the places over p, and the ramified places, construct

\[
 C_{F_\infty,S}=R\Gamma_c(\mathcal O_{F_\infty,S},\mathbb Z_p(1))
\]

in the precise Burns–Flach/Kurihara convention, by applying ArithmeticGaloisDuality's compact-support/real-place construction and SelmerIwasawaCohomology L3's Iwasawa limit. Prove perfectness, derived inverse-limit descriptions, and the cohomology identification with the S-ramified Iwasawa module in degree two and Z_p in degree three, in the exact form of Kurihara §4. Do not substitute a different “ordinary compact support” complex at p=2.

Construct the involution and cyclotomic twist τ on the coefficient ring. Prove the relation between the CM Stickelberger element and the totally real Deligne–Ribet pseudomeasure. List S, T, all twists, duals and involutions in each comparison square.

## I.9 — The all-prime determinant main conjecture

Formalise Kurihara's Theorem 4.1, using I.7 and I.8:

\[
 \operatorname{Det}(C_{F_\infty,S})^{-1}
   =g_{F_\infty/k,S}\,\Lambda_{F_\infty}.
\]

This theorem includes p=2. Prove its finite-level-to-Iwasawa transition, localisation at all relevant height-one primes, the determinant/Fitting comparison, and descent back to the integral coefficient ring. In the p=2 proof, account for the finite cokernel of order 2 in Kurihara's Lemma 4.2(1), equivalently the Z/2 kernel of the dual map, before explaining why the relevant height-one calculation discards it. This is a finite Z/2 correction, not a free Z_2 module.

The theorem is not restricted to fields abelian over Q: the base k is arbitrary totally real, and F=k is permitted. Conversely, a characterwise identity over a field of fractions is not yet this integral determinant identity.

## I.10 — Comparison with classical arithmetic formulations

Derive the nonequivariant characteristic-ideal statements, including `(γ−1)g` at the trivial character. Compare this formulation with the classical one used by Kolster in the implication from the 2-primary main conjecture to Birch–Tate. Prove the agreement of twists, duality, omitted primes, finite modules, and real-place conventions.

This is a substantive comparison theorem, not a change of terminology. A correction invisible after height-one localisation can affect an arithmetic specialisation, so recover the required finite-level correction before applying I.4. Export to B the resulting degree-two, weight-two finite-group equality with all factors visible.

## Tests and completion

Check interpolation at −1 for Q, the trivial-character pole and augmentation factor, and an abelian nontrivial character. Test an integral coefficient ring with p dividing the group order, where naive idempotents fail. Test the real-place correction at p=2 and a case where an S-Euler factor contributes to the valuation.

I.5 provides the historical odd-prime endpoint independently of the modern integral route. I.9–I.10 provide the modern all-prime endpoint. Completion requires proofs of the arithmetic main conjectures and their comparison, not just construction of the Λ-modules or an equality stored as a structure field.

## Scope and dependency order

Develop the arithmetic of RJW §13.1–13.4 and Appendix A, including the auxiliary results the notes quote without proof. Work with arbitrary Z_p-extensions for the growth theorem, and specialize to the real cyclotomic tower for the Vandiver argument. This does not assert Greenberg's conjecture for every totally real field.

**Dependencies:** R01/R03, GlobalNumberFields, NumberFieldArithmetic, LocalFieldsRamification, ProfiniteProPGroups and ClassFieldTheory. L0 is independent of R04 and supplies its global cyclotomic units. L1–L4 use R04's local quotient theorem. R08 uses this roadmap for the unconditional main conjecture.

## L0. Global cyclotomic arithmetic and the index formula

Construct Q(mu_m), its real subfield, roots of unity, ramification, discriminants, all embeddings and norm maps on units and ideals. Reuse the existing number-field carriers. Define cyclotomic units as a subgroup of actual units. Develop the relation between their real and full-field groups, torsion, Galois action and the explicit generators of prime-power conductor.

Prove the regulator determinant calculation for cyclotomic units using Gauss sums and the complex L(chi,1) formula. Combine it with the **proved** analytic class-number formula and unit theorem to obtain

    [O_(Q(mu_{p^n}))^× : D_n]
      = [O_(Q(mu_{p^n})^+)^× : D_n^+] = h_n^+.

This is RJW Theorem 11.7. Supply the variants and correction factors for general abelian fields rather than copying the prime-power formula to every conductor. The elementary ramification and unit-norm results precede any use of the main conjecture.

## L1. Infinite extensions and class field theory

Construct each maximal abelian pro-p extension with the stated ramification condition in a common algebraic closure; prove its universal property. Relate finite layers and inverse limits, including the composita needed to identify their Galois groups. Define X for the p-ramified extension and Y for the everywhere-unramified extension, preserving RJW's notation. Define the completed Galois action by conjugation and prove it is independent of a lift.

Derive from ray-class reciprocity the finite-level exact sequence relating closures of global principal units, semilocal principal units and the inertia part of the Galois group. Take inverse limits using a proved compactness/exactness result. Prove the exact sequence

    0 → E_infty^+/C_infty^+ → U_infty^+/C_infty^+
      → X_infty^+ → Y_infty^+ → 0.

All closures and principal-unit restrictions must agree with R04, and the quotient is U/E, not E/U.

## L2. Control and class-number growth

For the one-prime, totally ramified tower treated in RJW, prove the decomposition of the Galois group using inertia, compute its closed commutator subgroup, and prove

    Y_n ≃ Y_infty / ((gamma^(p^n)−1)Y_infty).

Prove compact Nakayama applies and deduce finite generation and torsion. Establish finite-error stabilization under pseudo-isomorphisms; compute the coinvariant orders for the elementary modules. Obtain

    v_p(#Cl(F_n)) = mu p^n + lambda n + nu  (n sufficiently large),

where mu,lambda≥0 and **nu is an integer**. Prove the bounded-p-rank characterization of mu=0.

Extend to an arbitrary Z_p-extension by proving eventual ramification control, the finite set of ramified primes, inertia-generated correction modules, and the bounded/stabilizing errors between class groups and the relevant coinvariants. Do not retain the exact one-prime control formula in the general theorem. Establish every Appendix A reduction that the notes quote from Washington.

## L3. Vandiver's condition and the notes' special-case proof

Define Vandiver(p) by p not dividing the class number of Q(mu_p)^+. Use L2 control plus topological Nakayama to prove Y_infty^+=0 under this hypothesis. Use the finite-level cyclotomic index formula, principal-unit intersections and flat scalar extension to prove E_infty^+=C_infty^+.

Combine L1 with R04.L4 to prove the actual isomorphism

    X_infty^+ ≃ Z_p[[G^+]] / (I(G^+) zeta_p).

Derive Theorem 13.8's ideal equality under Vandiver. The unconditional theorem is R08, and does not assume or prove that every prime is Vandiver. Supply the standard isotypic formulations with their Tate twists and involutions as proved comparisons.

## L4. Abelian Leopoldt and Ferrero–Washington

Where abelian Leopoldt is used, include its proof input: the p-adic logarithmic linear-independence theorem of Baker–Brumer with its auxiliary-polynomial/transcendence argument, and its application to abelian units. This is not an invocation of Leopoldt for an arbitrary number field. It can be independently packaged in the existing number-field roadmap, but the theorem and its proof remain a named deliverable of this programme.

Prove the Ferrero–Washington theorem for cyclotomic Z_p-extensions of abelian number fields, including p=2, following the primary proof or Washington §7.5 with its full auxiliary equidistribution/linear-independence argument. Separate the analytic vanishing of the relevant p-adic L-function mu-invariant from the arithmetic class-group mu-invariant; a transfer using the main conjecture belongs after R08 and must not enter R08's own proof as an input. The source-independent arithmetic theorem is available by the primary Ferrero–Washington argument.

State Greenberg's Conjecture A.15 on the defined invariants. The equivalence with bounded **p-primary** class-group orders is the formal statement; do not replace it by bounded full class numbers.

**Acceptance:** Theorem 11.7; Theorem 13.11 and its proof; all Appendix A lemmas with their actual ramification hypotheses; the general growth theorem; Theorem A.14; a correctly typed statement, not a proof, of A.15.

## Public API and review contract

Every construction supplies extensionality, constructor/eliminator lemmas, identity/composition, equivalence invariance and scalar-extension or base-change compatibility wherever defined. Quotients have universal properties and map-level comparison theorems. Finiteness, flatness, admissibility, convergence, perfectness and integral-lattice hypotheses are stated and proved in each unconditional application.

A completed target includes the intrinsic construction, its working API, the stated comparisons and the worked tests above. Matching abstract groups with different maps, rational substitutes for integral statements, or an expected equality stored in an unconstrained structure do not discharge the contract. Check current Mathlib/Tau Ceti APIs before selecting names. Suggested signatures involving unavailable higher objects remain explicit comments; no stand-in theorem asserts their conclusions.
