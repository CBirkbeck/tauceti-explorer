# Roadmap: perfectoid rings and spaces

This specification uses the shared [diamond conventions](../../campaign-guide/DIAMONDS_CONVENTIONS.md),
[source contracts](../../campaign-guide/DIAMONDS_SOURCE_CONTRACTS.md), and
[65-milestone source graph](../../campaign-guide/DIAMONDS_DEPENDENCY_ORDER.md). The original ECD source
coverage is retained in the 328-item index.
Canonical campaign ownership takes precedence over historical supplier paths in the source index.

This component supplies ECD §§3, 5–6 and the limit/approximation inputs subsequently used in
§7. Its starting point is AdicSpaces, not a pre-existing tilting or almost-purity theorem.
Primary proofs are Scholze's *Perfectoid spaces* (Sch12), Kedlaya–Liu's *Relative p-adic Hodge
theory: foundations* (KL15), Fontaine's Bourbaki account, and the numbered ECD statements.
General perfectoid Tate rings are required; working only over one perfectoid field is insufficient.

Coordinate ring, valuation and space interfaces with Mathlib PRs
[#42312](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/42312),
[#42314](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/42314),

[#26389](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/26389). Follow their API
direction while building all required general-ring mathematics in Tau Ceti. Mathlib's
`PreTilt.untilt` is an existing multiplicative function, not the global untilt functor;
compare against it without treating the tilting equivalence as already supplied.

## P0. Almost mathematics with a reusable base ideal

Start with an abstract ring `V` and an explicitly specified idempotent ideal satisfying the
flatness/tensor hypotheses of almost ring theory. Construct almost-zero modules, the Serre
quotient, almostification and its adjoints, tensor and Hom, almost-flat and
almost-finite-projective modules, almost algebras and almost finite étale algebras. All results
in this preliminary stage are conditional on the stated almost-base hypotheses; no perfectoid
ring or tilting theorem is required to formulate or prove them.

In P1–P2, instantiate this construction using the ideal generated by the positive fractional
powers of a pseudouniformizer. At that point prove idempotence, the required flatness,
independence of the choice, and comparison of the almost module categories from `R⁺` and
`R°`. This separation avoids making the construction of perfectoid rings depend on itself.

Prove descent for almost modules and almost algebras along faithfully flat maps, and the passage
between compatible integral reductions, completion and inversion of a pseudouniformizer.
State the torsion-freeness, completeness and uniform bounds needed for each limit argument.
The “almost exact ⇒ exact after inversion” theorem must not discard a derived inverse-limit
term without proving its vanishing. Construct the Čech and sheaf-cohomology formulations using
D0's ordinary sheaf-theoretic foundations. This is the almost-algebra input to rational acyclicity
and almost purity, not the almost-purity theorem itself.

## P1. Perfectoid Tate rings, tilts and marked untilts

Use ECD Definition 3.1 verbatim in mathematical content: a complete uniform Tate ring with a
pseudouniformizer `ϖ` such that `ϖ^p | p` in `R°` and Frobenius
`R°/ϖ → R°/ϖ^p` is an isomorphism. Prove the equivalent Frobenius-surjectivity criterion with
the pseudouniformizer condition still present. Prove the characteristic-`p` characterization
as perfect complete Tate rings and the perfectoid-field characterization. In particular, `ℚ_p`
is not perfectoid; surjectivity of Frobenius on its residue field is not enough.

Construct the multiplicative inverse limit defining `R♭`, the limiting addition, the topology,
the sharp map, and functoriality. Prove existence of the limits, completeness, uniformity,
characteristic `p`, and the descriptions of the integral subrings. The sharp map is multiplicative
and continuous but is not asserted additive. Construct Fontaine's `θ`, its surjectivity, and
the principal non-zero-divisor description of its kernel. Prove classification of marked untilts
by primitive degree-one ideals, including the converse construction with its topology and plus
ring (ECD 3.14–3.17).

Use the existing Witt-vector and Fontaine-map definitions where their types agree. A compatible
name in a file is not a substitute for the global classification theorem. Provide comparison
maps when the pre-existing definition has a narrower base. Construct the equivalence of
perfectoid algebras over a fixed perfectoid ring and over its tilt, with unit, counit and naturality.

**Tests:** completed `𝔽_p((t^{1/p^∞}))`, cyclotomic and compatible-root mixed-characteristic
fields, perfected Tate balls/tori, and a non-field perfectoid Tate ring as in ECD 3.4(iv).

## P2. Rational localization, sheafiness, and perfectoid spaces

Prove the tilting homeomorphism of valuation spectra and its rational-subset correspondence,
not merely the rank-one version. Construct perfectoid rational localizations and identify their
tilts. Prove the sheaf theorem and all-degree almost acyclicity on affinoids and their integral
structure sheaves, following ECD 3.18 and 3.24 with the Sch12/KL15 proofs. Supply the acyclic
basis comparison needed to pass from rational Čech exactness to sheaf cohomology.

Define perfectoid spaces as the full subcategory of adic spaces locally modeled on these
Huber pairs. Glue tilting and prove the slice-category equivalence of ECD 3.20. Do not introduce
a separate locally ringed topological-space carrier that then needs a second gluing theory.

Using A0, construct fibre products of perfectoid spaces and their affine formulas with their
correct completed/uniform integral algebra. Prove that these are again perfectoid and satisfy
the universal property, and that tilting commutes with them. Also construct products in `Perf`
without silently treating `Spec 𝔽_p` as a perfectoid Tate space; the absolute product of two
perfectoid fields need not be a point. Construct completed residue fields and their plus rings.

## P3. Almost purity and étale tilting

Prove ECD Theorem 6.1, with its three distinct conclusions: finite étale algebras over a
perfectoid Tate ring are perfectoid; tilting is an equivalence on finite étale algebras; integral
closures are almost finite étale. Build the integral almost-algebra lifting theorem and the
finite étale descent/approximation steps used by the Sch12/KL15 proof. Neither ordinary
étaleness of the integral closure nor finite freeness of an arbitrary finite étale algebra is
an acceptable replacement.

Compare the perfectoid finite étale category with the algebraic one and A1's analytic category.
Prove the local open/finite-étale description, base change, composition, and descent. Glue the
affine tilting equivalence to the equivalence of étale sites, ECD 6.3, and prove the étale
almost-acyclicity assertion. These proofs must not depend on diamondification.

## P4. Morphisms and perfectoid quotients

Prove the injection and isomorphism criteria of ECD §5, including the qcqs hypotheses and
tests on all algebraically closed valued pairs `(C,C⁺)`. Construct open and closed immersions,
Zariski closed immersions, diagonals, separatedness and valuative criteria. A closed immersion
of perfectoid spaces and a Zariski closed immersion are different notions; do not extend the
Zariski statement to every topologically closed subset.

The stronger assertion “Zariski closed implies strongly Zariski closed”, ECD Theorem 5.8,
is supplied by **Q4** in PerfectoidQuotients. Its prismatic proof is not an implicit part of
this elementary morphism stage. P4 proves the earlier definitions, the direction from an
already given surjective perfectoid quotient, the diagonal assertions, and the other §5
criteria; when the stronger theorem is needed later, import Q4 explicitly.

The integral perfectoid criteria used for compatible-root period annuli are shared with Q0
and the integral theory accompanying P1–P3. Do not infer those criteria from the name of an
integral model alone.

## P5. Limits and étale finite-stage descent

Construct cofiltered limits of affinoid perfectoid pairs by completing the directed union of
integral rings and inverting a compatible pseudouniformizer. Prove independence of tail and
choice, topological-spectrum continuity, and the universal property. Prove that `ω₁`-cofiltered
limits give the stated uncompleted colimits of rings, and the decomposition into countably
topologically generated affinoids (ECD 6.5).

Prove finite étale finite-stage approximation using the henselian-pair/approximation theorem
cited from Gabber–Ramero 5.4.53, with its lifting and uniqueness statements. This input is a
task here, not an assumption that all finitely presented algebras commute with completion.
Then prove ECD 6.4(i)–(iii): full faithfulness through graph approximation, descent of finite
étale objects, gluing of qcqs étale objects, and eventual recognition of separatedness. Include
size bounds, not just unbounded category equivalences.

## P6. Affinoid approximation and pro-étale presentations

Use **A3** to prove the additional affinoid assertion ECD 6.4(iv). Do not cite P5's qcqs result
as though it already asserted affinoidness at a finite stage. Then construct affinoid pro-étale
maps as actual cofiltered limits of affinoid étale maps, the pro-category equivalence in ECD
7.10, and the stability and limit assertions in 7.11. Prove that rational-intersection Zariski
closed immersions and diagonals are pro-étale in the stated perfectoid sense (7.9).

Instantiate the κ-small perfectoid-space definitions and closure theorems of ECD 4.2–4.4
using D0's abstract cardinal lemma and the completed-algebra size bounds. These bounds
are available before D1 constructs the countably iterated disconnected cover.

The disconnected-cover and v-descent theory is D1–D3. P6 exports exactly the affinoid
approximation and pro-étale calculus on which that theory depends. In particular, its proof
cannot appeal to v-descent, a diamond atlas, or the analytic-diamond comparison.

## P7. Tilde-limits and Frobenius-controlled towers

Dependencies: P1–P5 and AdicSpacesPartII's early tensor/product prefix. Construct Huber
tilde-limits with both the inverse-limit topology and affinoid density condition. Prove
uniqueness only in the perfectoid representable class and comparison with inverse limits of
represented functors. For an integral tower A_i^+ with compatible pseudouniformizer, spell out
the eventual Frobenius lifting condition modulo its p-th power, the uniformity bound, and
the completion/inversion formula; derive the perfectoid Frobenius criterion. A theorem
assuming each A_i is already perfectoid is a separate, easier limit theorem.

Prove restriction to compatible rational opens, cofinal change of index, finite étale
base change, and gluing of the resulting limits. Export the cofinality witness needed for
compact open subgroup towers. Test compatible-root discs and tori, a finite étale tower,
and a noncofinal descending level family for which no full infinite-level claim follows.
Sources: Scholze Perfectoid Spaces §§6–7; torsion paper II.1–II.2; the Shimura source P3.

## P8. Finite quotients and closed perfectoidization in towers

Dependencies: P3–P7, PerfectoidQuotients Q4 and DiamondsAndVStacks D4 for quotient comparison.
Under Hansen–Johansson §5's invariant affinoid-cover hypothesis, construct finite group
quotients and their invariant Huber pairs, prove affinoid perfectoidness and gluing, and
identify the quotient's represented v-sheaf. Include groups whose order is divisible by p;
the proof uses tilting and integral estimates, not division by the group order. Separate
the universal quotient statement from finite étale descent, which additionally requires
a free torsor action. Prove compatibility with invariant rational restrictions and scalar
extension in the actual source range.

For Hodge-type closed embeddings, combine the tower's closed loci with Q4's universal
perfectoidization, control the plus ring and integral almost surjection, and prove the
tilde-limit comparison on these loci. This is a perfectoid quotient construction, not the
assertion that every algebraic ideal quotient is already complete and perfectoid.
Tests: a cyclic p-group quotient and the compatible-root closed locus; the Shimura-specific
embedding is a downstream application. Source: Hansen–Johansson §5 and ECD 5.8.

## P9. Continuous torsor descent with coefficients

Dependencies: P0–P8, DiamondsAndVStacks D2–D3; the weight-space extension also uses the
sousperfectoid stage of AdicSpacesPartII. For a continuous profinite torsor of the prescribed
tower type, construct its Čech descent datum and prove descent for O, O^+, finite locally
free modules and morphisms. Distinguish almost integral statements from actual equalizers;
prove the additional effectivity needed to descend a finite locally free sheaf integrally.

For affinoid smooth rigid coefficient/weight spaces, construct the completed external tensor
product, prove the sheaf condition and descent after the indicated base changes, and identify
finite-level character sheaves with the descended sheaf. State separately flat coefficient
change, derived nonflat change, and inverse-system hypotheses giving vanishing of lim^1.
Pointwise group invariants need not commute with an arbitrary completion. Test the trivial
torsor, a finite étale torsor, a Z_p-tower and reduction modulo an ideal with a nonzero Tor
term. The locally analytic distribution theory itself has the campaign's distribution owner.

## Completion

All equivalences include functors, natural unit and counit isomorphisms, and compatibility with
plus rings, rational restriction, and composition. Cohomology statements refer to the same
structure sheaves as AdicSpaces. Definitions work for general perfectoid Tate rings, and the
field examples are proved instances, not global assumptions on the category.

## Completion contracts added on 2026-09-15

**Applies to:** `P0`, `P1`, `P2`, `P3`, `P5`, `P7`, `P8`, `P9`. Existing stage identifiers remain stable. These are required construction and validation tasks; they do not assert that the mathematics has been formalized.

### Intermediate constructions

At the integral descent seam prove completeness and the uniform annihilator estimates before taking inverse limits of almost exact sequences. P5 returns finite-stage equations and graph descent as well as object descent; P7 adds the Frobenius lifting argument for towers whose finite levels are not perfectoid. For P8 prove invariant-affinoid-cover existence in each geometric application before applying the quotient theorem.

### Producer–consumer contract

Export one Huber-pair tilt and its rational-localization comparisons, with the nonadditive sharp map distinguished from theta. P9's integral module descent requires its own effectivity proof; degree-zero function equalizers do not classify finite locally free modules.

### Acceptance and source route

A non-field perfectoid Tate ring, a p-group quotient, a noncofinal level tower and a nonflat coefficient reduction test the boundaries. Q_p remains a negative perfectoid example.
