# Cyclotomic completions and classical Habiro rings

Construct cyclotomic completions over commutative rings and modules, with topology, universal properties, Taylor expansions and evaluation. This is the classical foundation in the standalone [Habiro rings and cohomology family](../HabiroRings/README.md), independently of algebraic K-theory. The relative étale ring construction belongs to HabiroRings; explicit number-field Frobenius gluing belongs to HabiroNumberFields. Extending coefficients in this classical completion does not define either construction.

Use Mathlib's `Polynomial`, `PowerSeries`, cyclotomic polynomials, ideals, quotients, inverse limits, root-of-unity and topological-ring APIs. Suggested.lean prototypes the finite products and their divisibility, not an unavailable completed-ring API. Classical results are sourced to [Habiro, Cyclotomic completions of polynomial rings](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0209324), especially §§2–7. The normative numbering here is the published Publ. RIMS 40 (2004), 1127–1146 version, now acquired as R02_KI_HabiroPublished2004.pdf: published Theorems 4.1 and 5.2 correspond respectively to stored arXiv v1 Theorems 4.2 and 5.4; Theorem 6.1 agrees. Do not search the old PDF for the published labels without this concordance.

## HC.1. The directed system and its topology

For a set S of positive integers form the multiplicative monoid generated by Φ_n(q), n∈S, ordered by divisibility. Define R[q]^S as the inverse limit of the quotient rings R[q]/(f). Prove directedness, compatibility of quotient maps, independence of representatives and the universal property for continuous maps to complete separated rings. Supply the projections, dense polynomial map, extensionality by finite quotients, completeness and separatedness. Do not assert injectivity of the polynomial map for every coefficient ring and every S without its hypotheses.

For S=ℕ_{>0}, prove cofinality of P_N(q)=∏_{i=1}^N(1−q^i). This identifies the completion with lim_N R[q]/(P_N), and with the system of completions at q^m−1 using divisibility in m. Prove the comparison by mutually cofinal ideals; count multiplicities of each cyclotomic factor. The completion is neither the q-adic nor the (q−1)-adic completion. The empty product is 1; its quotient is the zero ring and contributes only the initial stage.

Develop continuous functoriality in R and contravariant restriction in S. Prove identity, composition, compatibility of the coefficient maps and the canonical topological equivalences for cofinal systems. Preserve the distinction between tensor product followed by completion and uncompleted base change; flatness alone does not license interchange with an arbitrary infinite inverse limit.

## HC.2. Expansions and coefficient algorithms

Construct representatives successively modulo P_N, and show every compatible element admits a convergent factorial expansion ∑ a_N(q)P_N(q). Give a normalized degree-bounded expansion using division by monic polynomials when that version is used; prove existence and uniqueness of that normalization. Unrestricted coefficient expansions are not unique.

Construct addition, multiplication and coefficient extraction algorithms on normalized expansions. A computation to precision N depends on finitely many coefficients, and each algorithm has a congruence correctness theorem. Show that q is invertible in the full Habiro ring, compute its inverse at finite precision and compare the polynomial/Laurent-polynomial constructions. Avoid formal division by a cyclotomic polynomial that is not a unit.

## HC.3. Root evaluation and Taylor maps

For a specified root ζ of order d∈S construct evaluation into R[ζ] by a finite quotient and prove independence of the truncation. Construct the Taylor map into R[ζ][[q−ζ]] from compatible quotient maps. Prove its constant term is evaluation; identify the higher coefficients with Hasse derivatives, retaining factorial denominators when comparing to ordinary derivatives.

Prove naturality under coefficient change, root conjugation, order restriction and q↦q^a whenever the index map preserves the chosen completion topology. Substitution by a nonzero constant into an arbitrary formal power series is not defined: every re-expansion uses topological nilpotence in a proved complete coefficient ring. Construct the relevant p-adic re-expansion maps and their cocycle law.

## HC.4. Arithmetic connectedness and rigidity

Define adjacency of cyclotomic orders through prime-power ratios and p-adic separatedness of R. Prove the cyclotomic resultant/comaximality lemmas and the passage from a connected chain of orders to injective restriction. Implement Habiro Theorem 4.1 with its full chain hypothesis. Prove the rootwise Taylor injectivity of Theorem 5.2 with its coefficient-domain and separation hypotheses.

For ℤ and all positive orders, deduce that a single Taylor expansion determines the completed element. Separately prove the evaluation uniqueness theorem with the precise infinite-set condition of Theorem 6.1. A finite set of values cannot determine a general element; infinitude by itself is not substituted for the source's adjacency condition. Include the non-surjectivity examples of §7.4 so that injective Taylor maps are never treated as isomorphisms.

## HC.5. Modules, inversion of primes and components

For a module M define the compatible completion of M[q] by the same ideals, with a complete module structure over R[q]^S. Prove functoriality, finite-direct-sum comparison, continuous scalar action and the hypotheses for exactness or a derived-limit correction. Extend the connectedness/injectivity theorem using the actual p-adic separation of M, as in Habiro §7.3.

Prove Chinese-remainder decomposition for disconnected collections of cyclotomic factors under the exact comaximality hypotheses. Compute R=ℚ and R=ℤ[1/p] examples: inversion changes which orders are adjacent. Do not infer domain or connectedness properties of the completion merely from those of R. Compare restricted-root-order rings with localizations and show where naive localization of the completed ring fails to give the desired universal property.

## HC.6. Interfaces and acceptance examples

Export complete topological rings, quotient projections, evaluation/Taylor maps, p-adic substitutions and ordinary completed modules to [HabiroNumberFields](../HabiroNumberFields/README.md). Export the ordinary comparison objects and order-change maps to [HabiroRings](../HabiroRings/README.md) HR.2, which constructs derived Habiro completion using EnhancedDerivedSheaves. [HabiroCohomologyFoundations](../HabiroCohomologyFoundations/README.md) then consumes that derived API; no completion functor is assumed exact here. The links to later consumers do not make HC depend on their completed theorems.

Test ℤ at q=1 and q=−1, a primitive third root, the polynomial 1−q, the factorial series ∑P_N, q-inversion, and a coefficient ring with an inverted prime. Check finite-precision algorithms, the difference between value and full Taylor series, and the map from the classical ℤ-Habiro ring to GSWZ's rational-number-field case. Every claimed element carries a compatible quotient family rather than just a divergent formal expression.
